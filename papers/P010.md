## 2. Research Background

Modern networked systems (smart grids, smart transportation, water/gas distribution, autonomous robotics) are built from large numbers of *homogeneous* components (generators, vehicles, valves) that belong to *heterogeneous* subsystems coupled to one another — e.g., transportation affects power demand, water networks use power-driven actuators [1], [2], [3]. Prior game-theoretic work has largely treated these two levels separately: mean-field/aggregative game theory solves the "many homogeneous agents" level [5], [7], while multipopulation mean-field games [6], [8], [9], coordinator-mediated multipopulation games [10]–[14] address interactions *among* populations. The author — a main contributor to the mean-field-type games engineering literature [4], [17], [18], [20] — proposes a two-level model that nests a stochastic aggregative (mean-field-type) game *inside* each homogeneous population (microscopic/intrapopulation level) and an LQ differential game *among* the heterogeneous populations (macroscopic/interpopulation level), the latter constrained by a possibly time-varying directed graph.

---

## 3. Research Problem and Gap

**Problem.** Compute equilibrium strategies and value functions for a society of $c \in \mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}$ populations, each containing a large number $n^p$ of homogeneous decision-makers, where (i) each agent $i \in \mathcal{I}^p$ obeys an SDE driven by both an intra-population drift and the population-level aggregate control, and plays a noncooperative aggregative game; (ii) each population $p$, characterized by its aggregative state $\bar{x}^p_t$ and control $v^p_t$, plays a differential game against the other populations over a directed graph $G=(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E},A)$, either noncooperatively (Nash) or cooperatively (team optimum).

**[Authors' claim of the gap]** (p. 2, col. 1–2): "Both works [6] and [9] consider interpopulation interactions but do not study the effect of intrapopulation strategic decisions in combination with interpopulation strategies"; coordinator-based multipopulation approaches [11], [12], [13], [14] "consider an interaction among different populations by means of a designated leader or coordinator," while the present article "proposes a model for interpopulation and intrapopulation interactions" without coordinators, over graphs, combining "both deterministic differential games and stochastic aggregative games."

**[Verified from cited-text context as printed in this paper]** The cited multipopulation mean-field works [6] (Cirant, multi-population MFG with Neumann conditions), [8] (Feleqi, ergodic MFG for several populations), [9] (Bauso, consensus via multi-population robust MFG) are described as population-level (macroscopic) formulations only; [12] (Kebriaei et al., TAC 2021, multipopulation aggregative equilibrium-seeking) and [13] (Ghavami et al., IEEE CSL 2023, EV charging) are aggregative *static/algorithmic* equilibrium-seeking schemes, not dynamic-game two-level models. The claim that the combination "intra + inter" with graph-constrained deterministic interpopulation dynamics and stochastic intrapopulation dynamics is not simultaneously handled in these references is consistent with the descriptions given in the paper's own related-work paragraph; I could not independently verify each cited paper's content here (only what is printed in P010), so this is a *verified-against-paper's-description*, not against the cited originals. The genuinely new elements claimed — semi-explicit solutions of the coupled two-level HJB system via ansatz, and the closed-loop "inter-intra-population equilibrium" of Theorem 1 — are indeed delivered within the paper (Propositions 1–3, Theorem 1), so the gap claim is internally substantiated.

## 4. Core Contributions

1. **Modeling (two-level game)**: A multipopulation model in which every agent $i\in\mathcal{I}^p$ obeys an SDE (eq. (5), p. 4) whose drift contains both agent-level terms and the population aggregates $\bar{x}^p_t,\bar{u}^p_t$, and every population obeys an ODE (eq. (1), p. 3) whose right-hand side aggregates the intra-population behavior $f^p$ (eq. (2a)) and the graph-coupled interpopulation control $g^p$ (eq. (2b)). Heterogeneity is allowed *across* populations; homogeneity is required *within* each population (p. 2: heterogeneous populations can be split into homogeneous subclusters).
2. **Solution-concept framework (theory)**: Definitions of interpopulation/intrapopulation best response and Nash equilibria (Definitions 1–6, pp. 3–5), plus HJB backward PDE systems for the three game problems — intrapopulation (9a)–(9b) with a diffusion term, interpopulation noncooperative (14a)–(14b), and interpopulation cooperative (17a)–(17b).
3. **Semi-explicit LQ solutions (theory)**: For quadratic costs and linear dynamics, an ansatz-based reduction of each HJB PDE to backward ODEs: Proposition 1 (intrapopulation Nash, 6 scalar ODEs (20a)–(20f)), Proposition 2 (interpopulation Nash, matrix ODEs for $P^p_t,\beta^p_t,\delta^p_t$), Proposition 3 (interpopulation team, matrix ODEs (36a)–(36c)).
4. **Closed-loop combination (theory)**: Theorem 1 (pp. 9–10, eqs. (44a)–(44i)) merges Propositions 1–2 into a single "inter-intra-population equilibrium" whose backward ODEs depend on neither the interpopulation nor the intrapopulation strategies, with a fixed-graph $A$ entering explicitly.
5. **Time-varying graphs at no extra cost (modeling remark)**: Because the system parameters are already time-dependent, the adjacency matrix can be taken as $A_t=[a_{pq,t}]$ without additional complexity (p. 3, remark under eq. (2b)); exercised numerically in scenario 2 of Section V.
6. **Numerical illustration (experiment)**: A 3-population, 3000-agent simulation (Section V, pp. 10–12, Table I, Figs. 3–4) demonstrating two-time-scale behavior: fast intra-population consensus, then slower interpopulation consensus of the aggregative states, and density concentration with vanishing variance; comparison of a fixed path graph vs. a switching (complete↔path) topology.

## 5. Key Mathematical Results

### 5.1 Model class, information structure, costs, termination

**Society** (p. 2, Sec. II): $\mathcal{I}=\{1,\dots,n\}$, partitioned into $\mathcal{D}=\{1,\dots,c\}$, $\mathcal{I}^p\subset\mathcal{I}$ with $\mathrm{card}(\mathcal{I}^p)=n^p$ large, $\cup_p\mathcal{I}^p=\mathcal{I}$, $\cap_p\mathcal{I}^p=\{\emptyset\}$. Time interval $[t_0,t_f]$, finite horizon.

**Microscopic dynamics** (eq. (5), p. 4): for $i\in\mathcal{I}^p$,
$$dx^i_t = g^p(t,v,A)\,dt + f^i(t,x^p,u^p)\,dt + \sigma^p_t\,dW^i_t,$$
with drift (eq. (6)) $f^i(t,x^p,u^p)=\mu^p_t x^i_t+\bar{\mu}^p_t\bar{x}^p_t+b^p_t u^i_t+\bar{b}^p_t\bar{u}^p_t$, and $\sigma^p_t\in\mathbb{R}$ a state- and input-independent diffusion. Aggregates (unnumbered displays, pp. 3–4): $\bar{x}^p_t=\frac{1}{n^p}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}^p}x^i_t$, $\bar{u}^p_t=\frac{1}{n^p}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}^p}u^i_t$. Note the equations (3), (4) do **not** exist in the numbering — the aggregation displays are unnumbered; numbering jumps from (2b) to (5).

**Macroscopic dynamics** (eq. (1), p. 3): $\dot{\bar{x}}^p_t=g^p(t,v,A)+f^p(t,\bar{x}^p,\bar{u}^p)$, with (eq. (2a)) $f^p=(\mu^p_t+\bar{\mu}^p_t)\bar{x}^p_t+(b^p_t+\bar{b}^p_t)\bar{u}^p_t$ and (eq. (2b)) $g^p(t,v,A)=v^p_t+\sum_{q\in\mathcal{D}\setminus\{p\}}a_{pq}v^q_t$. Consistency (p. 4): the macroscopic ODE is the large-$n^p$ average of the SDEs, the Martingale noise averaging to zero. Graph: directed, $a_{pq}=1$ iff $(p,q)\in\mathcal{E}$ meaning "$v^q_t$ affects population $p$"; $A_t$ may be time-varying (p. 3).

**Information structure**: agents' admissible controls are progressively measurable w.r.t. the filtration generated by $\{W^i_t\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}}$ (below eq. (7), p. 4); the interpopulation problem in (10) is stated with $v^p(\cdot)\in\mathcal{V}^p$ but no explicit observability restriction; crucially, the author states (p. 3, and again p. 8 after Prop. 2's proof) that although *interactions* are distributed (graph-structured), the *computation* of the solution in Proposition 2/Theorem 1 is **centralized** ("the computation of the solution, according to Proposition 2, requires centralized information as the backward ODEs are coupled with one another").

**Costs.** Intrapopulation (Sec. III-A, p. 4): $J^i(t_0,x^p,u^p)=\psi^i(t_f,x^p_{t_f})+\int_{t_0}^{t_f}L^i(t,x^p_t,u^p_t)\,dt$, specified in Sec. IV-A (p. 6) as
$$\psi^i(t_f,x^p_{t_f})=\tfrac12 q_{t_f}\left(x^i_{t_f}-\bar{x}^p_{t_f}\right)^2,\qquad L^i=\tfrac12 q_t(x^i_t-\bar{x}^p_t)^2+\tfrac12 r_t(u^i_t)^2+\tfrac12\bar{r}_t(\bar{u}^p_t)^2,$$
i.e., a *relative-to-population-mean* quadratic penalty (consensus incentive) penalizing both individual and aggregate control effort. Interpopulation noncooperative (p. 7, before Prop. 2): $\psi^p(t_f,\bar{x}_{t_f})=\frac12\tilde{q}^p_{t_f}(\bar{x}^p_{t_f})^2=\frac12\langle\tilde{Q}^p_{t_f}\bar{x}_{t_f},\bar{x}_{t_f}\rangle$, $L^p=\frac12\tilde{q}^p_t(\bar{x}^p_t)^2+\frac12\tilde{r}^p_t(v^p_t)^2$ with $\tilde{Q}^p_{t_f},\tilde{Q}^p_t\succeq 0$, $\tilde{r}^p_t>0$. Cooperative (p. 8): $\hat{\psi}=\frac12\langle\hat{Q}_{t_f}\bar{x}_{t_f},\bar{x}_{t_f}\rangle$, $\hat{L}=\frac12\langle\hat{Q}_t\bar{x}_t,\bar{x}_t\rangle+\frac12\langle\hat{R}_t v_t,v_t\rangle$.

**Well-posedness assumptions** (eqs. (8a)–(8b), p. 4): $J^i$ continuous, strictly convex, coercive in $u^i$ (existence); integrability $\int_{t_0}^{t_f}(|\mu^p_t|+|\bar{\mu}^p_t|+|b^p_t|+|\bar{b}^p_t|)\,dt<+\infty$ and $\int_{t_0}^{t_f}(\sigma^p_t)^2\,dt<+\infty$, $\mathbb{E}[(x^i_t)^2]<+\infty$; initial condition independent of the Brownian motion. Analogous convexity/coercivity assumed for $J^p$ (below (10), p. 5).

**Termination/winning conditions**: none — this is an infinite-population, finite-horizon optimal-control/Nash problem, not a pursuit-evasion capture/escape game; there is no terminal set, barrier, or reach-avoid condition. The "game end" is simply $t_f$.

**HJB systems.** Intrapopulation (eq. (9a)–(9b), p. 4): $\frac{\partial}{\partial t}h^i(t,x^p)=-H^i\!\left(u^p,x^p,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}h^i(t,x^p)\right)$, $h^i(t_f,x^p_{t_f})=\psi^i$, with Hamiltonian containing the expectation of the drift term plus $\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}h^i\,g^p(t,v,A)\right]$ and the Itô term $\frac12\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{\partial^2}{\partial(x^i)^2}h^i(t,x^p)(\sigma^p_t)^2\right]$. Interpopulation (eq. (14a)–(14b), p. 5) and cooperative (eq. (17a)–(17b), p. 6) analogous, deterministic, with inner products $\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{x}}h^p, g(t,v,A)\rangle$, $\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{x}}h^p, f(t,\bar{x},\bar{u})\rangle$.

**LQ closed-loop aggregation** (p. 5, eq. (11) and unnumbered displays): for the LQ structure the aggregate control is affine in the state, $\bar{u}_t=-\Xi_t\cdot\bar{x}_t-\Omega_t$, so that $f(t,\bar{x},\bar{u})=[M_t-B_t\Xi_t]\bar{x}_t-B_t\Omega_t$, $g(t,v,A)=Av_t$ with $A=[A^1\ A^2\ \cdots\ A^c]$, and $\dot{\bar{x}}_t=M_t\bar{x}_t+B_t\bar{u}_t+Av_t$.

### 5.2 Proposition 1 (p. 6, eqs. (20a)–(20f)) — Intrapopulation Nash equilibrium

**Statement.** Let $v^p$ be given for all $p\in\mathcal{D}$ (i.e., the population-level input is exogenous at this stage). Then the Nash equilibrium of the intrapopulation game for $p\in\mathcal{D}$ is, $\forall i\in\mathcal{I}^p$:
$$(u^i_t)^*=-\frac{\alpha_t b^p_t}{r_t}x^i_t-\frac{\gamma_t b^p_t}{r_t}\bar{x}^p_t-\frac{\zeta_t b^p_t}{r_t},\qquad (\bar{u}^p_t)^*=-\frac{\alpha_t+\gamma_t}{r_t}\,b^p_t\,\bar{x}^p_t-\frac{\zeta_t b^p_t}{r_t},$$
with value $J^i(t_0,x^p,(u^p)^*)=\frac12\alpha_0(x^0_i)^2+\frac12\beta_0(\bar{x}^p_0)^2+\gamma_0 x^i_0\bar{x}^p_0+\zeta_0 x^i_0+\eta_0\bar{x}^p_0+\delta_0$, where $\alpha_t,\beta_t,\gamma_t,\zeta_t,\eta_t,\delta_t$ solve the six scalar backward ODEs (20a)–(20f) with terminal conditions $\alpha_{t_f}=q_{t_f}$, $\beta_{t_f}=q_{t_f}$, $\gamma_{t_f}=-2q_{t_f}$, $\zeta_{t_f}=\eta_{t_f}=\delta_{t_f}=0$. Representative ODEs, as printed:
- (20a): $-\dot{\alpha}_t=2\alpha_t\mu^p_t-\alpha_t^2(b^p_t)^2/r_t+q_t$ (a scalar Riccati equation in $\alpha_t$).
- (20b): $-\dot{\beta}_t=2\beta_t\left[(\mu^p_t+\bar{\mu}^p_t)-(b^p_t+\bar{b}^p_t)\frac{\alpha_t+\gamma_t}{r_t}b^p_t\right]+\frac{\bar{r}_t(\alpha_t+\gamma_t)^2}{r_t^2}(b^p_t)^2+q_t-\frac{\gamma_t^2(b^p_t)^2}{r_t}+2\gamma_t\bar{\mu}^p_t-2\frac{\alpha_t+\gamma_t}{r_t}\gamma_t b^p_t\bar{b}^p_t$.
- (20c)–(20f) are the linear/affine counterparts for $\eta_t,\zeta_t,\gamma_t,\delta_t$; they contain the exogenous interpopulation input through $g^p(t,v,A)$ (e.g., (20c) contains $\beta_t\left[g^p(t,v,A)-(b^p_t+\bar{b}^p_t)\frac{\zeta_t b^p_t}{r_t}\right]$ and $\gamma_t g^p(t,v,A)$) and the Itô correction $\frac12\alpha_t(\sigma^p_t)^2$ in (20f).

**Assumptions used**: quadratic costs as in Sec. 5.1; $r_t>0$, $\bar{r}_t\geq 0$; integrability (8); $v^p$ square-integrable; agents homogeneous within $p$.

**Proof approach** (pp. 6–7): (1) *Ansatz* for the value functional $h^i(t,x^p)=\frac12\alpha_t(x^i_t)^2+\frac12\beta_t(\bar{x}^p_t)^2+\gamma_t x^i_t\bar{x}^p_t+\zeta_t x^i_t+\eta_t\bar{x}^p_t+\delta_t$ — quadratic plus cross plus linear plus constant; (2) substitute into the Hamiltonian $H^i$ (eq. (9a)), collecting terms in $u^i_t$; (3) pointwise minimization over $u^i_t$ (first-order condition, $\partial/\partial u^i_t[r_t(u^i_t)^2/2+(\alpha_t b^p_t x^i_t+\gamma_t b^p_t\bar{x}^p_t+\zeta_t b^p_t)u^i_t]=0$) gives the stated best response — the decisive step; (4) substitute $(u^i_t)^*$ back to get the minimized Hamiltonian (eq. (23), p. 7) and compute $\partial h^i/\partial t$ along the closed-loop drift (eq. (24), p. 7) — note (24) already uses the *aggregate* closed loop $\dot{\bar{x}}^p_t=g^p+(b^p+\bar{b}^p)\frac{\alpha_t+\gamma_t}{r_t}b^p\bar{x}^p+\dots$, i.e., the mean-field consistency is built in; (5) match coefficients of $(x^i)^2,(\bar{x}^p)^2,x^i\bar{x}^p,x^i,\bar{x}^p,1$ in $H^i+\partial_t h^i=0$, yielding (20a)–(20f) — the decisive equality is the coefficient matching of (23)+(24)=0; (6) match terminal condition at $t_f$: the printed identity is $\frac12\alpha_{t_f}(x^i_{t_f})^2+\frac12\beta_{t_f}(\bar{x}^p_{t_f})^2+\gamma_{t_f}x^i_{t_f}\bar{x}^p_{t_f}+\zeta_{t_f}x^i_{t_f}+\eta_{t_f}\bar{x}^p_{t_f}+\delta_{t_f}=\frac{q_{t_f}}{2}(x^i_{t_f})^2-q_{t_f}x^i_{t_f}\bar{x}^p_{t_f}+\frac{q_{t_f}}{2}(\bar{x}^p_{t_f})^2$ (p. 7), giving the announced boundary conditions. All steps analytic; result is global in time (valid on $[t_0,t_f]$ provided the ODE system has a solution — no blow-up analysis is given) and exact, not numerical.

**Mean-field consistency check** (mine, not the author's): averaging $(u^i_t)^*$ over $i\in\mathcal{I}^p$ gives $\frac1{n^p}\sum_i(u^i_t)^*=-\frac{\alpha_t+\gamma_t}{r_t}b^p_t\bar{x}^p_t-\frac{\zeta_t b^p_t}{r_t}=(\bar{u}^p_t)^*$ — the fixed-point requirement $\bar{u}^p_t=\frac1{n^p}\sum_i u^i_t$ is satisfied, so the best response against the aggregates is consistent with the aggregates of best responses. The paper does not display this verification explicitly.

**Physical meaning**: each agent pushes its state toward the population mean; the population mean, in turn, is steered by $v^p$ at the macro level. $\gamma_t\neq 0$ captures the coupling between own state and population mean in the value; the Itô term $\frac12\alpha_t(\sigma^p_t)^2$ is the only place the diffusion enters the cost.

**Extensibility**: scalar state only, "yet it is easily extended to the vector-valued case as the same method is applicable" (p. 2); extension to jumps/regime switching and input constraints referenced to [17], [20].

**Inconsistency flag (proof gap)**: the printed terminal condition $\gamma_{t_f}=-2q_{t_f}$ (p. 6, after (20f); repeated in Theorem 1, p. 10) contradicts the paper's own terminal matching identity on p. 7, whose cross-term coefficient gives $\gamma_{t_f}=-q_{t_f}$ for $\psi^i=\frac12 q_{t_f}(x^i_{t_f}-\bar{x}^p_{t_f})^2$. Indeed $\frac12 q (x^i)^2 - q\,x^i\bar{x}^p+\frac12 q(\bar{x}^p)^2=\frac12 q(x^i-\bar{x}^p)^2$ requires the cross coefficient $-q$. Hence either the boundary value of $\gamma$ should be $-q_{t_f}$, or the terminal cost in the statement of Sec. IV-A is misprinted. Downstream ODEs (20b)–(20e) are unaffected; only the integration of $\gamma_t$ backward in time changes. This is the paper's main internal inconsistency.

### 5.3 Proposition 2 (pp. 7–8, eqs. (28)–(32)) — Interpopulation (noncooperative) Nash equilibrium

**Statement.** Let $\bar{u}_t\triangleq[\bar{u}^p_t]_{p\in\mathcal{D}}$ be given (the intra-population aggregate control is exogenous at this stage). Then the differential Nash equilibrium of the interpopulation noncooperative game is
$$(v^p_t)^*=-\frac{1}{\tilde{r}^p_t}\langle A^p,P^p_t\bar{x}_t\rangle-\frac{1}{\tilde{r}^p_t}\langle A^p,\beta^p_t\rangle,\qquad\forall p\in\mathcal{D},$$
with value $J^p(t_0,\bar{x},v^*)=\frac12\langle P^p_0\bar{x}_0,\bar{x}_0\rangle+\langle\beta^p_0,\bar{x}_0\rangle+\delta^p_0$, where $P^p_t$ (symmetric), $\beta^p_t$, $\delta^p_t$ solve the coupled matrix backward ODEs printed on pp. 7–8 (unnumbered in Prop. 2; restated with numbers (44g)–(44i) in Theorem 1 with $M_t$ in place of $[M_t-B_t\Xi_t]$ and $\bar{u}_t$ in place of $\Omega_t$):
$$-\dot{P}^p_t=-\frac{1}{\tilde{r}^p_t}[P^p_t]^\top A^p[A^p]^\top P^p_t-2[P^p_t]^\top\!\!\sum_{q\in\mathcal{D}\setminus\{p\}}\frac{1}{\tilde{r}^q_t}A^q[A^q]^\top P^q_t+\tilde{Q}^p_t+2M_t^\top P^p_t,$$
$$-\dot{\beta}^p_t=-\sum_{q\in\mathcal{D}\setminus\{p\}}\frac{1}{\tilde{r}^q_t}[A^q]^\top A^q[P^q_t]^\top\beta^p_t-\sum_{q\in\mathcal{D}\setminus\{p\}}\frac{1}{\tilde{r}^q_t}[P^p_t]^\top A^q[A^q]^\top\beta^q_t+M_t^\top\beta^p_t+[P^p_t]^\top B_t\bar{u}_t,$$
$$-\dot{\delta}^p_t=-\frac{1}{2\tilde{r}^p_t}\langle A^p[A^p]^\top\beta^p_t,\beta^p_t\rangle-\Big\langle\beta^p_t,\sum_{q\in\mathcal{D}\setminus\{p\}}\frac{1}{\tilde{r}^q_t}A^q[A^q]^\top\beta^q_t\Big\rangle+\langle\beta^p_t,B_t\bar{u}_t\rangle,$$
with $P^p_{t_f}=\tilde{Q}^p_{t_f}$, $\beta^p_{t_f}=0$, $\delta^p_{t_f}=0$.

**Proof approach** (p. 8): (1) ansatz $h^p(t,\bar{x})=\frac12\langle P^p_t\bar{x}_t,\bar{x}_t\rangle+\langle\beta^p_t,\bar{x}_t\rangle+\delta^p_t$ (eq. (28), $P^p_t$ symmetric); (2) build Hamiltonian $H^p$ = $\min_{v^p}\{\frac12\tilde{r}^p_t(v^p_t)^2+\langle P^p_t\bar{x}_t+\beta^p_t,A^p v^p_t\rangle+\langle P^p_t\bar{x}_t+\beta^p_t,\sum_{q\neq p}A^q v^q_t\rangle+\frac12\langle\tilde{Q}^p_t\bar{x}_t,\bar{x}_t\rangle+\langle M_t^\top P^p_t\bar{x}_t,\bar{x}_t\rangle+\langle M_t^\top\beta^p_t,\bar{x}_t\rangle+\langle[P^p_t]^\top B_t\bar{u}_t,\bar{x}_t\rangle+\langle\beta^p_t,B_t\bar{u}_t\rangle\}$ — note the rivals' controls $v^q_t$ enter the minimizing bracket; (3) FOC in $v^p_t$ gives (29) = the equilibrium; (4) substitution yields the minimized Hamiltonian (30); (5) $\partial_t h^p$ (31); (6) coefficient matching in $H^p+\partial_t h^p=0$ (the decisive equality) gives the three ODEs; (7) boundary matching (32). Analytic; global on $[t_0,t_f]$ modulo ODE solvability.

**Physical meaning**: a standard coupled-Riccati multi-player LQ game among $c$ "macro players" whose individual "plants" are the aggregative states; the cross terms $2[P^p_t]^\top\sum_{q\neq p}\tilde{r}_q^{-1}A^q[A^q]^\top P^q_t$ encode the best-response coupling through the graph column space of the rivals' $A^q$.

**Key remark printed by the author** (p. 8): the model captures noncentralized interactions via $G$, but the computation of the solution requires centralized information because the backward ODEs are coupled — flagged honestly, with [16] cited for distributed differential game computation ideas.

### 5.4 Proposition 3 (pp. 8–9, eqs. (34)–(41)) — Interpopulation cooperative (team) solution

**Statement.** With $\bar{u}_t$ given and social costs $\hat{\psi},\hat{L}$ as in Sec. 5.1, the cooperative optimum ("differential equilibrium of the interpopulation cooperative game problem") is
$$(v_t)^*=-(\hat{R}_t)^{-1}A^\top\hat{P}_t\bar{x}_t-(\hat{R}_t)^{-1}A^\top\hat{\beta}_t \tag{34/40}$$
with value $\hat{J}^p(t_0,\bar{x},v^*)=\frac12\langle\hat{P}_0\bar{x}_0,\bar{x}_0\rangle+\langle\hat{\beta}_0,\bar{x}_0\rangle+\hat{\delta}_0$ (eq. (35)) and backward ODEs (36a)–(36c):
$$-\dot{\hat{P}}_t=\hat{Q}_t+2\hat{P}_t^\top M_t-\hat{P}_t^\top A(\hat{R}_t)^{-1}A^\top\hat{P}_t,\qquad -\dot{\hat{\beta}}_t=M_t^\top\hat{\beta}_t+\hat{P}_t^\top B_t\bar{u}_t-\hat{P}_t^\top A(\hat{R}_t)^{-1}A^\top\hat{\beta}_t,\qquad -\dot{\hat{\delta}}_t=-\frac12\langle A(\hat{R}_t)^{-1}A^\top\hat{\beta}_t,\hat{\beta}_t\rangle+\langle\hat{\beta}_t,B_t\bar{u}_t\rangle,$$
with $\hat{P}_{t_f}=\hat{Q}_{t_f}$, $\hat{\beta}_{t_f}=\hat{\delta}_{t_f}=0$ (eqs. (36a)–(36c), p. 8).

**Proof approach** (pp. 8–9): ansatz (38) $\hat{h}(t,\bar{x})=\frac12\langle\hat{P}_t\bar{x}_t,\bar{x}_t\rangle+\langle\hat{\beta}_t,\bar{x}_t\rangle+\hat{\delta}_t$ ($\hat{P}_t$ symmetric); Hamiltonian (39) minimized over the *whole vector* $v\in V$ (team problem, cf. [19]); FOC gives (40) (= (34)); substitution gives (41); matching gives (36a)–(36c). This is exactly the LQR/team Riccati recursion, i.e., the cooperative case "corresponds to an optimal control problem [19]" (p. 5). Analytic; global modulo ODE solvability.

**Physical meaning**: populations jointly minimize the sum of all individual quadratic costs — a Pareto/team solution; comparing Prop. 2 vs. Prop. 3 quantifies the price of anarchy/leadership at the macro level (not quantified in the paper).

**Note**: in both Prop. 2 and Prop. 3, $\bar{u}_t$ is treated as an exogenous input even though $\bar{u}_t=-\Xi_t\bar{x}_t-\Omega_t$ depends on $\bar{x}_t$; the closed-loop reconciliation is done only in Theorem 1 for the *noncooperative* macro case. **No cooperative counterpart of Theorem 1** (cooperative macro + intra simultaneously) is provided.

### 5.5 Theorem 1 (pp. 9–10, eqs. (44a)–(44i)) — Inter-intra-population equilibrium (main result)

**Statement.** The inter-intra-population equilibrium with noncooperative populations is given by
$$(v^p_t)^*=-\frac{1}{\tilde{r}^p_t}\langle A^p,P^p_t\bar{x}_t\rangle-\frac{1}{\tilde{r}^p_t}\langle A^p,\beta^p_t\rangle\ \ \forall p\in\mathcal{D},\qquad (u^i_t)^*=-\frac{\alpha_t b^p_t}{r_t}x^i_t-\frac{\gamma_t b^p_t}{r_t}\bar{x}^p_t-\frac{\zeta_t b^p_t}{r_t}\ \ \forall i\in\mathcal{I}^p,$$
$$(\bar{u}_t)^*=-\Xi_t\cdot\bar{x}_t-\Omega_t,\qquad \Xi_t=\frac{\alpha_t+\gamma_t}{r_t}\cdot\mathrm{diag}([b^1_t\ \cdots\ b^c_t]),\qquad \Omega_t=\frac{\zeta_t}{r_t}\cdot\mathrm{diag}([b^1_t\ \cdots\ b^c_t]),$$
with optimal costs as in Props. 1–2, where the coefficients solve the coupled backward ODE system (44a)–(44i) with boundary conditions $\alpha_{t_f}=q_{t_f}$, $\beta_{t_f}=q_{t_f}$, $\gamma_{t_f}=-2q_{t_f}$ [sic — see flag in 5.2], $\zeta_{t_f}=\eta_{t_f}=\delta_{t_f}=0$, $P^p_{t_f}=\tilde{Q}^p_{t_f}$, $\beta^p_{t_f}=\delta^p_{t_f}=0$. Structure of the system:
- (44a): unchanged scalar Riccati for $\alpha_t$.
- (44b)–(44f): the intrapopulation ODEs now *coupled to the macro solution*: every term that contained $g^p(t,v,A)$ in (20b)–(20f) is replaced by the closed-loop $g^p(t,v^*,A)$ expansion, adding terms like $-(\beta_t+\gamma_t)\frac{1}{\tilde{r}^p_t}[(A^p)^\top P^p_t]_p-(\beta_t+\gamma_t)\sum_{q\in\mathcal{D}\setminus\{p\}}\frac{a_{pq}}{\tilde{r}^q_t}[(A^q)^\top P^q_t]_p$ in (44b), and — importantly — *explicitly state-dependent* terms $\cdot\bar{x}^k_t$ in (44c), (44d), (44f).
- (44g)–(44i): the macro ODEs of Prop. 2 with $M_t\to[M_t-B_t\Xi_t]$ and $B_t\bar{u}_t\to-B_t\Omega_t$:
$$-\dot{P}^p_t=-\frac{1}{\tilde{r}^p_t}[P^p_t]^\top A^p[A^p]^\top P^p_t-2[P^p_t]^\top\sum_{q\neq p}\frac{1}{\tilde{r}^q_t}A^q[A^q]^\top P^q_t+\tilde{Q}^p_t+2[M_t-B_t\Xi_t]^\top P^p_t,$$
$$-\dot{\beta}^p_t=-\sum_{q\neq p}\frac{1}{\tilde{r}^q_t}[A^q]^\top A^q[P^q_t]^\top\beta^p_t-\sum_{q\neq p}\frac{1}{\tilde{r}^q_t}[P^p_t]^\top A^q[A^q]^\top\beta^q_t+[M_t-B_t\Xi_t]^\top\beta^p_t-[P^p_t]^\top B_t\Omega_t,$$
$$-\dot{\delta}^p_t=-\frac{1}{2\tilde{r}^p_t}\langle A^p[A^p]^\top\beta^p_t,\beta^p_t\rangle-\Big\langle\beta^p_t,\sum_{q\neq p}\frac{1}{\tilde{r}^q_t}A^q[A^q]^\top\beta^q_t\Big\rangle-\langle\beta^p_t,B_t\Omega_t\rangle.$$

**Proof approach** (p. 10): substitution only — "straight-forwardly obtained from Proposition 2 and by replacing the optimal control input in Proposition 1 of the form $(\bar{u}_t)^*=-\Xi_t\cdot\bar{x}_t-\Omega_t$. Hence, from Proposition 1 by computing $g^p(t,v,A)$ as follows": the printed expansion writes $g^p(t,v^*,A)=-\frac{1}{\tilde{r}^p_t}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{D}\setminus\{p\}}[(A^p)^\top P^p_t]_k\bar{x}^k_t-\sum_{q\neq p}\sum_{k\neq p}\frac{a_{pq}}{\tilde{r}^q_t}[(A^q)^\top P^q_t]_k\bar{x}^k_t-\frac{1}{\tilde{r}^p_t}[(A^p)^\top P^p_t]_p\bar{x}^p_t-\sum_{q\neq p}\frac{a_{pq}}{\tilde{r}^q_t}[(A^q)^\top P^q_t]_p\bar{x}^p_t-\frac{1}{\tilde{r}^p_t}(A^p)^\top\beta^q_t\,{}^{[\text{sic, should be }\beta^p_t]}-\sum_{q\neq p}a_{pq}\frac{1}{\tilde{r}^q_t}(A^q)^\top\beta^q_t$. The decisive equation is this expansion of the population-level drift under the interpopulation best response; inserting it into (20b)–(20f) and into the $x^i_t$/$\bar{x}^p_t$/$1$ coefficient matches yields (44a)–(44f).

**Existence discussion** (p. 10, informal): both problems have a solution because the cost functionals are "continuous, strictly convex and coercive in the decision variables" and all parameters satisfy integrability (8); for any square-integrable interpopulation strategy $v^p(\cdot)$ with $\int_{t_0}^{t_f}(v^p_t)^2dt<\infty$, the intrapopulation problem admits an equilibrium; the interpopulation problem depends only on $v^p_t$ and has a solution by coercivity; "Thus, the interpopulation optimal strategy can be found using the particular intrapopulation optimal strategy." This is a heuristic two-stage nesting argument, **not** a formal fixed-point/existence theorem for the simultaneous system (no Kakutani/Tikhonov argument, no uniqueness claim).

**Local/global, analytic/numerical**: analytic, global on $[t_0,t_f]$ conditional on solvability of the 9-fold coupled backward ODE system; no discussion of blow-up, monotonicity, or contraction that would guarantee a (unique) solution of the coupled system.

**Physical meaning**: the complete closed loop in Fig. 1 — agents best-respond to their population's aggregate and to the population-level input; populations best-respond to the other populations' strategies; the two levels close through $\Xi_t,\Omega_t$ (intra → macro) and through $g^p(t,v^*,A)$ (macro → intra). The graph $A$ enters the intra-level ODEs only *after* this closure.

**Extensibility**: author notes (p. 10) the interaction "may become very involved as conditions over both cost functionals and system dynamics are relaxed, e.g., nonquadratic cost and nonlinear dynamics."

**Proof gaps / typos found in Theorem 1 (verified against rendered pages 9–10)**:
1. *State-dependent "ODEs"*: (44c), (44d), (44f) contain the running state components $\bar{x}^k_t$ ($k\neq p$) multiplicatively, e.g., $-(\beta_t+\gamma_t)\Big[\frac{1}{\tilde{r}^p_t}\sum_{k\neq p}[(A^p)^\top P^p_t]_k+\sum_{q\neq p}\sum_{k\neq p}\frac{a_{pq}}{\tilde{r}^q_t}[(A^q)^\top P^q_t]_k\Big]\cdot\bar{x}^k_t$ in (44c). Strictly, the $\zeta,\eta,\delta$ equations are therefore not closed time ODEs; the ansatz $h^i(t,x^i,\bar{x}^p)$ cannot represent cross terms in $\bar{x}^k\ (k\neq p)$ introduced by the closed-loop $g^p$. The paper neither comments on this nor restricts $A$ (e.g., to make those coefficients vanish). This is a genuine technical looseness in the two-level closure.
2. *Index typo*: in (44c), (44d) and in the proof's $g^p$ expansion, the own-population term is printed $(A^p)^\top\beta^q_t$ with superscript $q$ where $\beta^p_t$ is required by the derivation ($v^{p*}= -\tilde{r}_p^{-1}(\langle A^p,P^p\bar{x}\rangle+\langle A^p,\beta^p\rangle)$).
3. *Terminal condition*: $\gamma_{t_f}=-2q_{t_f}$ contradicts the terminal matching identity in Prop. 1's proof (should be $-q_{t_f}$; see 5.2).
4. Minor: Definition 6 (p. 5) writes $(v^p_t)^*\in BR^p((u^{-p}_t)^*)$ — should be $v^{-p}_t$; p. 5, before Def. 5, the running cost is typed "$L^i:[t_0,t_f]\times\mathbb{R}^c\times\mathbb{R}^c\to\mathbb{R}$" — should be $L^p$; eq. (34) prints $x_t$ for $\bar{x}_t$.

**Counting**: the paper contains 3 Propositions + 1 Theorem (no lemmas/corollaries) and 6 Definitions; every proof is a verification-style ansatz substitution (no measure-theoretic existence theory, no viscosity-solution argument — the ansatz is claimed to *solve* the PDE by direct substitution, which is legitimate given smoothness of the quadratic ansatz).

## 6. Simulation and Experiments

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues

**Author-stated (pp. 11–12, Sec. VI)**:
- Scalar-valued state only; extension to vector/matrix-valued states left to future work ("as in [4, Ch. 12]").
- Nonlinear / non-LQ structures not handled; ansatz-based semi-explicit solutions for nonlinear cases are future work (citing the author's own [17] line of work).
- Other stochastic processes (jump–diffusion, switching regimes) not considered.
- Weighted-graph multipopulation interaction (heterogeneous weights in (2b)) left open.
- Other solution concepts (e.g., co-opetitive games, [18]) not studied here.
- Distributed *computation* of the solutions (vs. the centralized computation actually used) is open; the paper explicitly concedes that the optimality conditions of Prop. 2/Theorem 1 "are expressed in a noncentralized manner" is **not** guaranteed (p. 3) and that Prop. 2 "requires centralized information" (p. 8).
- Computational complexity: population size does not increase the burden (homogeneity), but adding populations adds heterogeneous problems (p. 11) — asserted, not benchmarked.

**Critical observations (mine, with page/eq.)**:
1. **Not closed ODEs in Theorem 1**: the $\eta,\zeta,\delta$ ODEs (44c), (44d), (44f) contain explicit state terms $\bar{x}^k_t$; the scalar ansatz cannot absorb cross-population state components, so the two-level closure is exact only if those coefficients vanish (restrictions on $A^p$, $P^p$, or the graph that are never stated) — pp. 9–10.
2. **Terminal-condition typo** $\gamma_{t_f}=-2q_{t_f}$ vs. the matching identity requiring $-q_{t_f}$ (p. 6/p. 10 vs. p. 7) — affects numerical integration of $\gamma_t$.
3. **Existence/uniqueness**: the simultaneous two-level equilibrium existence is argued informally (p. 10); no fixed-point theorem, no uniqueness, no blow-up analysis of the coupled Riccati system (44a)–(44i); convexity arguments are invoked per level, not for the composed problem.
4. **Information asymmetry**: macro players are assumed to know/react through a centralized computation while interactions are graph-distributed; no signalling/estimation model connects the macro players' information to the microscopic state distribution.
5. **No cooperative two-level closure**: Props. 2–3 keep $\bar{u}_t$ exogenous; only the noncooperative macro case is closed in Theorem 1.
6. **Simulation reporting gaps**: cost weights, horizon, solver, and noise realization all UNREPORTED; two figure/table inconsistencies (initial means apparently swapped; switching-direction contradiction) make the numerical section not reproducible as printed (p. 11–12, Table I, Fig. 4).
7. **Verification vs. derivation**: all "proofs" are substitution verifications of a postulated ansatz; sufficiency relies on the convexity assumptions stated for the cost functionals, but no formal sufficiency theorem (e.g., verifying the HJB concavity/convexity conditions in the state) is stated or cited for the stochastic intrapopulation problem with mean-field coupling.
8. **No pursuit–evasion content**: despite appearing in a pursuit-evasion-oriented collection, this paper contains no adversarial geometric engagement (no Apollonius/barrier/reach-avoid analysis); its relevance to that literature is methodological (multi-team LQ games) rather than direct.

## 8. Citation Value

**Paragraph role**: P010 is the reference for *two-level (intra + inter) multipopulation LQ differential games over directed graphs solved by ansatz-reduced coupled backward ODEs*. In a pursuit-evasion/attack-defense paper it plays the role of (i) related-work anchor for "team-of-teams" or multi-population adversarial formulations — e.g., when defenders form homogeneous clusters (sensors, interceptors) and clusters themselves play a game; (ii) methodological precedent for reducing high-dimensional multi-agent games to low-dimensional aggregate dynamics (mean-field-type reduction) with semi-explicit Riccati-type solutions; and (iii) a modeling template for graph-constrained interactions, including time-varying topologies, among heterogeneous groups. Its limitations (centralized computation, scalar state, informal two-level existence) are useful foils when motivating distributed/barrier-based or geometric pursuit-evasion approaches.

**Suggested citation sentences**:
- "Beyond single-population mean-field-type formulations, Barreiro-Gomez [P010] couples a stochastic aggregative game inside each homogeneous population with an LQ differential game among heterogeneous populations over a directed graph, and computes the inter-intra-population Nash equilibrium semi-explicitly via coupled backward ODEs (IEEE TCNS, 2025)."
- "A two-level decomposition of the kind used in [P010] — microscopic aggregative equilibrium nested in a macroscopic differential game — provides semi-explicit LQ solutions but assumes homogeneous agents per population and a centralized computation of the macro equilibrium, which motivates the distributed/geometric treatment developed in this paper."
