## 2. Research Background
The paper studies a multiplayer **border-defense** scenario in the half plane $y \ge 0$: a team of $N$ pursuers $P_1,\dots,P_N$ must stop $M$ evaders $E_1,\dots,E_M$ from reaching the border line (the $x$-axis). If all evaders are captured before crossing, the evader team's terminal cost is the sum of the evaders' terminal $y$-coordinates, i.e., their cumulative distance from the border; pursuers maximize this quantity and evaders minimize it. The application pull is team-vs-team combat / cooperative interception with UAVs or missiles, where a commander must simultaneously solve a **discrete** assignment problem (which pursuer intercepts which evader) and a **continuous** optimal-guidance problem (headings over space and time). Classical differential game theory (Isaacs) treats either single-pursuer games or pure continuous strategies, while multi-agent work typically approximates the HJI equation numerically over the joint high-dimensional state. The authors position this article as a closed-form, real-time-implementable foundation for formally analyzing high-dimensional team conflicts by differential game theory, overcoming the curse of dimensionality.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
**Problem**: pose and completely solve the $N$-pursuer vs. $M$-evader border defense differential game (BDDG) — state feedback saddle-point strategies *plus* the optimal pursuer-to-evader assignment — in the pursuer winning region $\mathcal R_P \subset \mathbb R^{2(N+M)}$ where capture of all evaders is guaranteed (game of degree), with the strategies also answering the game of kind.

**Gap as claimed by the authors**:
- [authors' claim, p. 2345–2346] Prior multi-pursuer reach-avoid work either uses open-loop Stackelberg constructions with numerical HJB approximation over the joint state ([27] Zhou, Ding, Huang, Takei, Tomlin, Automatica 2018), or approximates the HJI solution with "semi-open-loop" strategies ([28] Chen, Zhou, Tomlin, IEEE TAC 2017, *Multiplayer reach-avoid games via pairwise outcomes*), while [33] Yan, Shi, Zhong (IEEE T-RO 2020) "focus on the game of kind and do not provide saddle-point strategies." The hybrid continuous/discrete nature of the codesign problem "has rarely been tackled within the theory of differential games" ([27]–[29] cited). — *Verification status*: the characterizations of [27], [28], [33] are the authors' summary of cited texts; in this archive they were **not independently re-verified against the cited papers' full text** (none of [27],[28] is in the local library; [33] is in the library and its barrier/task-assignment focus is consistent with the claim, but the "no saddle-point strategies" statement was not checked line-by-line). What *is* verifiable from P037 itself: no prior cited work claims a closed-form, state-feedback, assignment-inclusive Value for an $N$-vs-$M$ border-defense game.
- [authors' claim, p. 2346] "The solution presented in this article is not an approximation but the optimal solution of the game over the complete state space," with $V(\mathbf x)$ obtained in closed form, shown continuously differentiable (except dispersal surfaces) and satisfying the HJI equation. — *Caveat verified from P037 itself*: the proofs of Theorems 2–4 and Corollary 1 are **not contained in this paper**; they are deferred to the extended arXiv version [35] (arXiv:1911.03806). So the "shown" claims rest on [35].

**Constraint setting** (p. 2346): $N \ge M$ assumed; each pursuer is assigned to at most one evader ($\sum_{j=1}^M \mu_{ij} \le 1$), but several pursuers may be assigned to the same evader (exploited when $N>M$); pursuers make a **firm commitment** to the initial assignment, $\mu_{ij}(t)=\mu_{ij}(t_0)$ (no switching); speed ratios $\alpha_{ij}=v_{E_j}/v_{P_i}<1$ for all pairs (extension to slower pursuers by forbidding such assignments is asserted, not analyzed).

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Theory**: Closed-form Value and state-feedback saddle-point strategies for the $N$-vs-$M$ BDDG: $V(\mathbf x)=\max_{\iota} y_{s_\iota}(\mathbf x)$ over feasible assignments $\mathcal A_\iota$, proved (in [35]) to be continuously differentiable except at dispersal surfaces $y_{s_\iota}=y_{s_{\iota'}}$ and to satisfy the HJI equation — the game's hybrid (continuous guidance + discrete assignment) solution is obtained without numerically solving any PDE in $\mathbb R^{2(N+M)}$.
2. **Modeling**: The BDDG formulated as a zero-sum, perfect-information, Mayer-type team game with binary assignment variables $\mu_{ij}$, frozen dynamics after each individual capture time (regularization, p. 2346), and a terminal payoff $\sum_j y_{E_j}(t_f)$ that measures cumulative penetration of the border.
3. **Algorithm/Structure**: An explicit enumeration procedure: (i) feasibility pruning of assignments via Apollonius-circle/border intersection tests; (ii) pairwise capture-height metrics $\underline y_{ij}$ (7) for one-on-one matches and a closed-form two-on-one cooperative metric $V_s(\mathbf x)$ (20)–(22) for simultaneous capture by a pair of pursuers with different speeds; (iii) optimal assignment $\iota^*=\arg\max_\iota y_{s_\iota}$; (iv) all players head to printed closed-form aimpoints. Real-time computable (Table II: ≈1 s for 12 v 6).
4. **Experiment**: MATLAB assignment-computation study with 18 agents (12 pursuers vs. 6 evaders) under random initial conditions, quantifying computation time of interception coordinates/distances vs. assignment search (Table II); robustness-under-nonoptimal-play simulations are relegated to [35].
5. **Robustness result**: Corollary 1 shows the commitment assumption can be dropped in the 2-vs-2 case — the committed-assignment feedback strategies remain robust (same guaranteed payoff) for the game *without* commitment; Remark 3 notes cooperation-and-switch enlarges the pursuer winning region $\mathcal R_P$.

## 5. Key Mathematical Results

### 5.0 Model, information structure, cost, termination (pp. 2345–2347)
- **Dynamics (eq. (1))**: simple motion for all $2(N{+}M)$ players,
  $\dot x_{P_i}=v_{P_i}\cos\psi_i,\ \dot y_{P_i}=v_{P_i}\sin\psi_i,\ \dot x_{E_j}=v_{E_j}\cos\phi_j,\ \dot y_{E_j}=v_{E_j}\sin\phi_j$, with initial conditions $x_{P_{i0}},y_{P_{i0}},x_{E_{j0}},y_{E_{j0}}$. Controls are heading angles $\psi_i,\phi_j\in[-\pi,\pi)$. Players holonomic; optimal play demands maximum speed so $v_{P_i}=v^*_{P_i}=\bar v_{P_i}$, $v_{E_j}=v^*_{E_j}=\bar v_{E_j}$; speed ratios $\alpha_{ij}=v_{E_j}/v_{P_i}<1$ for all $i,j$ (pursuers faster than evaders, pairwise).
- **State**: $\mathbf x:=(x_{P_i},y_{P_i},x_{E_j},y_{E_j})\in\mathbb R^{2(N+M)}$ (p. 2346, pursuers listed first; note Section III reorders evaders first — minor inconsistency).
- **Termination**: binary assignment $\mu_{ij}=1$ iff $P_i$ is assigned to $E_j$; for every assigned pair the game ends when either $y_{E_j}=0$ (evader reaches the border) or capture ($x_{P_i}=x_{E_j}, y_{P_i}=y_{E_j}$). Terminal set (eq. (2)): $\mathcal T:=\{\mathbf x \mid \forall j\in\{1..M\}\ \exists i\in\{1..N\},\ \mu_{ij}=1,\ x_{P_i}=x_{E_j},\ y_{P_i}=y_{E_j}\}$. $t_f$ = instant the **last** evader is captured; individual terminal times $t_{f_{ij}}$ with $\dot x_{P_i}=\dot y_{P_i}=\dot x_{E_j}=\dot y_{E_j}=0$ for $t\ge t_{f_{ij}}$ (freezing regularization so the game continues until all evaders are captured). Point capture (zero radius).
- **Cost (eqs. (3)–(4))**: Mayer term only, $J(u_P,u_E;x_0)=\Phi(\mathbf x_f)$, $\Phi(\mathbf x_f):=\sum_{j=1}^{M} y_{E_j}(t_f)$ — evader team minimizes cumulative terminal distance to the border; pursuer team maximizes it.
- **Value (eq. (5))**: $V(\mathbf x_0):=\max_{u_P(\cdot)}\min_{u_E(\cdot)}J(u_P(\cdot),u_E(\cdot);x_0)$ subject to (1)–(2), with $u_P(\cdot),u_E(\cdot)$ *state feedback* strategies.
- **Information pattern** (p. 2346): perfect information — every agent knows dynamics (1) and $\alpha$, uses causal strategies, and has access to the current state $\mathbf x(t)$; agents do **not** know the opponent's current decision; pursuers have firm commitment $\mu_{ij}(t)=\mu_{ij}(t_0)$. Saddle-point (max–min) guaranteed-performance strategies.
- **Hamiltonian (eq. (6))**: $H=\sum_{i=1}^N v_{P_i}(\lambda_{x_{P_i}}\cos\psi_i+\lambda_{y_{P_i}}\sin\psi_i)+\sum_{j=1}^M v_{E_j}(\lambda_{x_{E_j}}\cos\phi_j+\lambda_{y_{E_j}}\sin\phi_j)$, with costate $\lambda\in\mathbb R^{2(N+M)}$ ordered pursuers-then-evaders.

### 5.1 Theorem 1 (p. 2347)
- **Statement**: In the cooperative differential game (1)–(5), the headings of every $E_j$ and $P_i$ are **constant under optimal play** and the optimal trajectories are straight lines.
- **Assumptions**: simple motion (1); Mayer cost (3)–(4).
- **Proof approach** (as printed, 1 step): immediate from simple motion + Mayer-type cost — with linear-in-control, terminal-only payoff dynamics, the optimal control of each double integrator-free agent is constant heading (standard Isaacs/Pontryagin argument: $\partial H/\partial\psi_i = v_{P_i}(-\lambda_{x_{P_i}}\sin\psi_i+\lambda_{y_{P_i}}\cos\psi_i)=0$ with $|\lambda|$ constant along straight-line optimals). No equation numbered; no intermediate inequalities.
- **Meaning**: reduces the infinite-dimensional game to finitely many aimpoints per player; all subsequent theorems only need the (constant) optimal aimpoint of each player. Global result on $\mathcal R_P$; analytic. Extensibility: breaks for non-simple-motion dynamics (acknowledged in Conclusion, p. 2350).

### 5.2 Pair metric (eq. (7)) and 2-vs-2 Value (Theorem 2, p. 2347, eqs. (8)–(12))
- **Pair metric (eq. (7))**: for any pair $(P_i,E_j)$,
  $$\underline y_{ij}(\mathbf x)=\frac{y_{E_j}-\alpha_{ij}^2\,y_{P_i}-\alpha_{ij}\sqrt{(x_{E_j}-x_{P_i})^2+(y_{E_j}-y_{P_i})^2}}{1-\alpha_{ij}^2}.$$
  Geometric meaning (verifiable from (17) below): $\underline y_{ij}=y_{c_{ij}}-r_{ij}$ is the **y-coordinate of the lowest point of the Apollonius circle** of the pair $(E_j,P_i)$ — i.e., the guaranteed capture height of the one-on-one border-defense subgame played in isolation. It is also the feasibility test: $\underline y_{ij}<0$ (circle crosses $y=0$) means that $E_j$ can reach the border against $P_i$ (p. 2347, Fig. 1 discussion).
- **Assignment sums (eq. (8))**: $y_{s1}(\mathbf x)=\underline y_{11}(\mathbf x)+\underline y_{22}(\mathbf x)$, $y_{s2}(\mathbf x)=\underline y_{12}(\mathbf x)+\underline y_{21}(\mathbf x)$ — the two feasible 2-vs-2 matchings.
- **Theorem 2**: Consider the 2-vs-2 BDDG (1)–(5) with $\alpha_{ij}=v_{E_j}/v_{P_i}<1$ and $\mathbf x\in\mathcal R_P$. Then: (i) $V(\mathbf x)$ is continuously differentiable **except on the dispersal surface** $y_{s1}=y_{s2}$; (ii) $V$ satisfies the HJI equation; (iii) explicitly, $V(\mathbf x)=y_{s1}(\mathbf x)$ if $y_{s1}>y_{s2}$ and $V(\mathbf x)=y_{s2}(\mathbf x)$ if $y_{s2}>y_{s1}$; (iv) the optimal state-feedback strategies are (eq. (9)) each player steering at unit-speed direction toward its optimal aimpoint, e.g. $\cos\phi_1^*=\frac{x^*_{E_1}-x_{E_1}}{\sqrt{(x^*_{E_1}-x_{E_1})^2+(y^*_{E_1}-y_{E_1})^2}}$, $\sin\phi_1^*$ analogously, and identically for $\phi_2^*,\psi_1^*,\psi_2^*$; (v) the aimpoints (eqs. (10)–(11)) are, if $y_{s1}>y_{s2}$ (direct matching $E_1$–$P_1$, $E_2$–$P_2$):
  $x^*_{E_1}=x^*_{P_1}=\frac{x_{E_1}-\alpha_{11}^2 x_{P_1}}{1-\alpha_{11}^2}$, $y^*_{E_1}=y^*_{P_1}=\frac{y_{E_1}-\alpha_{11}^2 y_{P_1}-\alpha_{11}d_{11}}{1-\alpha_{11}^2}$, and the same with $(22)$ indices for the pair $(E_2,P_2)$; if $y_{s2}>y_{s1}$ (cross matching $E_1$–$P_2$, $E_2$–$P_1$): $x^*_{E_1}=x^*_{P_2}=\frac{x_{E_1}-\alpha_{21}^2 x_{P_2}}{1-\alpha_{21}^2}$, $y^*_{E_1}=y^*_{P_2}=\frac{y_{E_1}-\alpha_{21}^2 y_{P_2}-\alpha_{21}d_{21}}{1-\alpha_{21}^2}$, etc., where (eq. (12)) $d_{ij}=\sqrt{(x_{E_j}-x_{P_i})^2+(y_{E_j}-y_{P_i})^2}$, $i,j=1,2$. Note $\alpha_{21}=v_{E_1}/v_{P_2}$ per the definition $\alpha_{ij}=v_{E_j}/v_{P_i}$.
- **Assumptions**: all four speed ratios $<1$; $\mathbf x\in\mathcal R_P$; firm commitment to initial assignment; point capture.
- **Proof approach**: "Proof of this theorem is given in the extended version of this article [35]" — **no proof in this paper** (proof gap w.r.t. the TAC text itself; see §5.7 for an independent structural check). The decisive object is the candidate Value $V=\max(y_{s1},y_{s2})$: additive separability across assigned pairs (straight-line optimals decouple the joint game into independent one-on-one BDDGs) plus maximization over the two matchings; the dispersal surface $y_{s1}=y_{s2}$ is where both matchings are equally good and the optimal assignment is non-unique.
- **Local/global, analytic/numerical**: global on $\mathcal R_P$; fully analytic; the assignment decision is reduced to comparing two scalars $y_{s1},y_{s2}$.
- **Physical meaning**: each pursuer–evader pair fights its own miniature border-defense game whose value is the Apollonius-circle lowest point; the team simply picks the matching with the larger summed guaranteed capture height. If an evader deviates from (9)–(11), it is still captured by its assigned pursuer and its terminal cost *increases* over $V$ — the feedback laws exploit nonoptimal adversaries (Remark 1, p. 2347).
- **Extensibility**: the pattern "sum of pair values, max over matchings" is exactly what Theorem 3 generalizes to $N=M$ and Theorem 4 to $N>M$.

### 5.3 Theorem 3 (N = M case, p. 2348, eqs. (13)–(16))
- **Enumeration machinery**: feasible assignments $\mathcal A_\iota$, $\iota=1,\dots,\bar\iota$, are those matchings where **all** evaders can potentially be captured; feasibility test = the pair's Apollonius circle must not intersect the $x$-axis (p. 2347–2348 and Fig. 1: the $P_1$–$E_3$ Apollonius circle crosses the border, so any matching with $\mu_{13}=1$ is infeasible; for the pictured 3-vs-3 instance the feasible set is $\mathcal A_1:\mu_{11}=\mu_{22}=\mu_{33}=1$; $\mathcal A_2:\mu_{11}=\mu_{23}=\mu_{32}=1$; $\mathcal A_3:\mu_{12}=\mu_{21}=\mu_{33}=1$; $\mathcal A_4:\mu_{12}=\mu_{23}=\mu_{32}=1$). Assignment value (eq. (13)): $y_{s_\iota}(\mathbf x)=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\mu^\iota_{ij}\,\underline y_{ij}(\mathbf x)$.
- **Statement**: Consider the $N$-vs-$M$ BDDG where $N=M$, $\alpha_{ij}=v_{E_j}/v_{P_i}<1$, $\mathbf x\in\mathcal R_P$. The Value is continuously differentiable (except at the dispersal surfaces $y_{s_\iota}=y_{s_{\iota'}}$ for any $\iota,\iota'$) and satisfies the HJI equation; explicitly $V(\mathbf x)=\max_\iota y_{s_\iota}(\mathbf x)$, with optimal assignment $\iota^*=\arg\max_\iota y_{s_\iota}(\mathbf x)$. Optimal feedback strategies are (eq. (14)) the heading-toward-aimpoint laws $\cos\phi_j^*=\frac{x^*_{E_j}-x_{E_j}}{\sqrt{(x^*_{E_j}-x_{E_j})^2+(y^*_{E_j}-y_{E_j})^2}}$ (and analogously $\sin\phi_j^*,\cos\psi_i^*,\sin\psi_i^*$), with aimpoints (eq. (15)) for every assigned pair $\mu^*_{ij}=1$:
  $x^*_{E_j}=x^*_{P_i}=\frac{x_{E_j}-\alpha_{ij}^2 x_{P_i}}{1-\alpha_{ij}^2}$, $y^*_{E_j}=y^*_{P_i}=\frac{y_{E_j}-\alpha_{ij}^2 y_{P_i}-\alpha_{ij}d_{ij}}{1-\alpha_{ij}^2}$, where (eq. (16)) $d_{ij}=\sqrt{(x_{E_j}-x_{P_i})^2+(y_{E_j}-y_{P_i})^2}$, $i,j=1,\dots,N$.
- **Assumptions**: perfect matching regime ($N=M$, each pursuer $\le 1$ evader, so every feasible assignment covers all evaders); $\alpha_{ij}<1$; $\mathbf x\in\mathcal R_P$; firm commitment.
- **Proof approach**: **none printed** — Theorem 3 is stated without even a "proof in [35]" pointer (the text passes directly from (16) to Section IV-B). Proof gap w.r.t. this paper; the arXiv version [35] carries the argument. Structure (by analogy to Theorem 2 and the printed machinery): (i) straight-line optimals decouple pairs; (ii) each pair's subgame value is $\underline y_{ij}$; (iii) assignment chosen by the maximizing team; (iv) differentiability of $\max$ of finitely many $C^\infty$ functions away from ties.
- **Decisive equation**: $V(\mathbf x)=\max_{\iota}\sum_{j}\mu^\iota_{ij}\underline y_{ij}(\mathbf x)$ (13) — the entire $2(N{+}M)$-dimensional HJI problem collapses to $\bar\iota\le M!$ scalar sums of Apollonius quantities.
- **Meaning**: simultaneous codesign — the argmax returns the discrete weapon-target assignment while (14)–(15) return continuous guidance; both are state feedback, recomputable in real time. Analytic, global on $\mathcal R_P$.
- **Extensibility**: heterogeneous speeds allowed within $\alpha_{ij}<1$; slower-pursuer/faster-evader pairs handled by deleting infeasible matchings (asserted p. 2346).

### 5.4 Theorem 4 (N > M case: cooperative two-on-one capture, p. 2349, eqs. (17)–(22))
- **Cooperation rationale** (p. 2348): two pursuers on one evader in most cases pushes the capture point farther from the border than the best one-on-one match, and helps prevent escape; **three** pursuers on one evader bring no extra benefit (attributed to [35]).
- **Apollonius circles (eq. (17))**: for pairs $(E_j,P_i)$ and $(E_j,P_{i'})$, $(x-x_{c_{ij}})^2+(y-y_{c_{ij}})^2=r_{ij}^2$ and $(x-x_{c_{i'j}})^2+(y-y_{c_{i'j}})^2=r_{i'j}^2$, with $x_{c_{ij}}=\frac{1}{1-\alpha_{ij}^2}(x_{E_j}-\alpha_{ij}^2 x_{P_i})$, $y_{c_{ij}}=\frac{1}{1-\alpha_{ij}^2}(y_{E_j}-\alpha_{ij}^2 y_{P_i})$, $r_{ij}=\frac{\alpha_{ij}}{1-\alpha_{ij}^2}d_{ij}$.
- **Pursuer–pursuer circle (eq. (18))**: WLOG $P_{i'}$ faster, $\alpha_{i'i}=v_{P_i}/v_{P_{i'}}<1$; $(x-x_{c_{i'i}})^2+(y-y_{c_{i'i}})^2=r_{i'i}^2$ with $x_{c_{i'i}}=\frac{1}{1-\alpha_{i'i}^2}(x_{P_i}-\alpha_{i'i}^2 x_{P_{i'}})$, $y_{c_{i'i}}=\frac{1}{1-\alpha_{i'i}^2}(y_{P_i}-\alpha_{i'i}^2 y_{P_{i'}})$, $r_{i'i}=\frac{\alpha_{i'i}}{1-\alpha_{i'i}^2}d_{i'i}$, $d_{i'i}=\sqrt{(x_{P_i}-x_{P_{i'}})^2+(y_{P_i}-y_{P_{i'}})^2}$.
- **Assignment enumeration**: same $\mathcal A_\iota$, $\iota=1,\dots,\bar\iota$, but now candidates include two-pursuer matches "where simultaneous capture helps the pursuers to increase their payoff"; worked 3-vs-2 example in **Table I** (p. 2348): $\mathcal A_1$: $P_1P_2\Rightarrow E_1,\ P_3\Rightarrow E_2$ with $\mu_{11}=\mu_{21}=\mu_{32}=1$; $\mathcal A_2$: $P_1\Rightarrow E_1,\ P_2P_3\Rightarrow E_2$ with $\mu_{11}=\mu_{22}=\mu_{32}=1$; $\mathcal A_3$: $P_1P_2\Rightarrow E_2,\ P_3\Rightarrow E_1$ with printed $\mu_{22}=\mu_{31}=1$; $\mathcal A_4$: $P_1\Rightarrow E_2,\ P_2P_3\Rightarrow E_1$ with printed $\mu_{12}=\mu_{21}=1$; $\mathcal A_5$: $P_1P_3\Rightarrow E_1,\ P_2\Rightarrow E_2$ with printed $\mu_{11}=\mu_{22}=1$; $\mathcal A_6$: $P_1P_3\Rightarrow E_2,\ P_2\Rightarrow E_1$ with $\mu_{12}=\mu_{32}=\mu_{21}=1$. (See §7: rows $\mathcal A_3$–$\mathcal A_5$ print only two $\mu$'s although their "potential match" column involves three.)
- **Assignment value (eq. (19))**: $y_{s_\iota}(\mathbf x)=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\mu^\iota_{ij}\,\underline y_{ij}$, where $\underline y_{ij}$ is the one-on-one metric (7) when $E_j$ is captured by a single pursuer, and, when $E_j$ is captured **simultaneously by two pursuers** $i,i'$ (eq. (20)):
  $$\underline y_{ij}(\mathbf x)=\frac{F_{ij}-\sqrt{(x_{c_{ij}}-x_{c_{i'j}})^2\,G_{ij}}}{D_{ij}}\;=\;V_s(\mathbf x),$$
  with (eq. (21)):
  $F_{ij}=y_{c_{i'i}}(x_{c_{ij}}-x_{c_{i'j}})^2-(y_{c_{ij}}-y_{c_{i'j}})\Big(\frac{R_{ij}}{2}-x_{c_{i'i}}(x_{c_{i'j}}-x_{c_{ij}})\Big)$;
  $G_{ij}=r_{i'i}^2 D_{ij}-\Big(\frac{R_{ij}}{2}+x_{c_{i'i}}(x_{c_{ij}}-x_{c_{i'j}})+y_{c_{i'i}}(y_{c_{ij}}-y_{c_{i'j}})\Big)^2$;
  $D_{ij}=(x_{c_{ij}}-x_{c_{i'j}})^2+(y_{c_{ij}}-y_{c_{i'j}})^2$;
  $R_{ij}=r_{ij}^2-r_{i'j}^2-x_{c_{ij}}^2+x_{c_{i'j}}^2-y_{c_{ij}}^2+y_{c_{i'j}}^2$.
  (Subscripts $c_{i'i}, r_{i'i}$ verified at 400 dpi — the formula genuinely mixes the two evader–pursuer circles with the pursuer–pursuer circle (18).)
- **Statement (Theorem 4)**: Consider the $N$-vs-$M$ BDDG where $N>M$, $\alpha_{ij}=v_{E_j}/v_{P_i}<1$, $\mathbf x\in\mathcal R_P$. The Value is continuously differentiable (except at dispersal surfaces $y_{s_\iota}=y_{s_{\iota'}}$) and satisfies the HJI equation; $V(\mathbf x)=\max_\iota y_{s_\iota}(\mathbf x)$, $\iota^*=\arg\max_\iota y_{s_\iota}(\mathbf x)$; optimal strategies are (14); aimpoints are (15) for single-pursuer capture and, for simultaneous two-pursuer capture (eq. (22)):
  $$x^*=x^*_{E_j}=x^*_{P_i}=x^*_{P_{i'}}=\frac{R_{ij}-2\,(y_{c_{i'j}}-y_{c_{ij}})\,V_s(\mathbf x)}{2\,(x_{c_{i'j}}-x_{c_{ij}})},\qquad y^*=y^*_{E_j}=y^*_{P_i}=y^*_{P_{i'}}=V_s(\mathbf x).$$
- **Assumptions**: as in Theorem 3 plus $N>M$; two pursuers per evader at most ("assignment of three pursuers to one evader does not bring any extra benefit [35]"); both pursuers of a pair must be able to reach the simultaneous-capture point exactly when the evader does.
- **Proof approach**: "Proof of this theorem can be found in the extended version of this article [35]" — no proof printed.
- **Independent verification of the geometric content (this archive's check, not a paper claim)**: simultaneous capture at $\mathbf z$ requires equal arrival times $t_{P_i}(\mathbf z)=t_{P_{i'}}(\mathbf z)=t_{E_j}(\mathbf z)$, i.e., $\mathbf z$ lies on both evader–pursuer Apollonius circles (17) and, equivalently (radical-center concurrency), on (17a) $\cap$ (18). Eq. (22) is exactly the **radical line** of circles (17a),(17b): $2(x_{c_{i'j}}-x_{c_{ij}})x+2(y_{c_{i'j}}-y_{c_{ij}})y=R_{ij}$; eq. (20) is the **lower** intersection ordinate. Numerical test (evader $(10,8)$, $v_E=1$; pursuers $(8,2)$, $v=2$ and $(14,3)$, $v=2.5$): (20)–(22) give $(x^*,y^*)=(9.5894,\,5.9236)$, matching the true lower circle intersection where $t_E=t_{P_i}=t_{P_{i'}}=2.1166$ s, and the cooperative height $5.9236$ exceeds both one-on-one values ($\underline y_{i1}=5.7836$, $\underline y_{i'1}=5.9033$), confirming the printed claim that cooperation raises the pursuers' guaranteed payoff. Caveats not discussed in the paper: (20)–(22) degenerate when $x_{c_{ij}}=x_{c_{i'j}}$ (division by zero in (22)), and $G_{ij}<0$ (non-intersecting circles → no real simultaneous-capture point) is excluded only implicitly by the enumeration rule.
- **Meaning**: with superior numbers the pursuer team codesigns a *cooperative* guidance law — both pursuers of a pair aim at a common point $x^*$ computed from the radical line of the two Apollonius circles, at height $V_s$ — and the team-level discrete choice again reduces to $\max_\iota$ over scalar sums. Analytic, global on $\mathcal R_P$.
- **Game of kind (Remark 2, p. 2349)**: complete protection is decided by the initial-assignment solution: if every $\underline y_{ij}>0$ within $y_{s_{\iota^*}}$, all evaders are captured before reaching the border ($V>0$); if some $\underline y_{ij}<0$, the best assignment minimizes the number of evaders reaching the border (partial protection), and pursuers can still use the ideas to minimize damage (intercept as many as possible).

### 5.5 Corollary 1 (no commitment, 2-vs-2; pp. 2349–2350, eq. (23))
- **Statement**: Consider the 2-vs-2 BDDG (1)–(5), $\alpha_{ij}<1$, $\mathbf x\in\mathcal R_P$, **without** pursuer commitment to the initial assignment. The pursuers' strategies *with* commitment, given by (eq. (23)) the heading laws $\cos\psi_1^*=\frac{x^*_{P_1}-x_{P_1}}{\sqrt{(x^*_{P_1}-x_{P_1})^2+(y^*_{P_1}-y_{P_1})^2}}$ (and $\sin\psi_1^*,\cos\psi_2^*,\sin\psi_2^*$ analogously), with aimpoints: if $y_{s1}>y_{s2}$, $x^*_{P_1}=\frac{x_{E_1}-\alpha_{11}^2 x_{P_1}}{1-\alpha_{11}^2}$, $y^*_{P_1}=\frac{y_{E_1}-\alpha_{11}^2 y_{P_1}-\alpha_{11}d_{11}}{1-\alpha_{11}^2}$, $x^*_{P_2}=\frac{x_{E_2}-\alpha_{22}^2 x_{P_2}}{1-\alpha_{22}^2}$, $y^*_{P_2}=\frac{y_{E_2}-\alpha_{22}^2 y_{P_2}-\alpha_{22}d_{22}}{1-\alpha_{22}^2}$; if $y_{s2}>y_{s1}$, $x^*_{P_2}=\frac{x_{E_1}-\alpha_{21}^2 x_{P_2}}{1-\alpha_{21}^2}$, $y^*_{P_2}=\frac{y_{E_1}-\alpha_{21}^2 y_{P_2}-\alpha_{21}d_{21}}{1-\alpha_{21}^2}$, $x^*_{P_1}=\frac{x_{E_2}-\alpha_{12}^2 x_{P_1}}{1-\alpha_{12}^2}$, $y^*_{P_1}=\frac{y_{E_2}-\alpha_{12}^2 y_{P_1}-\alpha_{12}d_{12}}{1-\alpha_{12}^2}$ ($d_{ij}$ from (12)) — are **robust state-feedback strategies for the game without commitment**, and the pursuers' guaranteed payoff is $y_s(\mathbf x)=y_{s1}(\mathbf x)$ if $y_{s1}>y_{s2}$, $y_s(\mathbf x)=y_{s2}(\mathbf x)$ if $y_{s2}>y_{s1}$, with $y_{s1},y_{s2}$ from (8).
- **Proof approach**: presented as "a corollary to Theorem 2" — no proof printed; the argument is that a pursuer keeping its committed-assignment heading guarantees $y_s$ regardless of whether switching is permitted (commitment can only restrict the pursuers' option set, and the evader side of the guarantee is unchanged).
- **Meaning**: the commitment restriction of Section II is *not load-bearing* in the 2-vs-2 game: allowing assignment switching does not reduce the pursuers' guaranteed payoff below $y_s$ if they simply ignore the freedom. Global, analytic.
- **Remark 3 (p. 2350)**: dropping commitment and adding "cooperation and switch" enlarges $\mathcal R_P$: two pursuers first cooperate to push an evader's dominance region down until one of them can single-handedly capture it, freeing the other to switch to a different opponent.

### 5.6 Summary of the analytic pipeline (all equations)
(1) dynamics → (6) Hamiltonian → Theorem 1 (straight-line optimals) → (7) pair metric $\underline y_{ij}$ = Apollonius lowest point → (8)/(13)/(19) assignment sums → Theorems 2/3/4: $V=\max_\iota y_{s_\iota}$, HJI-satisfying, $C^1$ off dispersal surfaces → (9)/(14) heading feedback → (10)–(11)/(15) one-on-one aimpoints, (20)–(22) two-on-one cooperative aimpoint → Corollary 1 robustness without commitment. Every formula is closed-form in the current state; the only combinatorial step is the $\arg\max$ over pruned matchings.

### 5.7 Proof-status annotations (traceability)
- Theorem 1: 1-line proof printed (p. 2347). Theorems 2 and 4: proofs explicitly deferred to [35] (arXiv:1911.03806). Theorem 3 and Corollary 1: **no proof or deferral statement printed**. Consequently, within P037 itself the claims "V is $C^1$ and satisfies the HJI equation" are *asserted*, not proven; the HJI PDE is not even displayed. Readers needing the verifications must consult [35]. No internal contradictions were found: all printed formulas were re-derived or numerically confirmed in this archive (§5.4; also (7) equals $y_{c_{ij}}-r_{ij}$ identically).

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
**Author-stated**:
- Simple-motion kinematics only; "more realistic models" explicitly deferred (Conclusion, p. 2350).
- Switching assignments, containment, and capture in succession are future work (p. 2350); firm commitment assumed throughout except the 2-vs-2 Corollary 1 (pp. 2346, 2349).
- Assignment search time varies with the number of feasible assignments and grows with team size; heuristics (e.g., greedy) needed for larger teams, yielding possibly suboptimal (but still guaranteed-payoff) assignments (p. 2349).
- Extensions flagged: decoys, self-sacrificing players, players of different importance (p. 2349); robustness examples in [35] rather than here.
- $N\ge M$ assumed (p. 2346); the $N<M$ regime is not addressed.

**Critical observations (this archive, with page/eq. anchors)**:
- *Proof transparency*: Theorems 2–4 and Corollary 1 carry no proofs in the TAC paper (deferred to [35], or silent — Theorem 3, Corollary 1); the HJI verification and the $C^1$ claim cannot be checked from this text alone (pp. 2347–2349).
- *Table I inconsistencies (p. 2348)*: rows $\mathcal A_3$, $\mathcal A_4$, $\mathcal A_5$ list only two $\mu_{ij}$ entries although their "potential match" column involves three (e.g., $\mathcal A_3$: "$P_1P_2\Rightarrow E_2,\ P_3\Rightarrow E_1$" but only $\mu_{22}=\mu_{31}=1$ printed, $\mu_{12}$ missing), whereas $\mathcal A_1$, $\mathcal A_2$, $\mathcal A_6$ list all three. Either typographical omissions or an undocumented "resulting variables after analysis" convention — ambiguous as printed.
- *Degenerate configurations of the cooperative metric*: (22) divides by $2(x_{c_{i'j}}-x_{c_{ij}})$, which vanishes when the two evader–pursuer Apollonius centers are vertically aligned; and (20) requires $G_{ij}\ge0$ (real intersection). Neither edge case is discussed (pp. 2348–2349).
- *Dispersal surfaces*: on $y_{s_\iota}=y_{s_{\iota'}}$ the Value is not differentiable and the optimal assignment is non-unique; no rule (e.g., tie-breaking or chattering analysis) is given (Theorems 2–4 statements).
- *Notation slips*: the terminal state on p. 2346 is written $\mathbf x_f:=(x_{E_f},y_{E_f},x_{1f},y_{1f},x_{2f},y_{2f})$ — a 6-component (one-evader/two-pursuer) leftover inside the general $2(N{+}M)$ formulation; Section II orders the state pursuers-first while Section III orders evaders-first (p. 2346 vs. 2347); a garbled sentence precedes Theorem 2 ("It dictates what the optimal assignment it provides the state feedback optimal headings…", p. 2347).
- *Heterogeneity*: $\alpha_{ij}<1$ for **all** pairs is assumed; slower-pursuer/faster-evader matchups are only handled by the asserted assignment-exclusion constraint (p. 2346), whose effect on $\mathcal R_P$ and on the guaranteed payoff is not analyzed.
- *Payoff modeling*: $\Phi=\sum_j y_{E_j}(t_f)$ weights all evaders identically and treats a captured evader's contribution as its capture height; strategic sacrifices/decoys are excluded by construction (acknowledged as future work, p. 2349).
- *Feasibility vs. optimality gap*: if no full matching is feasible, the paper's Remark 2 gives a qualitative prescription (minimize the number of evaders reaching the border) but no Value characterization for that regime within $\mathcal R_P$-excluded states (p. 2349).

## 8. Citation Value
This is the flagship "complete closed-form solution" reference for team-vs-team pursuit-evasion with a discrete assignment layer: it is the natural citation whenever an introduction needs to (i) motivate differential-game codesign of weapon-target assignment and guidance, (ii) contrast closed-form Apollonius-based saddle-point solutions against numerical HJI/HJB approximations ([27], [28]) or game-of-kind-only barrier analyses ([33]), or (iii) anchor the border-defense/reach-avoid line of Garcia–Casbeer–Von Moll–Pachter work. Its $V(\mathbf x)=\max_\iota\sum_j\mu^\iota_{ij}\,\underline y_{ij}(\mathbf x)$ construct — Apollonius pair values summed over a matching, maximized over feasible matchings, with $C^1$-off-dispersal HJI satisfaction — is the template later reused for 3-D, heterogeneous, and switching variants, and its two-on-one simultaneous-capture metric (20)–(22) is a ready-made cooperative-guidance baseline. Ready-to-adapt citation sentence: "Garcia et al. [P037] solved the $N$-pursuer/$M$-evader border-defense differential game in closed form, showing that the Value is the maximum over feasible assignments of the sum of Apollonius-based pair capture heights, that it satisfies the HJI equation off dispersal surfaces, and that the resulting state-feedback strategies codesign the discrete assignment and continuous guidance in real time, including cooperative two-on-one capture when the pursuers outnumber the evaders." A companion sentence for related-work paragraphs: "Unlike reach-avoid approaches that approximate the HJI equation over the joint state space or prove only barrier-based winning conditions, [P037] obtains the exact saddle-point strategies and the assignment simultaneously; its proofs, however, reside in the extended version [35] (arXiv:1911.03806)."
