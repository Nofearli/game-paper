## 2. Research Background
Orbital pursuit–evasion (PE) games between spacecraft with magnitude-bounded continuous thrust are normally attacked through the Hamilton–Jacobi–Isaacs (HJI) partial differential equations, whose analytic feedback solutions are rarely obtainable; hence the literature ([2]–[7] of the paper) concentrates on open-loop saddle-point trajectories obtained by converting the game into a two-point boundary value problem and solving it by direct, indirect, or semi-direct methods (Pontani's semi-direct method [1,5], direct method [7], homotopic sensitivity approach [6]). When the two spacecraft are close, the nonlinear dynamics reduce to the linear CW equations in a local rotating frame attached to a virtual chief on a circular (or, in [4], Tschauner–Hempel for elliptic) reference orbit, which sharply cuts the TPBVP size — the paper cites [2] as reducing the unknowns from 13 in [5] to 4. All of this prior work, however, assumes *identical* thrust configurations: every player carries one single thruster producing a magnitude-limited acceleration in any direction (an $L_2$-ball control set). The authors observe that real pursuing satellites are often designed with several orthogonal thrusters (a modeling assumption already standard in formation flying [14], station keeping [15], and rendezvous [16]–[18]), which yields a per-axis box-constrained ($L_\infty$-type) control set, and they set out to initiate the study of PE games under such *dissimilar* thrust configurations.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
- **Problem**: a pursuer $P$ and an evader $E$ move near a circular reference orbit under identical linear CW dynamics, with different actuation: $P$ has three orthogonal thrusters with independent per-axis bounds $|u^{P,x}|,|u^{P,y}|,|u^{P,z}| \le \rho^P$ (eq. (5), a cube), while $E$ has one thruster with $\|u^E\|_2 \le \rho^E$ (eq. (6), a ball), and $\rho^P > \rho^E$ is assumed "to make the interception achievable" (p. 2). $P$ minimizes and $E$ maximizes the capture time $J = t_f$ (eq. (7)); the game ends when the positions coincide, $\mathbf{r}^P_f = \mathbf{r}^E_f$ (eq. (8)) — exact position capture, zero capture radius, no velocity matching. The task is to compute the open-loop saddle-point controls.
- **[Authors' claim]** (i) All prior satellite PE-game literature [2]–[7], [27] assumes the same single-thruster configuration for both players; dissimilar configurations were unexplored, although three-axis independent thrusters are routinely assumed in formation flying [14], station keeping [15], and rendezvous [16]–[18] (p. 1–2). (ii) Exploiting the CW equations, the unknowns of the TPBVP can be decreased "from 13 in [5] to 4" (p. 1, attributed to [2]; the same count is retained here) — this specific 13→4 figure cannot be verified from this PDF (ref. [2] is an AIAA conference paper not in the library), so it remains an author-cited claim. (iii) Early near-optimal closed-loop methods [8,9] for the minmax-range two-spacecraft game still require the pursuer to re-solve the open-loop solution periodically (p. 1). (iv) Rendezvous-with-disturbance methods [23–25] do not fit the PE game because [23,25] assume unbounded chaser input and [24] cannot prove exact position convergence (p. 2) — claims about [23]–[25] not verifiable from this PDF.
- **[Verified from the paper's own text/derivations]** The reduction machinery (adjoint antisymmetry (18), Theorem 1) is explicitly acknowledged as an adaptation of the observation proved in [2,3] (and [4] for elliptic orbit/TH equations) to the new control sets (p. 3); the genuinely new elements are (a) the PMP control laws for the mixed cube/ball constraint pair, (b) the verification that the dimension reduction survives dissimilar control sets, and (c) the empirical negative result — the PSO+Newton indirect pipeline that works for the single-thruster game can **fail** on some initial states for the dissimilar-configuration game, which the authors conjecture is caused by the discontinuous sign-function control of the pursuer (p. 9–10).
- **Gap filled**: first formulation of the proximate satellite PE game with different thrust configurations, plus a cautionary demonstration that the standard indirect method is fragile for it — an explicit open problem rather than a complete solution.

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Modeling**: formulation of a two-player zero-sum capture-time differential game under CW relative dynamics with dissimilar continuous-thrust constraints — cube-constrained pursuer (eq. (5)) vs. ball-constrained evader (eq. (6)), $\rho^P > \rho^E$ — serving "as a starting point for considering more complex and real satellite pursuit-evasion game" (p. 2).
2. **Theory (derivation)**: PMP-based open-loop saddle controls (eqs. (11)–(12)); adjoint antisymmetry observation (18); **Theorem 1** — equivalence of the original 12-state/12-adjoint game to a reduced 6-state/6-adjoint game (eqs. (19)–(22)), halving the state+adjoint count and leaving only 4 scalar unknowns $(\boldsymbol{\lambda}^r_f, \tau_f)$ for the TPBVP (eq. (29)).
3. **Algorithm**: an indirect solver (Algorithm 1) — PSO minimization of the weighted residual $F^\top K F$ (eq. (38)) to generate the initial guess, followed by a damped Newton iteration (eq. (30)) whose Jacobian's two analytically intractable blocks are approximated by forward finite differences (eqs. (35)–(36)) because the sign function forbids the sensitivity-ODE approach of [6] (Remark 1).
4. **Numerical findings (the paper's headline)**: two successfully solved examples (capture in 360.124 s and 452.782 s, terminal miss 0.18 m and 0.14 m) and one initial state on which Algorithm 1 fails (terminal miss > 2 km) even under scaling/normalization and enlarged PSO budgets, while the *same* initial state is solved in 15.8 s when the pursuer is given a weaker single-thruster configuration (miss 0.61 m) — showing the difficulty is specific to the dissimilar configuration, conjecturally its discontinuous $\operatorname{sign}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}^v)$ pursuer control and/or the observed mid-game sign change of $u^{P}_x$ (Fig. 3(c)); the paper closes by calling for efficient algorithms for this problem class.

## 5. Key Mathematical Results (exhaustive)

### 5.0 Dynamics, thrust configurations, information structure, termination (Sec. 2–3, pp. 2–3)
- **Relative orbital dynamics (CW equations, eq. (1))**: in a local rotating frame $Oxyz$ (origin at the chief, $x$ radial, $y$ along-track, $z$ along orbital angular momentum),
  $$\ddot x_\delta - 2\omega \dot y_\delta - 3\omega^2 x_\delta = u_x,\quad \ddot y_\delta + 2\omega \dot x_\delta = u_y,\quad \ddot z_\delta + \omega^2 z_\delta = u_z,$$
  valid when the deputy–chief distance is small vs. the orbital radius. State-space form (eq. (2)–(3)): $\dot x = Ax + Bu$ with $x = [x_\delta\ y_\delta\ z_\delta\ \dot x_\delta\ \dot y_\delta\ \dot z_\delta]^\top$,
  $$A = \begin{bmatrix} O_{3\times 3} & I_{3\times 3} \\ A_1 & A_2 \end{bmatrix},\quad B = \begin{bmatrix} O_{3\times 3} \\ I_{3\times 3} \end{bmatrix},\quad A_1 = \operatorname{diag}(3\omega^2, 0, -\omega^2),\quad A_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 2\omega & 0 \\ -2\omega & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}.$$
- **Virtual-chief reduction (p. 2)**: if both players are close and travel short distances, a virtual chief on a circular reference orbit is placed near both trajectories and the *same* dynamics $\dot x^P = Ax^P + Bu^P$, $\dot x^E = Ax^E + Bu^E$ (eq. (4)) hold for both, with $x^P = [(\mathbf{r}^P)^\top (\mathbf{v}^P)^\top]^\top, x^E \in \mathbb{R}^6$. Both dynamics share one $A$ — the fact that makes the adjoint antisymmetry below possible.
- **Thrust configurations (continuous thrust, not impulsive)**: pursuer $|u^{P,x}| \le \rho^P$, $|u^{P,y}| \le \rho^P$, $|u^{P,z}| \le \rho^P$ (eq. (5), cube $\mathcal{U}^P$); evader $\|u^E\|_2 \le \rho^E$ (eq. (6), ball $\mathcal{U}^E$); assumption $\rho^P > \rho^E$ (p. 2). The evader could also carry multiple thrusters and other configurations are possible, but "we will not elaborate on these situations" (p. 3).
- **Information structure**: open-loop — the derived controls (19)/(27) are functions of time parameterized by the initial states; no feedback law or replaying schedule is analyzed (the introduction notes that even the closed-loop methods of [8,9] rely on periodic open-loop re-solving).
- **Payoff and termination**: $J = t_f$ (eq. (7)); termination $\mathbf{r}^P_f = \mathbf{r}^E_f$ (eq. (8)) — exact position coincidence, zero capture radius, terminal velocities unconstrained (p. 2 explicitly excludes the velocity-matching/rendezvous reading, citing [23–25] as inapplicable).

### 5.1 PMP open-loop saddle controls (Sec. 3.1, p. 3, eqs. (9)–(17))
- **Hamiltonian (eqs. (9)–(10))**: with adjoints $\lambda = [(\lambda^P)^\top (\lambda^E)^\top]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{12}$, $\lambda^P = [(\lambda^{P,r})^\top (\lambda^{P,v})^\top]^\top$ etc.,
  $$H = H^P + H^E,\qquad H^P = (\lambda^P)^\top A x^P + (\lambda^P)^\top B u^P,\quad H^E = (\lambda^E)^\top A x^E + (\lambda^E)^\top B u^E.$$
  $H$ is minimized by the pursuer and maximized by the evader. *Existence claim (proof gap, see §7)*: "It is trivial that the Hamiltonian is separable since $H^P$ and $H^E$ are decoupled. Then, the minimization and maximization of $H$ commute, which implies that the saddle solution of the differential game $(u^{P,*}, u^{E,*})$ exists" (p. 3) — separability only decouples the PMP stationarity conditions; it is not a proof of game-theoretic saddle existence.
- **Optimal controls** — the modeling heart of the paper:
  - Pursuer (eq. (11)): minimizing $(\lambda^{P,v})^\top u^P$ over the cube gives the componentwise bang-bang law
    $$u^{P,*} = -\rho^P \operatorname{sign}(\lambda^{P,v})$$
    (each axis saturated at $\pm\rho^P$; discontinuous in $\lambda^{P,v}$).
  - Evader (eq. (12)): maximizing $(\lambda^{E,v})^\top u^E$ over the ball gives the saturated alignment law
    $$u^{E,*} = \rho^E \frac{\lambda^{E,v}}{\|\lambda^{E,v}\|}$$
    (continuous in $\lambda^{E,v}$; undefined at $\lambda^{E,v}=0$ — never discussed).
- **Adjoint dynamics (eq. (13))**: $\dot\lambda^P = -A^\top \lambda^P$, $\dot\lambda^E = -A^\top \lambda^E$ (same linear ODE for both).
- **Terminal function (eq. (14))**: $\psi(x^P_f, x^E_f, t_f) = \upsilon^\top (\mathbf{r}^P_f - \mathbf{r}^E_f) + t_f$, with $\upsilon \in \mathbb{R}^3$ the Lagrange multiplier of the capture constraint and $t_f$ the terminal cost (capture time). Adjoint terminal conditions (eq. (15)) — the second equation prints $\partial\psi(x^E_f, x^E_f, t_f)/\partial x^E_f$, a typo for $\psi(x^P_f, x^E_f, t_f)$ — yield (eq. (16)):
  $$\lambda^{P,r}_f = \upsilon,\quad \lambda^{E,r}_f = -\upsilon,\quad \lambda^{P,v}_f = 0_3,\quad \lambda^{E,v}_f = 0_3.$$
- **Free-time transversality (eq. (17))**: $(\lambda^P_f)^\top(Ax^P_f + Bu^P_f) + (\lambda^E_f)^\top(Ax^E_f + Bu^E_f) + 1 = (\lambda^{P,r}_f)^\top(\mathbf{v}^P_f - \mathbf{v}^E_f) + 1 = 0$ (velocity-row terms drop because $\lambda^{P,v}_f = \lambda^{E,v}_f = 0$; the $+1$ comes from $\partial\psi/\partial t_f$).

### 5.2 Adjoint antisymmetry and Theorem 1 (Sec. 3.2, pp. 3–4, eqs. (18)–(23))
- **Observation (eq. (18))**: $\lambda^P(t) + \lambda^E(t) = 0_6$ for all $t \in [t_0, t_f]$. Justification: both adjoints obey the same linear ODE (13) with opposite terminal values (16); uniqueness of linear ODEs propagates the antisymmetry backwards. Attributed to [2,3] (single-thruster case) and [4] (elliptical orbit), "one can verify that this observation also holds for the problem considered in this paper" (p. 3) — the verification is immediate because (18) depends only on the shared $A$, not on the control sets.
- **Theorem 1 (p. 3, eqs. (19)–(22))**: *For the PE game defined on the system dynamics (4), the saddle-point control strategies of the players are equivalent to the control inputs*
  $$u^P(t) = -\rho^P \operatorname{sign}(\lambda^v),\qquad u^E(t) = -\rho^E \frac{\lambda^v}{\|\lambda^v\|}, \tag{19}$$
  *where $\lambda^v \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the sub-vector of $\lambda := [(\lambda^r)^\top\, (\lambda^v)^\top]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^6$, which together with $x = [\mathbf{r}^\top\, \mathbf{v}^\top]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^6$ solves*
  $$\dot x = Ax + B(u^P - u^E), \tag{20}$$
  $$\dot\lambda = -A^\top \lambda, \tag{21}$$
  *with the initial and terminal conditions*
  $$x_0 = x^P_0 - x^E_0,\qquad \mathbf{r}_f = 0_3,\qquad \lambda^v_f = 0_3,\qquad \tilde H_f := (\lambda^r_f)^\top \mathbf{v}_f + 1 = 0. \tag{22}$$
  - *Assumptions*: dynamics (4) with shared $A$; control sets (5)–(6); termination (8); $\rho^P > \rho^E$; PMP necessary conditions of the original game.
  - *Proof approach (p. 4)*: define $x = x^P - x^E$; (4) gives (20) with $x_0 = x^P_0 - x^E_0$; the ending condition gives $\mathbf{r}_f = 0_3$ (the proof sentence prints "yields $x_f = 0_3$" — sic; position-only termination (8) gives $\mathbf{r}_f = 0_3$, matching (22)). Let $\lambda = \lambda^P$; by (18) the pursuer control takes the first form of (19); $\lambda^v_f = 0_3$ from (16); and (eq. (23)) $\tilde H_f + 1$-type condition $H_f + 1 = (\lambda^r_f)^\top(\mathbf{v}^P_f - \mathbf{v}^E_f) + 1 = (\lambda^r_f)^\top \mathbf{v}_f + 1 = 0$, the fourth condition of (22); the evader control is the second form of (19) because $\lambda^E = -\lambda^P$. $\square$
  - *Decisive identity*: eq. (18) (adjoint antisymmetry) — it collapses the two 6-dimensional adjoint pairs into one and transfers the terminal multiplier role to $\lambda^r_f = \upsilon$.
  - *Meaning*: the game is equivalent to a single "relative-state" game whose state is the players' difference under CW dynamics; state+adjoint count drops from 24 to 12 (p. 4), and the free unknowns drop to 4.
  - *Extensibility*: the proof uses only (i) shared linear dynamics and (ii) decoupled Hamiltonians, so it extends verbatim to any pair of convex control sets (and, per [4], to TH/elliptic dynamics); what changes per configuration is only the PMP control map (here: sign vs. normalized alignment).
  - *Proof gap (directionality)*: "equivalent" is proven only in the direction original-saddle ⇒ reduced TPBVP solution; the converse (a solution of (19)–(22) is a saddle point of the original game) is asserted, not proven — no sufficiency/verification argument (e.g., Isaacs condition or value-function check) appears anywhere in the paper.

### 5.3 Four-unknown TPBVP (Sec. 4.1, p. 4, eqs. (24)–(29))
- Let $\Phi(\tau, t_0)$ and $\Psi(\tau, t_0) \in \mathbb{R}^{6\times 6}$ be the state transition matrices of (2) and (21) (i.e., of $\dot x = Ax$ and $\dot\lambda = -A^\top\lambda$); partition (eq. (24)) $\Psi = \begin{bmatrix} \Psi_{11} & \Psi_{12} \\ \Psi_{21} & \Psi_{22}\end{bmatrix}$. With $\tau = t - t_0$, $\tau_f = t_f - t_0$, both STMs depend only on $\tau$ and satisfy (eq. (25)) $\Phi(\tau_1)^{-1} = \Phi(-\tau_1)$, $\Psi(\tau_1)^{-1} = \Psi(-\tau_1)$, $\Phi(\tau_1)\Phi(\tau_2) = \Phi(\tau_1{+}\tau_2)$, $\Psi(\tau_1)\Psi(\tau_2) = \Psi(\tau_1{+}\tau_2)$.
- **Adjoint reconstruction (eq. (26))** — decisive intermediate equation: since $\lambda^v_f = 0_3$, given only $\lambda^r_f$,
  $$\lambda(\tau) = \Psi(\tau)\lambda_0 = \Psi(\tau)\Psi(\tau_f)^{-1}\lambda_f = \Psi(\tau - \tau_f)\begin{bmatrix}\lambda^r_f \\ 0_3\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}\Psi_{11}(\tau - \tau_f) \\ \Psi_{21}(\tau - \tau_f)\end{bmatrix}\lambda^r_f,$$
  so $\lambda^v_f = 0_3$ is enforced *by construction* and never enters the solver.
- **Controls as functions of the unknowns (eq. (27))**:
  $$u^P(\tau) = -\operatorname{sign}\!\big(\Psi_{21}(\tau - \tau_f)\lambda^r_f\big)\rho^P,\qquad u^E(\tau) = -\frac{\Psi_{21}(\tau - \tau_f)\lambda^r_f}{\|\Psi_{21}(\tau - \tau_f)\lambda^r_f\|_2}\,\rho^E.$$
- **Final state by variation of constants (eq. (28))**: $x_f = \Phi(\tau_f)x_0 + \int_0^{\tau_f}\Phi(\tau_f - s)B(u^P(s) - u^E(s))\,ds$.
- **Reduced root problem (eq. (29))**: with the unknown $\xi = [(\lambda^r_f)^\top, \tau_f]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^4$ (numerically, $\lambda^r_f = \upsilon$, the terminal multiplier),
  $$F(\xi) := \begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{r}_f \\ \tilde H_f\end{bmatrix} = 0_4,$$
  i.e., 3 position-capture equations + 1 free-time transversality equation. This is the paper's "4 nonlinear equations in 4 unknowns" TPBVP; this solving formulation is called the indirect method [3,7] (p. 4).

### 5.4 Damped Newton with finite-difference Jacobian (Sec. 4.2, pp. 4–5, eqs. (30)–(37), Remark 1)
- **Iteration (eq. (30))**: $\xi_{n+1} = \xi_n - \alpha J^{-1}(\xi_n)F(\xi_n)$, step size $\alpha > 0$ chosen by trial and error; $J$ is the $4\times 4$ "sensitivity matrix" (term from [6]) (eq. (31)):
  $$J(\xi_n) = \frac{\partial F}{\partial \xi}\Big|_{\xi_n} = \begin{bmatrix} \partial\mathbf{r}_f/\partial\lambda^r_{f,n} & d\mathbf{r}_f/d\tau_{f,n} \\ \partial\tilde H_f/\partial\lambda^r_{f,n} & d\tilde H_f/d\tau_{f,n} \end{bmatrix}.$$
- **Analytic blocks (eqs. (32)–(33))**: from differentiating (28) via Leibniz,
  $$\frac{dx_f}{d\tau_{f,n}} = Ax_f + B(u^P(\tau_{f,n}) - u^E(\tau_{f,n})) \tag{32}$$
  whose position rows give $\partial\mathbf{r}_f/\partial\tau_{f,n} = \mathbf{v}_f$ ($B$ feeds velocities only), and
  $$\frac{\partial \tilde H_f}{\partial \tau_{f,n}} = \mathbf{v}_f^\top \frac{\partial \lambda^r_{f,n}}{\partial \tau_{f,n}} + (\lambda^r_{f,n})^\top \frac{\partial \mathbf{v}_f}{\partial \tau_{f,n}} = (\lambda^r_{f,n})^\top \frac{\partial \mathbf{v}_f}{\partial \tau_{f,n}}, \tag{33}$$
  where dropping the first term is correct only because $\lambda^r_f$ is held fixed when differentiating w.r.t. $\tau_f$ ($\partial\lambda^r_f/\partial\tau_f \equiv 0$) — the paper does not state this (minor notational slip, p. 4).
- **Remaining blocks (eq. (34))**: $\partial\tilde H_f/\partial\lambda^r_{f,n} = \mathbf{v}_f^\top + (\lambda^r_{f,n})^\top \partial\mathbf{v}_f^\top/\partial\lambda^r_{f,n}$, with $\partial\mathbf{r}_f/\partial\lambda^r_{f,n}$ and $\partial\mathbf{v}_f/\partial\lambda^r_{f,n}$ (sub-blocks of $\partial x_f/\partial\lambda^r_{f,n}$) approximated by forward finite differences (eqs. (35)–(36)):
  $$\frac{\partial x_f}{\partial\lambda^r_{f,n}} \approx \left[\frac{x_f(\xi^1_n) - x_f(\xi_n)}{h},\ \frac{x_f(\xi^2_n) - x_f(\xi_n)}{h},\ \frac{x_f(\xi^3_n) - x_f(\xi_n)}{h}\right],\quad \xi^i_n = \xi_n + h\,e_i,\ i = 1,2,3,$$
  $h > 0$ "sufficiently small" (value **UNREPORTED**). Procedure: (a) form $\xi^i_n$; (b) recompute $\lambda(\xi^i_n)$ by (26) and $x_f(\xi^i_n)$ by integrating (28); (c) assemble (35).
- **Remark 1 (p. 5)**: the sensitivity-ODE method of [6], $ \frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial x(t)}{\partial\lambda_f} = A\frac{\partial x}{\partial\lambda_f} + B\frac{\partial u^P}{\partial\lambda_f} - B\frac{\partial u^E}{\partial\lambda_f}$, cannot be used here because "the discontinuity in the sign function prevents us to derive an explicit expression for $\partial u^P/\partial\lambda_f$" — hence finite differences. This remark is the technical seed of the paper's later failure analysis.
- **Stopping rule (eq. (37))**: $\|F(\xi_{n+1})\| < \varepsilon$.

### 5.5 PSO initialization and Algorithm 1 (Sec. 4.3–4.4, p. 5, eq. (38))
- **Initial guess (eq. (38))**: $\min_\xi F^\top(\xi) K F(\xi)$ with $K = \operatorname{diag}[k_i]$, $k_i > 0$; $F(\xi) = 0_4$ iff the objective is minimized. Solvable by GA, cuckoo search, or PSO; PSO is chosen (population-based stochastic optimizer of Eberhart–Kennedy; claimed equal effectiveness to GA with significantly better computational efficiency, citing [28]).
- **Algorithm 1 (p. 5)** — "Solving the open-loop solution of the game": Input $x^P(0), x^E(0), t_0, \omega, \varepsilon$; Result $t_f, \lambda^r_f, u^{P,*}, u^{E,*}$ and the game trajectory. Initialize $x_0 = x^P(0) - x^E(0)$, $K, h, \alpha, n_{\max}$, $n = 0$. (i) heuristic search solves (38) → $\xi_0$; (ii) compute $x_f$ by (28), $F(\xi_0)$ by (29); (iii) while $\|F(\xi_n)\| \ge \varepsilon$ and $n \le n_{\max}$: assemble the two analytic Jacobian blocks (33), perturbations $\xi^i_n$ (36) with $\lambda(\xi^i_n)$ (26), $x_f(\xi^i_n)$ (28), $F(\xi^i_n)$ (29); FD matrix (35); blocks (34); form $J(\xi_n)$ and update (30); recompute $F(\xi_{n+1})$; (iv) extract $t_f, \lambda^r_f$; reconstruct $\lambda(t)$ (26), controls (27), and the open-loop saddle trajectory.

### 5.6 Summary of strategy/value content
The paper's only formal result is **Theorem 1** (reduction); there is no closed-form value function, no barrier surface, no feedback strategy, and no explicit capture-time formula — the value $J = t_f$ is a *numerical output* per initial condition ($t_f = 360.124$ s, $452.782$ s here; $532.606$ s in the single-thruster comparison). The PE strategy structure revealed: the pursuer's open-loop saddle control is per-axis bang-bang (saturated at $\pm\rho^P$ on each axis, switching whenever a component of $\Psi_{21}(\tau-\tau_f)\lambda^r_f$ crosses zero), while the evader's is a continuous saturated vector aligned with its (antisymmetric) adjoint — and in both successful examples $\lambda^v$ never changes sign, so no switching occurs (Figs. 1(c)–(d), 2(c)–(d)); in the failed example $u^P_x$ switches once (Fig. 3(c)). Everything is local/numerical: Newton is locally convergent (no proof given), PSO is a global heuristic (no guarantee), and all claims are open-loop.

Three examples: two solvable, one not; plus a single-thruster-pursuer counter-scenario on the failed initial condition.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
- **Author-stated**: (i) only the specific 3-thruster-vs-1-thruster configuration is treated; other configurations "we will not elaborate on" (p. 3); (ii) the indirect method fails for some initial states and the remedy is left open — "how to design an efficient algorithm to solve the open-loop control of the satellite pursuit-evasion game with different thrust configurations" (p. 10); (iii) the failure cause is a two-way conjecture (sign-function discontinuity vs. control sign change, p. 10), explicitly not proven.
- **Existence/sufficiency gap**: saddle-point existence is declared "trivial" from Hamiltonian separability (p. 3, below eq. (10)); separability only decouples PMP stationarity — no Isaacs-equation check, no value argument, and no proof that the TPBVP solution is a saddle (robust to unilateral deviation). Theorem 1's "equivalence" is likewise one-directional (p. 4). All "saddle-point" language should be read as "PMP candidate open-loop solution".
- **Non-smooth solver theory absent**: $F(\xi)$ is only piecewise smooth in $\xi$ (switching when a component of $\Psi_{21}(\tau-\tau_f)\lambda^r_f$ vanishes, eq. (27)); the damped Newton (30) with finite-difference Jacobian (35) across switch surfaces has no convergence analysis, $\alpha$ is by trial and error, and PSO (38) can stall in local minima of the weighted residual — the failed case is not diagnosed beyond conjecture (no multi-start study, no continuation/smoothing attempted; the $[-1,1]^3$ adjoint box used in the normalized retry, p. 9, could itself exclude the solution).
- **Idealized termination**: exact position capture with zero capture radius (eq. (8)) and free terminal velocity; numerically "capture" means 0.14–0.61 m residual; post-capture relative velocity is unconstrained and unreported.
- **Singular evader control**: $u^E = -\rho^E\lambda^v/\|\lambda^v\|$ (eq. (19)) is undefined where $\lambda^v = 0$; no sliding/measure-zero analysis (p. 3–4).
- **Scope of evidence**: only three initial conditions, with the evader always at rest at the frame origin (a symmetric special case); no sensitivity study, no statistical evaluation, $n_{\max}$ and $h$ unreported; the CW linearization error and virtual-chief placement error are never quantified (the model is stated to require "close" players and short games, p. 2 — satisfied but unverified a posteriori).
- **Open-loop only**: no feedback implementation or replaying schedule is proposed, although the introduction itself notes closed-loop play needs periodic open-loop re-solving (p. 1) — so the demonstrated 24 s solve time per scenario matters for any onboard use.
- **Typos/incompleteness noted (verified against page renders)**: eq. (15) second equation prints $\psi(x^E_f, x^E_f, t_f)$ (p. 3); Theorem 1's proof prints "$x_f = 0_3$" for the position condition (p. 4); eq. (33) drops the term $\mathbf{v}_f^\top\partial\lambda^r_{f,n}/\partial\tau_{f,n}$ without stating $\partial\lambda^r_f/\partial\tau_f \equiv 0$ (p. 4); "shown in Fig. 1" should be Fig. 2 (p. 7); "the optimal control of the evader is the same as (21)" should be (19) (p. 10); "swarmpaticle" (p. 8); reference [3] (Li et al., Acta Astronaut.) is printed without volume/year/pages and [28] without venue/year (p. 10).

## 8. Citation Value
This paper is the natural **modeling-baseline and cautionary reference for actuator-asymmetric orbital pursuit–evasion games**: it is, to the knowledge recorded here, the first archive entry to mix an $L_\infty$-box (three orthogonal thrusters) pursuer with an $L_2$-ball (single-thruster) evader under CW dynamics, and its reusable assets are (i) the cube-vs-ball PMP control pair (eqs. (11)–(12)) showing exactly where the discontinuity enters (componentwise sign vs. normalized alignment), (ii) the configuration-independent reduction template of Theorem 1 (adjoint antisymmetry (18) + 4-unknown TPBVP (29)), and (iii) the documented failure of the standard PSO+Newton indirect pipeline on a feasible instance — an ideal motivator for direct methods, sign-smoothing/homotopy, sensitivity-free Jacobians, or learning-based strategies in follow-up work. Suggested citation sentences: "Ye et al. [P108] formulated the proximate satellite pursuit-evasion game with dissimilar thrust configurations — a box-constrained three-axis pursuer against a ball-constrained single-thruster evader under CW dynamics — reduced the open-loop saddle-point problem to a four-unknown TPBVP, and showed that the classical indirect method can fail on feasible initial states owing to the discontinuous sign-function control of the pursuer [P108]." Or, as a gap statement: "Unlike the single-thruster games of [P002-style CW formulations], for which the indirect method is efficient [P108], the dissimilar-thrust-configuration game lacks a reliable open-loop solver, as first reported in [P108]."
