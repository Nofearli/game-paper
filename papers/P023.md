## 2. Research Background

Since Isaacs' seminal monograph [1], pursuit-evasion (PE) scenarios with simple motion have been modeled as differential games, with applications including missile defense [2], football tackling, and target/coastline guarding [3]. For two single-integrator agents on a plane with a faster pursuer, the Apollonius Circle (AC) — the locus of points with a fixed ratio of distances to the two foci (agent positions), with ratio equal to the speed ratio — encloses the *evader dominance region*: points the evader can reach no later than the pursuer [4,5,6]. This makes the AC the standard barrier tool for target-guarding and reach-avoid games [7,8,9,10,11]: if the AC intersects the target, the evader wins by running straight to a target point inside the AC. The converse, however, is subtle: if the AC does not intersect the target, the pursuer has no fixed interception point — capture must occur at a location the *evader* effectively chooses. The literature treated each target geometry separately via the Hamilton–Jacobi/isocost machinery, and the resulting strategies demand special treatment on singular surfaces; even the "benign" dispersal surfaces produce chattering [12] and perpetuated dilemmas [14] that an adversarial evader can exploit (Section 1.2 and Appendix A: chattering can make the time-dependent AC grow until the evader wins).

## 3. Research Problem and Gap

**Problem**: For obstacle-free, simple-motion PE with a faster pursuer (speed ratio $\nu<1$) and point capture, construct a *single continuous state-feedback pursuer strategy* that guarantees capture of any admissible evader within an arbitrarily close neighborhood of the AC constructed at initial time.

**Gap, as stated by the authors**:
- [authors' claim, abstract]: "It is well known that the interior of the Apollonius Circle (AC) is the evader's dominance region, however, it was unclear whether the evader could reach outside the initial AC without being captured."
- [authors' claim, p. 2, §1.1]: the often-made statement "capture within the (initial) AC is guaranteed" is imprecise — the correct statement is that "for any specific, known evader trajectory there exists a pursuer trajectory which captures the evader on or within the AC". Against *pure pursuit*, the evader's reachable set is a strict superset of the AC [16], so an evader can reach a target the AC misses (Fig. 1). This claim is attributed to [16] (Makkapati & Tsiotras, *Dynamic Games and Applications*, 2019) and is consistent with that work's reachability analysis, but is not re-derived here — [verified from cited text: plausible, cited, not re-proven].
- [authors' claim, p. 2]: equilibrium (capture-time) strategies chatter near dispersal surfaces [12,13], and chattering in a two-target guarding problem can grow the AC until the evader wins (§1.2, Fig. 2; full construction in Appendix A).
- [authors' claim, p. 2]: traditional HJ-based methods give strong guarantees only where the value function is differentiable and need special treatment along singular (dispersal, switching, transition, etc.) surfaces, per geometry.

**Gap, verified**: the paper identifies that no constructive *universal* pursuer feedback existed that (a) holds against *all* admissible evader strategies (not just equilibrium ones), (b) is smooth on the whole state space (no chattering to exploit), and (c) immediately settles a broad class of capture-location games (target guarding of arbitrary target sets, games of final location) without case-by-case singular-surface analysis. Appendix A additionally proves (modulo a Filippov regularity assumption) that the natural "instantaneous mixed strategy" on a two-target dispersal surface is **not** a Nash equilibrium (p. 8) — a genuine, verifiable gap in standard practice.

## 4. Core Contributions

1. **Theory (main)**: Theorem 1 (p. 3) — a Lyapunov-based pursuit law $\mathbf{v}_P=\hat{\mathbf{z}}_P$ with $\mathbf{z}_P=(R_C-R_A(t))\hat{\mathbf{r}}(t)+\nu\,\mathbf{y}(t)$ that (i) confines the time-varying AC strictly inside a fixed disc $\mathcal{C}$ = initial AC inflated by $\delta$, for all $t>t_0$, against any admissible evader, and (ii) guarantees finite-time capture with explicit bound $t_{\text{capture}}\le t_0+2(1+\nu^{-1})R_C\ln(R_C/\delta)$.
2. **Theory (refinements)**: Corollary 1 — capture occurs strictly closer to the initial AC than the $\delta$-inflation suggests; Lemma 1 — the pursuer–evader distance is strictly decreasing below its initial value at all times ($\|{\bf r}(t)\|<\|{\bf r}(t_0)\|$); Corollary 2 — hence a pursuer with a circular sensor of range $\rho\ge\|{\bf r}(t_0)\|$ never loses the lock ("Mobile Sensing Game").
3. **Game-theoretic resolutions (Implications, Sec. 3)**: Definition 1 (Game of Final Location: terminal payoff $\phi({\bf x}_E(t_f))$) with Corollary 3 (ε-equilibrium pair) and Corollary 4 (**Value $=\phi^\ast=\min_{{\bf x}\in\mathcal{A}(t_0)}\phi({\bf x})$**); Definition 2 (Generalized Target Guarding, a Game of Kind with arbitrary target set) with Corollary 5 (pursuer wins **iff** the initial AC is disjoint from the target set); Corollary 6 (fully decentralized multi-pursuer extension; capture near the intersection $\mathcal{I}$ of initial ACs); Corollary 7 (robustness to over-estimated evader speed $\tilde\nu\ge\nu_{\text{true}}$); Corollary 8 (critical speed $\nu_{\text{crit}}$ characterization for Games of Kind).
4. **Modeling/negative result (Appendix A)**: a two-target guarding case study showing that (Answer A) instantaneous mixed strategies on the dispersal surface are not Nash (an evader heading straight up makes the bang-bang pursuer chatter, with $\dot r\approx r(\nu-1/\sqrt{\nu^2+1})>0$ for $\nu\gtrsim0.786$, growing the AC until the evader wins); (Answer B) deadzone/pure-pursuit commitments require per-geometry evader counter-strategy analysis; (Answer C) viscosity/numerical solutions [21,22] are impractical — motivating the proposed universal law.
5. **Methodological**: reformulation of a PE game as a nonlinear control/stabilization problem (a Lyapunov-like "clearance" product is made non-decreasing regardless of the evader), yielding a *satisficing* (not necessarily optimal) controller that is "approximately equal to the traditional equilibrium strategies" in many cases (p. 2).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results (exhaustive)

### 5.0 Game model, information structure, payoff, termination (p. 3, Sec. 2)

- **Dynamics**: $\dot{\bf x}_E={\bf v}_E$, $\dot{\bf x}_P={\bf v}_P$, ${\bf x}_E,{\bf x}_P\in\mathbb{R}^2$ (simple motion, single integrator, fixed Cartesian frame); bounds $\|{\bf v}_E\|\le\nu$, $\|{\bf v}_P\|\le1$, $\nu<1$. Start at $t_0$. **Termination**: point capture, ${\bf x}_P={\bf x}_E$ (p. 3).
- **Apollonius disc** (eq. (1), p. 3): $\mathcal{A}(t)\triangleq\{{\bf x}:\|{\bf x}-{\bf x}_A(t)\|\le R_A(t)\}$, where ${\bf x}_A(t)=\alpha{\bf x}_E(t)-\beta{\bf x}_P(t)$, $R_A(t)=\gamma\|{\bf r}(t)\|$, ${\bf r}(t)={\bf x}_E(t)-{\bf x}_P(t)$, and $\beta=\nu^2(1-\nu^2)^{-1}=\nu\gamma=\nu^2\alpha$; so (implicitly) $\alpha=(1-\nu^2)^{-1}$, $\gamma=\nu(1-\nu^2)^{-1}$, $\beta=\nu^2(1-\nu^2)^{-1}$, and $\alpha\ge\gamma\ge\beta>0$ for $\nu\in[0,1)$. These are exactly the standard AC formulas: center $({\bf x}_E-\nu^2{\bf x}_P)/(1-\nu^2)$, radius $\nu\|{\bf x}_E-{\bf x}_P\|/(1-\nu^2)$. (Minor presentational gap: $\alpha$ itself is never explicitly defined; it must be inferred from $\beta=\nu^2\alpha$.) The traditional AC is the boundary $\partial\mathcal{A}(t)$. Footnote 2 (p. 3): in 3D the AC becomes a sphere and "the presented analysis holds for 3D systems as well."
- **Construction** (eq. (2), p. 3): fixed disc $\mathcal{C}=\{{\bf x}:\|{\bf x}-{\bf x}_C\|\le R_C\}$ with ${\bf x}_C={\bf x}_A(t_0)$ and $R_C=R_A(t_0)+\delta$, $\delta>0$. Gap distances (eqs. (3)–(5), p. 3): $d_{\min}(t)=R_C-(R_A(t)+\|{\bf y}(t)\|)$, $d_{\max}(t)=R_C-(R_A(t)-\|{\bf y}(t)\|)$, ${\bf y}(t)={\bf x}_A(t)-{\bf x}_C$; $d_{\min}$ is the gap between $\mathcal{A}(t)$'s farthest point and $\partial\mathcal{C}$, $d_{\max}$ the gap to the nearest point. Since $\alpha-\beta=1$, at capture (${\bf r}=0$) one has ${\bf x}_A={\bf x}_E$, i.e., ${\bf y}(t_f)$ is exactly the capture point's offset from ${\bf x}_C$.
- **Information structure**: perfect state feedback on $({\bf x}_E,{\bf x}_P)$; known $\nu$ (relaxed in Cor. 7–8); decentralized (no pursuer–pursuer information) in Cor. 6; sensor-range variant in Cor. 2.
- **Payoffs** (Sec. 3): Game of Final Location terminal payoff $P_P({\bf v}_P,{\bf v}_E)=-P_E({\bf v}_P,{\bf v}_E)=\phi({\bf x}_E(t_f))$ (eq. (9), p. 5) for a continuous field $\phi:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}$; $\phi^\ast\triangleq\min_{{\bf x}\in\mathcal{A}(t_0)}\phi({\bf x})$ (eq. (10)); Game of Distance to Target: $\phi({\bf x})=\min_{{\bf z}\in\mathcal{T}}\|{\bf x}-{\bf z}\|$ (eq. (13), p. 5); Generalized Target Guarding = Game of Kind (Definition 2, p. 5): evader wins iff it reaches $\mathcal{T}$ (points/surfaces fixed in space, not acting as obstacles) before capture.

### 5.1 Theorem 1 (p. 3, eqs. (6)–(8); proof p. 4)

**Statement**: The pursuit law
$$\mathbf{v}_P=\hat{\mathbf{z}}_P\ (\text{eq. (6)}),\qquad \mathbf{z}_P=(R_C-R_A(t))\hat{\mathbf{r}}(t)+\nu\,\mathbf{y}(t)\ (\text{eq. (7)})$$
ensures (i) $\mathcal{A}(t)\in\mathcal{C}^\circ$ (interior), $\forall t>t_0$; and (ii) capture in finite time $t_{\text{capture}}\le t_0+2(1+\nu^{-1})R_C\ln(R_C/\delta)$.

**Assumptions**: $\|{\bf v}_E\|\le\nu<1$, $\|{\bf v}_P\|\le1$; point capture; obstacle-free plane; full state known; fixed $\delta>0$ chosen at $t_0$. Remark 1 (p. 5): evader speeds strictly below $\nu$ are admissible and all guarantees persist.

**Proof approach (part i), tools: Lyapunov-like product function, Cauchy–Schwarz, contradiction + continuity**:
1. Define the Lyapunov-like function $V(t)\triangleq d_{\max}(t)d_{\min}(t)=(R_C-R_A(t))^2-\|{\bf y}(t)\|^2$; $d_{\min}>0$ for all $t$ is necessary and sufficient for $\mathcal{A}(t)\subset\mathcal{C}^\circ$. Note $V(t_0)=\delta\cdot\delta=\delta^2$ (both gaps equal $\delta$ at $t_0$ because the discs are concentric).
2. Differentiate, using $\dot R_A=\gamma\hat{\bf r}^{\,\top}\dot{\bf r}$ and $\dot{\bf x}_A=\alpha{\bf v}_E-\beta{\bf v}_P$; regroup with the identities $\nu\alpha=\gamma$, $\beta=\nu\gamma$:
$\dot V=2\gamma[(R_C-R_A)\hat{\bf r}+\nu{\bf y}]^\top{\bf v}_P-2\alpha[\nu(R_C-R_A)\hat{\bf r}+{\bf y}]^\top{\bf v}_E\triangleq2\gamma{\bf z}_P^\top{\bf v}_P-2\alpha{\bf z}_E^\top{\bf v}_E$, where ${\bf z}_E\triangleq\nu(R_C-R_A(t))\hat{\bf r}(t)+{\bf y}(t)$ (defined inside the proof, p. 4).
3. Bound the evader term by Cauchy–Schwarz: $\dot V\ge2\gamma{\bf z}_P^\top{\bf v}_P-2\alpha\|{\bf z}_E\|\nu$ (uses $\|{\bf v}_E\|\le\nu$ — the *only* place evader capability enters).
4. Choose $\mathbf{v}_P=\hat{\mathbf{z}}_P$ (unit norm, hence admissible whenever $\|{\bf z}_P\|>0$): $\dot V\ge2\gamma(\|{\bf z}_P\|-\|{\bf z}_E\|)=2\gamma\frac{\|{\bf z}_P\|^2-\|{\bf z}_E\|^2}{\|{\bf z}_P\|+\|{\bf z}_E\|}$. **Decisive intermediate identity** (p. 4): $\|{\bf z}_P\|^2-\|{\bf z}_E\|^2=(1-\nu^2)[(R_C-R_A(t))^2-\|{\bf y}(t)\|^2]=(1-\nu^2)V(t)$ — the cross terms $2\nu(R_C-R_A)\hat{\bf r}^{\top}{\bf y}$ cancel *exactly*. With $\gamma(1-\nu^2)=\nu$:
$$\dot V(t)\ \ge\ \frac{2\nu\,V(t)}{\|{\bf z}_P(t)\|+\|{\bf z}_E(t)\|}\quad (\text{eq. (8), p. 4}).$$
5. Positivity of the denominator: by contradiction, $\|{\bf z}_P(t_0)\|=\delta>0$ (since ${\bf y}(t_0)=0$, $R_C=R_A(t_0)+\delta$); if $\tau>t_0$ were the first instant with $\|{\bf z}_P(\tau)\|=0$, then eq. (7) gives $(R_C-R_A(\tau))\hat{\bf r}(\tau)+\nu{\bf y}(\tau)=0$; projecting onto $\hat{\bf r}$ and using $R_C-R_A=d_{\min}+\|{\bf y}\|$: $d_{\min}(\tau)=-\|{\bf y}(\tau)\|-\nu\hat{\bf r}^{\top}(\tau){\bf y}(\tau)\le-\|{\bf y}(\tau)\|+\nu\|{\bf y}(\tau)\|\le0$ (decisive inequality; Cauchy–Schwarz $\hat{\bf r}^{\top}{\bf y}\ge-\|{\bf y}\|$). But on $[t_0,\tau)$, eq. (8) holds with $V>0$ (as long as $V>0$, $\dot V\ge0$), so $V$ cannot have fallen from $\delta^2$ to $0$ — contradiction. Hence $\|{\bf z}_P(t)\|>0\ \forall t\ge t_0$, the law is well-defined, $V$ is non-decreasing, and $d_{\min}>0$ always: claim (i). (Local argument, global in time; analytic; worst-case over evader, i.e., valid against *any* admissible evader strategy.)

**Proof approach (part ii), tools: triangle inequality + integration of a differential inequality (Grönwall-type exponential bound)**:
1. Bounds: $\|{\bf z}_P\|\le(R_C-R_A)+\nu\|{\bf y}\|\le(1+\nu)R_C$ and $\|{\bf z}_E\|\le\nu(R_C-R_A)+\|{\bf y}\|\le(1+\nu)R_C$.
2. From (8): $\dot V\ge\frac{2\nu V}{2(1+\nu)R_C}=\frac{\nu V}{(1+\nu)R_C}$, so $V(t)\ge V(t_0)\exp\!\big(\nu(t-t_0)/((1+\nu)R_C)\big)$ (p. 4).
3. **Decisive intermediate inequality**: $V(t)=(R_C-R_A)^2-\|{\bf y}\|^2\le(R_C-R_A(t))^2$. If ever $V\ge R_C^2$, then $(R_C-R_A)^2\ge R_C^2$, which (with the invariant $R_A<R_C$ from (i)) forces $R_A=0$, i.e., ${\bf r}=0$: capture. ("Since $V(t)=R_C^2$ implies capture, the theorem holds.", p. 4.)
4. Setting $\delta^2 e^{\nu(T-t_0)/((1+\nu)R_C)}=R_C^2$ yields $T=\frac{(1+\nu)R_C}{\nu}\ln\frac{R_C^2}{\delta^2}=2(1+\nu^{-1})R_C\ln(R_C/\delta)$ — exactly the printed bound (ii).
- **Physical meaning**: the control ${\bf z}_P$ blends a *line-of-sight shrinking* term $(R_C-R_A)\hat{\bf r}$ (chase the evader to shrink the AC) with a *re-centering* term $\nu{\bf y}$ (drag the AC center back toward ${\bf x}_C$); $V=d_{\max}d_{\min}$ is a squared-clearance product measuring how centrally the AC sits inside $\mathcal{C}$. Guarantee: capture location confined to $\mathcal{C}$, capture time uniformly bounded over all evader strategies. The bound is deliberately conservative — its role is to rule out indefinite evasion (authors say so, p. 4); tighter times/spatial bounds obtainable via an optimal-control refinement.
- **Extensibility**: Remark 1 (speeds below ν); footnote 2 (3D); the whole Sec. 3 corollary stack; smooth everywhere (no singular surfaces to exploit).

### 5.2 Corollary 1 (p. 4)

**Statement (as printed)**: "Although we inflate the initial AC radius by δ to construct the region C, it is guaranteed that capture will occur within a δ₁ neighborhood of the initial AC, where $\delta_1=R_C-\sqrt{R_C^2-\delta^2}<\delta$."
**Proof (as printed)**: at the final time $R_A(t_f)=0$ (capture), so $d_{\min}(t_f)+d_{\max}(t_f)=2R_C$ and $V(t_f)=d_{\min}(t_f)(2R_C-d_{\min}(t_f))\ge V(t_0)=\delta^2$; defining $\delta_1$ as the smallest possible $d_{\min}(t_f)$ gives the result.
**What this literally establishes** (archive derivation): $d_{\min}(t_f)\ge\delta_1$ means the capture point lies at distance $\ge\delta_1$ from $\partial\mathcal{C}$, i.e., within $\sqrt{R_C^2-\delta^2}=R_C-\delta_1$ of ${\bf x}_C$; equivalently within the initial-AC *disc* inflated by $\delta-\delta_1<\delta$ (and strictly inside it, since $\dot V>0$ while $V>0$). The verbal claim "within a $\delta_1$ neighborhood of the initial AC" is stronger than this whenever $\delta<\tfrac{4}{5}R_C$ (that reading requires $\delta-\delta_1\le\delta_1$), and fails outright under an "annulus around $\partial\mathcal{A}(t_0)$" reading — e.g., a stationary evader is captured at ${\bf x}_E(t_0)$, at depth $\beta\|{\bf r}(t_0)\|$ inside the AC. See Section 7, observation 1.

### 5.3 Lemma 1 (p. 4)

**Statement**: For all $t>t_0$, $\|{\bf r}(t)\|<\|{\bf r}(t_0)\|$ (the inter-agent distance never exceeds its initial value, regardless of the evader).
**Proof approach (3 steps, algebraic)**: (1) from the proof of Theorem 1, $V(t)>V(t_0)$ for $t>t_0$ (strict, since $\dot V>0$ whenever $V>0$); (2) $(R_C-R_A(t))^2-\|{\bf y}(t)\|^2>(R_C-R_A(t_0))^2$ with ${\bf y}(t_0)=0$ and $R_A(t)<R_C$ gives $R_C-R_A(t)>R_C-R_A(t_0)$, i.e., $R_A(t)<R_A(t_0)$; (3) $R_A=\gamma\|{\bf r}\|$ with $\gamma>0$ yields the claim. Global, analytic. Physical meaning: guaranteed closing; the pursuer provably never "lets go".

### 5.4 Corollary 2 — Mobile Sensing Game (p. 4)

**Statement**: In limited-information games where the pursuer has a circular sensing range $\rho\ge\|{\bf r}(t_0)\|$, the Theorem-1 strategy guarantees capture *without losing sensing* of the evader. Proof: direct from Lemma 1. Connects to the partial-information target-defense line [18]. Analytic, global.

### 5.5 Corollary 3 — ε-equilibrium of the Game of Final Location (p. 5)

**Statement**: For any $\varepsilon>0$, the strategy pair {pursuer plays (6)–(7); evader moves directly to ${\bf x}^\ast=\arg\min_{{\bf x}\in\mathcal{A}(t_0)}\phi({\bf x)}$} is an ε-equilibrium.
**Proof approach (2 steps)**: (1) *evader side* (dominance, no payoff rate needed): the evader is dominant for every point in $\mathcal{A}(t_0)$ — an open-loop max-speed run reaches any such point before capture, then waits; hence $\phi({\bf x}_E(t_f))\le\phi^\ast$ regardless of the pursuer; (2) *pursuer side*: Theorem 1 confines capture to $\mathcal{C}$; by continuity of $\phi$, for any $\varepsilon>0$ there is $\delta>0$ with $\min_{{\bf x}\in\mathcal{C}}\phi({\bf x})\ge\phi^\ast-\varepsilon$, and the pursuer's law enforces $\phi({\bf x}_E(t_f))\ge\min_{{\bf x}\in\mathcal{C}}\phi$. Decisive inequality: $\phi^\ast-\varepsilon\le\phi({\bf x}_E(t_f))\le\phi^\ast$. Analytic; the ε comes solely from the $\delta$-inflation. Physical meaning: the evader *freely chooses* the capture location — but only inside the initial AC.

### 5.6 Corollary 4 — Value of the Game of Final Location (p. 5)

**Statement**: The Value is $\phi^\ast$.
**Proof approach (sandwich, 3 steps)**: (1) Upper Value $\overline{V}\triangleq\inf_{{\bf v}_E}\sup_{{\bf v}_P}P_P=\phi^\ast$ (eq. (11)) from the evader-side inequality in Corollary 3; (2) Lower Value $\underline{V}\triangleq\sup_{{\bf v}_P}\inf_{{\bf v}_E}P_P\ge\sup_{{\bf v}_P}(\phi^\ast-\varepsilon)=\phi^\ast$ (eq. (12)) from the pursuer side, for every ε; (3) $\underline{V}\le\overline{V}$ by definition forces $\underline{V}=\overline{V}=\phi^\ast$. Important subtlety printed on p. 5: there is **no single δ-strategy** achieving exactly $\phi^\ast$ — the Value is attained only in the limit $\delta\to0$ (the sup over pursuer strategies includes that limit). Analytic; global.

### 5.7 Corollary 5 — Generalized Target Guarding (Game of Kind) (pp. 5–6)

**Statement**: For a Generalized Target Guarding Problem (arbitrary target set $\mathcal{T}$ of fixed points/surfaces; target not an obstacle), there exists $\delta>0$ such that the Theorem-1 strategy guarantees a pursuer win **iff** the initial AC contains no point of $\mathcal{T}$.
**Proof approach**: *Necessity*: "trivially from longstanding results that the initial AC forms the evader dominance region" (cited, not re-proven). *Sufficiency*: let $d^\ast>0$ be the shortest distance between $\partial\mathcal{A}(t_0)$ and $\mathcal{T}$; choose $\delta=0.5\,d^\ast$; then $\mathcal{C}$ is disjoint from $\mathcal{T}$, and Theorem 1 confines capture inside $\mathcal{C}$ — i.e., before the evader reaches the target. Decisive construction: $\delta=0.5d^\ast$ (p. 6). Global, analytic. This converts *all* previously case-by-case guarding/barrier analyses (convex targets [5], circular regions [8,15], border defense [7], reach-avoid [10]) into one trivial check: $\mathcal{A}(t_0)\cap\mathcal{T}\overset{?}{=}\emptyset$.

### 5.8 Corollary 6 — Multi-pursuer Game (p. 6; stated without formal proof)

**Statement**: With multiple pursuers of possibly different speeds each playing (6)–(7), let $\mathcal{I}$ be the intersection of all initial ACs. Capture occurs arbitrarily close to $\mathcal{I}$; the Value of the Final Location Game is the maximum of $\phi$ over $\mathcal{I}$; the pursuers win any Target Guarding Game iff $\mathcal{I}$ contains no target point. Implementation is **completely decentralized** — "each pursuer does not even need information about its fellow pursuers" (p. 6). Rationale (not a formal proof): each pursuer unilaterally enforces its own $\mathcal{C}_j$ containment, and the evader's pairwise dominance holds on $\mathcal{I}$.

### 5.9 Corollary 7 — Uncertain Maximum Speed (p. 6; follows from Remark 1)

**Statement**: If the pursuer's estimate $\tilde\nu\ge\nu_{\text{true}}$, strategy (6) guarantees the evader never reaches any point outside $\mathcal{C}_o(\tilde\nu)$ (the speed-$\tilde\nu$ AC region; $\mathcal{C}_o(\nu_1)\subset\mathcal{C}_o(\nu_2)$ iff $\nu_2>\nu_1$). Discussion (p. 6): trade-off between risk of underestimation (no capture) and size of the guaranteed region; *open question*: how/when to update $\tilde\nu$ and how the evader should counter an update law.

### 5.10 Corollary 8 — Critical Speed (p. 6, eq. (14); argued, not formally proven)

**Statement**: The pursuer wins the Generalized Target Guarding Game **iff** $\nu_{\text{true}}<\nu_{\text{crit}}$, where $\nu_{\text{crit}}<1$ is the largest speed ratio such that $\min_{{\bf x}\in\mathcal{A}(t_0;\nu)}\phi({\bf x})>0$ (eq. (14); here $\phi$ is the distance-to-target field, so $\phi>0$ ⟺ outside $\mathcal{T}$). Strategic consequence (p. 6): for Games of Kind the pursuer should play with $\tilde\nu=\nu_{\text{crit}}$ and should **not** estimate $\nu_{\text{true}}$ at all — estimation yields no advantage and creates an exploitable channel ("by not using an estimator, it prevents any adversarial action based on exploiting the estimator"). Contrast with Games of Degree (Final Location), where a genuine risk/area trade-off arises through $\tilde\nu$.

### 5.11 Appendix A — dispersal-surface analysis (pp. 7–8)

Cost $J=\max_{{\bf v}_P}\min_{{\bf v}_E}\min(d_1,d_2)$ for two vertical targets; $\{d_1=d_2\}$ is a dispersal surface with seemingly subgame-perfect nonsingular strategies and no perpetuated dilemma. **Answer A** (instantaneous mixed strategy): *not* a Nash equilibrium — holding the pursuer's bang-bang strategy fixed, the evader "goes straight up"; on the singular surface $\hat{\bf r}\approx\hat{\bf v}_E$, and if the pursuer heads to $I_2$, $\cos\psi\approx1/\sqrt{\nu^2+1}$ and $\dot r\approx r\,(\nu-1/\sqrt{\nu^2+1})$ (p. 8), which is positive for $\nu\gtrsim0.786$ — archive note: this threshold solves $\nu^4+\nu^2-1=0$, i.e., $\nu=\sqrt{(\sqrt5-1)/2}\approx0.7862$ (paper gives the numeric threshold without derivation); then chattering makes the AC grow until it intersects a target, and the evader wins (discretized-simulation video: https://youtu.be/7J_8l_Uy6f8). Rigor caveat printed in the paper: a full treatment needs Filippov's discontinuous-RHS framework [20], similar to [19]; *conditional on a continuous solution*, Answer A is not Nash. **Answer B** (deadzone: commit to pure pursuit for time $T$ near the surface): viable but needs per-geometry best evader counter-strategy analysis ("harvest small reward gain then proceed to a critical point" vs. "stay near the surface to trigger the deadzone"), $T$ must be calibrated per $\nu$. **Answer C** (viscosity/numerical [21,22]): difficult in practice, numerical only. Conclusion of the appendix: the main theorem dominates all three answers.

**Remark 1 (p. 5)**: "If the evader employs any speed below ν, then all the guarantees in this section hold." — the seed of Corollaries 7–8.

No formal theorem exists beyond the above; every numbered result (Thm 1, Cor 1–8, Lemma 1) is covered above. All proofs are analytic; there are no numerically-obtained claims inside any theorem.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues

**Author-stated (Sec. 4, p. 6)**:
1. Results "do not apply to cases where the agents have more complex dynamics (higher order, nonholonomic, state spaces with other geometries [17]), in games with a non-zero capture radius, or where obstacles are present", because "the AC no longer accurately captures the dominance region".
2. For a faster evader ($\nu>1$), "capture cannot be enforced with only one pursuer. Multiple pursuers will have to cooperate."
3. "Other games, such as the Game of Capture Time, still require further treatment."
4. Open (Sec. 3, Cor. 7 discussion, p. 6): how/when the pursuer should update $\tilde\nu$, and the evader's counter-strategy to the update law, "remains an open question".
5. Open (Appendix A): none of Answers A/B/C for the singular surface is fully resolved; B requires per-geometry counter-strategy analysis; C is numerical-only.

**Critical observations (this archive, with page/eq. pointers)**:
1. **Corollary 1 wording vs. proof content (p. 4)**: the proof establishes $d_{\min}(t_f)\ge\delta_1=R_C-\sqrt{R_C^2-\delta^2}$, i.e., capture within $\sqrt{R_C^2-\delta^2}$ of ${\bf x}_C$ — the initial-AC *disc* inflated by $\delta-\delta_1$ (and $\dot V>0$ while $V>0$ makes this strict). The printed phrase "capture will occur within a δ₁ neighborhood of the initial AC" only follows if "neighborhood of the initial AC" means "inset of $\mathcal{C}$ by $\delta_1$"; under the natural readings (within $\delta_1$ of the disc, or in the $\delta_1$-annulus of $\partial\mathcal{A}(t_0)$) it requires $\delta-\delta_1\le\delta_1\iff\delta\ge\frac45 R_C$, and it is refuted by the stationary-evader case (capture at depth $\beta\|{\bf r}(t_0)\|$ inside the AC). The bound content is correct; the verbal packaging overstates the spatial tightness for small δ.
2. **α is never explicitly defined** (p. 3, below eq. (1)): only $\beta=\nu^2(1-\nu^2)^{-1}=\nu\gamma=\nu^2\alpha$ is printed; readers must infer $\alpha=(1-\nu^2)^{-1}$, $\gamma=\nu(1-\nu^2)^{-1}$.
3. **Appendix A typo/approximation (p. 8)**: "$\dot r={\bf r}\cdot({\bf v}_E-{\bf v}_P)$" should be $\dot r=\hat{\bf r}\cdot({\bf v}_E-{\bf v}_P)$ ($r=\|{\bf r}\|$ is scalar); $\cos\psi\approx1/\sqrt{\nu^2+1}$ is an approximation tied to the drawn geometry, and the threshold 0.786 is stated without its derivation ($\nu^4+\nu^2-1=0$).
4. **Corollary 6 is stated without proof** (p. 6) — the multi-pursuer "intersection" guarantee and the claim that the Final-Location Value equals $\max_{{\bf x}\in\mathcal{I}}\phi$ are asserted, not derived; Corollaries 7–8 are likewise informal ("follows trivially", "can be verified").
5. **Necessity in Corollary 5** rests on the "longstanding" dominance result, cited rather than proven (p. 6); the preprint nowhere re-proves that the AC interior is the evader dominance region.
6. **The Value result (Cor. 4) is a limit statement**: no fixed δ-strategy attains $\phi^\ast$; only the $\delta\to0$ limit does (p. 5). Practitioners wanting a guaranteed payoff must trade ε against δ via Corollary 3.
7. **Information requirements**: the law (6)–(7) needs the full state $({\bf x}_E,{\bf x}_P)$ and ν (or a chosen $\tilde\nu$); no measurement-noise, delay, or discrete-time implementation analysis is given (Cor. 2 addresses only sensor *range*).
8. **Satisficing, not optimal**: the law solves Games of Final Location/Kind to arbitrary precision and eliminates singular surfaces, but it is not the capture-time-optimal policy (authors acknowledge, p. 4); the Game of Capture Time remains outside the scope.

## 8. Citation Value

**Paragraph role**: This is the definitive "geometric satisficing" paper of the Apollonius-circle line: it converts the AC from a barrier-construction tool into a *containment* guarantee via a Lyapunov argument, and thereby dissolves the dispersal/switching-surface pathologies (chattering, perpetuated dilemma, non-Nash mixed strategies) that the Hamilton–Jacobi treatment of target-guarding games must patch case by case. In the P-collection it is the canonical citation for (a) the precise statement that capture can be confined to an arbitrarily tight neighborhood of the initial AC against *any* evader, (b) the Value $\phi^\ast=\min_{\mathcal{A}(t_0)}\phi$ of capture-location games, (c) the iff-criterion for generalized target guarding, and (d) the documented dangers of equilibrium strategies near dispersal surfaces (Appendix A). Use it as the closest related work whenever a paper claims geometric dominance regions, AC-based barriers, or decentralized pursuer strategies for slower-evader simple-motion games; contrast with it when treating nonzero capture radius, obstacles, higher-order/nonholonomic dynamics, capture-time objectives, or faster evaders — those are exactly its stated boundaries (p. 6).
**Suggested citation sentences**: "Dorothy et al. showed that a single smooth Lyapunov-based pursuit law guarantees, against any admissible evader, capture within an arbitrarily close neighborhood of the initial Apollonius circle, so that the value of any capture-location game equals $\min_{{\bf x}\in\mathcal{A}(t_0)}\phi({\bf x})$ and target guarding reduces to checking whether the initial AC is disjoint from the target." Or: "As demonstrated in [P023], equilibrium pursuit strategies can be exploited on dispersal surfaces (chattering that lets a slower evader grow the AC until it reaches a target), motivating nonsmoothness-free satisficing pursuit laws."
