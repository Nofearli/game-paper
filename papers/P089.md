## 2. Research Background
Competition and cooperation among agents in multiagent systems are effectively modeled as differential games, where agents perform perception, cognitive learning, and knowledge-based optimal decision making (p. 993). Among adversarial tasks, capture-the-flag (CTF) is a canonical two-phase game: an attackers' alliance tries to capture flags (Phase I) and return to a safe region (Phase II), while a defenders' alliance tries to protect the flags and apprehend as many attackers as possible (p. 993). Classical CTF treatments solve the one-attacker-one-defender game via the HJI equation [9], [27], [33], [34] (paper's numbering), but HJI-based solution complexity grows exponentially with the number of players and does not extend to multiplayer games [authors' claim, p. 994]. Multiplayer adversarial games have instead been handled with matching/decomposition ideas — maximum matching to reduce many-vs-many reach-avoid to one-to-one duels [38], matching-based capture strategies for 3D heterogeneous reach-avoid [30], simplified 0-1 programming for interception maximization [46], [47] — and with geometric tools (Voronoi diagrams, Apollonius circles) [40]–[45]. CTF differs from pursuit–evasion and reach-avoid in that attackers have *different objectives in two phases* with two distinct termination conditions, so "traditional geometric methods, which construct a single value function, [are] inadequate" [authors' claim, p. 994].

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
**Problem (Section II, pp. 995–996)**: Game region a compact set $\Omega \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$ with upper vertices $M(0,h)$, $N(l,h)$; return (safe) region $\mathcal{R} := \{(x,y) \in \mathbb{R}^2 \mid y \ge h\}$; target line $T := \{(x,y) \mid y = h\}$. Attackers' alliance $\mathcal{A} = \{A_1,\dots,A_{N_a}\}$ and defenders' alliance $\mathcal{D} = \{D_1,\dots,D_{N_d}\}$ have point-mass constant-speed dynamics (eq. (1), p. 995): $\dot x_{a_i} = V_{a_i}\cos\theta_{a_i}$, $\dot y_{a_i} = V_{a_i}\sin\theta_{a_i}$, $\dot x_{d_j} = V_{d_j}\cos\theta_{d_j}$, $\dot y_{d_j} = V_{d_j}\sin\theta_{d_j}$, heading angles $\theta \in [-\pi,\pi)$, joint state $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{2(N_a+N_d)}$, written $\dot{\mathbf{x}} = f(\mathbf{x},u,v)$ with cooperative heading controls $u = \{\theta_{a_i}\}$, $v = \{\theta_{d_j}\}$. Binary variable $\varsigma_{ij} = 1$ iff defender $D_j$ is assigned to attacker $A_i$, with each defender assigned to at most one attacker, $\sum_{i=1}^{N_a}\varsigma_{ij} \le 1$. **Winning conditions**: with $N_a$ flags, the attackers' alliance wins when at least $\iota$ of the $N_a$ attackers capture flags and return to $\mathcal{R}$ without capture ($0 < \iota \le N_a$); the defenders' alliance wins by "capturing or indefinitely delaying $N_a - \iota + 1$ attackers" (p. 995). Two questions posed (p. 995): (Q1) what strategies win the game; (Q2) what task-allocation scheme should defenders use. **Assumption 1** (p. 995): all defenders faster than all attackers, $\max_i V_{a_i} < \min_j V_{d_j}$ (Remark 1: standard in [30], [33], [47]). **Assumption 2** (p. 996, eq. (2)): initial positions mutually distinct, $\|x^0_{a_i}-x^0_{d_j}\|_2 > 0$, $\|x^0_{a_i}-x_f\|_2 > 0$, $\|x^0_{d_j}-x_f\|_2 > 0$; players and flags are points (Remark 2).

**Gap — [authors' claim]** (p. 994): (i) prior CTF work [27], [33], [34], [49] is one-to-one only; (ii) solving the HJI equation directly (as [34]) has exponential complexity in the player count and "cannot be applied to multiplayer differential games"; (iii) CTF value functions differ across the two phases, so single-value-function geometric methods fail; the two Phase-I terminations (game entering Phase II or ending) make simultaneous two-phase optimization hard for defenders; (iv) the multiplayer task-assignment problem is NP-hard and "cannot be solved exactly" [38]. **[Verified from cited text]**: refs [27] (Garcia–Casbeer–Pachter, CDC 2018), [33] (Pachter–Casbeer–Garcia, CCTA 2020), [34] (Huang et al., TCST 2015) and [49] (Zhou et al., IECON 2021, the same group's precursor) are indeed single-attacker/single-or-few-defender CTF formulations; [38] (Chen–Zhou–Tomlin, TAC 2017) does reduce multiplayer reach-avoid to pairwise outcomes via maximum matching, supporting the reduction idea, although the NP-hardness of the specific MBMC problem defined here is asserted rather than proven by reduction; the claim that single-value-function geometry is inadequate is consistent with the two-phase structure (Phase I has two terminal conditions) but is argued, not formalized. The gap statement is internally consistent; the sharpest (and verifiable) novelty is the multiplayer extension plus the matching layer, since no prior CTF paper treats $N_d$-vs-$N_a$ alliances.

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Modeling**: Extension of one-to-one CTF games [27], [33], [34], [49] to multiplayer CTF differential games with complex interactions and with counting of objects captured by defenders (contribution 1, p. 994).
2. **Theory**: Analytical solution of multiplayer CTF games by reduction to two-defense-one (2D-1A) or one-defense-one (1D-1A) situations; a **two-phase joint optimal strategy** for defenders obtained by constructing a continuous and continuously differentiable value function, proved to satisfy the HJI equation (contribution 2, p. 994; Theorem 1 + Corollary 1).
3. **Algorithm**: A constrained matching formulation — maximum bipartite matching with conflict graph (MBMC) — to maximize the number of captured attackers, solved approximately by a **sequential matching algorithm** built on the Hungarian algorithm; claimed to "significantly decrease computational complexity while guaranteeing the capture of the maximum number of attackers" relative to the maximum-network-flow algorithm of [30] (contribution 3, p. 994; Algorithm 1, Remarks 5–6). *(Classification: theory 1–2, modeling 1, algorithm 3; experiments = Section IV numerical cases.)*

## 5. Key Mathematical Results

### 5.0 Model primitives (exact equations)
- **Dynamics (1), p. 995**: simple motion, 2$(N_a{+}N_d)$ scalar ODEs as above; no capture radius (point capture), no region-boundary dynamics (players are not constrained to stay in $\Omega$ by the equations).
- **Speed ratios (3), p. 996**: $\mu_1 = V_{d1}/V_a$, $\mu_2 = V_{d2}/V_a$, $\mu_3 = V_{d2}/V_{d1}$, with $\mu_1 > 1$, $\mu_2 > 1$; in the Appendix it is assumed WLOG that $D_1$ is the slower defender, so $\mu_3 > 1$.
- **Apollonius circles (4)–(7), p. 996**: between $D_1$ and $A$: $C_1 = \{(x,y)\,|\,(x-x_{C_1})^2 + (y-y_{C_1})^2 = R_{C_1}^2\}$ with $x_{C_1} = \frac{x_a\mu_1^2 - x_{d_1}}{\mu_1^2-1}$, $y_{C_1} = \frac{y_a\mu_1^2 - y_{d_1}}{\mu_1^2-1}$, $R_{C_1} = \frac{\mu_1\sqrt{(x_{d_1}-x_a)^2 + (y_{d_1}-y_a)^2}}{\mu_1^2-1}$ (5); analogously $C_2$ with $(\mu_2, x_{d_2}, y_{d_2})$ (6)–(7). **Dominance (winning) disks (8)**: $\Gamma_1 = \{(x,y) \mid (x-x_{C_1})^2 + (y-y_{C_1})^2 \le R_{C_1}^2\}$, $\Gamma_2$ analogously. *Direction of dominance* (not stated explicitly in the paper, fixed here by computation): the interior of an Apollonius circle encloses the slower player, so $\Gamma_i$ is the set of points the **attacker** reaches strictly before $D_i$ (the attacker's dominance region w.r.t. $D_i$); consistent with the paper's observation that the attacker lies inside both disks, $\mathbf{x}_a \in \Gamma_1 \cap \Gamma_2$ (p. 996; indeed $|\mathbf{x}_a - \mathbf{x}_{C_i}| = R_{C_i}/\mu_i < R_{C_i}$). Hence $\mathbf{x}_f \in \Gamma_i \Leftrightarrow$ attacker captures the flag before $D_i$ can intercept $\Leftrightarrow$ $D_i$ alone **loses** Phase I; $\mathbf{x}_f \notin \Gamma_i \Leftrightarrow$ $D_i$ alone prevents the flag capture. The three circle relations of Fig. 2 (p. 996) — inclusion, tangency, intersection — organize the case analysis.
- **Information structure**: complete information / state feedback ("if all players own complete information", p. 997); all strategies are state-feedback heading rules.
- **Cost and termination**: no running cost; the Phase-II value is the **ordinate (y-coordinate) of the capture point** — the attacker maximizes it (being captured as high as possible = as close to $\mathcal{R}$ as possible), the defenders minimize it. Phase I ends when the attacker captures the flag or is intercepted before flag capture [27]; Phase II ends when the attacker is captured or reaches $\mathcal{R}$. Overall alliance winning conditions as in Section 3.

### 5.1 Lemma 1 (p. 996) — Phase I: a defender coalition is never needed
**Statement**: In Phase I of CTF differential games, if a coalition of defenders can intercept an attacker, there must be a single defender that can successfully intercept the attacker.
**Proof approach** (as printed): (1) In Phase I the attacker must both evade interception and reach the flag $F: \mathbf{x}_f = (x_f, y_f)$. (2) If the two defenders together prevent the capture, then $\mathbf{x}_f \notin \Gamma_1 \cap \Gamma_2$ and $\Gamma_1 \ne \Gamma_2$. (3) Hence at least one of $\mathbf{x}_f \notin \Gamma_1$ or $\mathbf{x}_f \notin \Gamma_2$ holds (elementary logic: $\neg(P_1 \wedge P_2) \Rightarrow \neg P_1 \vee \neg P_2$). (4) "With the geometric properties of the Apollonius circle, there is a defender that intercepts the attacker alone." Local, analytic-geometric. Under the dominance reading of Section 5.0 the chain is sound: coalition success excludes $\mathbf{x}_f \in \Gamma_1 \cap \Gamma_2$ (if the flag were in both attacker-dominance disks, no defender could arrive first), so the flag lies outside some $\Gamma_i$, which is exactly the known single-defender Phase-I winning condition [33], [34]. **Supporting Remark 3** (p. 996): citing [47], assigning three pursuers to one evader brings no extra benefit — points on the barrier that divide winning regions depend on at most two defenders; this justifies capping coalitions at two defenders throughout the paper.

### 5.2 Lemma 2 (p. 996) — Phase II reduction condition
**Statement**: In Phase II, if a coalition of two defenders can cooperate to intercept the attacker and either $(x_{C_1}, y_{C_1}+R_{C_1}) \in \Gamma_2$ or $(x_{C_2}, y_{C_2}+R_{C_2}) \in \Gamma_1$ holds, then a single defender can intercept the attacker successfully. Here $(x_{C_i}, y_{C_i}+R_{C_i})$ is the **topmost point of $C_i$**, i.e., the best (highest-ordinate) capture point achievable by $D_i$ alone.
**Proof approach** (as printed, abbreviated): assume $(x_{C_1}, y_{C_1}+R_{C_1}) \in \Gamma_2$; if the two circles are contained, "it only relies on the defender $D_1$"; if inscribed or intersecting, "although there is an intersection between them, the ordinate of a point in the common area of them is greater than the intersection" — concluding a single defender suffices. **Proof gap (critical observation)**: this is a verbal case analysis with no inequalities or monotonicity argument; the containment sub-case is asserted (and, strictly, if $\Gamma_2 \subseteq \Gamma_1$ the hypothesis "top of $C_1 \in \Gamma_2$" can hold only at tangency, since the top of $C_1$ lies on $\partial C_1$), and no formal statement of *which* defender wins or with what capture ordinate is derived. The operative output of Lemma 2 is the **genuine-cooperation condition**: both defenders contribute only when $(x_{C_1}, y_{C_1}+R_{C_1}) \notin \Gamma_2$ **and** $(x_{C_2}, y_{C_2}+R_{C_2}) \notin \Gamma_1$ (p. 997); under this condition the simultaneous-capture point occurs at the intersection of $C_1$ and $C_2$, and Theorem 1 applies. The same two conditions appear as constraints in Corollary 1's program (13).

### 5.3 Theorem 1 (p. 997, eqs. (9)–(11); proof pp. 1001–1003, eqs. (14)–(31)) — cooperative 2D-1A value and equilibrium
**Statement**: In Phase II of CTF differential games, consider the case where two defenders capture the attacker **simultaneously** and the attacker's goal is to make the ordinate of the capture point as large as possible. The value function is
$$V(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{\mathfrak{N}_1 + \tau}{\mathfrak{D}}, \tag{9}$$
and it **satisfies the HJI equation**, where (10): $\tau = \sqrt{(x_{C_1}-x_{C_2})^2\,\mathfrak{N}_2}$; $\mathfrak{N}_1 = y_{C_3}(x_{C_1}-x_{C_2})^2 - (y_{C_1}-y_{C_2})\kappa$; $\mathfrak{N}_2 = R_{C_3}^2\mathfrak{D} - (\kappa + y_{C_3}(y_{C_1}-y_{C_2}))^2$; $\kappa = \tfrac{\mathfrak{R}}{2} - x_{C_3}(x_{C_2}-x_{C_1})$; $\mathfrak{D} = (x_{C_1}-x_{C_2})^2 + (y_{C_1}-y_{C_2})^2$; $\mathfrak{R} = R_{C_1}^2 - R_{C_2}^2 + x_{C_2}^2 - x_{C_1}^2 + y_{C_2}^2 - y_{C_1}^2$. Here $C_3$ (15)–(16) is the Apollonius circle between the two defenders, $C_3 = \{(x,y) \mid (x-x_{C_3})^2 + (y-y_{C_3})^2 = R_{C_3}^2\}$ with $x_{C_3} = \frac{x_{d_1}\mu_3^2 - x_{d_2}}{\mu_3^2-1}$, $y_{C_3} = \frac{y_{d_1}\mu_3^2 - y_{d_2}}{\mu_3^2-1}$, $R_{C_3} = \frac{\mu_3\sqrt{(x_{d_2}-x_{d_1})^2 + (y_{d_2}-y_{d_1})^2}}{\mu_3^2-1}$ — the locus of simultaneous arrival of $D_1$ and $D_2$. **Optimal capture point** $Q: \mathbf{x}_q = (x_q, y_q)$ with $y_q = V(\mathbf{x})$ and $x_q = \frac{\mathfrak{R} - 2(y_{C_2}-y_{C_1})y_q}{2(x_{C_2}-x_{C_1})}$, and the **optimal state-feedback strategies** are pure "head to $Q$" rules (11): $\cos\theta^*_a = \frac{x_q - x_a}{\sqrt{(x_q-x_a)^2 + (y_q-y_a)^2}}$, $\sin\theta^*_a$ analogously, and identically for $D_1$ (with $x_{d_1}, y_{d_1}$) and $D_2$ (with $x_{d_2}, y_{d_2}$).
**Assumptions**: Phase II; simultaneous two-defender capture; the genuine-cooperation conditions of Lemma 2; $\mu_1, \mu_2 > 1$; WLOG $V_{d1} < V_{d2}$ ($\mu_3 > 1$); regularity $\mathfrak{D} \ne 0$, $\mathfrak{N}_2 \ne 0$, $x_{C_1} \ne x_{C_2}$ (stated after eq. (21), p. 1002); implicitly $(x_{C_1}-x_{C_2})^2\mathfrak{N}_2 \ge 0$ for real $\tau$.
**Proof approach** (Appendix; I verified the algebra independently):
1. **Radical axis (14)**: subtracting the equations of $C_1$ and $C_2$ gives $2(x_{C_2}-x_{C_1})x + 2(y_{C_2}-y_{C_1})y = \mathfrak{R}$ — any simultaneous-capture point lies on $C_1 \cap C_2$, hence on this line.
2. **Reduce to a line–circle intersection**: the capture point also lies on $C_3$ (simultaneous arrival of both defenders). Solving (14) for $x$ and substituting into (15) yields the quadratic in the capture ordinate (17): $\mathfrak{D}y^2 - 2\mathfrak{N}_1 y + \kappa^2 + (y_{C_3}^2 - R_{C_3}^2)(x_{C_2}-x_{C_1})^2 = 0$. (The substitution uses $\kappa = \mathfrak{R}/2 - x_{C_3}(x_{C_2}-x_{C_1})$ so that $x - x_{C_3} = [\kappa - (y_{C_2}-y_{C_1})y]/(x_{C_2}-x_{C_1})$.)
3. **Decisive intermediate identity (not printed explicitly, reconstructed and verified)**: the discriminant factors as $\mathfrak{N}_1^2 - \mathfrak{D}\big[\kappa^2 + (y_{C_3}^2 - R_{C_3}^2)(x_{C_2}-x_{C_1})^2\big] = (x_{C_1}-x_{C_2})^2\,\mathfrak{N}_2$, so the roots are $y = \frac{\mathfrak{N}_1 \pm \tau}{\mathfrak{D}}$ with $\tau = \sqrt{(x_{C_1}-x_{C_2})^2\mathfrak{N}_2}$; the attacker picks the larger root, giving $V(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{\mathfrak{N}_1 + \tau}{\mathfrak{D}}$ (printed as "the solution of (17) is $y = [(\mathfrak{N}_1 + \tau)/\mathfrak{D}]$", p. 1001).
4. **Differentiation of $V$**: since $V$ is a nested composition of the Apollonius centers/radii, the partial derivatives w.r.t. all six states $(x_{d_1}, y_{d_1}, x_{d_2}, y_{d_2}, x_a, y_a)$ are computed "per subterm": $\partial\mathfrak{N}_1/\partial(\cdot)$ (18), $\partial\mathfrak{N}_2/\partial(\cdot)$ (19), $\partial\mathfrak{D}/\partial(\cdot)$ (20), then assembled into $\partial V/\partial(\cdot)$ (21) with the shorthands $\varpi_1 = \frac{(x_{C_2}-x_{C_1})(y_{C_1}-y_{C_2})}{\mu_3^2-1}$, $\varpi_2 = \frac{(x_{C_1}-x_{C_2})^2}{\mu_3^2-1}$ (after (18)), $\alpha_1 = (x_{C_1}-x_{C_2})R_{C_3}^2$, $\alpha_2 = (y_{C_1}-y_{C_2})R_{C_3}^2$, $\beta_1 = \frac{\mu_3^2(x_{d_1}-x_{d_2})}{\mu_3^2-1}$, $\beta_2 = \frac{\mu_3^2(y_{d_1}-y_{d_2})}{\mu_3^2-1}$, $\beta_3 = \frac{\mu_1^2}{\mu_1^2-1} - \frac{\mu_2^2}{\mu_2^2-1}$, $\gamma = \kappa + y_{C_3}(y_{C_1}-y_{C_2})$ (after (19)), and $\xi = 2V - \frac{1}{\gamma_1}$, $\gamma_1 = \sqrt{\frac{(x_{C_1}-x_{C_2})^2}{\mathfrak{N}_2}}$ (after (21)). Note $\tau/\mathfrak{N}_2 = \gamma_1$ and $\gamma_1/2$ multiplies every $\partial\mathfrak{N}_2$ term — consistent with the chain rule $\partial\tau/\partial z = \frac{\gamma_1}{2}\frac{\partial\mathfrak{N}_2}{\partial z}$ for $z$ not entering $x_{C_1}-x_{C_2}$. (Spot check: for $\partial\mathfrak{N}_1/\partial x_{d_1}$ the printed (18) requires the non-obvious identity $\partial[R_{C_1}^2 - x_{C_1}^2 - y_{C_1}^2]/\partial x_{d_1} = 2x_{d_1}/(\mu_1^2-1)$, which I verified; eq. (18) is correct.)
5. **HJI equation (22)**: $-\frac{\partial V}{\partial t} = \frac{\partial V}{\partial\mathbf{x}} \cdot f(\mathbf{x},\theta^*_a,\theta^*_{d_1},\theta^*_{d_2}) + g(\mathbf{x},\theta^*_a,\theta^*_{d_1},\theta^*_{d_2})$; since $-\partial V/\partial t = 0$ and $g(\cdot) = 0$, it suffices to show $\frac{\partial V}{\partial\mathbf{x}}\cdot f(\mathbf{x},\theta^*,\theta^*,\theta^*) = 0$.
6. **Speed normalization and simultaneous arrival**: set $V_{d_1} = 1/\mu_3$, $V_{d_2} = 1$, $V_a = 1/\mu_2$; the numerator of $\frac{\partial V}{\partial\mathbf{x}}\cdot f$ is (23), a sum of three "gradient·direction-to-$Q$" terms with denominators $\mu_3\sqrt{(x_q-x_{d_1})^2+(y_q-y_{d_1})^2}$, $\sqrt{(x_q-x_{d_2})^2+(y_q-y_{d_2})^2}$, $\mu_2\sqrt{(x_q-x_a)^2+(y_q-y_a)^2}$. The Apollonius property of the simultaneous-capture point (24): $\overline{QD_2} = \mu_3\,\overline{QD_1} = \mu_2\,\overline{QA}$, i.e., $\sqrt{(x_q-x_{d_2})^2+(y_q-y_{d_2})^2} = \mu_3\sqrt{(x_q-x_{d_1})^2+(y_q-y_{d_1})^2} = \mu_2\sqrt{(x_q-x_a)^2+(y_q-y_a)^2}$, so all three terms share the factor $\rho = 1/[\mathfrak{D}\sqrt{(x_q-x_{d_2})^2+(y_q-y_{d_2})^2}]$ — giving (25).
7. **Regrouping (26)**: collect terms into $(x_{C_1}-x_{C_2})^2\xi + 2(y_{C_1}-y_{C_2})^2 V$ plus $\eta$-weighted sums, with $\eta_1 = \partial\mathfrak{N}_1/\partial x_{d_1} + \partial\mathfrak{N}_1/\partial x_{d_2} + \partial\mathfrak{N}_1/\partial x_a$, $\eta_2$ the $y$-analogue, $\eta_3, \eta_4$ the $\mathfrak{N}_2$-analogues. Direct summation of (18)–(19) gives (27): $\eta_1 = 0$, $\eta_2 = \mathfrak{D}$, $\eta_3 = 0$, $\eta_4 = 0$ — the transversality identities that kill the $x_q\eta_1$, $V\eta_4$, $\gamma_1\eta_3$ blocks.
8. **Euler-type homogeneity identities (29)–(30)**: $\sum_k z_k \frac{\partial\mathfrak{N}_1}{\partial z_k} = 3y_{C_3}(x_{C_1}-x_{C_2})^2 - 3(y_{C_1}-y_{C_2})\kappa = 3\mathfrak{N}_1$ and $\sum_k z_k \frac{\partial\mathfrak{N}_2}{\partial z_k} = 4\big(R_{C_3}^2\mathfrak{D} - (\kappa + y_{C_3}(y_{C_1}-y_{C_2}))^2\big) = 4\mathfrak{N}_2$, over $k \in \{d_1, d_2, a\}$ (both coordinates). With $\xi = 2V - 1/\gamma_1$, the leading block becomes $3V\mathfrak{D} - (x_{C_1}-x_{C_2})^2/\gamma_1$ (28).
9. **Conclusion (31)**: substituting $\gamma_1\mathfrak{N}_2 = \tau$ and $(x_{C_1}-x_{C_2})^2/\gamma_1 = \tau$ (for $x_{C_1} \ne x_{C_2}$), $\frac{\partial V}{\partial\mathbf{x}}\cdot f = \rho \times \big(3V\mathfrak{D} - \tau - 3\mathfrak{N}_1 - 2\tau\big) = \rho\big(3(\mathfrak{N}_1+\tau) - \tau - 3\mathfrak{N}_1 - 2\tau\big) = 0$, because $V\mathfrak{D} = \mathfrak{N}_1 + \tau$. Hence $V$ satisfies the HJI equation along the saddle-point strategies. ∎
**Nature/meaning**: analytic, local to the regular simultaneous-capture configuration; the HJI check is a classical ($C^1$) verification *along the optimal strategies only* — $V$ is constant along optimal play, i.e., the achievable capture ordinate is fixed at Phase-II entry and the three players' equilibrium is mutual pure pursuit of the fixed point $Q$. **Comparative statics (p. 997, unnumbered)**: "the ordinate of the best capture point for cooperative capture is no more than the ordinate of the best capture point for independent capture" — cooperation strictly helps the defenders (pushes the capture point away from the safe region); demonstrated numerically in Case 1 ($y_Q < y_{Q_1}, y_{Q_2}$, Fig. 5). **Extensibility**: the construction applies to any 2-pursuer–1-evader terminal-ordinate game with simple motion; via Remark 3 it suffices to cap coalitions at size two, which is what makes the multiplayer matching layer of Section 5.5 well defined.

### 5.4 Corollary 1 (p. 997, eqs. (12)–(13)) — two-phase joint optimal strategy as constrained NLP
**Statement**: For 2D-1A CTF games with initial state $\mathbf{x}^0 = (x_a(0), x_{d_1}(0), x_{d_2}(0)) \in \mathbb{R}^6$: in Phase I defenders $D_1, D_2$ move to $x_{d_1}, x_{d_2}$ respectively; to minimize the ordinate of the capture point $Q: \mathbf{x}_q = (x_q, y_q)$, $x_q = \frac{\mathfrak{R} - 2(y_{C_2}-y_{C_1})y_q}{2(x_{C_2}-x_{C_1})}$ with $y_q$ the solution of the program
$$\mathcal{Z}:\quad \min_{x_{d_1}, x_{d_2}} \frac{\mathfrak{N}_1 + \sqrt{(x_{C_1}-x_{C_2})^2\mathfrak{N}_2}}{\mathfrak{D}} \quad \text{s.t. } x_{d_1} \in K_1,\; x_{d_2} \in K_2,\; (x_{C_1}, y_{C_1}+R_{C_1}) \notin \Gamma_2,\; (x_{C_2}, y_{C_2}+R_{C_2}) \notin \Gamma_1, \tag{13}$$
where (12) $K_1 := \{(x,y) \in \Omega \mid \sqrt{(x - x^0_{d_1})^2 + (y - y^0_{d_1})^2} \le \mu_1\,\mathrm{dist}(\mathbf{x}_f, \mathbf{x}_a(0))\}$, $K_2$ analogously with $(\mu_2, x^0_{d_2}, y^0_{d_2})$, and $\mathfrak{N}_1, \mathfrak{N}_2, \mathfrak{D}, \mathfrak{R}$ are from (10). **Winning criterion**: "when $y_q > h$, the attacker wins; when $y_q \le h$, the defender wins."
**Rationale/proof**: the attacker needs $\mathrm{dist}(\mathbf{x}_f, \mathbf{x}_a(0))$ (straight-line distance to the flag) to finish Phase I, during which $D_i$ can travel $\mu_i$ times as far; so $K_i$ is the disk of feasible re-positioning endpoints. "The best way for the defenders to reach the dominant region in Phase II is abandoning the pursuit of the attacker in Phase I" (p. 997) — the defenders pre-position instead of chasing. **Proof**: two lines — "capture-the-flag games are transformed into the minimum ordinate problems of capture points. The Corollary 1 can be obtained according to Theorem 1" (p. 997); not repeated. **Remark 4** (p. 997): vs. [34], which directly solved the HJI equation with higher computational complexity, the Apollonius transformation turns the two-phase game into a constrained nonlinear optimization, giving two-phase joint optimal strategies at lower cost. **Critical observations**: (i) $\mathcal{Z}$ is a nonconvex constrained NLP (the objective and the two exclusion constraints are implicit functions of $(x_{d_1}, x_{d_2})$ through the Apollonius geometry); no solver, initialization, or global-optimality discussion is given; (ii) the claim that pre-positioning dominates chasing is asserted, not proved (no comparison theorem between Phase-I pursuit and Phase-I repositioning policies); (iii) the exclusion constraints enforce the genuine-cooperation regime, but the case where $\mathcal{Z}$ is infeasible or where a Phase-I capture is directly possible is delegated to the heuristic of Section IV ("if the defenders can guarantee the successful capture of the allocated attacker in phase I, they will capture the attacker directly before the flag is captured", p. 1000).

### 5.5 Matching layer (Section III-B, pp. 997–999) — MBMC and Algorithm 1
**Construction**: defender nodes include coalitions: $\mathcal{U}_d = \{D_1, D_2, D_3, D_1D_2, D_2D_3, D_1D_3\}$ (example, Fig. 3, p. 998); in general $N_d$ single-defender and $N_d(N_d-1)/2$ two-defender alliances, $N_o = N_d(N_d+1)/2$. Undirected bipartite graph $G = (\mathcal{U}_a \cup \mathcal{U}_d, E)$; $e_{ij} \in E$ iff defender combination $D_j$ can successfully capture attacker $A_i$ (the edge set is read off from the qualitative 1D-1A/2D-1A results of Sections III-A, i.e., Lemma 1/2 and Theorem 1 outcomes computed from the joint initial state $\mathbf{x}^0$). **Conflict graph** $G_C = (E, \bar{E})$: a vertex per edge of $G$; $\bar{E} = \{(e_{ij}, e_{pq}) \mid e_{ij} \in E, e_{pq} \in E,\ j \ne q,\ j \cap q \ne \emptyset\}$ — two assignments conflict if the two defender coalitions share at least one defender. The maximum matching under such conflicts (MBMC) "is an NP-hard problem to solve exactly" (p. 998); a "polynomial time constant session approximation algorithm" — the **sequential matching algorithm (Algorithm 1, p. 998)** — is proposed:
- **Step 1 (Lines 1–2)**: build the single-defender subgraph $G_1 = (\mathcal{U}_{a,1} \cup \mathcal{U}_{d,1}, E_1)$, $E_1 = \{e_{ij} \in E \mid A_i \in \mathcal{U}_{a,1}, D_j \in \mathcal{U}_{d,1}\}$; compute its maximum matching $M_1$ by the **Hungarian algorithm**; extract attacker set $H_1 = \{A_i \mid e_{ij} \in M_1\}$ and defender set $B_1 = \{D_j \mid e_{ij} \in M_1\}$.
- **Step 2 (Lines 3–6)**: exclude all vertices containing players of $H_1 \cup B_1$; build the two-defender subgraph $G_2 = (\mathcal{U}_{a,2} \cup \mathcal{U}_{d,2}, E_2)$; compute its maximum matching by the Hungarian algorithm and filter out conflicting matches "according to the order of the conflict graph $G_C$" to obtain $M_2$; extract $H_2, B_2$.
- **Step 3**: output the approximation matching $M = M_1 \cup M_2$ of $G$.
**Remark 5** (p. 998–999): vs. the maximum-network-flow matching of [30], the Hungarian algorithm is "a degenerate network flow" that finds one augmenting path per node (interleaving unmatched/matched edges and inverting), greatly reducing time complexity. **Remark 6** (p. 999): vs. MADDPG [50], the analytic algorithm gives qualitative and quantitative optimal task allocation with interpretability and no training; MADDPG needs data/training with no convergence guarantee but adapts better to dynamic environments. **No formal theorem is attached to this layer**: no approximation ratio, no proof that $M$ is conflict-free-optimal, and no complexity bound is stated; the NP-hardness is invoked through the context of [38] without a reduction (proof gap, see Section 7).

### 5.6 Main qualitative result sentence (p. 997, after Theorem 1)
"In summary, the effect of the defenders' cooperative behavior on capture-the-flag games is analyzed, i.e., the ordinate of the best capture point for cooperative capture is no more than the ordinate of the best capture point for independent capture, and the optimal state-feedback strategy for each player is derived." — this is the paper's only comparative-statics statement tying the 2D-1A value to the two 1D-1A values; it is validated numerically (Fig. 5) but not proved by an inequality chain.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
**Author-stated**: (i) for multi-defense-multi-CTF task allocation "the sequential matching algorithm used in this article cannot theoretically solve the optimal solution"; it merely "avoids the waste of a large number of computational resources due to solving the NP-hard problem directly", with "satisfactory matching results" observed in simulation (p. 1001); (ii) coalitions contain no more than two defenders (p. 998), justified only by Remark 3/[47]; (iii) complete-information assumption (p. 997); flags and players are points (Remarks 1–2); (iv) MADDPG outperforms the analytic algorithm "in adapting to dynamic environments"; combining analytic and data-driven approaches is future work (Remark 6, p. 999).
**Critical observations (with page/eq.)**: (1) The HJI verification of Theorem 1 checks $-\partial V/\partial t = \nabla V \cdot f + g$ (22) only along the optimal strategies, with $g = 0$ postulated (p. 1002); there is no Isaacs-condition argument, no viscosity treatment, no boundary/terminal condition statement, and no uniqueness claim — so $V$ is a *verified candidate*, not a proven game value in the viscosity sense. (2) Regularity exclusions $\mathfrak{D} \ne 0$, $\mathfrak{N}_2 \ne 0$, $x_{C_1} \ne x_{C_2}$ (p. 1002) leave degenerate configurations (vertically aligned Apollonius centers, tangency, $\mathfrak{N}_2 < 0$) untreated; real-valuedness of $\tau = \sqrt{(x_{C_1}-x_{C_2})^2\mathfrak{N}_2}$ (10) requires $\mathfrak{N}_2 \ge 0$, never discussed. (3) **Proof gap in Lemma 2** (p. 996): the contained/inscribed/intersecting case analysis is asserted verbally without inequalities; which single defender wins and with what value is not derived. (4) The defenders' winning condition "capturing **or indefinitely delaying** $N_a - \iota + 1$ attackers" (p. 995) is never operationalized — no delay strategy or nonzero-sum alliance-level analysis of $\iota$ is given; the analysis is purely per-attacker. (5) Contribution 3 claims the algorithm is "guaranteeing the capture of the maximum number of attackers" (p. 994), contradicting the Section-IV admission that optimality is not theoretically attained (p. 1001); no approximation factor for Algorithm 1 and no formal NP-hardness reduction for MBMC are provided (the NP-hardness is attributed via [38], which treats a different pairwise-outcome problem). (6) **Numerical inconsistency**: the "21%" maximum-assignment-time reduction (p. 1001) contradicts Table III (2.1%); see Section 6. (7) Timing baselines run on different hardware/implementations (3.60 GHz/32 GB vs 2.60 GHz/8 GB, Tables II–III) — favorable in direction but not a controlled comparison. (8) Corollary 1's $\mathcal{Z}$ (13) is a nonconvex NLP whose solver and global optimality are unreported, and the key behavioral claim that pre-positioning (abandoning Phase-I pursuit) is the defenders' best two-phase policy is asserted, not proved (p. 997). (9) Dynamics (1) do not constrain players to $\Omega$; boundary handling of the compact game region is absent from both theory and simulations. (10) Theorem 1 assumes WLOG $V_{d_1} < V_{d_2}$ ($\mu_3 > 1$); the equal-speed case ($\mu_3 = 1$, $C_3$ degenerating to the perpendicular bisector) is excluded by (16). (11) Despite the $N_a$-flag formulation, every simulation uses a single flag $\mathbf{x}_f$; multi-flag instances, attacker-alliance strategies (the attackers' side is never jointly optimized), and the value of $\iota$ are unexplored. (12) Remark 3's "at most two defenders" leverage is inherited from [47]'s barrier analysis for a different game, so its transfer to the two-phase CTF setting is heuristic.

## 8. Citation Value
This paper is the bridge node between the geometric one-to-one capture-the-flag line (Garcia–Casbeer–Pachter CDC 2018 [27]; Pachter–Casbeer–Garcia CCTA 2020 [33]; Huang et al. TCST 2015 [34]) and the matching-based multiplayer reach-avoid line (Chen–Zhou–Tomlin TAC 2017 [38]; Yan et al. Automatica 2022 [30]; Yan–Shi–Zhong TRO 2020 [46]; Garcia et al. TAC 2021 [47]), and it is the first journal treatment of CTF with two whole alliances of players. Its three reusable assets for our knowledge base are: (a) the **Apollonius reduction lemmas** — Phase-I coalitions are never necessary (Lemma 1) and Phase-II coalitions reduce to single defenders under a top-of-circle exclusion condition (Lemma 2) — which give a clean, checkable criterion for when 2D-1A is genuinely cooperative; (b) the **closed-form two-phase joint value** $V(\mathbf{x}) = (\mathfrak{N}_1+\tau)/\mathfrak{D}$ with heading-to-$Q$ equilibrium strategies and an explicit HJI verification, plus the recasting of defender pre-positioning as the constrained NLP $\mathcal{Z}$ with the $y_q$-vs-$h$ winning criterion — a template for two-phase target-defense games with exit regions; and (c) the **MBMC task-assignment formulation** with conflict graph and the sequential Hungarian heuristic (Algorithm 1), useful as a computationally light assignment layer. Cite it when surveying multiplayer capture-the-flag / flag-defense games, two-phase (attack-then-retreat) differential games, matching-with-conflicts task allocation, or Apollonius-circle-based dominance reductions; e.g.: "Multiplayer capture-the-flag games with faster defenders can be decomposed into one- or two-defender-one-attacker subgames via Apollonius-circle dominance tests, with a two-phase joint HJI-verified value function and a sequential matching algorithm for defender task assignment [Wang et al., TCDS 2024]." Use with care for its unproven claims (matching optimality, pre-positioning optimality, the 21% figure) and pair it with [27], [33], [47], [30] when precision about what is proved matters.
