## 2. Research Background

Perimeter defense studies how defending agents intercept intruders before the intruders reach a protected boundary; since Isaacs' seminal work [1] the dominant formulation has been a pursuit–evasion/reach-avoid differential game whose solution requires Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman–Isaacs (HJBI) computations, tractable only in low-dimensional state spaces (p. 3350) [authors' claim]. A practically important variant places a single turret (fixed base, rotating barrel) at the center of a protected region: the turret must physically slew to the intruder's angle, and — realistically — needs a non-zero *startup (spool-up) time* ∆ during which its heading must not change, and it has a finite *range* r (pp. 3350–3351). Prior turret-defense differential games [7]–[11] study one-or-few intruders with a priori information, and prior dynamic-vehicle-routing treatments of defense assume stochastic arrivals or zero/stochastic service times (pp. 3350–3351) [authors' claim]. This paper instead asks: how many intruders can one range-limited, spool-up-limited turret guarantee to capture when an arbitrary (possibly coordinated, adversarial) number N ≥ 2 of radially-incoming intruders is released — answered offline (all releases known) and online (releases revealed over time) via *competitive analysis* (p. 3350–3352). The application need is defense of a protected asset (perimeter) against coordinated saturation attacks that defeat reactive, one-at-a-time engagement.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap

**Problem.** A planar conical environment E(θ) = {(y, α) : 0 < y ≤ 1, −θ ≤ α ≤ θ} contains a concentric, coaxial protected region R(ρ, θ) = {(z, α) : 0 < z ≤ ρ < 1, −θ ≤ α ≤ θ} whose boundary ∂R(θ) = {(ρ, α) : −θ ≤ α ≤ θ} is the perimeter (p. 3351). Intruders are released on the circumference y = 1 at arbitrary instants and move radially inward at fixed speed v; the jth intruder released at t_j has z_j(t) = 1 − v(t − t_j) for t ∈ [t_j, t_j + (1−ρ)/v] (p. 3351). A single turret at the origin has heading γ_t ∈ [−θ, θ], simple kinematics γ̇_t = u with u ∈ [−ω, +ω], range r ∈ [ρ, 1], and startup time ∆ > 0 (p. 3351). Two problems on instance P = (θ, ρ, v, ω, r, N, ∆) (p. 3352): **P1** (offline) — given N intruders already present with known locations and initial heading γ_0, find the control policy maximizing the number captured; **P2** (online) — design online algorithms with finite competitive ratios and establish fundamental guarantees on their existence (pp. 3352–3353).

**Gap** (provenance marked):

## 4. Core Contributions

1. **Modeling**: First perimeter-defense formulation with a fixed-base turret characterized by (heading angle, bounded slew rate ω, range r ∈ [ρ, 1], startup time ∆ > 0), including a lock-on/spool-up capture semantics and a collocated-intruder rule n∆ (Sec. II-A, pp. 3351–3352); offline (P1) and online (P2) problem statements with a competitive-ratio definition adapted to partial capture counts (Sec. II-B, pp. 3352–3353).
2. **Complexity/equivalence (theory)**: Theorem 3.1 — P1 is equivalent to a Travelling Repairperson Problem with Time Windows (TRP-TW) on a line segment (p. 3352); Proposition 3.2 — a 2-approximation algorithm exists for P1 for all parameter regimes, by adapting [28] (p. 3352).
3. **Polynomial-time optimal offline control (theory + algorithm)**: for the regime r = ρ, ∆ > 0, a time-invariant *reachability graph* (digraph eq. (1), p. 3353) is acyclic (Lemma 3.4), and computing its longest path yields the capture-maximizing control algorithm (Theorem 3.5, p. 3353).
4. **Fundamental limit (theory)**: Theorem 4.1 — in a stated parameter regime and speed interval, no online algorithm can capture all intruders and the problem's competitive ratio is c(P) = N−1, i.e., "at best (N−1)-competitive" algorithms (p. 3353); Remark 4.2 — a family of relaxed bounds c(P) = N−j, 1 < j < N (p. 3353).
5. **Online algorithms (algorithm + theory)**: Sweeping Turret (SiT), open loop and memoryless, provably 1-competitive (Theorem 4.3, p. 3353); Dynamically Project and Capture (DPaC), memoryless but closed-loop, provably 2-competitive (Lemma 4.4, Corollaries 4.5–4.6, Theorem 4.7, pp. 3354–3355), each with explicit parameter regimes.
6. **Numerical regime synthesis**: (ρ, v) parameter-space plot (Fig. 3, pp. 3354–3355) overlaying the two algorithms' valid regimes against the Theorem 4.1 / Remark 4.2 fundamental-limit curves.

## 5. Key Mathematical Results

### 5.0 Dynamics, information structure, payoff, termination (pp. 3351–3353)

- **Turret dynamics**: γ̇_t = u, u a measurable signal in [−ω, +ω] (simple kinematics, bounded slew in either direction) (p. 3351). Heading γ_t ∈ [−θ, θ]. Range r ∈ [ρ, 1] is "the radial distance until which the turret can neutralize an intruder" (p. 3351).
- **Startup/spool-up semantics**: to capture intruder j the turret must lock on (γ_t = θ_j and decides to capture) and then hold γ_t fixed for ∆ time units; "if the turret locks on to the jth intruder at time t, then the jth intruder is captured and removed from E(θ) at time t + ∆" (pp. 3351–3352). Consequently there is "no benefit for the turret to spool up ... if z_j(t) > r + ∆v"; the turret may wait at θ_j until z_j(t) = r + ∆v so that capture occurs exactly as the intruder crosses radius r (p. 3351; cf. Fig. 1, p. 3351). Alternative convention stated to be equivalent with trivial modification: startup time coincides with capturing, so capture begins when the intruder is at radius r and completes at r − ∆v (p. 3351).
- **Collocated intruders**: if 1 < n ≤ N intruders are collocated with z_1(t) = · · · = z_n(t) ≤ r + ∆v and θ_n = γ_t, "the turret requires n∆ time to capture all intruders" (p. 3352). [my observation] This sequential-service rule is the load-bearing idealization: it makes a simultaneous burst cost n∆ of pure service and is what produces the (N−2)∆-type terms in Theorems 4.1/4.3.
- **Intruder model**: release on y = 1 at arbitrary instants; radial motion at fixed speed v; z_j(t) = 1 − v(t − t_j), ∀t ∈ [t_j, t_j + (1−ρ)/v] (p. 3351). N > 1 (N = 1 "trivial") (p. 3351).
- **Information structure**: information set I_t = locations and release times of every intruder released up to t; feedback policy u(t, γ_t, I_t) : [0, +∞) × [−θ, θ] × Φ_N → [−ω, +ω], Φ_N the set of all possible locations/release times of N intruders until t (p. 3351). Online algorithm A assigns angular velocity of magnitude ω as a function of the revealed prefix I_t ⊂ I; the optimal offline algorithm O is non-causal (p. 3352). Input sequence I = {t, n(t), {(1, α_1), …, (1, α_{n(t)})}}_{t=0}^T with α_l ∈ [−θ, θ] — every release is at radius 1 (p. 3352).
- **Payoff and competitive ratio** (Definition 1, p. 3352): with m_A(I, P) (resp. m_O(I, P)) the number captured by A (resp. O), C_A(I, P) = m_O(I,P)/m_A(I,P) ≥ 1; c_A(P) = sup_I C_A(I, P); c(P) = inf_A c_A(P); A is c-competitive for P if c_A(P) ≤ c. Restriction: inputs I are restricted to those with m_O(I, P) ≥ 1; if m_A(I, P) = 0 on some such I, A is not c-competitive for any finite c (p. 3352). Footnote 1: "Determining an optimal competitive ratio is equivalent to determining the value of the game" — competitive analysis is a two-person zero-sum game between the online player and an adversary who knows A [26] (p. 3352).
- **Termination/winning**: an intruder "wins" by reaching ∂R(θ) uncaptured (this happens at t_j + (1−ρ)/v, p. 3351); the turret's score is the number of captures; there is no terminal cost or discount. [my observation] Intruders are non-adaptive after release (fixed radial paths) — the strategic content lives entirely in the adversary's choice of the input sequence, so this is an online-optimization game rather than an HJI-type differential game.
- **P1** (Problem Statement I, p. 3352): given N intruders in E(θ) and initial heading γ_0, determine u*(t, γ_0, I_0) maximizing the number captured. **P2** (Problem Statement II, p. 3353): design online algorithms with finite competitive ratios and establish fundamental guarantees on their existence.

### 5.1 Theorem 3.1 (p. 3352) — equivalence to TRP-TW

- **Statement**: "Problem P1 is equivalent to solving the TRP-TW on the line segment L(θ)".
- **TRP-TW definition as printed** (p. 3352): on a segment [−L, L], a repairperson serves N locations; location i requires service time ∆ and has a time window T_i = [s_i, t_i] (s_i first / t_i last instant at which i is available); waiting at the location before s_i is allowed; service must finish before t_i; unit reward per completed service; maximize total reward.
- **Assumptions**: none beyond the model; the equivalence is structural.
- **Proof approach** (printed sketch, 2 steps): (i) map every intruder location in E(θ) onto L(θ) (the conical environment with a turret maps onto a linear environment L(θ) of length 2θ with a point-mass vehicle of linear speed ω — the angular coordinate is the position); (ii) map "time taken by every intruder to reach the perimeter" to a per-intruder time window in L(θ). No equations are given.
- [my observation — reconstruction, not printed]: the natural window for intruder i released at t_i^rel is s_i = t_i^rel + (1 − r − ∆v)/v (first instant lock-on can start so that capture at radius r is still possible) and t_i = t_i^rel + (1−ρ)/v (breach time), with service time ∆; angular position θ_i maps to coordinate θ_i ∈ [−θ, θ]; slew at rate ω maps to travel at speed ω. The paper itself does not print s_i, t_i.
- **Physical meaning**: maximizing captures offline is exactly maximum-reward scheduling with release/deadline windows; the turret's slew angle budget plays the role of the repairperson's travel.
- **Extensibility**: any environment where the defender state is 1-D (angle) and intruder deadlines are known reduces to this class; the equivalence makes NP-hardness machinery and approximation algorithms from TRP-TW transferable.
- **proof gap**: the two-line sketch contains no explicit window construction and no proof that every TRP-TW tour back-translates to a feasible turret control (and vice versa); the authors state "The detailed proofs are contained in [27]" (p. 3352), i.e., arXiv:2302.02186.

### 5.2 Proposition 3.2 (p. 3352) — 2-approximation for P1

- **Statement**: "There exists a 2-approximation algorithm for problem P1."
- **Proof approach** (printed): an offline TRP-TW variant is studied in [28] (Nagamochi & Ohnishi, Theoretical Computer Science, 2008) "with a difference that the repair person can keep on servicing the ith demand after its time window, if the repair person reaches the demand before t_i. Thus, the 2-approximation algorithm in [28] can be applied to this setup with slight modifications."
- [my observation] The printed direction of the difference reads oddly (the standard [28] model allows service to continue past t_i if started in the window; that is exactly the "late completion allowed" semantics beneficial to the defender), so the "slight modifications" presumably define the payoff convention for services finishing after t_i (an intruder serviced "after its window" has already breached). This is the all-parameter-regime fallback algorithm; it is approximation-grade, not optimal.
- **Physical meaning**: even in hard regimes (r > ρ, arbitrary ∆), one can guarantee at least half of the offline optimum's capture count.
- **proof gap**: no modification is spelled out; deferred to [27]/[28].

If r = ρ and ∆ = 0, P1 is equivalent to [20] (Smith, Bopardikar, Bullo, dynamic boundary guarding with translating targets, CDC 2009) (p. 3353). ### 5.4 Reachability and eq. (1) (p. 3353) — the section's analytic core

Special regime: **r = ρ and ∆ > 0** (p. 3353).

- **Reachability of intruder i from heading γ_t** (unnumbered display, p. 3353):
  |γ_t − θ_i|/ω ≤ (z_i(t) − ρ − ∆v)/v  ⇔  z_i(t) ≥ ρ + ∆v + (v/ω)|γ_t − θ_i|.
  Meaning [my observation]: the slew time to align plus the spool-up allowance ∆ must fit into the intruder's remaining time (z_i(t) − ρ)/v above the perimeter; equivalently the intruder must still be at least ∆v + (v/ω)|γ_t − θ_i| beyond the perimeter radius when the turret commits.
- **Inter-intruder reachability, eq. (1)** (p. 3353): intruder j is reachable from intruder i (turret initially oriented toward i, capturing i first), for z_i(t) ≥ ρ + ∆v:
  ∆ + |θ_j − θ_i|/ω + max{(z_i(t) − ρ − ∆v)/v, 0} ≤ (z_j(t) − ρ − ∆v)/v
  ⇔ z_j(t) − z_i(t) ≥ v∆ + (v/ω)|θ_j − θ_i|.  (eq. (1))
  The left side is: finish servicing i (≥ ∆) + slew from θ_i to θ_j + (if i has not yet reached ρ + ∆v) wait until i is at ρ + ∆v; the right side is j's remaining slack.
- **Reachability graph**: vertices = the N initial intruder positions; directed edge i → j iff j is reachable from i per eq. (1); "Since v∆ + (v/ω)|θ_i − θ_j| does not depend on time, the reachability graph does not change with time" (p. 3353). [my observation] Time-invariance is the crucial structural fact: all intruders move at the same speed v, so radial gaps z_j − z_i are constant, and eq. (1) reduces to a static pairwise condition on (θ_i, z_i(0), θ_j, z_j(0)).
- **Lemma 3.4** (p. 3353): "If r = ρ, then both intruder i and j cannot be reachable from each other." Printed proof: "follows directly from the definition of reachability and is omitted for brevity. The complete proof is contained in [27]." [my observation — one-line reconstruction: if both i→j and j→i held, eq. (1) would give z_j − z_i ≥ v∆ + (v/ω)|θ_j − θ_i| and z_i − z_j ≥ v∆ + (v/ω)|θ_j − θ_i|; adding yields 0 ≥ 2v∆ + (2v/ω)|θ_j − θ_i| > 0 since ∆ > 0 — a contradiction. Note the strictness fails if ∆ = 0 and θ_i = θ_j, consistent with the ∆ > 0 regime.] The graph is therefore an acyclic digraph (DAG).
- **proof gap**: none material for Lemma 3.4 given the reconstruction above; but note Lemma 3.4 is stated only for r = ρ (mutual reachability is possible in general when capture can occur at different radii, r > ρ).

### 5.5 Theorem 3.5 (p. 3353) — polynomial-time optimal offline control for r = ρ

- **Statement**: "If r = ρ, then a control algorithm that maximizes the number of intruders intercepted is obtained by computing the longest path on the reachability graph."
- **Proof approach** (printed sketch, 3 steps): (i) establish the reachability graph is *directed*; (ii) establish it is *acyclic and time independent* (Lemma 3.4 + eq. (1)); (iii) "the optimal number of intruders intercepted is obtained by computing the longest path on this reachability graph." A chain i_1 → i_2 → · · · → i_k in the graph is exactly a feasible capture sequence (each successor still capturable after the previous service plus slew), and any feasible capture order must be such a chain, so the longest path (in vertex count) equals the maximum number of captures; the control is then synthesized by slewing along the path and holding ∆ at each vertex.
- **Locality/globality**: globally optimal *within the regime r = ρ, ∆ > 0*; analytic (combinatorial), not numerical.
- **Complexity**: the paper claims only "polynomial time" (Sec. III title, p. 3352; Sec. V, p. 3355). [my observation] Longest path on a DAG is solvable in O(|V| + |E|) after topological sort; with the natural dense edge set |E| ≤ N(N−1), this is O(N²) — but this bound is not printed in the paper.
- **Physical meaning**: when capture must happen exactly at the perimeter radius (r = ρ) and each kill costs a fixed ∆, "who can be killed after whom" is a static partial order; best defense = longest chain in that order. The authors immediately caution: "Although Theorem 3.5 provides an optimal algorithm, the effective parameter regime is limited" (p. 3353) — motivating the online section.
- **proof gap**: the timing-feasibility details of the back-translation (waiting at θ_i until z_i = r + ∆v; interactions with γ_0; whether the path must be appended to the initial slew from γ_0) are not shown in this paper; deferred to [27].

### 5.6 Theorem 4.1 and Remark 4.2 (p. 3353) — fundamental limit for the online problem

- **Statement (Theorem 4.1)**: "For any problem instance P such that (N−2)(1−ρ) − 2(r−ρ) < 2θ(r−ρ)/(∆ω) holds, no algorithm can capture all intruders and c(P) = N−1 for all choice of intruder speed in v ∈ ( ω(1−ρ)/(2∆ω+2θ), (r−ρ)/((N−2)∆) ]." [All fractions verified against the rendered page image.]
- **Assumptions**: the printed inequality on the instance parameters; v in the half-open interval; the standing restriction m_O(I, P) ≥ 1 on admissible inputs (p. 3352).
- **Proof approach** (printed sketch, 3 steps): (i) "construct an input sequence for which any online algorithm A captures at best a single intruder while an optimal offline algorithm O captures the maximum number of intruders in the same input sequence"; (ii) apply Definition 1: c_A(I, P) = m_O/m_A ≥ N−1; (iii) conclude c(P) = N−1. The construction itself (referred to as "Case 1" with a "burst" in Remark 4.2) is **not included in this paper** — full proofs are in [27].
- **Decisive intermediate conditions** (printed, interpretable): the upper endpoint of the v-interval is exactly (r−ρ)/((N−2)∆), i.e., (N−2)∆ ≤ (r−ρ)/v — Remark 4.2 confirms this term is O's requirement to capture all burst intruders: "for every 1 < j < N, the relaxed requirement (N−j−1)∆ ≤ (r−ρ)/v yields c(P) = N−j" (p. 3353). The lower endpoint ω(1−ρ)/(2∆ω+2θ) is not derived in-print; [my observation] it rearranges to 2θ/ω + 2∆ ≤ (1−ρ)/v, i.e., a full end-to-end slew (2θ/ω) plus two service times must fit into the intruders' total travel time (1−ρ)/v — the feasibility condition for the adversary's two-phase (initial intruder + burst) construction.
- **Remark 4.2 (p. 3353)**: "A family of upper bounds on v in Theorem 4.1 can be obtained by relaxing the requirement that O must capture all burst intruders in Case 1. Specifically, for every 1 < j < N, the relaxed requirement (N−j−1)∆ ≤ (r−ρ)/v yields c(P) = N−j."
- **Local/global**: global (worst case over all inputs and all online algorithms); analytic.
- **Physical meaning**: at moderate speeds, coordinated saturation (a simultaneous burst) forces any reactive turret to kill at most 1 of N intruders while the clairvoyant defender kills N−1 — a factor-(N−1) worst-case gap intrinsic to the information structure, not to algorithm design. [my observation] Dimensional check: (N−2)(1−ρ) and 2(r−ρ) are lengths; 2θ(r−ρ)/(∆ω) is (rad·length)/(s·rad/s) = length — consistent. With Fig. 3's parameters (N = 40, θ = π/4, ∆ = 0.01, r = 1) the hypothesis reads 38(1−ρ) < 157.1(1−ρ), true for all ρ < 1 — so the whole plotted region satisfies it [my observation].
- [my observation — wording caveat]: the theorem asserts the *equality* c(P) = N−1, but the sketch only exhibits a lower bound (an adversary making every A capture ≤ 1 while O captures N−1). An equality claim requires also that some online algorithm achieves N−1 on every admissible input; nothing in this 6-page version establishes that direction. The Conclusion itself hedges: "A necessary condition on the existence of at best N−1-competitive algorithms was also established" (p. 3355), and Fig. 3's legend labels the curve "Theorem 4.1 lower bound" (p. 3354). Treat c(P) ≥ N−1 as what is proven here; the matching upper bound, if any, lives in [27].
- **proof gap**: "Case 1" and the burst geometry are undefined in this version; the role of the hypothesis inequality is not derivable from the sketch; both deferred to [27].

### 5.7 Algorithm SiT and Theorem 4.3 (p. 3353) — 1-competitive sweeping turret

- **Algorithm (Sweeping Turret, SiT)** — open loop, memoryless, for θ < π (p. 3353): at t = 0 start at γ_0 = −θ and slew toward θ continuously; if at time t there exist 1 ≤ n ≤ N collocated intruders with z_n(t) ≤ min{v∆ + r, 1}, z_1(t) = · · · = z_n(t), θ_n = γ_t, the turret stops, locks on, and captures all n (finishing at t + n∆), then resumes the sweep; upon reaching θ it reverses toward −θ; the next epoch starts upon reaching −θ. If θ = π it never reverses — it rotates in a circular motion forever. [my observation] The min{v∆ + r, 1} cap handles intruders still outside the environment bound (a spool-up begun at release is legal if r + ∆v ≥ 1); "epoch" is defined at the top of Sec. IV-B: the interval from the instant the turret leaves its starting location until it is about to leave it again (p. 3353).
- **Statement (Theorem 4.3)**: "For any problem instance P, c_SiT = 1 if v ≤ min{(1−r)/∆, ω(r−ρ)/(aθ+(N−1)∆ω)} or (1−r)/∆ < v ≤ ω(1−ρ)/(aθ+N∆ω), where a = 4 when θ < π and a = 2, otherwise." [verified against the page image; c_SiT denotes SiT's competitive ratio.]
- **Proof approach** (printed, 2 steps): (i) construct a worst-case input for SiT; (ii) "the result is established by using the fact that the time taken by the turret to capture intruders from that input must be at most the time taken by the intruders to reach the perimeter." Details are in [27].
- **Decisive conditions** [my observation — algebraic rearrangements of the printed bounds]: the first branch's second term is equivalent to aθ/ω + (N−1)∆ ≤ (r−ρ)/v (total sweep time plus N−1 services fits inside the time an intruder spends between the capture radius r and the perimeter ρ); the second branch is equivalent to aθ/ω + N∆ ≤ (1−ρ)/v (sweep plus N full services fits inside the total release-to-perimeter travel time). The v ≤ (1−r)/∆ term says: any intruder takes at least ∆ to traverse the annulus [r, 1], so a lock-on at release always completes. [my observation] With r = 1 the first branch degenerates ((1−r)/∆ = 0), which is why only the second branch is visible in Fig. 3.
- **Meaning**: 1-competitive = SiT captures *every* intruder the clairvoyant optimum could capture, on every input — optimal in the strongest sense (c = 1 is the floor of Definition 1). [authors' claim] SiT has "the best competitive ratio, i.e., c_SiT = 1" (p. 3353).
- **Cost of optimality** [authors' claim]: SiT "does not consider the intruders with radial coordinate beyond r + ∆v to plan its motion. This yields a conservative parameter regime" (p. 3353) — the stated motivation for DPaC.
- **proof gap**: the worst-case input and the case split (why a = 4 for θ < π vs a = 2 for θ = π) are not justified in-print; deferred to [27].

### 5.8 Algorithm DPaC: Lemma 4.4, Corollaries 4.5–4.6, Theorem 4.7 (pp. 3354–3355) — 2-competitive

- **Algorithm (Dynamically Project and Capture)** — memoryless, closed loop (p. 3354, Algorithm 1 p. 3354). At the start k_s of every epoch k the turret is at angle 0 and defines four sets (p. 3354):
  - R_k (resp. L_k): intruders with radial coordinate ≤ r + ∆v at time k_s and angle in [0, θ] (resp. [−θ, 0)).
  - R'_k (resp. L'_k): intruders with radial coordinate strictly more than r + ∆v and at most min{1, r + (θ/ω + (|R_k| + 1)∆)v} (resp. min{1, r + (θ/ω + (|L_k| + 1)∆)v}), same angular halves. [my observation] The depth θ/ω + (|R_k|+1)∆ times v is exactly how much deeper the turret can still "project" a capture during the epoch: θ/ω to reach the far angle, (|R_k|+1)∆ to service the R_k side first; the sets require r + ∆v < 1, otherwise R'_k = L'_k = ∅ (p. 3354).
  - Decision rule (Algorithm 1, lines 1–10): if |R_k| + |R'_k| ≥ |L_k| + |L'_k|, turn toward θ capturing R_k on the way out, then turn back toward 0 capturing R'_k; else mirror toward −θ with L_k, L'_k. Epoch k+1 begins at angle 0. "We assume that all of the intruders that the turret decided not to capture in epoch k are lost by time (k+1)_s" (p. 3354).
- **Lemma 4.4** (p. 3354): "Given a problem instance P, let v ≤ min{(1−r)/∆, ω(1−ρ)/(3θ+⌈0.5N⌉∆ω), ω(r−ρ)/(2θ+(⌈0.5N⌉−1)∆ω)} or (1−r)/∆ < v ≤ ω(1−ρ)/(3θ+⌈0.5N⌉∆ω). Further, let the number of intruders is equal on each side in every epoch. Then, any intruder that is not considered for comparison in epoch k is considered for comparison in epoch k+1 and is capturable in epoch k+1." [verified against the page image.] [my observation — rearrangements]: the two nontrivial terms read 3θ/ω + ⌈0.5N⌉∆ ≤ (1−ρ)/v and 2θ/ω + (⌈0.5N⌉−1)∆ ≤ (r−ρ)/v: with ⌈0.5N⌉ intruders per side, out-and-back slew (2θ/ω) plus the per-side service budget must fit inside the range-annulus dwell time; 3θ/ω adds one more half-sweep for the deeper R'_k/L'_k leg and counts against the full travel time. "An important consequence of Lemma 4.4 is that any intruder that is contained in L_k or R_k can be captured in epoch k" (p. 3354).
- **Corollary 4.5** (p. 3354): "Suppose that the turret decides to turn towards θ (resp. −θ) at time instant k_s. Then, the turret captures all intruders in the set R'_k (resp. L'_k) by the end of epoch k in the parameter regimes of Lemma 4.4." [my observation] This is the "projection" guarantee: intruders beyond r + ∆v at k_s, but within the projected depth, drift into range during the epoch and are intercepted on the return leg.
- **Corollary 4.6** (p. 3354): "In the parameter regime specified in Lemma 4.4, Algorithm DPaC captures at least ⌈0.5N⌉ intruders in input sequences even with unequal number of intruders arriving on either side." Proof technique: relax the equal-sides assumption; whichever side is chosen, its side total is at least half of N.
- **Theorem 4.7** (p. 3355): "For any P, c_DPaC = 2 in the parameter regimes specified in the statement of Lemma 4.4." Printed proof (1 step): "The proof follows from the fact that turret turns to the side with higher number of intruders and captures all of the intruders it considers for comparison and thus, has been omitted for brevity." [my observation] The argument structure: per epoch, DPaC captures ≥ ⌈0.5N⌉ of the intruders present (Lemma 4.4 + Cor. 4.5 + Cor. 4.6) while O captures ≤ N, and summing/charging over epochs yields the factor 2; the printed sketch does not make the charging explicit.
- **Local/global**: global worst-case guarantee (factor 2 vs. clairvoyant optimum), analytic; the equal-per-side analysis is first established then relaxed (p. 3354).
- **proof gaps**: Lemma 4.4's proof is omitted entirely ("Please refer to [27] for the complete proofs", p. 3354); the 2-competitive charging argument is not shown; Corollary 4.6's extension from ⌈0.5N⌉-per-side to arbitrary splits is asserted without an argument in-print.

### 5.9 Summary of the analytical frontier

[my observation, synthesizing pp. 3353–3355]: the paper's regime map is: (i) offline, r = ρ, ∆ > 0 — exact polynomial-time optimum (Thm. 3.5); (ii) offline, all regimes — 2-approximation (Prop. 3.2); (iii) online, v below the SiT curve — 1-competitive (Thm. 4.3); (iv) online, v below the DPaC curve — 2-competitive (Thm. 4.7); (v) online, v above the Thm. 4.1 curve — no algorithm better than N−1-competitive (lower bound). The open region between the DPaC curve (Thm. 4.7) and the Thm. 4.1 curve is explicitly named as the gap to close (p. 3355).

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues

**Author-stated**:
- All proofs in this version are outlines: "we only provide an outline of our proofs for brevity. The detailed proofs are contained in [27]" (p. 3352; repeated pp. 3354) — [27] is the arXiv preprint "Perimeter Defense using a Turret with Finite Range and Service Times", arXiv:2302.02186 (p. 3355).
- The optimal offline algorithm's "effective parameter regime is limited" (r = ρ, ∆ > 0) (p. 3353).
- The main open problem: "Apart from closing the gap between the curve defined by Theorem 4.7 and Theorem 4.1, key future directions include multi-vehicle scenarios with energy constraints. Analyzing the problem with a weaker model of the adversary or the turret with a look-ahead are also potential extensions" (p. 3355).
- DPaC's headline analysis first assumes equal numbers of intruders per side per epoch, relaxed afterwards (p. 3354).

**Critical observations** [mine, with citations]:
- Theorem 4.1 asserts c(P) = N−1 but the sketch proves only the lower bound c(P) ≥ N−1 (adversary construction); the Fig. 3 legend ("Theorem 4.1 lower bound") and the Conclusion's "necessary condition ... on at best N−1-competitive algorithms" (pp. 3354–3355) both frame it as a lower bound. The equality should be quoted with care.
- Remark 4.2 references "Case 1" and a "burst" that are never defined in this 6-page version (p. 3353) — the adversarial construction is inaccessible without [27].
- Intruders are non-strategic after release (fixed radial motion, p. 3351); the game-theoretic content is confined to the adversary's offline choice of release schedule (footnote 1, p. 3352). Readers from the HJI/barrier reach-avoid community should note this is an online-optimization, not a differential-game, solution concept.
- The collocation rule (n∆ for n simultaneous collocated intruders, p. 3352) and instantaneous kill after spool-up (p. 3351) are idealizations; realistic single-target engagement would add per-kill uncertainty absent here.
- SiT's sweep ignores everything beyond r + ∆v by design (p. 3353), and DPaC only partially compensates via R'_k/L'_k sets whose depths assume the turret services an entire side before turning back (p. 3354); both are heuristic on inputs outside their certified regimes.
- No complexity constants are given even for the polynomial-time results (Sec. III); only "polynomial time" is claimed (pp. 3352, 3355). [my observation: O(N²) is immediate for the dense reachability DAG but is not printed.]
- The 2-approximation of Prop. 3.2 inherits the modeling difference noted in its proof ([28] allows servicing past the window end); what "slight modifications" entail is unstated (p. 3352).
- No stochastic or empirical evaluation exists; the numerical section is a plot of closed-form bounds (pp. 3354–3355).

## 8. Citation Value

**Role in a paper's narrative**: use as (a) *related work* for turret-defense and perimeter-defense with actuation constraints (startup/spool-up time, finite range, slew-rate limit) — it is the fixed-base-defender counterpart of the mobile-defender competitive line [24], [25] and the P008 journal extension; (b) *gap support* when arguing that differential-game/HJI approaches do not scale to many online-released intruders and that worst-case (competitive) analysis is the right lens for coordinated saturation attacks; (c) *method comparison* — its SiT/DPaC algorithms and Theorem 4.1 lower bound are the natural baselines or motivating benchmarks for any learning-based or look-ahead turret policy; (d) *background* for the TRP-TW/dynamic-vehicle-routing connection: offline perimeter defense is scheduling with time windows, not an HJI problem. Note for positioning: the intruder model is non-adversarial in path, adversarial in timing — cite it as the "worst-case release schedule" extreme of the spectrum whose other end is stochastic arrivals [19]–[21].
