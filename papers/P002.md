## 2. Research Background
Most pursuit-evasion game (PEG) research is formulated in 2D (classic differential games since Ho–Bryson–Baron 1965 [2]), which the author argues is inadequate for realistic scenarios involving complex 3D environments. Geometric dominance tools in 2D include the Apollonius circle (AC) used for capture-region definition and reward shaping in fuzzy-learning trackers [7], multi-pursuer multi-evader games [8], sacrifice strategies in two-evader reach-avoid games [9], Voronoi-partition relay pursuit [10], and risk-based feedback for reach-avoid games [11]. Existing 3D treatments either use Pontryagin's minimum principle with quaternion attitudes [12], apply FACL to UAV pursuit in continuous 3D space [13], or use the "occupied angle" concept for a faster evader [14]. The paper positions itself at the intersection: extend the 2D AC geometry to 3D and combine it with fuzzy actor-critic learning and APF-based reward shaping so that a single pursuer captures a slower evader among obstacles in 3D.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
**Problem**: a single-pursuer single-evader PEG in unbounded 3D workspace with spherical obstacles, both agents with simple point-mass kinematics (steering angles bounded), capture at distance $d_s$. The pursuer must learn a capture strategy and the evader an evasion strategy purely from observed relative geometry (distances and angle differences), with obstacle avoidance built into the reward.

**Gap as claimed by the authors** (p. 2): (i) AC-based works [7–10] are all 2D; (ii) the 3D works [12], [13] "employed the 3D Euclidean distance to design PEG strategies" without a dominance geometry; (iii) the occupied angle of [14] "has limited application scenarios"; therefore "the generalized AC proposed in this paper has good advantages in solving 3D PEG."

**Verified from cited text**: [7] (Awheda & Schwartz, JIRS 2016) indeed uses the 2D AC in a decentralized fuzzy learning PEG; [8] (Garcia et al., IEEE TAC 2020) is AC-based MPME differential games; [9] (Yan, Shi & Zhong, IJSS 2021) uses dominant regions with a sacrifice strategy for two evaders; [10] (Pan & Yuan, IEEE TSMC 2022) is Voronoi-based relay pursuit; [11] (Selvakumar & Bakolas, IEEE TCYB 2019) is state-space-partitioned risk for reach-avoid; [12] (Kartal et al., IJRNC 2021) solves the 3D intercept via PMP + on-policy RL; [13] (Al-Mahbashi, Schwartz & Lambadaris, IEEE SMC 2020) is machine-learning multi-drone 3D pursuit; [14] (Fang, Cheng & Xie, IEEE CCC 2020) is 3D multi-player PE with a faster evader. The claim that no explicit 3D Apollonius-sphere analytical form appears in these specific references is consistent with their titles/abstracts as cited. However, the 3D Apollonius sphere is the mathematically natural lift of the 2D circle (the locus $|EX| = a|PX|$ is a quadric of revolution), and the resulting tangent-cone analysis is classical proportional-navigation geometry; the novelty is thus incremental — an explicit analytical write-up plus integration into a learning pipeline, not a new game-theoretic solution concept. No formal optimality/guarantee comparison against [12]–[14] is attempted.

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Geometry (theory, elementary)**: extension of the 2D Apollonius circle to a "generalized AC" in 3D space — an Apollonius sphere with explicit center and radius (eq. (3), p. 5) — and classification of dominant regions by speed ratio $a = v_E/v_P$ ($a<1$: ball around evader is evader-dominant; $a=1$: perpendicular bisector plane; $a>1$: pursuer side), illustrated in Fig. 2 (p. 4).
2. **Strategy geometry (theory, heuristic)**: sine-rule analysis on the pursuer–evader–AC-point triangle giving each agent an "optimal motion space": a pursuit cone of half-angle $\alpha_2^* = \sin^{-1}(v_E/v_P)$ about the line of sight (eqs. (4)–(6), p. 5, Fig. 3).
3. **Algorithm (modeling)**: a Fuzzy Actor-Critic Learning (FACL) scheme — first-order Takagi-Sugeno (TS) fuzzy actor + TS fuzzy critic — with the full update equations (7)–(14) and a 4-input state per agent (eqs. (15)–(16)), applied to both players simultaneously.
4. **Reward design (modeling)**: an APF-based reward (eqs. (17)–(20)) combining exponential obstacle repulsion, exponential evader attraction, and a capture-success term, giving the learning agents obstacle-avoidance functionality.
5. **Experiments**: four 3D scenarios with obstacles; capture in all cases without collision; 20-run averaged comparison against the algorithm of [13] on path length and capture time (Figs. 4–5, pp. 8–9).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results (MOST IMPORTANT — exhaustive)
The paper contains **no formal theorem/lemma/proposition/corollary environments**; all results are stated inline with numbered equations. The four analytic pillars are recorded below with full detail.

### 5.1 Agent kinematics and game definition (p. 2, eq. (1))
- **Dynamics (discrete-time, eq. (1), p. 2)**:
$$x_{t+1} = x_t + v\sin\theta\cos\alpha,\quad y_{t+1} = y_t + v\sin\theta\sin\alpha,\quad z_{t+1} = z_t + v\cos\theta,$$
  where $(x_t,y_t,z_t)$ is position, $v$ constant speed, $\alpha$ the angle between the $x$-$y$ projection of $\vec v$ and the $x$-axis, $\theta$ described as "the angle between speed $v$ and the $z$-axis" (p. 2). Control/steering $U = [\alpha,\theta]^\top$ with $U \in [-\pi/4, \pi/4]^2$.
- **Information structure**: agents know each other's location but not the opponent's strategy (p. 2). No velocity observation is used in the control law (only distances and angle differences, eqs. (15)–(16)).
- **Termination/winning (p. 2)**: PEG ends at $t_f$ when $d_{PE}(t_f) \le d_s$ (capture distance $d_s$), or when time exceeds a designated threshold (evader wins by surviving).
- **Proof gap (kinematics convention)**: with $\theta \in [-\pi/4,\pi/4]$ and $v_z = v\cos\theta > 0$ as printed, vertical velocity is strictly positive — agents can never descend or hold altitude, and motion is confined within $\pm 45°$ of the $+z$ axis. If $\theta$ were the elevation from the $x$-$y$ plane, eq. (1) should read $v\cos\theta\cos\alpha$, $v\cos\theta\sin\alpha$, $v\sin\theta$. The simulations corroborate the as-printed behavior: all captures occur at $z = 15$–$19$ m in a 20 m-high arena (Fig. 4 annotations, p. 9), i.e., both agents climb monotonically to near the ceiling — a modeling artifact, not a deliberate tactic. Also, Fig. 1 (p. 3) misprints the third coordinate of $P_t$, $E_t$, $P_{t+1}$, $E_{t+1}$ as $x^P_t, x^E_t, \dots$ instead of $z^P_t, z^E_t$.

### 5.2 Generalized AC in 3D space (pp. 3–5, eqs. (2)–(3)) — main geometric result
- **Statement**: for pursuer $P=(x^P_t,y^P_t,z^P_t)$, evader $E=(x^E_t,y^E_t,z^E_t)$, speed ratio $a = v_E/v_P < 1$, the generalized AC is the locus of points $A$ (etc.) on the sphere satisfying (eq. (2), p. 3)
$$a = \frac{v_E}{v_P} = \frac{|EA|}{|PA|} = \frac{|EB|}{|PB|} = \frac{|EC|}{|PC|} < 1 .$$
  Its center and radius (eq. (3), p. 5) are
$$O_{AC} = \left( \frac{x^E_t - a^2 x^P_t}{1-a^2},\ \frac{y^E_t - a^2 y^P_t}{1-a^2},\ \frac{z^E_t - a^2 z^P_t}{1-a^2} \right),\qquad R_{AC} = \left|\frac{a}{1-a^2}\right| \sqrt{(x^P_t - x^E_t)^2 + (y^P_t - y^E_t)^2 + (z^P_t - z^E_t)^2}.$$
- **Assumptions**: constant speeds, $0<a<1$, full position knowledge; no obstacle interaction in this construction (the sphere is built ignoring obstacles).
- **Proof approach**: none given — the formulas are asserted ("we have (2)" … "The center and radius … are (3)"). Verification by direct algebra (3 steps): (i) $|X - O_{AC}|^2 = R_{AC}^2$ expands to $|X-E|^2 = a^2|X-P|^2$; (ii) the decisive identity $P - O_{AC} = (P - E)/(1-a^2)$ gives $|P O_{AC}| = |PE|/(1-a^2)$, so $O_{AC}$ lies on segment $PE$; (iii) $\sin(\text{half-angle of tangent cone from } P) = R_{AC}/|P O_{AC}| = a$, confirming internal consistency with §5.3. Analytic, exact, global; holds for any 3D configuration (nonzero $|PE|$).
- **Physical meaning**: the ball bounded by the sphere is the **evader's dominant region** — for any point inside, the slower evader arrives no later than the pursuer; the exterior is the pursuer's dominant region. Tangent lines $PA$, $PB$ from $P$ ($PA \perp A O_{AC}$, $PB \perp B O_{AC}$, p. 3, Fig. 3) separate capture and escape regions. Fig. 2 (p. 4) shows the family: yellow sphere ($a<1$, around $E$), blue plane ($a=1$, the perpendicular bisector plane of $PE$ — the correct degenerate case), green sphere ($a>1$, around $P$), drawn for $P=(10,10,10)$, $E=(-10,-10,10)$.
- **Extensibility**: this is the standard Apollonius sphere; the formulas are the componentwise lift of the 2D AC (identical to [7]'s 2D center/radius with a third coordinate). It plugs directly into any dominant-region argument (multi-pursuer assignments, capture-region certification) but is itself textbook material; the authors' contribution is the explicit write-up in the PEG context.

### 5.3 Optimal motion space via sine rule (p. 5, eqs. (4)–(6))
- **Statement**: for a random point $C$ on the generalized AC, with $\alpha_1$ the angle at $E$ between $\overrightarrow{EP}$ and $\overrightarrow{EC}$, and $\alpha_2$ the angle at $P$ between $\overrightarrow{PE}$ and $\overrightarrow{PC}$ (p. 5):
$$\frac{\sin\alpha_1}{\sin\alpha_2} = \frac{v_P}{v_E} \quad (4),\qquad \alpha_2 = \sin^{-1}\!\Big(\frac{v_E}{v_P}\sin\alpha_1\Big) \quad (5),\qquad \alpha_2^* = \sin^{-1}\!\Big(\frac{v_E}{v_P}\Big) \text{ when } \alpha_1 = \tfrac{\pi}{2} \quad (6).$$
  Claimed conclusion (p. 5): "the optimal motion space for both agents lies within the blue conical region shown in Fig. 3" — the pursuer should move within the cone envelope $\alpha_2 \le \alpha_2^* = \sin^{-1}(v_E/v_P)$ about the line of sight to "efficiently capture the evader", while the evader "maneuvers within the remaining cone envelope, satisfying $\alpha_1 \ge \pi/2$."
- **Proof approach** (3 steps): (i) in triangle $PEC$, the sine rule gives $|EC|/|PC| = \sin\alpha_2/\sin\alpha_1$; (ii) combining with the AC defining property $|EC|/|PC| = a$ yields eq. (4)–(5) — the decisive equation is (5); (iii) since $\sin\alpha_1 \le 1$, $\alpha_2$ is maximized at $\alpha_1 = \pi/2$, giving eq. (6). Tools: Euclidean sine rule + the Apollonius ratio. Analytic, geometric, local (per-instant, straight-line dash to an AC point).
- **Decisive intermediate inequality/equation**: eq. (5) with $\sin\alpha_1 \le 1 \Rightarrow \alpha_2 \le \sin^{-1}(a) = \alpha_2^*$; consistency check (mine): $\alpha_2^*$ equals the half-angle of the tangent cone from $P$ to the sphere (§5.2 step iii), so the cone in Fig. 3 is exactly the tangency cone.
- **Physical meaning**: headings within the tangent cone are exactly those that still intercept the evader's reachable boundary (the AC) in a straight dash; aiming outside the cone concedes the interior of the evader-dominant ball. For the evader, $\alpha_1 \ge \pi/2$ keeps its heading on the far side relative to the pursuer's cone.
- **Proof gap (optimality, p. 5)**: the word "optimal" is asserted, not proven. Maximizing $\alpha_2$ shows the cone is the *feasible intercept envelope* for one straight dash; there is no time-optimality proof, no HJI/barrier certification, no proof that the evader's $\alpha_1 \ge \pi/2$ prescription is a winning/safe strategy (against the tangent-cone pursuit it is not obviously sufficient), and no formal connection between this geometry and the FACL policy that is actually trained (see 5.4 — the AC never enters the learning inputs or reward).

### 5.4 FACL algorithm: fuzzy actor-critic architecture (pp. 5–6, eqs. (7)–(16))
Recorded precisely, as this is the paper's algorithmic core:
- **Actor (first-order TS fuzzy logic controller, FLC)**: with $n$ inputs $\bar x = [x_1,\dots,x_n]$, $L$ rules,
$$u_t = \sum_{l=1}^{L} \Phi_t^l w_t^l \quad (7),\qquad \Phi_t^l = \frac{\prod_{i=1}^n \mu^{A_i^l}(x_i)}{\sum_{l=1}^L \big(\prod_{i=1}^n \mu^{A_i^l}(x_i)\big)} \quad (8),$$
  where $\mu^{A_i^l}$ is the membership degree in fuzzy set $A_i^l$ (triangular MFs in the experiments), $\Phi_t^l$ the normalized-product firing strength, and $w_t^l$ the actor's consequent (output) parameter for rule $l$.
  - **proof gap (definition of $w_t^l$, p. 5, below eq. (7))**: as printed, "$w_t^l = \max_{u_t, x_i \in \bar x} \mu^{A_i^l}(x_i)$ is the output parameter of the actor for rule $l$" — this defines $w_t^l$ as a max of membership degrees (an input-dependent quantity), which contradicts eq. (11), where $w_t^l$ is an adjustable parameter updated by a gradient rule. In the source FACL ([15] Schwartz 2014; [7] Awheda & Schwartz 2016) $w_t^l$ are learnable consequent parameters initialized independently. The printed definition is erroneous/garbled and must be read as "adjustable consequent parameter."
- **Critic (TS fuzzy inference system, FIS)**: $\hat V_t = \sum_{l=1}^L \Phi_t^l \zeta_t^l$ (eq. (9), p. 6), $\zeta_t^l$ the critic's consequent parameter.
- **TD error**: $\delta_t = r_{t+1} + \gamma \hat V_{t+1} - \hat V_t$ (eq. (10), p. 6), $\gamma = 0.95$ discount factor.
- **Exploration**: Gaussian white noise $N_0 \sim (0, \sigma_a^2)$ added to the control, $u_t' = u_t + N_0$ (p. 6).
- **Actor update** (eq. (11), p. 6): $w_{t+1}^l = w_t^l + \alpha_a \delta_t \left(\frac{u_t' - u_t}{\sigma_a}\right) \frac{\partial u_t}{\partial w_t^l}$ — the Gaussian-perturbation/likelihood-style gradient used in FACL. **Critic update** (eq. (13), p. 6): $\zeta_{t+1}^l = \zeta_t^l + \alpha_c \delta_t \frac{\partial \hat V_t}{\partial \zeta_t^l}$.
- **Gradient computations** (eqs. (12), (14), p. 6): $\frac{\partial u_t}{\partial w_t^l} = \frac{\prod_i \mu^{A_i^l}(x_i)}{\sum_l (\prod_i \mu^{A_i^l}(x_i))} = \Phi_t^l$ and identically $\frac{\partial \hat V_t}{\partial \zeta_t^l} = \Phi_t^l$ — valid because firing strengths do not depend on $w$ or $\zeta$ (normalized weighted sum).
- **Learning-rate heuristic (p. 6)**: $\alpha_a < \alpha_c$ "so that the actor will converge slower than the critic, preventing instability of the actor."
- **State/inputs (eqs. (15)–(16), pp. 6–7)**: pursuer $\bar x_P = [d_{PE}, \delta_P, d_{PO}, \delta_{PO}]$ and evader $\bar x_E = [d_{PE}, \delta_E, d_{EO}, \delta_{EO}]$, where $d_{\cdot}$ are Euclidean distances to the opponent/nearest obstacle and $\delta_{\cdot}$ are the angle differences between the agent's heading $\vec v(\cdot)(t)$ and the line of sight to the opponent/obstacle ($\overrightarrow{PE}(t)$, $\overrightarrow{PO}(t)$, $\overrightarrow{EP}(t)$, $\overrightarrow{EO}(t)$). Note: **no AC-derived quantity appears in the inputs** — the generalized AC of §5.2–5.3 is motivational geometry only, not a feature of the trained controller.
- **Output structure (p. 8)**: controller output is the tuple set $\{(\alpha,\theta) \mid \alpha,\theta \in [-\tfrac{\pi}{4}, \tfrac{\pi}{4}]\}$; with 4 inputs × 5 MFs there are $5^4 = 625$ rules. How the single rule base (eq. (7)) produces the two output channels $\alpha$ and $\theta$ (shared consequents vs. two actors) is **UNREPORTED** — a reproducibility gap.
- **Proof gap (no guarantees)**: no convergence, stability, or approximation-quality analysis of the FACL loop is provided; the update equations are transcribed from [15]/[16] without adaptation-specific analysis.

### 5.5 APF-based reward function (p. 7, eqs. (17)–(20))
- **Obstacle repulsion** (eq. (17), p. 7): $r_{PO} = 1 - \exp(-\alpha_r * \Delta d_{PO})$, $\Delta d_{PO} = d_{PO}(t+1) - d_{PO}(t)$ — positive (reward) when the pursuer moves away from the obstacle, negative (penalty) when approaching; "the evader has the same form of reward function for obstacle."
- **Evader attraction** (eq. (18), p. 7): $r_{PE} = \exp(-\beta_a \Delta d_{PE}) - 1$, $\Delta d_{PE} = d_{PE}(t+1) - d_{PE}(t)$ — positive when closing the distance; "for the evader, (18) is inverted" (the evader is repelled by the pursuer).
- **Success term** (eq. (19), p. 7): $r_s = \gamma_s * g_s$, $g_s = 1$ iff the pursuer captures the evader, else $0$; inverted (i.e., $-\gamma_s$ penalty) for the evader.
- **Total reward** (eq. (20), p. 7): $r_{total} = w_r * r_{PO} + w_a * r_{PE} + r_s$, with balance weights $w_r, w_a$.
- **Notes**: (i) symbol clash — $\gamma$ is the discount factor in eq. (10) while $\gamma_s$ is the success coefficient in eq. (19); (ii) the reward is a pure difference-of-distances shaping with exponential saturations, i.e., potential-field shaping of the reward rather than a potential function with guaranteed policy invariance; (iii) obstacle shape enters only through point-to-center distance $d_{PO}$ (spheres of radius 1 m in tests), so avoidance margin is implicit.
- **Termination of learning (p. 7)**: episodes run until $d_{PE}(t_f) \le d_s$ or "the maximum learning time is reached the designated threshold."

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
**Author-stated** (p. 8, Conclusions): the work covers single-pursuer single-evader PEG only; future work targets multiple agents in 3D and multi-objective RL for multi-objective PEGs. Obstacle avoidance is claimed as achieved but with only spherical, randomly placed obstacles of one size.

**Critical observations (with page/eq.)**:
1. **No formal results**: the entire paper has no theorem/proof; the "optimal motion space" (eqs. (4)–(6)) is a sine-rule feasibility argument, with optimality asserted, not proven (proof gap, p. 5). No HJI, barrier, or reachability certification is attempted.
2. **The AC is disconnected from the algorithm**: the trained FACL inputs (eqs. (15)–(16)) and reward (eqs. (17)–(20)) contain no AC-derived features or cone constraints; the generalized AC (eq. (3)) never enters the controller. Consequently the central claimed contribution cannot be ablated from the experiments — the improvement over [13] may stem entirely from reward shaping/tuning. There is no experiment isolating the benefit of the generalized AC.
3. **Kinematics artifact**: as printed, eq. (1) with $\theta \in [-\pi/4, \pi/4]$ makes $v_z = v\cos\theta > 0$ always — agents monotonically climb; all four captures occur at $z = 15$–$18.9$ m near the 20 m ceiling (Fig. 4 annotations). The θ definition ("angle between speed $v$ and the $z$-axis", p. 2) is inconsistent with the physical steering semantics; 3D maneuvering is effectively 2D horizontal steering plus a forced climb, which weakens the "true 3D" claim.
4. **Definition error**: $w_t^l = \max \mu^{A_i^l}(x_i)$ (p. 5, below eq. (7)) contradicts the learnable-parameter status of $w_t^l$ used in eq. (11); likely a transcription error from [7]/[15] but confusing as printed.
5. **Reproducibility gaps**: obstacle count unreported; mapping from the single 625-rule TS rule base to the two output channels $(\alpha, \theta)$ unexplained; platform UNREPORTED; no code/data availability; baseline [13]'s configuration for fairness (same inputs? same MFs? tuned how?) unreported; only averages over 20 runs, no dispersion statistics (p. 8, Fig. 5).
6. **Internal inconsistencies**: Fig. 5 legend "Ref. [3]" vs text "[13]" (p. 9 vs p. 8); 500 plays × 0.1 s = 50 s vs 100 s timeout (p. 8); Fig. 1 coordinate misprint ($x$ for $z$, p. 3); symbol clash $\gamma$ (discount, eq. (10)) vs $\gamma_s$ (success, eq. (19)); reference [14] author list garbled ("Fang, Xu, C. Cheng, and L. Xie", p. 10) while the in-text attribution says "Xie et al." (p. 2).
7. **Scope**: slower evader only ($a < 1$); single obstacle-nearest distance input (multiple obstacles collapse to one $d_{PO}$); no partial observability, no sensing noise; no comparison with analytic AC-guided pursuit or with [12]'s PMP-optimal solution; capture-time optimality never benchmarked against the geometric lower bound implied by eq. (6).
8. **Preprint-specific**: this is arXiv:2503.08013v1 with no peer review, no journal DOI, and no accepted-venue note; several of the inconsistencies above would likely be caught in review; treat all quantitative claims as unreviewed.

## 8. Citation Value
**Paragraph role**: useful as a 3D-PEG engineering reference at the intersection of geometric dominance reasoning and fuzzy reinforcement learning — citable for (i) the explicit 3D Apollonius-sphere center/radius formulas (eq.
