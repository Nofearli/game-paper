## 2. Research Background

Multi-agent coordination tasks (intelligent transportation, military applications) naturally formulate as multi-player differential games (MPDGs): N players coupled through shared ODE dynamics ẋ = f(x) + Σ g_i(x)u_i, each minimizing its own infinite-horizon integral cost (p. 690). The classical solution tool is dynamic programming, which requires solving the coupled Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman (CHJB) equations and suffers the curse of dimensionality. Adaptive dynamic programming (ADP) replaces the CHJB solve with function approximators and online learning, and most state-of-the-art ADP game solvers are built on policy iteration (PI) [6], [10], [11], which requires a stabilizing (admissible) initial policy — an overly restrictive assumption for complicated, strongly nonlinear systems (p. 690). Value iteration (VI) removes that requirement, and a continuous-time VI algorithm for single-controller nonlinear systems exists in Bian–Jiang [12], but a continuous-time VI counterpart for multi-player games with continuous state/action spaces was missing (p. 690). Separately, incomplete information (players not knowing teammates'/opponents' dynamics, objectives, or control laws) is practically relevant — environmental noise and communication limits break the complete-information assumption — and most existing incomplete-information ADP schemes still require each player to know the other players' critic weights (p. 690).

## 3. Research Problem and Gap

**Problem 1 (p. 691).** For the N-player system (1) below with infinite-horizon costs (2), find feedback strategies u*_i = μ*_i(x) such that the N-tuple {u*_1, …, u*_N} constitutes an **interactive Nash equilibrium** (Definition 1, from Vamvoudakis–Lewis–Hudas [22]): condition (3) (standard Nash inequalities) holds for all i ∈ [1 : N], and additionally for each player k there exists an alternative policy u'_k (k ≠ i) such that J_i(x0, u*_k, u*_{−k}) ≠ J_i(x0, u'_k, u*_{−k}) (eq. (5)) — this rules out the degenerate case (eq. (4)) where no player influences any other's cost and every tuple of single-controller optima is trivially Nash (p. 691).

**Problem 2 (p. 691).** Solve Problem 1 when player i has access only to its information set F_i([t0, t1]) = {x([t0,t1]), g_i, Q_i, R_i, u_i} (eq. (6)): i.e., the shared state trajectory x is the **only global information**; each player knows its own input gain g_i, its own cost parameters (Q_i, R_i) and its own control, but **not** the others' dynamics contributions, objective functions, or control laws (Remark 1, p. 691).

- **[authors' claim]** "Most of state-of-the-art developments are based on policy iteration (PI) … A common feature of these PI-based methods is that it requires a stabilizing control policy to start the learning process [12]" (p. 690). — *Verified as the authors' positioning*: they cite [6], [10], [11] (Fu–Chai TNNLS 2016; Vamvoudakis–Lewis IJRNC 2012; H. Zhang et al. TCYB 2017) as PI-based game solvers and [12] for the admissibility requirement; this matches the known PI literature.
- **[authors' claim]** "a continuous-time counterpart of the VI method is missing for continuous-time MPDG problem with continuous state and action spaces"; discrete-time VI variants with convergence proofs exist [13], [14], and Bian–Jiang [12] give a continuous-time VI algorithm for (single-controller) nonlinear systems that "can be a basis for a VI-based ADP framework for MPDG" (p. 690). — *Verified from the paper's own related-work paragraph*; the extension from [12] to games is exactly what Section III performs.
- **[authors' claim]** For incomplete information, existing remedies either identify unknown dynamics first [15], [16], cancel dynamics parameters from the CHJB via PI [8], [17], [18] — but "most studies above require knowledge of the weights of the other players" — while [19], [20] estimate neighbors' inputs from limited information and [21] uses Q-learning for **discrete-time** cooperative games without others' dynamics and objective functions (p. 690). — *Verified from the cited-text descriptions in the paper*; the residual gap the authors claim is: a **continuous-time** MPDG method that (i) needs no admissible initial policy (VI-based), and (ii) needs no knowledge of other players' dynamics parameters, cost parameters, or critic weights.
- **[critical note on scope]** The "incomplete information" here is structural/model privacy, not stochastic partial observability: the full state x is measurable by all players at all times (Remark 1), and the infinite-horizon cost has no explicit u_{−i} dependence — u_{−i} affects J_i only through the closed-loop trajectory (p. 691).

## 4. Core Contributions

1. **(Modeling/theory)** Extension of the finite-horizon value-iteration HJB equation of Bian–Jiang [12] to a **best-response HJB equation** for MPDGs (eq. (10), p. 692): with all other players' policies frozen, player i's problem reduces to a single-controller optimal control problem, so the single-system VI convergence lemma (Lemma 1 of [12]) carries over (Lemma 2).
2. **(Theory)** A **cooperative value-iteration (cVI) scheme** in which players are partitioned into an *adapting* player and *non-adapting* players (concept borrowed from Köpf et al. [21]) and adapt **sequentially** in a pre-ordered loop (policy evaluation (11) → policy improvement (12) → role switching), with a convergence proof to the optimal value functions V*_i (Theorem 1, p. 692–693) — the first continuous-time VI-based convergence result claimed for MPDGs.
3. **(Algorithm)** The **cVIADP algorithm** (Algorithm 1, p. 694): an NN-critic implementation in which each player's value function is approximated by a linearly parametrized basis family and the critic weights evolve as the solution of an **ordinary differential equation** in the VI time s (eq. (27)–(28)), discretized by forward Euler with a diverging learning-rate series (eq. (29)); the reparametrization dx_{−i} = ẋ − g_iμ_i (eq. (25)) removes any dependence on other players' dynamics/policies, so each player's learning uses only {x(t), μ_i, r_i} data plus probing noise on the adapting player. Convergence of the estimated weights/policies to the optimum (Theorem 2) and closed-loop asymptotic stabilization outside a ball B(ε) (Theorem 3) are proved (pp. 693–694).
4. **(Experiment)** Three numerical validations (Section V, pp. 694–696): a 2-player nonlinear game with constant input gains (converges in 33 iterations/Controller; learned policy removes the initial policy's static error), a 3-player nonlinear game with state-dependent gains taken from D. Liu et al. [15] where the **initial (zero-weight) policy is unstable** yet learning stabilizes the system — the headline demonstration that no admissible initial policy is needed — and a 3-agent linear decoupled 6-dimensional game (converges in 7 iterations/agent).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results

### 5.0 Dynamics, information structure, cost, equilibrium concept

- **Dynamics (eq. (1), p. 691)**: ẋ = f(x) + Σ_{i=1}^N g_i(x) u_i, x ∈ Ω ⊂ R^n a compact set containing the origin, u_i ∈ U_i ⊂ R^m admissible; f : R^n → R^n and g_i : R^n → R^{n×m} locally Lipschitz. (One shared state; players add control channels.)
- **Cost (eq. (2), p. 691)**: J_i(x0; u_i, u_{−i}) = ∫_0^∞ r_i(x, u_i) dt, with r_i(x, u_i) ≜ Q_i(x) + u_i^T R_i u_i; Q_i : Ω → R positive semidefinite, R_i ∈ R^{m×m} positive definite symmetric. r_i does not depend on u_{−i} explicitly; u_{−i} acts on J_i through the trajectory of x (p. 691).
- **Nash equilibrium (eq. (3), p. 691)**: J_i(x0; u*_i, u*_{−i}) ≤ J_i(x0; u_i, u*_{−i}) ∀ i ∈ [1 : N]. **Interactive Nash equilibrium (Definition 1, [22], eqs. (4)–(5), p. 691)**: (3) plus non-degeneracy — for each k there exists u'_k (k ≠ i) with J_i(x0, u*_k, u*_{−k}) ≠ J_i(x0, u'_k, u*_{−k}) (5).
- **Information set (eq. (6), p. 691)**: F_i([t0,t1]) = {x([t0,t1]), g_i, Q_i, R_i, u_i} — state trajectory shared; everything else private (Problem 2).
- **Single-controller HJB / CHJB limit (eqs. (7)–(9), pp. 691–692)**: for N = 1 the finite-horizon VI HJB of [12] is ∂_s V̄(x, s) = inf_u H(x, ∂_x V̄, u) = inf_u {r(x, u) + ∂_x V̄^T B u}, s ≥ 0 (7), where in [12]'s formulation the system is ẋ = Bu. For N > 1 with u*_{−i} fixed, the optimal V*_i ∈ P with V*_i(0) = 0 satisfies inf_{u_i∈U_i} H_i(x, ∂_x V*_i, u_i, u*_{−i}) = 0 (8), with Hamiltonian H_i(x, p, u_i, u_{−i}) = r_i(x, u_i) + p^T (f(x) + Σ_{i=1}^N g_i(x) u_i), and the optimal policy is μ*_i(x) = arg inf_{u_i} H_i(x, ∂_x V*_i, u_i, u*_{−i}) (9). (The printed dimension statement "(x, p, u) ∈ R^{nN} × R^{nN} × R^{mN}" is a leftover from a stacked notation and is inconsistent with x ∈ R^n for the single-system state; harmless but sloppy.)
- **Notation for iterations (p. 692)**: μ_i^{[k_i]} = current policy of player i, k_i its update counter; μ_{−i}^{[k_{−i}]} = {μ_j^{[k_j]}}_{j∈[1:N]\{i}}; x(t) = trajectory under {μ_i^{[k_i]}}; V̄_i(x, s) written V̄_i(s) with x-dependence suppressed; class P = {V ∈ C¹(R^n) : V positive semidefinite}, "proper" in the sense of [12].

### 5.1 Lemma 1 (p. 691–692; imported from [12])

**Statement.** Assume V̄(·,0) = V_0(·), V_0 ∈ P is proper and (7) admits a unique solution; then lim_{s→∞} V̄(·, s) = V*(·) **uniformly on any compact set in R^n**. Furthermore, let μ̄ ∈ C⁰(R^n × R_+) satisfy μ̄(x, s) = arg inf_μ H(x, ∂_x V̄, μ); then lim_{s→∞} μ̄(·, s) = μ*(·) **pointwise in Ω**.
**Proof**: none given here — "its proof can be found in [12]" (Bian–Jiang, TNNLS vol. 33 no. 7, 2022). This is the analytic engine of the whole paper: VI in the artificial "s" direction solves the infinite-horizon HJB without any admissibility assumption on the initial policy.

### 5.2 Lemma 2 (p. 692, eq. (10)) — best-response VI convergence

**Statement.** Define the best-response HJB for player i with arbitrary fixed policies μ_{−i}: ∂_s V̄_i(x, s) = inf_{μ_i} H_i(x, ∂_x V̄_i, μ_i, μ_{−i}), with V̄_i(·, 0) = V_0(·), V_0(·) ∈ P (**Assumption 1**: V_0 ∈ P is proper and (10) admits a unique solution). With Assumption 1 and μ_{−i} fixed, V̄_i(·, s) converges uniformly on any compact set in R^n, and any minimizer policy μ̄_i ∈ C⁰(R^n × R_+), μ̄_i(x, s) = arg inf_{μ_i} H_i(x, ∂_x V̄_i, μ_i, μ_{−i}), converges pointwise in Ω.
**Proof** (2 steps, essentially free): (i) since μ_{−i} is fixed and is a function of x, r_i(x, μ_i) + ∂_xV̄_i^T(f + g_iμ_i + Σ_{j≠i} g_jμ_j^{[k_j]}) is an H of the single-controller form (7) with an effective drift; (ii) apply Lemma 1. ■
**Physical meaning**: freezing opponents makes each player's best-response learning a well-posed single-controller VI; this is the theoretical justification for the sequential (one-adapting-player-at-a-time) architecture.

### 5.3 The cVI scheme (p. 692, eqs. (11)–(12))

For the adapting player i (all others non-adapting, policies frozen at μ_{−i}^{[k_{−i}]}):
1. **Policy evaluation** (eq. (11)): solve ∂_s V̄_i^{[k_i]}(s) = inf_μ H_i(x, ∂_x V̄_i^{[k_i]}, μ, μ_{−i}^{[k_{−i}]}) over s ∈ [0, ∞) with initial condition V̄_i^{[k_i]}(0) = V_i^{[k_i]}. (V̄ is a "VI value": a function of the artificial time s parameterizing value iterations.)
2. **Policy improvement** (eq. (12)): set V_i^{[k_i+1]}(x) = lim_{s→∞} V̄_i^{[k_i]}(s) and update μ_i^{[k_i+1]} = arg inf_μ H_i(x, ∂_x V_i^{[k_i+1]}, μ, μ_{−i}^{[k_{−i}]}) = −(1/2) R_i^{-1} g_i^T ∂_x V_i^{[k_i+1]T}. (The closed form follows because r_i is quadratic in u_i with weight R_i ≻ 0.)
3. **Role switching**: player i+1 becomes adapting (players pre-ordered in a loop, Remark 2); cooperation requires inter-player communication of iteration data and a negotiated order (Remark 3).

### 5.4 Theorem 1 (p. 692–693, eqs. (13)–(19)) — convergence of cooperative VI

**Statement.** For system (1), suppose V_i^{[0]} ∈ P is proper and all players update their policies μ_i^{[k_i]} by (11) and (12); then the value functions V_i^{[k_i]} converge to the optimal value functions V*_i (as k_i → ∞).
**Assumptions used**: Assumption 1 (proper V_0 ∈ P, unique solution of (10)); r_i ≥ 0 (from Q_i ⪰ 0, R_i ≻ 0); classical Nash setting of Section III — the proof is for the complete-information loop (Section III explicitly assumes temporary complete information).
**Proof approach (7 steps, as printed)**:
1. Let s → ∞ in (11); the limit satisfies the fixed-point HJB H_i(x, ∂_x V_i^{[k_i+1]}, μ_i^{[k_i+1]}, μ_{−i}^{[k_{−i}]}) = 0 (13).
2. By (12) the updated policy minimizes H_i against the frozen ∂_xV_i^{[k_i+1]}: H_i(x, ∂_xV_i^{[k_i+1]}, μ_i^{[k_i+1]}, μ_{−i}^{[k_{−i}]}) = min_μ H_i(x, ∂_xV_i^{[k_i+1]}, μ, μ_{−i}^{[k_{−i}]}) ≤ H_i(x, ∂_xV_i^{[k_i+1]}, μ_i^{[k_i]}, μ_{−i}^{[k_i]}) (14). *Printed typo: the last superscript appears as μ_{−i}^{[k_i]} (undefined index); from (15) it must read μ_{−i}^{[k_{−i}]} (verified at 600 dpi).*
3. Hence H_i(x, ∂_xV_i^{[k_i+1]}, μ_i^{[k_i]}, μ_{−i}^{[k_{−i}]}) ≥ 0 (15) — the current policy is suboptimal against the new value gradient.
4. Orbital derivative of V_i^{[k_i+1]} along the trajectory generated by (μ_i^{[k_i+1]}, μ_{−i}^{[k_{−i}]}): V̇_i^{[k_i+1]} = ∂_xV_i^{[k_i+1]}ẋ = −r_i(x, μ_i^{[k_i+1]}) + H_i(x, ∂_xV_i^{[k_i+1]}, μ_i^{[k_i+1]}, μ_{−i}^{[k_{−i}]}) = −r_i(x, μ_i^{[k_i+1]}) (16) — the Hamiltonian term cancels by (13).
5. If all players apply the updated tuple {μ_i^{[k_i+1]}}, then for the next-round value function V̇_i^{[k_i+2]} = −r_i(x, μ_i^{[k_i+1]}) + H_i(x, ∂_xV_i^{[k_i+2]}, μ_i^{[k_i+1]}, μ_{−i}^{[k_{−i+1}]}) ≥ −r_i(x, μ_i^{[k_i+1]}) (17), because V_i^{[k_i+2]} is the best response to μ_{−i}^{[k_{−i+1}]} so its Hamiltonian at any μ_i is ≥ 0 by the analogue of (15).
6. Decisive inequality: (16) + (17) ⇒ V̇_i^{[k_i+1]} ≤ V̇_i^{[k_i+2]} (18), and "by integration" V_i^{[k_i+2]} ≤ V_i^{[k_i+1]} (19).
7. {V_i^{[k_i]}} is nonincreasing and bounded below by zero, hence converges pointwise to a limit V_i^∞; "According to [22], V_i^∞ = V_i*" (final step delegated to Vamvoudakis–Lewis–Hudas, Automatica 2012). ■
**Character**: analytic (monotone-convergence argument), global statements on Ω, but see the proof-gap notes below. **Physical meaning**: each sequential best-response round never increases player i's value; iteration drives every player to its Nash best response simultaneously — a Gauss–Seidel-type (sequential) value iteration on the CHJB system.
**Proof gap (noted by this archive, p. 692–693, eqs. (16)–(19))**: the orbital derivative in (16) is taken along the vector field F(x; μ_i^{[k_i+1]}, μ_{−i}^{[k_{−i}]}) while that in (17) is taken along F(x; μ_i^{[k_i+1]}, μ_{−i}^{[k_{−i+1}]}). These are **different vector fields** whenever the other players' policies actually changed in the passing round, so (18) is a pointwise-in-x comparison of derivatives along two different flows; integrating to obtain the function inequality (19) silently requires a common trajectory along which d/dt[V_i^{[k_i+2]} − V_i^{[k_i+1]}] ≤ 0 holds — along the all-updated flow, V̇_i^{[k_i+1]} would pick up the extra cross term ∂_xV_i^{[k_i+1]} Σ_{j≠i} g_j(μ_j^{[k_j+1]} − μ_j^{[k_j]}) that (16) does not contain. The step from monotone pointwise convergence to "V_i^∞ = V_i*" is additionally delegated to [22] without an argument specific to the sequential switching order. The conclusion is very likely true (it matches the discrete-time Gauss–Seidel VI folklore), but as printed the proof is incomplete.

### 5.5 NN-based implementation: critic structure, information surgery, weight ODE (pp. 693–694, eqs. (20)–(29))

- **Approximators (eqs. (20)–(23), p. 693)**: a C¹, linearly independent basis family {φ_i^{(j)}(x)}_{j=1}^∞ approximates both V_i^{[k]} and V̄_i^{[k_i]}(s): lim_{l→∞} V̂_i^{[k_i]}(x, W_i^{[k_i]}) = V_i^{[k]} (20), lim_{l→∞} V̄̂_i^{[k_i]}(x, W̄_i^{[k_i]}(s)) = V̄_i^{[k_i]}(s) (21), with V̂_i^{[k_i]} = Σ_{j=1}^l w_i^{(j)} φ_i^{(j)} = W_i^{[k_i]T} Φ_i (22) and V̄̂_i^{[k_i]} = W̄_i^{[k_i]T}(s) Φ_i (23). Remark 4: since V_i^{[k+1]} is the s → ∞ limit of V̄_i^{[k_i]}(s), the same basis is used, and W̄_i^{[k_i]} is assumed a function of s only. Remark 5: polynomial bases give global approximation on P; sigmoid/tanh give local NN-style approximation.
- **Actor estimate (eq. (24), p. 693)**: μ̂_i^{[k_i]} = −(1/2) R_i^{-1} g_i^T (∂_xΦ_i)^T W_i^{[k_i]}.
- **Information surgery (eq. (25), p. 693)**: the evaluation step (11) apparently needs μ_{−i}^{[k_{−i}]} through the drift; rewrite ∂_s V̄_i^{[k_i]}(s) = inf_μ {r_i(x, μ) + ∂_x V̄_i^{[k_i]} (dx_{−i}^{[k_{−i}]} + g_i μ)}, where dx_{−i}^{[k_{−i}]} = ẋ − g_i μ_i^{[k_i]} (the "others + drift" residual), estimable by the finite difference (x_{−i}(t+Δt) − x_{−i}(t))/Δt for sufficiently small Δt. Since dx_{−i}^{[k_{−i}]} = f(x) + Σ_{j≠i} g_j μ_j^{[k_j]} is **measured** (from ẋ or state differences) rather than modeled, player i never needs g_j, Q_j, R_j or μ_j, j ≠ i. This is the paper's key structural trick for Problem 2.
- **Assumption 2 (from [12]; eq. (26), p. 693)**: with K_{Φ_i}(t_f) = ∫_0^{t_f} Φ_i Φ_i^T dt, there exist a compact set Ω, γ_i > 0 and t_0 > 0 such that ∀ t_f > t_0, x(0) in the interior of Ω, (x, μ_i(x)) ∈ Ω × U_i on [0, t_f], and (1/t_f) K_{Φ_i}(t_f) > γ_i I_N (26). *Printed identity matrix is I_N; dimensionally it must be I_l (l = number of basis functions) — typo (verified at 400 dpi).* Remark 6: (26) makes K_{Φ_i} invertible and "is essentially consistent with persistence of excitation" in adaptive control.
- **Weight ODE (eq. (27), p. 693)**: integrating (25) against Φ_i over [0, t_f] gives ∂_s W̄_i^{[k_i]} = K_{Φ_i}^{-1}(t_f) [∫_0^{t_f} Φ_i r_i(x, μ_i^{[k_i]}) dt + ∫_0^{t_f} Φ_i W̄_i^{[k_i]T} (∂_xΦ_i)(dx_{−i}^{[k_{−i}]} + g_i μ_i^{[k_i]}) dt], with μ̂_i^{[k_i]} = −(1/2)R_i^{-1}g_i^T(∂_xΦ_i)^T W_i^{[k_i]} ≜ (1/2) Γ_i W_i^{[k_i]} (so Γ_i = −R_i^{-1} g_i^T (∂_xΦ_i)^T). The right side is computable from F_i(t_f) — the adapting player's own data only.
- **Simplified form (eq. (28), p. 693)**: ∂_s W̄_i^{[k_i]} = K_{Φ_i}^{-1}(t_f) (L_i^I[W̄_i^{[k_i]}] + L_i^{II}[W̄_i^{[k_i]}] + L_i^{III}), where L_i^I = ∫_0^{t_f} Φ_i W̄_i^{[k_i]T} ∂_xΦ_i dx_{−i}^{[k_{−i}]} dt, L_i^{II} = −(1/4) ∫_0^{t_f} Φ_i (W̄_i^{[k_i]T} Γ_i R_i Γ_i^T W̄_i^{[k_i]}) dt, L_i^{III} = ∫_0^{t_f} Φ_i Q_i dt. *Typo note (verified at 400 dpi): with Γ_i of size m×l, the scalar quadratic form printed as W̄^T Γ_i R_i Γ_i^T W̄ is ill-posed; consistency with the −(1/4) factor (which comes from completing the square at μ = μ̂ = (1/2)Γ_iW̄) requires Γ_i^T R_i Γ_i. The intended term is the standard −(1/4)W̄^T(∂_xΦ_i)g_iR_i^{-1}g_i^T(∂_xΦ_i)^TW̄.*
- **Discretization (eq. (29), p. 693)**: forward Euler W̄_i^{[k_i]}(k+1) = W̄_i^{[k_i]}(k) + h_k K_{Φ_i}^{-1} (L_i^I[W̄(k)] + L_i^{II}[W̄(k)] + L_i^{III}), where h_k is a learning rate and Σ_{k=0}^∞ h_k diverges (Robbins–Monro-type condition in the VI time s).
- **Algorithm 1 (cVIADP, p. 694)**: choose x_0 ∈ Ω; start with weights W_i^{[0]} such that Assumption 1 is satisfied (note: properness, **not** admissibility — this is the VI advantage); choose t_f > 0, s_f > 0 and terminal threshold ε; repeat {for i = 1 to N: select player i as adapting and add probing noise to its control input (Line 7; only to the adapting player, because Lemma 2's convergence needs fixed μ_{−i} — Remark 7); collect trajectory data {x(t), μ_i^{[k_i]}, r_i(x, μ_i^{[k_i]})} over [0, t_f] (Line 8); solve the weight ODE (28) over [0, s_f] with W̄_i^{[k_i]}(0) = W_i^{[0]} (Line 9); set W_i^{[k_i+1]} = W̄_i^{[k_i]}(s_f) and update μ̂_i^{[k_i+1]} by (24) (Line 10); k_i ← k_i + 1 (Line 11)} until ‖W_i^{[k_i]} − W_i^{[k_i−1]}‖ ≤ ε ∀i (Line 13). Remark 8: since no stabilizing initial policy is assumed, trajectories may diverge; a bound C > 0 is imposed — the run is stopped when |x(τ)| > C and restarted at x(0).

### 5.6 Theorem 2 (p. 693–694) — convergence of the NN implementation

**Statement.** Under Assumptions 1 and 2, if for each iteration index k_i, V̂_i^{[k_i]} and μ̂_i^{[k_i]} are obtained by Algorithm 1, there exist large enough t_f, s_f, l and Ω such that lim_{k→∞} V̂_i^{[k]} = V*_i and lim_{k→∞} μ̂_i^{[k]} = μ*_i uniformly on Ω.
**Proof approach (2 steps, as printed)**:
1. **Approximation step**: decompose the exact VI value along the basis V̄_i^{[k_i]} = Σ_{j=1}^∞ w_i^{(j)} φ_i^{(j)} = V̂_i^{[k_i]} + E_i (30); by the dominated convergence theorem lim_{l→∞}|E_i| = 0, so for any ε > 0 one can choose l large and appropriate φ_i(x) with |V̂_i^{[k_i]} − V̄_i^{[k_i]}| < ε; combined with Lemma 2 (V̄_i^{[k_i]} → V_i^{[k_i]} uniformly) this gives V̂_i^{[k_i]} → V_i^{[k_i]} for x ∈ Ω.
2. **Iteration step**: "The VI-based algorithm comprised of (12) and (13) is equivalent to the PI introduced in [23, Theorem 12.1 and 12.2], and a detailed proof of convergence is given therein" (Song–Wei–Li, *Adaptive Dynamic Programming: Single and Multiple Controllers*, Springer 2019); by differentiability of the basis family the gradients converge, ∂_x V̂_i^{[k_i]} → ∂_x V*_i in Ω, hence lim_{k→∞} μ̂_i^{[k]} = μ*_i. ■
**Proof gap (this archive's note)**: the interchange of the two limits (l → ∞ in step 1 and k → ∞ in step 2) is asserted through the existential "there exist large enough t_f, s_f, l" rather than proven uniformly over k; the equivalence of the sequential cVI with the (parallel/serial) PI analysis of [23, Thms. 12.1–12.2] is claimed without checking that [23]'s hypotheses (e.g., admissibility-free convergence for the multi-controller case) transfer verbatim. Theorem 2 should be read as a convergence *schema* whose rigorous completion would need a uniform-in-k approximation argument.

### 5.7 Theorem 3 (p. 694, eq. (31)) — learned policies are stabilizing

**Statement.** Under the conditions of Theorem 2, for any ε > 0 satisfying B(ε) ⊂ Ω, there exist parameters l, t_f, s_f such that for i ∈ [1 : N]: ∂_x V̂_i (f(x) + Σ_{j=1}^N g_j(x) μ̂_j) < 0, ∀ x ∈ Ω \ B(ε) (31), where B(ε) = {x ∈ Ω : |x| < ε}.
**Proof approach (5 steps, as printed)**:
1. From Theorem 2, for any δ_i > 0 with appropriate parameters |μ̂_i − μ*_i| < δ_i ∀ x ∈ Ω.
2. Let F(x, μ) = f(x) + Σ_{i=1}^N g_i(x) μ_i; F is locally Lipschitz hence bounded on compact Ω, so there is L > 0 with ∂_xV*_i [F(x, μ̂) − F(x, μ*)] ≤ L Σ_{i=1}^N |μ̂ − μ*| < Lδ ∀ x ∈ Ω, δ = Σ δ_i.
3. Decisive inequality: from the HJB (8), ∂_xV*_i F(x, μ̂) < −r_i(x, μ*_i) + Lδ (unnumbered, p. 694).
4. For any ε_1 with B(ε_1) ⊂ Ω, make δ sufficiently small that r_i(x, μ*_i(x)) > Lδ on Ω \ B(ε_1); then ∂_xV*_i F(x, μ̂) < 0 there. *(Implicit assumption flagged by this archive: this requires r_i(x, μ*_i(x)) = Q_i(x) + μ*_i^T R_i μ*_i to be bounded away from 0 outside B(ε_1); with Q_i merely positive semidefinite this needs an observability/detectability-type condition that is not stated.)*
5. From ∂_x-convergence, |∂_xV̂_i − ∂_xV*_i| < ν_i ∀ x ∈ Ω; then ∂_xV̂_iF(x, μ̂) < ∂_xV*_iF(x, μ̂) + ν_i F(x, μ̂) ≤ ∂_xV*_iF(x, μ̂) + ν_i M, M = sup_{x∈Ω} F(x, μ̂) *(as printed F is vector-valued; a norm bound |F| is intended)*; choosing ε_2 > ε_1 with B(ε_2) ⊂ Ω and ν_i small enough that ∂_xV*_iF(x, μ̂) + ν_iM < 0 on Ω \ B(ε_2) (possible since Ω\B(ε_2) ⊂ Ω\B(ε_1) and ε_1 can be arbitrarily small) yields (31) with ε_2. ■
**Character**: analytic, semi-global (all of Ω minus a ball), constructive in the approximation parameters (l, t_f, s_f exist but are not characterized quantitatively). **Physical meaning**: the jointly learned policy tuple renders every player's estimated value function a Lyapunov function for the closed loop outside any prescribed ball — asymptotic stability of the equilibrium point x = 0 under learned policies, with no stabilizing initialization.

### 5.8 Summary of the analytic chain

Lemma 1 [12] (single-controller VI) → Lemma 2 (best-response VI, frozen opponents) → Theorem 1 (sequential cVI monotone convergence, complete information) → Theorem 2 (+NN critic +PE Assumption 2, incomplete information) → Theorem 3 (stability of the learned tuple). The equilibrium concept delivered is the standard coupled-HJB Nash solution (8)–(9); the interactive-Nash refinement of Definition 1 is used to motivate the formulation but is not re-invoked in any convergence proof.

## 6. Simulation and Experiments

No experimental hardware; all results are numerical simulations (platform not stated — UNREPORTED; figures are MATLAB-style). Common protocol across examples: probing/excitation signal = sum of sine signals (amplitudes/frequencies UNREPORTED); learning rate = cosine function (actual h_k sequence UNREPORTED); weights initialized at W_i^{[0]} = 0 (zero policy, possibly unstable — deliberately violating PI admissibility); convergence criterion ‖W_i^{[k_i]} − W_i^{[k_i−1]}‖ ≤ ε with ε UNREPORTED; t_f, s_f, γ_i, C values UNREPORTED; no baseline algorithms, no quantitative cost/error metrics, no run-time data.

**Example 1 — 2-player nonlinear game, constant input gains (pp. 694–695, Figs. 1–3)**
- Dynamics: ẋ = [x₂; −x₁³ − 0.5x₂] + [1; 0]ᵀu₁ + [0; 2]ᵀu₂ (state dim 2).
- Parameters **as printed**: Q₁ = Q₂ = I₂; R₁ = 1; R₂ = 4; basis Φ_i(x) = [x₁², x₁x₂, x₂²]ᵀ (l = 3); Ŵ_i^{[0]} = 0; x₀ = [1, 1]ᵀ; after each player updates, input–state data within 1 s is used for the next player's learning.
- Results: Fig. 1 (weight updates) — both critics' three weight components converge monotonically after **33 iterations per controller** (Ŵ₁ components → ≈ 1.27 / transient-to-0 / ≈ 0; Ŵ₂ components → ≈ 1.28 / 1.12 / 0.78, read off the plot). Fig. 2 — value surfaces V̂_i^{[k]} for k = 1, 4, 16, 33 grow progressively "bowl-shaped" and V̂_i ∈ P is verified visually. Fig. 3 — closed-loop states: learned policy drives x to the equilibrium faster and "eliminates the static error of initial policy" (p. 694). **Validates**: convergence of cVIADP from a non-stabilizing zero initialization on a stable-plant problem; improvement over the initial policy.
- Remark 9 (p. 694–695): the value surfaces *increase* with iteration as functions of x, which does **not** contradict Theorem 1's nonincreasing claim — the latter is a statement about values along trajectories in time t, the former about the graph over x; reconciled via Theorem 3.

**Example 2 — 3-player nonlinear game, state-dependent gains, dynamics adopted from [15] (p. 695, Figs. 4–6)**
- Dynamics **as printed**: f(x) = [x₂ − 2x₁; −(1/2)x₁ − x₂ + x₁²x₂ + (1/4)x₂(cos(2x₁) + 2)² + (1/4)x₂(sin(4x₁²) + 2)²]; g₁(x) = [0, 2x₁]ᵀ; g₂(x) = [0, cos(2x₁) + 2]ᵀ; g₃(x) = [0, sin(4x₁²) + 2]ᵀ.
- Parameters **as printed**: Q₁ = 0.5I₂, Q₂ = 2I₂, Q₃ = I₂; R₁ = 0.5, R₂ = 2, R₃ = 1; Φ_i(x) = [x₁², x₁x₂, x₂²]ᵀ; W_i^{[0]} = 0; state initialized randomly around the origin; excitation as in Example 1.
- Results: Figs. 4–5 — weights and value surfaces converge after **15 iterations per player**. Fig. 6 — under the initial (zero) policy the system is **unstable**; the learned policy stabilizes it. **Validates**: the headline claim that cVIADP works "without the dependence of initial admissible policy, which is the main limit of PI-based algorithms" (p. 695).

**Example 3 — 3-agent linear game, decoupled 6-dimensional plant (pp. 695–696, eq. (32), Figs. 7–9)**
- Dynamics **as printed** (eq. (32)): ẋ_i = A_i x_i + B_i u_i, i = 1,2,3, with A₁ = [−2 1; −4 −1], B₁ = [2; 1]; A₂ = [−1 1; −4 −1], B₂ = [2; 3]; A₃ = [−2 1; −3 −1], B₃ = [1; 2]. Stacked form (1) via x = [x₁, x₂, x₃]ᵀ, f = diag{A₁, A₂, A₃} (footnote 1: block diagonal), g₁ = [B₁ᵀ, 0₁ₓ₂, 0₁ₓ₂]ᵀ, g₂ = [0₁ₓ₂, B₂ᵀ, 0₁ₓ₂]ᵀ, g₃ = [0₁ₓ₂, 0₁ₓ₂, B₃ᵀ]ᵀ.
- Parameters **as printed**: Q₁ = I₂, Q₂ = 2I₂, Q₃ = 0.5I₃ (*sic* — inconsistent with the 2-dim agent state; presumably 0.5I₂, typo), R₁ = 2R₂ = R₃ = 1 (chained exactly as printed; natural reading R₁ = 2, R₂ = R₃ = 1); basis {φ_i(x)} = ∪_{1≤p≤q≤3} {x_pᵀx_q} (6 pairwise cross-terms); W_i^{[0]} = 0; initial state random near the origin; excitation as before.
- Results: Fig. 7 — value surfaces V̂_i(x_i, x_{−i}) with x_{−i} frozen at x_{−i}(0), for k = 1, 3, 4, 6; Fig. 8 — weights converge after **7 iterations per player**; Fig. 9 — learned policies stabilize all three agents. **Validates**: scalability of the machinery beyond the shared-plant setting to a multi-agent (decoupled dynamics, coupled costs) nonzero-sum game, and fast convergence in the linear case.

**Figure-to-claim map**: Figs. 1, 4, 8 → weight convergence (Theorem 2); Figs. 2, 5, 7 → value-function shaping and V̂_i ∈ P (Theorems 1–2, Remark 9); Figs. 3, 6, 9 → closed-loop stabilization (Theorem 3) and superiority over PI w.r.t. initialization (Example 2 being the decisive one).

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues

**Author-stated (p. 697, Conclusion)**: future work will address "practical implementation aspects such as role switching mechanism and efficient excitation" — i.e., the loop order is currently fixed/exogenous and the probing-noise excitation is crude.

**Critical observations (with page/eq. anchors)**:
1. **Theorem 1 proof gap (pp. 692–693, eqs. (16)–(19))**: the monotonicity argument compares orbital derivatives taken along two different closed-loop vector fields (only player i updated vs. all players updated); the "by integration" step to (19) needs a common-trajectory comparison that is not provided, and the final identification V_i^∞ = V_i* is delegated to [22] without adaptation to the sequential switching order.
2. **Theorem 2 limit interchange (p. 693–694, proof)**: uniformity of the NN approximation over all iterations k is asserted ("there exist large enough t_f, s_f, l") rather than proven; the reduction to the PI convergence of [23, Thms. 12.1–12.2] is by equivalence claim only. No rate of convergence is given anywhere.
3. **Theorem 3 hidden assumption (p. 694, step "r_i(x, μ*_i(x)) > Lδ on Ω\B(ε₁)")**: with Q_i only positive semidefinite, the running cost under μ*_i may approach 0 away from the origin; a detectability/lower-bound condition on r_i is silently needed. Also "ν_i F(x, μ̂)" mixes a scalar with the vector F (norm intended), and M = sup F(x, μ̂) is likewise a vector supremum as printed.
4. **Typographical defects (verified against 400–600 dpi renders)**: eq. (14) last term printed μ_{−i}^{[k_i]} (must be μ_{−i}^{[k_{−i}]}); eq. (26) printed I_N (must be I_l); L_i^{II} printed with Γ_i R_i Γ_i^T (dimensionally must be Γ_i^T R_i Γ_i); Example 3 prints Q₃ = 0.5I₃ (state is 2-dim per agent; presumably 0.5I₂); the Introduction promises "two numerical simulations" (p. 691) while three are presented.
5. **Information structure is stronger than the title suggests**: the full state x of the *joint* system is measurable by every player (Remark 1, eq. (6)), and the residual dx_{−i} = ẋ − g_iμ_i must be measured or finite-differenced (eq. (25)) — so "incomplete information" means privacy of models/costs/policies, not partial state observation; noise robustness of the Δt finite difference is not analyzed.
6. **PE condition is global-state-dependent (Assumption 2, eq. (26); Remark 7)**: K_{Φ_i} is built from Φ_i(x(t)) of the *joint* state, hence its excitation depends on all players' behavior; only the adapting player is excited, and nothing guarantees (26) holds for arbitrary non-adapting policies — a structural tension the paper does not resolve.
7. **Equilibrium concept vs. guarantee**: Definition 1 (interactive Nash, eq. (5)) motivates the formulation, but the convergence theorems establish only convergence to the coupled-HJB solution (8)–(9); uniqueness of the Nash equilibrium, and whether the sequential limit is the interactive one, are not discussed. No comparison of the achieved costs against the true Nash costs (which are unavailable in closed form for these nonlinear examples).
8. **Numerical evidence is thin**: 2–6-dimensional states, l = 3–6 polynomial bases, no baselines (no PI-ADP, no [12] single-controller VI, no [21] Q-learning comparison), no Monte-Carlo statistics, no sensitivity study on t_f, s_f, h_k, noise amplitude; iteration-count claims (33/15/7) are for the specific seeds/settings only. The divergence-guard (Remark 8, bound C > 0) concedes that learning runs may be aborted and restarted — the interplay between restarts and the convergence proofs is not analyzed.
9. **Scope**: all players share the same U-dimension m and the formulation is deterministic, infinite-horizon, discounted-free; extensions to stochastic dynamics, output feedback, or heterogeneous information are open (as the authors' own future-work sentence implies).

## 8. Citation Value

**Paragraph role**: this paper is the canonical reference in this library for the *learning-based* branch of multi-player differential games: it is the continuous-time, VI-based (admissible-policy-free) ADP answer to the CHJB solve for N-player nonzero-sum games under model/cost/policy privacy, complementing the geometric/analytic pursuit–evasion and reach-avoid results that dominate the collection. Use it (a) as the VI-vs-PI contrast point whenever an archive discusses stabilizing-initial-policy requirements of PI-ADP game solvers ([6], [10], [11] lineage); (b) as the incomplete-information baseline where players know only {x, g_i, Q_i, R_i, u_i} (eq. (6)) — the weakest-information end of the spectrum compared with works that identify dynamics [15], [16] or know others' weights [8], [17], [18]; (c) as the source of the sequential "adapting/non-adapting player" schedule with an ODE-based critic update and probing noise, i.e., the Gauss–Seidel alternative to synchronous multi-critic schemes; and (d) as a methodological caution, since its Theorems 1–3 contain identifiable proof gaps (Section 7.1–7.3) that a rigorous follow-up could close.

**Citation sentences**: "For continuous-time multi-player differential games under incomplete information, Zhang, Zhang and Cai (IEEE/CAA JAS 2024) proposed a cooperative value-iteration ADP scheme in which players sequentially adapt their policies — each solving a best-response HJB via an ODE-based critic update — without any admissible initial policy or knowledge of the other players' dynamics, costs, or control laws, with convergence and closed-loop stabilizability proofs." Or, in a methods comparison: "Unlike PI-based multi-player ADP that requires stabilizing initial policies [6], [10], [11], the cVIADP scheme of [P085] initializes all critics at zero and still stabilizes the game (their Example 2), at the price of a persistence-of-excitation condition on the joint trajectory (Assumption 2)."
