## 2. Research Background

Target guarding is a classical branch of pursuit-evasion games (PEGs) since Isaacs [4], with modern variants including reach-avoid games [27–29], coastline guarding [30], border defense [31], perimeter defense [32, 33] and target-attacker-defender games [35–37] (p. 2). While HJI-equation methods [18–22] give complete solutions, they suffer from the curse of dimensionality, so geometric tools — Apollonius circles [24], Voronoi partitions [25], reachable sets [26] — are used instead (pp. 1–2). Two strands are combined here: (i) sensing-limited / partial-information games, where Bopardikar et al. [9] studied discrete-time PEGs with equal sensing ranges and Shishika et al. [10] (ICRA 2021) introduced a partial-information *target defense* game solved by a three-phase reverse analysis; and (ii) games with obstacles, where Oyler et al. [5] built dominance regions for PEGs with obstacles but under full information (p. 2). The paper's premise is that realistic intruders have limited sensing and environments contain obstacles, and that a fixed line-segment obstacle inside the target sensing region (TSR) changes both the intruder's optimal path and the defender's winning probability — an effect the authors quantify as an additive angular gain.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap

- **Problem**: a two-player planar target defense game. An intruder I (speed $\nu<1$) tries to breach a circular target of radius $r_0$; a defender D (speed 1) tries to capture I inside the annular TSR of width $\rho_T$; a fixed line segment obstacle of length $l_o$, tangent at its midpoint to the circle of radius $r_0+\rho_T/2$, sits in the TSR. I senses only within radius $\rho_I$ and cannot see the obstacle until (part of) it enters $\mathcal{S}_{\rho_I}(x_I)$, and may react only after sensing an obstacle *endpoint*; D shares the static target's full information about I once I is inside the TSR (pp. 2–3). The game is a Game of Kind: maximize the defender's probability of winning when the intrusion angle is uniformly random (p. 2, p. 6).

## 4. Core Contributions

1. **(Modeling)** Extension of the classic target defense game to partial information *with a fixed line segment obstacle*: three-phase decomposition by information possession — deployment (Def. 1, eq. (1)–(2)), asymmetric information (Def. 2, eq. (3)–(4)), engagement (Def. 3, eq. (3)+(5)) — with engagement termination rules (capture / breach / escape back to deployment, p. 4); fairness Assumption 1 restricting $(\rho_T,\rho_I,l_o)$ (p. 3).
2. **(Strategy design)** Redesigned closed-loop strategies for both players in the asymmetric phase: intruder $\mu_I^{\text{asym}}$ = radial invasion with an endpoint-directed detour after sensing an obstacle endpoint (p. 5); defender $\mu_D^{\text{asym}}$ = move to the optimal defense point on the radial direction of the *predicted* endpoint (chosen by the sign of $\Delta\theta=\theta(t_{\text{asym}})-\theta_o$ vs. $\beta$) (p. 6).
3. **(Theory — quantitative gain)** Lower bound on the defender's winning probability with the obstacle, $P'_D=(2\Theta(R^*_{dep})+\Delta\theta_l+\Delta\theta_r)/(2\pi)$ (Theorem 1, eq. (39)), with the obstacle-induced critical angles $\Delta\theta_l,\Delta\theta_r$ characterized in Lemmas 3–4 (eqs. (29)–(38)); degenerates to $P_D=\Theta(R^*_{dep})/\pi$ of [10] when the obstacle does not help (eq. (26)).
4. **(Theory — parameter regimes)** Apollonius-circle sufficient conditions for breach/escape/capture at the engagement instant (Lemma 1, eq. (8)–(11)) and the diameter criteria $2r_A \gtrless \rho_T$ (Corollaries 1–2, eqs. (12)–(14)); optimal deployment radius $R^*_{dep}=\sqrt{r_D^{*2}-l_D^2}$ and max guaranteed angle $\Theta(R^*_{dep})$ (Corollary 3, eqs. (24)–(25)).
5. **(Experiment)** Two snapshot-based simulations, without (Fig. 7) and with (Fig. 8) the obstacle, showing the obstacle flipping the outcome at an intrusion angle outside the guaranteed cone; a Monte Carlo verification "on the entire circumference at 0.1 degree" is announced (p. 9) but no results are reported.

## 5. Key Mathematical Results

### 5.0 Setup, information structure, termination (pp. 2–4)

- **Geometry**: target disk radius $r_0$ centered at origin $O$; TSR $=\{r_0<\|x\|\le r_0+\rho_T\}$ (p. 3 — printed with a typo as $\mathcal{S}_{r_0+\rho_I}(0)\setminus\mathcal{S}_{r_0}(0)$, the subscript should be $r_0+\rho_T$; verified on the p. 3 render). Obstacle: segment of length $l_o$, midpoint at $(r_0+\rho_T/2,\theta_o)$ in polar coordinates, tangent to the circle $\|x\|=r_0+\rho_T/2$ (hence perpendicular to the radial ray $\theta_o$). Endpoint polar angles $\theta_o\pm\beta$, with $\beta=\arctan\!\big((l_o/2)/(r_0+\rho_T/2)\big)$ (p. 6).
- **Dynamics**: never written explicitly; implicitly single-integrator (simple motion) with speeds $\nu<1$ (intruder) and 1 (defender) — "assume that the speed of the defender is 1 and the speed of the intruder is $\nu$" (p. 3). All results are kinematic/geometric.
- **Information structure**: target is static, has full information of I inside the TSR; D shares it (angle and speed of I) from the moment I enters the TSR; I knows the target center and TSR boundary always, senses D and the obstacle only within $\mathcal{S}_{\rho_I}(x_I)$, and after first sensing the obstacle "cannot immediately make a judgment to change the direction of the intrusion and can only make a decision after sensing the endpoint of the obstacle" (p. 3). Intrusion (breaching) angle is randomly chosen, modeled as $\theta(t_{\text{asym}})\sim\text{Unif}[-\pi,\pi]$ (p. 6), making the outcome "a probabilistic outcome".
- **Terminal conditions (p. 3)**: breach $\|x_I(t)\|<r_0$; capture $\|x_I-x_D\|=0$ with $r_0<\|x_I\|<r_0+\rho_T$.
- **Assumption 1 (p. 3)**: (1) $\rho_T<r_0\nu$; (2) $\rho_I<\rho_T/2$; (3) $l_o<2\rho_I$. Stated roles: (1) prevents the degenerate always-win defender game; (2) restricts intruder strategies and keeps the obstacle invisible from outside the TSR; (3) guarantees a vertically-invading intruder can still sense both endpoints (so a detour exists). The full parameter space is explicitly declared out of scope (p. 3).
- **Phases**: Deployment (Def. 1): $0\le\|x_D\|\le r_0+\rho_T-\rho_I$ (1), $\|x_I\|>r_0+\rho_T$ (2), $x_D(0)=0$, $\|x_I(0)\|>D\gg r_0$. Asymmetric information (Def. 2): $r_0<\|x_I\|\le r_0+\rho_T$ (3), $\|x_I-x_D\|>\rho_I$ (4) — only D knows I (the paper assumes the second scenario: I has entered the TSR). Engagement (Def. 3): (3) plus $\|x_I-x_D\|<\rho_I$ (5). Order $t_{\text{asym}}<t_{\text{eng}}$; engagement ends by capture / breach / escape $\|x_I\|>r_0+\rho_T$ returning to deployment (p. 4).

### 5.1 Apollonius circle machinery (p. 4, eqs. (6)–(11); Lemma 1)

- **Apollonius circle (eq. (6)–(7))**: for fixed $x_I,x_D$ and ratio $k=\nu$, center $x_A=\frac{1}{1-\nu^2}x_I-\frac{\nu^2}{1-\nu^2}x_D$, radius $r_A=\frac{\nu}{1-\nu^2}\|x_I(t)-x_D(t)\|$. Since $\nu<1$, the intruder lies inside the circle; the open disk bounded by it is I's dominance region (standard property, used without proof).
- **Lemma 1 (p. 4, unproven)**: (i) if the circle meets the target, $\|x_A\|-r_A\le r_0$ (8), I can always breach; (ii) if part of the circle lies outside the TSR, $\|x_A\|+r_A>r_0+\rho_T$ (9), I can always escape; (iii) if $\|x_A\|-r_A>r_0$ (10) and $\|x_A\|+r_A<r_0+\rho_T$ (11), D can always prevent intrusion. *Proof gap*: no proof ("All the conclusions can be derived from the definition of the Apollonius circle", p. 5). The claims are the standard dominance-region sufficiencies and are sound for unobstructed simple motion; the obstacle is argued away heuristically: "capture must occur after the intruder bypasses the obstacle and escape must be based on the fact that both players are co-linear, that is, the escape trajectory would not pass through the obstacle. Therefore, the presence of the obstacle does not affect the judgment" (p. 4) — this co-linearity argument is asserted, not proven, and is the paper's only justification that Lemma 1 survives the obstacle.

### 5.2 Engagement-instant diameter criteria (p. 5, eqs. (12)–(14))

- **Corollary 1 (eq. (12))**: if $2r_A=\frac{2\nu\rho_I}{1-\nu^2}>\rho_T$, then at $t=t_{\text{eng}}$ I has a strategy to breach or escape. *Proof*: at $t_{\text{eng}}$, $\|x_I(t_{\text{eng}})-x_D(t_{\text{eng}})\|=\rho_I$ (13), hence $r_A=\frac{\nu\rho_I}{1-\nu^2}$; a circle of diameter $2r_A\ge\rho_T$ cannot be contained in the TSR, "and Lemma 1 apply". *Proof gap*: the proof cites "(10)" for $r_A$, but $r_A$ is defined in (7) (verified on the p. 5 render); also the step "cannot be contained in TSR ⇒ Lemma 1" silently uses that the circle's interior is I's dominance region.
- **Corollary 2 (Capture, eq. (14))**: if $2r_A=\frac{2\nu\rho_I}{1-\nu^2}<\rho_T$, D can always capture regardless of I's strategy. Unproven (converse reading of Corollary 1). Note both are statements about the *instant* $t_{\text{eng}}$ with the (strong) assumption that at that instant the separation is exactly $\rho_I$.

### 5.3 Critical moment and no-obstacle deployment (pp. 5–7, eqs. (15)–(20))

- **Deployment constraint (eq. (15))**: polar axis aligned with D's position at $t_{\text{asym}}$; D's radius satisfies $R_{dep}<r_0+\rho_T-\rho_I$ (so D is never sensed during deployment, cf. (1)).
- **Critical moment $t_{\text{eng}}$ (p. 5, three conditions, from [10])**: (1) the Apollonius circle of (I, D) is tangent to the target boundary; (2) I becomes aware of D; (3) I and D are on the same radial line. **Critical radii (eqs. (16)–(17))**: $r_D^*=r_0-\frac{\rho_I}{1+\nu}$, $r_I^*=r_0+\frac{\nu\rho_I}{1+\nu}$. *Independently verified*: with $\|x_I-x_D\|=r_I^*-r_D^*=\rho_I$, eq. (6) gives $\|x_A\|=r_0+\frac{\nu\rho_I}{1-\nu^2}=r_0+r_A$ and $r_A=\frac{\nu\rho_I}{1-\nu^2}$, so the circle is tangent to $\|x\|=r_0$ from outside and spans $r_0$ to $r_0+\frac{2\nu\rho_I}{1-\nu^2}$ — tangency and the (14) criterion both check out.
- **Defender time budget (eq. (18))**: $l_D=\frac{r_0+\rho_T-r_I^*}{\nu}=\frac{\rho_T}{\nu}-\frac{\rho_I}{1+\nu}$ — the distance D can cover (speed 1) while I travels radially from the TSR boundary to $r_I^*$.
- **Lemma 2 (pp. 6–7, eqs. (19)–(20))**: under (14), choosing the deployment radius with $r_D^*-l_D\le R_{dep}\le r_0+\rho_T-\rho_I$ (19), the defender guarantees capture whenever $|\theta(t_{\text{asym}})|\le\Theta(R_{dep})$, where $\Theta(R_{dep})=\arccos\!\Big(\frac{1}{2r_D^*}\Big[R_{dep}+\frac{r_D^{*2}-l_D^2}{R_{dep}}\Big]\Big)$ (20). *Verified reconstruction (proof is only "by selecting the deployment radius [10]"):* (20) is the law of cosines — the defense point at $(r_D^*,\theta)$ is reachable within budget $l_D$ iff $l_D^2\ge R_{dep}^2+r_D^{*2}-2R_{dep}r_D^*\cos\theta$, i.e. $\cos\theta\le\frac{R_{dep}^2+r_D^{*2}-l_D^2}{2R_{dep}r_D^*}$, which is exactly (20)'s argument; the lower bound in (19) is precisely the condition that this arccos argument stays $\le 1$ ($(R_{dep}-r_D^*)^2\le l_D^2$). Global, analytic, exact. Note Lemma 2 says "Assume that Corollaries 1, 2 hold", which is sloppy phrasing for "assume (14) holds" (Corollary 1 and 2 are conditional statements, not hypotheses).
- **Remark 1 (p. 7)**: the intruder does not deviate when first *sensing* the obstacle, only after sensing an *endpoint*; at vertical incidence the deflection choice is arbitrary (both endpoints sensed simultaneously).

### 5.4 Obstacle-affected kinematics (pp. 7–8, eqs. (21)–(28))

- **Three incidence cases (p. 6)**: (a) vertical $\theta(t_{\text{asym}})=\theta_o$; (b) beyond $0<\theta(t_{\text{asym}})-\theta_o<\beta$; (c) within $-\beta<\theta(t_{\text{asym}})-\theta_o<0$. Only $|\Delta\theta|\le\beta$ paths interact with the obstacle; after sensing an endpoint the intruder goes straight to it, then resumes radial invasion along $\theta_o+\beta$ or $\theta_o-\beta$.
- **Defender budget with detour (eq. (21))**: $l'_D=\frac{x_1+x_2+x_3}{\nu}$, where $x_1$ = radial path before sensing an endpoint (varies with $\theta_{\text{asym}}$), $x_2=\rho_I$ (straight run to the endpoint from the sensing instant), $x_3=\frac{l_o}{2\sin\beta}-r_0-\frac{\nu\rho_I}{1+\nu}$ (radial run from the endpoint, at radius $\frac{l_o}{2\sin\beta}=\sqrt{(r_0+\rho_T/2)^2+(l_o/2)^2}$, down to the critical radius $r_I^*$). *Verified*: $\frac{l_o}{2\sin\beta}$ is indeed the endpoint radius since $\sin\beta=(l_o/2)/\sqrt{(r_0+\rho_T/2)^2+(l_o/2)^2}$, and $x_3=\|E\|-r_I^*$.
- **Defender travel distances to the two endpoint defense positions (eqs. (22)–(23))**: $l_1=\sqrt{r_D^{*2}+R_{dep}^2-2r_D^*R_{dep}\cos(\theta_o+\beta)}$, $l_2=\sqrt{r_D^{*2}+R_{dep}^2-2r_D^*R_{dep}\cos(\theta_o-\beta)}$ — law of cosines from $(R_{dep},0)$ to $(r_D^*,\theta_o\pm\beta)$. **Claim "one can easily derive that $l_1<l_2$" (p. 7) is sign-dependent and false for the paper's own example**: $l_1<l_2\iff\cos(\theta_o+\beta)>\cos(\theta_o-\beta)\iff\sin\theta_o\sin\beta<0\iff\theta_o\in(-\pi,0)$; the simulation uses $\theta_o=0.78>0$, for which $l'_1\approx0.705>l'_2\approx0.626$ (my computation). The comparison flips with the sign of $\theta_o$ and the paper never states this.
- **Corollary 3 (p. 8, eqs. (24)–(26))**: if $|\Delta\theta|>\beta$ (obstacle irrelevant), $R^*_{dep}=\sqrt{r_D^{*2}-l_D^2}$ (24) and $\Theta(R^*_{dep})=\arccos\Big(\sqrt{1-\frac{l_D^2}{r_D^{*2}}}\Big)$ (25), recovering the no-obstacle winning probability $P_D=\arccos\big(\sqrt{1-l_D^2/r_D^{*2}}\big)/\pi=\Theta(R^*_{dep})/\pi$ (26). *Verified*: minimizing (20)'s argument over $R_{dep}$ (derivative $\propto 1-(r_D^{*2}-l_D^2)/R_{dep}^2$) gives exactly (24); substituting into (20) gives (25) — the printed one-line proof ("If Assumption 1.1 holds and (20) is satisfied...") omits this but the result is correct. *Numerical check with the paper's parameters*: $R^*_{dep}=0.68552$, $\Theta=0.76022$ — matches the printed 0.6855 / 0.7602 (p. 9).
- **Probability-level claims (p. 8)**: "the probability of the defender's winning is $P_D<0.5$ as long as Assumption 1.3 is satisfied, since the term within arccos is positive. However, the violation of Assumption 1 will immediately lead to $P_D=1$." *Issue*: $\sqrt{1-l_D^2/r_D^{*2}}>0\iff l_D<r_D^*\iff \rho_T/\nu<r_0\iff$ **Assumption 1.(1)**, not 1.3 — the attribution is a typo; and "violation ⇒ $P_D=1$" is heuristic (for $l_D>r_D^*$ the arccos argument in (25) is negative and (25) itself breaks down rather than delivering $P_D=1$).
- **Optimized distances (eqs. (27)–(28))**: $l'_1,l'_2$ = (22)–(23) with $R_{dep}\to R^*_{dep}$.

### 5.5 Lemma 3 — implicit critical angles (pp. 8–9, eqs. (29)–(33))

- **Statement**: setting $l'_1=l'_D$ (resp. $l'_2=l'_D$) and solving for the unknown crossing offset $a_i$ yields eqs. (29)/(30); $\Delta\theta_l=\arctan\!\big(a_1/(r_0+\rho_T/2)\big)$ (31, second branch), $\Delta\theta_r=\arctan\!\big(a_2/(r_0+\rho_T/2)\big)$ (32, second branch). Auxiliary definitions (verified on render): $b_i=\sqrt{a_i^2+(r_0+\rho_T/2)^2}$, $c_i=(l_o/2-a_i)$, $i=1,2$; "$a_1$, $a_2$ are the solutions of (29), (30), respectively".
- **Eq. (29) as printed**: $l'_1=l'_D=\frac{x_1+x_2+x_3}{\nu}=\Big(x_1+\rho_I+\sqrt{(r_0+\frac{\rho_T}{2})^2+(\frac{l_o}{2})^2}-r_0+\frac{\rho_I}{1+\nu}\Big)/\nu=\Big(\rho_I+\sqrt{(r_0+\frac{\rho_T}{2})^2+(\frac{l_o}{2})^2}-r_0+\frac{\rho_I}{1+\nu}+r_0+\rho_T-b_1-\frac{a_1c_1}{b_1}+\sqrt{\frac{a_1^2c_1^2}{b_1^2}-c_1^2+\rho_I^2}\Big)/\nu$; eq. (30) identical with index 2.
- **Verified reconstruction of the last expression** (the proof says only "combined with the Cosine Theorem, it is actually solving for $x_1$"): let $P$ be the crossing point of the intruder's radial ray with the obstacle line, at tangential offset $a_1$ from the midpoint ($|OP|=b_1$), $E$ the sensed endpoint ($|PE|=c_1$), $S$ the sensing point on ray $OP$ ($|OS|=r_{\text{sense}}$, $|SE|=\rho_I$). Law of cosines in $\triangle SPE$ with $\cos\angle(SPE)=-a_1/b_1$ gives $|PS|=-\frac{a_1c_1}{b_1}+\sqrt{\frac{a_1^2c_1^2}{b_1^2}-c_1^2+\rho_I^2}$, so $x_1=r_0+\rho_T-r_{\text{sense}}=r_0+\rho_T-b_1-\frac{a_1c_1}{b_1}+\sqrt{\frac{a_1^2c_1^2}{b_1^2}-c_1^2+\rho_I^2}$ — exactly the printed expression. The discriminant is $\rho_I^2-(\text{perpendicular distance from }E\text{ to the ray})^2\ge 0$, i.e. the endpoint is sensed at all.
- **Sign inconsistency (proof gap)**: (29)/(30) print "+$\frac{\rho_I}{1+\nu}$" where (21) requires "$-\frac{\nu\rho_I}{1+\nu}$" ($x_3$ contains $-\nu\rho_I/(1+\nu)$, p. 7). As printed, (29)/(30) contradict (21); the correct form is $\big(\rho_I+\sqrt{(\cdot)}-r_0-\frac{\nu\rho_I}{1+\nu}+\ldots\big)/\nu$.
- **Case structure (eqs. (31)–(33))**, exactly as printed: (31) $\Delta\theta_l=0$ if $l'_D>l'_1,\ l'_D\le l'_2$; $\Delta\theta_l=\tan^{-1}(a_1/(r_0+\rho_T/2))$ if $l'_2<l'_D\le l'_1$. (32) (when $\theta_o-\Delta\theta_r>\Theta(R^*_{dep})$): $\Delta\theta_r=0$ if $l'_D>l'_1$; $\Delta\theta_r=\tan^{-1}(a_2/(r_0+\rho_T/2))$ if $l'_D\le l'_1$. (33) (otherwise): $\Delta\theta_r=0$ if $l'_D>l'_1$; $\Delta\theta_r=\theta_o-\Theta(R^*_{dep})$ if $l'_D\le l'_1$. *Proof gaps*: (i) under the paper's own claim $l'_1<l'_2$ the interval $l'_2<l'_D\le l'_1$ in (31) is empty, so Lemma 3 would always give $\Delta\theta_l=0$ — the intended partition evidently assumes the opposite ordering (which holds for $\theta_o>0$, as in the example); (ii) (32)/(33) gate on $l'_1$ while the parallel Lemma 4 equations (37)/(38) gate on $l'_2$, with no explanation; (iii) the condition $\theta_o-\Delta\theta_r>\Theta(R^*_{dep})$ and the fallback value $\theta_o-\Theta(R^*_{dep})$ appear without derivation; (iv) no uniqueness/existence analysis of the quadratic in $a_i$.

### 5.6 Lemma 4 — analytic critical angle (p. 9, eqs. (34)–(38))

- **Eq. (34)**: $x_1=\frac{\rho_T-\sqrt{4\rho_I^2-l_o^2}}{2}$. *Independently re-derived and verified*: this is the **vertical-incidence** value ($\theta_{\text{asym}}=\theta_o$): the intruder at radius $r$ on the ray $\theta_o$ is at distance $\sqrt{(r-(r_0+\rho_T/2))^2+(l_o/2)^2}$ from either endpoint (the endpoints' projection onto the ray is the tangent midpoint), so both endpoints are sensed at $r=(r_0+\rho_T/2)+\sqrt{\rho_I^2-l_o^2/4}$, and $x_1=(r_0+\rho_T)-r=\frac{\rho_T}{2}-\sqrt{\rho_I^2-l_o^2/4}$ = (34). Positivity uses Assumption 1.(2)–(3) ($\sqrt{\rho_I^2-l_o^2/4}<\rho_I<\rho_T/2$). Despite this, the Lemma 4 header says "as described in Section 3 about **beyond incidence**" and contains the dangling text "can be obtained space.This time" (verbatim typo, p. 8–9) — the incidence-case labeling of Lemmas 3 vs. 4 is muddled.
- **Eq. (35) as printed**: $l'_D=\frac{\big[2(\rho_T+\rho_I-r_0)-\sqrt{4\rho_I^2-l_o^2}\big]\sin\beta+l_o}{\nu}-\frac{\rho_I}{1+\nu}$. **Inconsistent with (21)+(34)**: substituting (34) into (21) gives $\big[\rho_T/2+\rho_I-r_0+\frac{l_o}{2\sin\beta}-\sqrt{\rho_I^2-l_o^2/4}\big]/\nu-\frac{\rho_I}{1+\nu}=\frac{\big[(\rho_T+2\rho_I-2r_0-\sqrt{4\rho_I^2-l_o^2})\sin\beta+l_o\big]}{2\nu\sin\beta}-\frac{\rho_I}{1+\nu}$. Numerically with the paper's own simulation parameters ($r_0=1,\nu=0.85,\rho_T=0.6,\rho_I=0.1,l_o=0.15$, $\beta=0.0576$): (21)+(34) gives $l'_D\approx0.6942$, printed (35) gives $\approx0.0728$ — an order-of-magnitude discrepancy. The printed (35) appears to lack a $2\sin\beta$ divisor and to carry $2(\rho_T+\rho_I-r_0)$ where $(\rho_T+2\rho_I-2r_0)$ is required. The subsequent inequalities still "work" qualitatively because only the comparison $l'_D\lessgtr l'_i$ matters, but the reported closed form cannot be correct as stated.
- **Eqs. (36)–(38)**: $\Delta\theta_l=\beta$ if $l'_D\le l'_1$ else 0 (36); when $\theta_o-\Delta\theta_r>\Theta(R^*_{dep})$: $\Delta\theta_r=\beta$ if $l'_D\le l'_2$ else 0 (37); otherwise $\Delta\theta_r=\theta_o-\Theta(R^*_{dep})$ if $l'_D\le l'_2$ else 0 (38). (I.e., if the defender can make it even in the best detour case, the whole obstacle arc $\beta$ converts to guaranteed capture; if not, only the sliver between $\Theta(R^*_{dep})$ and $\theta_o$ counts on that side.) No proofs beyond reference to the geometry of Figs. 4–5.

### 5.7 Theorem 1 and remarks (p. 9, eqs. (39)–(40))

- **Theorem 1 (eq. (39))**: "Due to the presence of the obstacle, there is a probability of winning outside the maximum defensive angle": $P'_D=\frac{2\Theta(R^*_{dep})+\Delta\theta_l+\Delta\theta_r}{2\pi}$, with $\Delta\theta_{l,r}$ from Lemmas 3–4. *Proof (one line)*: "The values of $\Delta\theta_l$ and $\Delta\theta_r$ in different cases can be solved by Lemmas 3 and 4, and $P'_D$ degenerates to $P_D$ when $l'_2<l'_D$, with the same conclusion as in the work [10]." The counting logic: the no-obstacle guaranteed arc is $\{|\theta|\le\Theta\}$ (length $2\Theta$ out of $2\pi$), and obstacle-induced guaranteed arcs of length $\Delta\theta_l,\Delta\theta_r$ adjoin the obstacle's own angular interval $[\theta_o-\beta,\theta_o+\beta]$. Global, analytic, but a *lower bound* on the true win probability by construction (only the guaranteed-capture set is counted). Since $\Delta\theta_{l,r}\le\beta$ and Assumption 1 forces $\beta<\arctan(\rho_I/(r_0+\rho_T/2))$ with $\rho_I<\rho_T/2$, the structural gain is tiny: for the paper's parameters $\beta/\pi\approx1.8\%$ — the paper itself concedes "the probability of the intruder encountering the obstacle during the intrusion is extremely low, and the assumed probability of vertical incidence is close to zero" (p. 7).
- **Remark 2 (eq. (40))**: if $|\theta_o|=\Theta(R^*_{dep})+\beta$, two special positions make the gain contiguous with the guaranteed cone: $P''_D\ge\frac{\Theta(R^*_{dep})+\beta}{\pi}$ (Fig. 6).
- **Remark 3**: the maximum dominance angle "is not always continuous"; the gain is continuous with $\Theta(R^*_{dep})$ if $\theta_o-\Delta\theta_r\le\Theta(R^*_{dep})$ in Lemmas 2–3 or in the case of Remark 2. (Note: Lemma 2 here should presumably read Lemma 3; another cross-reference slip.)
- **Remark 4**: if the intruder escapes beyond the guaranteed angle and encounters the obstacle, it can remember the (static) obstacle and "definitely bypass the obstacle in the next invasion"; "Therefore, when (14) is not satisfied, the intruder can always win, even in an environment with an obstacle." This is the paper's only repeated-game/learning statement and it is qualitative.

### 5.8 Barrier / winning-region reading

There is no barrier in the HJI sense; the "winning region" is angular: the defender's guaranteed-capture set of intrusion angles $|\theta(t_{\text{asym}})|\le\Theta(R_{dep})$ (Lemma 2, eq. (20)), enlarged near the obstacle by $[\theta_o-\Delta\theta_l,\theta_o+\Delta\theta_r]$ (Theorem 1). The Apollonius circle plays the role of the barrier's level set: its non-intersection with target and TSR (eqs. (10)–(11)) is the capture certificate, tangency to the target at $t_{\text{eng}}$ defines the critical configuration $(x_D^*,x_I^*)$ (eqs. (16)–(17)), and the diameter test $2r_A\lessgtr\rho_T$ (eqs. (12)/(14)) separates the parameter regimes where capture can/cannot be forced.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues

**Author-stated**: parameter space restricted by Assumption 1, "consideration of the entire parameter space will be beyond the scope of this paper" (p. 3); two-player only — future work: relax speed ratio and sensing range, cooperative multiplayer games, "games with uncertainty where game fairness cannot be guaranteed in two-player games" (pp. 10–11); the obstacle's influence on the optimal deployment radius is dismissed as "probably small" without analysis (p. 8).

**Critical observations (with loci)**:
1. **Example violates the capture criterion (14)**: $2r_A=2\nu\rho_I/(1-\nu^2)\approx0.6126>\rho_T=0.6$ at the printed parameters, so Corollary 2 does not apply and Remark 4 asserts the intruder "can always win" — yet Fig. 8d shows the defender capturing. Either Remark 4's categorical claim or the illustrative example is misleading; the tension is never discussed.
2. **Eq. (35) is numerically inconsistent with (21)+(34)** (0.073 vs 0.694 on the paper's own parameters); correct closed form requires division by $2\sin\beta$ and $(\rho_T+2\rho_I-2r_0)$ instead of $2(\rho_T+\rho_I-r_0)$ (p. 9).
3. **Eqs. (29)–(30) carry "+$\rho_I/(1+\nu)$" where (21) requires "$-\nu\rho_I/(1+\nu)$"** (p. 8 vs p. 7).
4. **Case logic of (31) is degenerate** under the paper's own $l'_1<l'_2$ claim (empty second branch); and the claim $l_1<l_2$ itself holds only for $\theta_o\in(-\pi,0)$, while the example uses $\theta_o=0.78>0$ where $l'_1>l'_2$ (p. 7–8).
5. **Lemma 1 is unproven** and its obstacle-exemption rests on an asserted co-linearity of escape trajectories (p. 4); Corollary 1's proof mis-cites "(10)" for the definition of $r_A$ (eq. (7)) (p. 5); the $P_D<0.5$ claim is attributed to Assumption 1.3 but follows from 1.1 (p. 8).
6. **The winning probability is a lower bound over an assumed intruder behavior**: the intruder is restricted to radial-plus-endpoint-detour paths by assumption (any deviation "mak[es] the time to move towards the target increase", p. 5 — plausible but unproven, e.g. a sensing-maximizing slaloming intruder is not analyzed); $\theta(t_{\text{asym}})\sim\text{Unif}[-\pi,\pi]$ is a modeling choice.
7. **Structurally small effect**: under Assumption 1 the gain $\Delta\theta_l+\Delta\theta_r\le2\beta$ with $\beta=\arctan((l_o/2)/(r_0+\rho_T/2))<\arctan(\rho_I/(r_0+\rho_T/2))$, i.e. at most ~1–2% of probability for the paper's own regime — the paper admits the encounter probability is "extremely low" (p. 7) without confronting what this means for the contribution's practical weight.
8. **Announced Monte Carlo verification (0.1° over the full circle) is never reported** (p. 9); the example's operating point is within ~0.2% of the guarantee boundary (§6), so the boundary's exact location is empirically untested.
9. Minor: TSR printed as $\mathcal{S}_{r_0+\rho_I}(0)\setminus\mathcal{S}_{r_0}(0)$ (p. 3, should be $\rho_T$); "the defender is waiting for the defender to invade" (p. 10); "can be obtained space.This time" (p. 8); Fig. 5 legend prints the intrusion angles as "$\Delta\theta_l+\theta_o$" / "$\Delta\theta_r-\theta_o$"; dynamics are never written as equations.

## 8. Citation Value

**Paragraph role**: P095 is the canonical "obstacle + partial information" point in the target-defense lineage Isaacs → [9] Bopardikar et al. (sensing limits, discrete time) → [10] Shishika et al. ICRA 2021 (partial-information target defense, three phases, $P_D=\Theta/\pi$) → this paper, which grafts a fixed tangent line-segment obstacle onto [10]'s framework and shows that the forced zig-zag detour enlarges the defender's guaranteed angular set by $\Delta\theta_l+\Delta\theta_r$ (Theorem 1). Its Apollonius-circle dominance analysis with obstacles links it to the Oyler et al. full-information obstacle line [5]. Value for the knowledge base: (a) a concrete template for how static obstacles enter dominance-region/winning-probability computations in sensing-limited games; (b) a cautionary example of geometry-driven probability bounds whose assumptions ((14)) the authors' own simulation violates; (c) the three-phase information decomposition as a reusable modeling pattern for asymmetric-information pursuit-evasion.

**Suggested citation sentences**:
- "Beyond full-information obstacle games [Oyler et al.], Xu et al. embed a line segment obstacle in a partial-information target defense game and show that the resulting endpoint-detour enlarges the defender's guaranteed capture arc, raising the winning probability from $\Theta(R^*_{dep})/\pi$ to $(2\Theta(R^*_{dep})+\Delta\theta_l+\Delta\theta_r)/2\pi$."
- "The obstacle-aware deployment of [Xu et al. 2024] inherits the three-phase decomposition and the critical-moment construction of the partial-information target defense game [Shishika et al. 2021], quantifying the defender's angular gain via the incidence geometry of the obstacle ($\beta=\arctan((l_o/2)/(r_0+\rho_T/2))$)."
