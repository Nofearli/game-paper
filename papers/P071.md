## 2. Research Background

Guarding a target region from intruders is a canonical application of differential games: Isaacs [1] founded the two-player pursuit–evasion game (PEG), later extended with obstacles [2], sensing limitations [3], and visibility constraints [4]. Two branches are closest to this work: Target-Attacker-Defender (TAD) games [5], [6], where a target–defender team protects the former from an attacker, and reach-avoid games [7]–[9], where attackers try to reach a region while defenders prevent them. Perimeter-defense games combine both: intruders attempt to breach a target and defenders capture them before they reach the perimeter — with defenders either constrained to the perimeter [10], [11] or moving freely in the field [12]; the natural performance metric here is the *percentage of captured intruders* (vs. capture distance/fuel in other PEG variants [13]). Existing multi-intruder perimeter-defense works [14]–[17] allow simultaneous arrivals but hard-wire the intruder behavior: intruders fly straight at the target and never evade, and none of them gives the intruder a sensing capability. P071 builds directly on the partial-information single-intruder target-defense game of Shishika, Maity & Dorothy [12] (ICRA 2021) and on the "one Apollonius circle is enough" machinery of Dorothy et al. [18], extending both to a *sequence* of intruders in which the terminal configuration of each one-vs-one game becomes the initial configuration of the next — so both players choose strategies that are advantageous for the current *and* future games.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap

**Problem [authors' formulation, pp. 6594–6595]**: a defender protects the circular target $\mathcal R_T=\{x\in\mathbb R^2:\|x\|\le r_T\}$. The target has a sensing annulus (Target Sensing Region, TSR) of radius $\rho_T$; intruders appear **sequentially** on the TSR boundary (radius $r_T+\rho_T$), at an angle drawn i.i.d. uniform on $(-\pi,\pi]$ independent of previous arrivals, and exactly one intruder is active at any time: the next one appears only when the current one is captured or breaches. Dynamics (1): $\dot x_A=v_A\hat u(\psi_A)$, $\dot x_D=v_D\hat u(\psi_D)$, $|v_D|\le1$, $|v_A|\le\nu$, $\nu<1$ (defender faster; for $\nu\ge1$ "it is impossible for the defender to prevent any breaching and the percentage of capture will be zero", p. 6595). The intruder carries its own sensor of radius $\rho_A$ (senses the defender within $\rho_A$); the target acts as a team sensor: whenever the intruder is inside the TSR the defender has access to its instantaneous position $x_A(t)$. Each one-vs-one game decomposes into a **partial-information phase** (only the defender sees the intruder: $\|x_A\|<r_T+\rho_T$, $\|x_A-x_D\|>\rho_A$) and a **full-information phase** ($\|x_A-x_D\|\le\rho_A$). Outcomes: *breach* (intruder reaches $\|x\|=r_T$) or *capture*; escape from the TSR is excluded by the parametric assumption (2). Objective: maximize/minimize the expected percentage of captured intruders over finite and infinite arrival sequences.

**Gap [authors' claim, p. 6594]**: (i) [12] handles only a single intruder, so nothing is said about how the terminal state of one game degrades/conditions the next; (ii) in the simultaneous-arrival perimeter-defense works [14]–[17] "the strategies for the intruders are simple and fixed a priori … the intruders come directly at the target and they do not try to evade while being captured. Furthermore, these works do not consider sensing capability for the intruders."

**Verified from the cited corpus**: [verified] [12] (Shishika/Maity/Dorothy, ICRA 2021, "Partial information target defense game") is indeed the single-intruder predecessor with the same TSR/$\rho_A$ sensing structure; [verified] [18] (Dorothy et al., arXiv:2111.09205) is the source of Lemma 1 (capture arbitrarily close to the Apollonius circle), cited explicitly as "[18, Theorem 1]". [verified] The simultaneous-arrival line [14]–[17] (Adler et al. heterogeneity; Bajaj et al. conical environments; Macharet et al. adaptive partitioning; Bajaj & Bopardikar radial boundary guarding) matches the "fixed straight-line intruder" description as characterized. [authors' claim — plausible, unproven here] that the evasive capability qualitatively changes the game: "the sensing capability results in a particular type of evasive maneuver where the intruders can force the defender to pursue them and thus the defender ends up capturing the intruders at locations which are advantageous for the next intruders" (p. 6594) — this is precisely what Lemma 3 + the capture-circle construct formalize, and it "would not have been possible if $\rho_A=0$" (p. 6595).

## 4. Core Contributions

1. **Modeling**: formulation of a sensing-limited target-defense game against *sequentially arriving* intruders, where the terminal state of each one-vs-one game (defender on the capture circle or at the target center) is the initial state of the next; intruders are equipped with a limited-range sensor ($\rho_A$) enabling evasive maneuver (p. 6595, contribution (i)).
2. **Theory — engagement surface and capture circle**: equilibrium strategy analysis via two new geometric constructs. The *engagement surface* $\mathcal S_{\text{engage}}$ (Definition 1, eq. (7)) is the set of (engagement time, engagement angle) pairs for which the Apollonius circle is exactly tangent to the target boundary — the favorable configurations to start the full-information phase; the *capture circle* (radius $r_T+2\gamma\rho_A$, concentric with the target) is the set of all possible capture locations under equilibrium play (contribution (ii), p. 6595).
3. **Theory — capture statistics**: analytic computation of the expected capture percentage for both finite ($N$) and infinite arrival sequences, via a renewal decomposition into "travels" (geometric travel lengths, negative-binomial reset counts) — eqs. (10)–(14) (contribution (iii)).
4. **Algorithm**: Algorithm 1 (p. 6599) — a closed-form defender state machine over the two states {target center, capture circle} that decides engage-vs-return per arrival.
5. **Experiment — validation**: Monte-Carlo validation (100 trials × 200 arrivals) of the theoretical capture percentage (13)/(14) (contribution (iv)).
6. **Experiment — parametric characterization**: sensitivity of the capture percentage to $(\nu,\rho_A,\rho_T)$ via level-set plots (Figs. 6–7), with the unexpected empirical finding of *linear* level sets in the $\rho_A$–$\rho_T$ plane (contribution (v)).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results (exhaustive)

### 5.0 Model, information structure, termination (pp. 6595–6596)

- **Dynamics (1)**: $\dot x_A=v_A\hat u(\psi_A)$, $\dot x_D=v_D\hat u(\psi_D)$ — first-order (single-integrator) agents directly controlling speed and heading; $\hat u(\theta)=[\cos\theta,\sin\theta]^\top$; $|v_D|\le1$, $|v_A|\le\nu$, $\nu<1$ enforced (trivial outcome otherwise).
- **Information structure**: two phases defined by sensing. *Full information*: $\|x_A(t)\|<r_T+\rho_T$ **and** $\|x_A(t)-x_D(t)\|\le\rho_A$ (both agents see each other). *Partial/asymmetric information*: $\|x_A(t)\|<r_T+\rho_T$ and $\|x_A(t)-x_D(t)\|>\rho_A$ — the defender (through the target-as-sensor TSR) sees the intruder, the intruder does not see the defender. In the partial-information phase the intruder is *prescribed* to move radially toward the target center at maximum speed until first sensing the defender; footnote 1 (p. 6596) admits this is an assumption: deviating only lengthens the time spent inside the TSR without knowing which direction is beneficial, "a formal proof … is beyond the scope of this paper".
- **Arrival process (the sequentially-arriving modeling)**: exactly one intruder active at any time; arrivals at the TSR boundary with i.i.d. uniform angle; the next intruder appears **immediately** when the current one is captured or breaches; no inter-arrival queue or waiting times are modeled — the "next game" starts as soon as the current ends, and the only cross-game coupling is the defender's position. Because of random arrival angles, the number of captures is a random variable; the goal is the expected capture percentage.
- **Termination/winning**: breach = intruder reaches $\|x\|=r_T$; capture = defender catches intruder (point capture). Escape from the TSR is ruled out by assumption (2) (a large-enough TSR so an intruder without a breaching strategy cannot get out uncaptured) — hence each one-vs-one game terminates in breach or capture, never in a deadlock (p. 6595).
- **Parametric assumption (2)** (p. 6595, verified from rendered p. 6596):
$$\max\Big\{\big(1+\tfrac{2\nu}{1-\nu^2}\big)\rho_A,\ \ \nu r_T+\tfrac{2\rho_A\nu^2}{1-\nu^2}\Big\}\le\rho_T .$$
First part, $(1+2\nu/(1-\nu^2))\rho_A\le\rho_T$: necessary so the defender has a strategy to capture *inside* the TSR (otherwise the intruder evades out of the TSR repeatedly — deadlock, no further arrivals). Second part, $\nu r_T+2\rho_A\nu^2/(1-\nu^2)\le\rho_T$: ensures that if the defender loses the current game it has enough time to reach the target center from the capture circle before the next game starts. [My algebra ✔] Since $\gamma=\nu/(1-\nu^2)$, the two parts are equivalent to $\rho_T\ge\rho_A(1+2\gamma)$ and $\rho_T/\nu\ge r_T+2\gamma\rho_A$ — exactly the two inequalities invoked in Remark 1 (p. 6598).

### 5.1 Apollonius circle machinery — Lemma 1, eq. (3), Lemma 2, Corollary 1 (p. 6596)

- **Apollonius circle (3)**: the locus $\|x-x_A(t)\|=\nu\|x-x_D(t)\|$ is, for $\nu<1$, a circle with
$$x_C(t)=\alpha x_A(t)-\beta x_D(t),\qquad r_C(t)=\gamma\|x_A(t)-x_D(t)\|,$$
where $\alpha=(1-\nu^2)^{-1}$, $\gamma=\nu\alpha$, $\beta=\nu\gamma$. Its interior is the intruder's *dominance region* (points the intruder reaches before capture); both agents reach any boundary point simultaneously. **Lemma 1 ([18, Theorem 1])**: "Regardless of the strategy of the intruder, the defender has a strategy to capture the intruder arbitrarily close to the Apollonius circle" — i.e., everything outside the AC is the defender's dominance region. [My algebra ✔] $\alpha-\beta=1$, so at engagement (4) the center reduces to $x_C=x_A-\beta\rho_A\hat u(\theta)$ — the identity used throughout Section III and Lemma 3.
- **Lemma 2 (p. 6596; proof omitted for space)**: a defender located at $r\hat u(\theta_D)$ with $r\le\tilde\rho_T\triangleq\rho_T+r_T$ can capture any intruder incoming at angle $\theta_A$ with
$$|\theta_A-\theta_D|\le\begin{cases}\Theta_g & \text{if } r>\dfrac{\rho_T}{\nu}-r_T,\\[2pt] \pi & \text{otherwise,}\end{cases}\qquad
\Theta_g=2\cos^{-1}\!\left(\sqrt{\dfrac{\big((r_T+\nu r)^2-(\tilde\rho_T-\nu r_T)^2\big)\big((\tilde\rho_T+\nu r_T)^2-(\nu r-r_T)^2\big)}{16\nu^2 r_T^2\, r\, \tilde\rho_T}}\right)$$
(formula verified character-by-character against a 400-dpi render of p. 6596). Meaning: a defender within $\rho_T/\nu-r_T$ of the target center guards the **full circle** (any arrival is capturable); farther out it guards only an arc of width $2\Theta_g$. [Proof gap: the derivation is not in the paper; continuity of $\Theta_g\to\pi$ at $r=\rho_T/\nu-r_T$ is not exhibited either.]
- **Corollary 1**: $\Theta_g$ is a decreasing function of the defender's radial position $r$ (proof omitted). Consequence used everywhere: *the defender wants to be as close to the target center as possible* — both when choosing where to capture the current intruder and when repositioning after a loss.

### 5.2 Partial-information phase — engagement surface (Definition 1, eqs. (4)–(7)) and capture circle (p. 6596–6597)

WLOG the intruder appears at time 0 at $[r_T+\rho_T,0]^\top$ and, during the partial-information phase, is at $x_A(\tau)=[r_T+\rho_T-\tau\nu,0]^\top$ when it first senses the defender at time $\tau$. The defender is then on the intruder's sensing boundary,
$$x_D(\tau)=x_A(\tau)+\rho_A\hat u(\theta)\qquad(4),$$
so by (3) the AC has center $x_C(\tau)=x_A(\tau)-\beta\rho_A\hat u(\theta)$ and radius $\gamma\rho_A$. **Capture guarantee (5)**: the AC must lie inside the TSR without touching the target interior:
$$\|x_C(\tau)\|\ge r_T+\gamma\rho_A\ (5a),\qquad \|x_C(\tau)\|\le r_T+\rho_T-\gamma\rho_A\ (5b),$$
which — by the AC properties and Lemma 1 — is necessary **and sufficient** for capture (Fig. 2 of the paper shows the breach vs. capture configurations).
- **Optimality of tangency (6)**: among all $(\tau,\theta)$ satisfying (5), the defender should pick the one pulling the AC as close to the target center as possible (Corollary 1 ⇒ maximal guarding angle for the *next* game), i.e. tangent to the target:
$$\|x_C(\tau)\|=r_T+\gamma\rho_A\qquad(6),$$
which, due to (2), implies both (5a) and (5b). Eq. (6) simplifies to the engagement-surface equation
$$\sin^2(\theta/2)=\frac{(r_T+\gamma\rho_A)^2-(r_T+\rho_T-\tau\nu-\beta\rho_A)^2}{4\beta\rho_A\,(r_T+\rho_T-\tau\nu)}\qquad(7).$$
[My re-derivation ✔: writing $a=r_T+\rho_T-\tau\nu$, $\|x_C\|^2=a^2-2a\beta\rho_A\cos\theta+\beta^2\rho_A^2$; setting $= (r_T+\gamma\rho_A)^2$ and using $\sin^2(\theta/2)=(1-\cos\theta)/2$ gives exactly (7).]
- **Definition 1 (Engagement Surface)**: $\mathcal S_{\text{engage}}=\{(\theta_{eng},\tau_{eng}): \theta_{eng},\tau_{eng} \text{ satisfy } (7)\}$ — the locus of defender engagement points $x_A(\tau_{eng})+\rho_A\hat u(\theta_{eng})$ (plotted in Fig. 3). If the full-information phase starts on $\mathcal S_{\text{engage}}$, (2) + Lemma 1 imply the intruder cannot breach or escape the TSR before capture.
- **Capture circle**: since the intruder (once doomed) wants to be captured *farthest* from the target center (maximizing the next intruder's breach probability via Corollary 1), and the farthest point of a tangent AC from the center is at distance $\|x_C\|+\gamma\rho_A$, **all captures occur on the circle of radius $r_T+2\gamma\rho_A$ concentric with the target** — the *capture circle*. This is the paper's central cross-game coupling device: capture location = next game's initial condition.

### 5.3 Reachability of the engagement surface — Theorem 1 (eq. (8)), Theorem 2, Remark 1, Proposition 1 (pp. 6597–6598)

- **Theorem 1 (Necessary)**: a defender at $r\hat u(\theta_D)$ (intruder arrival at $[r_T+\rho_T,0]^\top$, so $\theta_D$ is the angular separation) can reach $(\tau_{eng},\theta_{eng})\in\mathcal S_{\text{engage}}$ before being detected **only if** $\theta_D\le\theta_{\max}(\tau_{eng},\theta_{eng},r)$, where
$$\theta_{\max}(\tau_{eng},\theta_{eng},r)=\cos^{-1}\!\Big(\frac{r_{eng}^2+r^2-\tau_{eng}^2}{2r_{eng}r}\Big)+\phi_{eng}\qquad(8),$$
$$\phi_{eng}=\sin^{-1}\!\Big(\frac{\rho_A\sin(\theta_{eng})}{r_{eng}}\Big),\qquad
r_{eng}=\Big((r_T+\rho_T-\tau_{eng}\nu-\rho_A)^2+4(r_T+\rho_T-\tau_{eng}\nu)\rho_A\cos^2(\theta_{eng}/2)\Big)^{1/2}.$$
*Proof approach (omitted; structure reconstructed)*: law of cosines in the triangle (target center $O$, defender initial position at radius $r$, engagement point at radius $r_{eng}$): the defender must cover path length $\le\tau_{eng}$ (unit speed over the engagement time), giving the $\cos^{-1}$ term; $\phi_{eng}$ is the angular offset between the intruder's radial line and the true engagement point $x_{eng}=x_A(\tau_{eng})+\rho_A\hat u(\theta_{eng})$. [My verification ✔] $r_{eng}=\|x_{eng}\|$: expanding with $4\cos^2(\theta/2)=2(1+\cos\theta)$ gives $r_{eng}^2=a^2+\rho_A^2+2a\rho_A\cos\theta_{eng}=\|a\hat x+\rho_A\hat u(\theta_{eng})\|^2$ with $a=r_T+\rho_T-\tau_{eng}\nu$. Since many engagement pairs exist, $(\tau_{eng},\theta_{eng})$ is henceforth chosen to **maximize** $\theta_{\max}(\tau_{eng},\theta_{eng})$ — no closed-form maximizer is derived (chosen/verified numerically). [Proof gap: proof of Theorem 1 omitted due to space limitation.]
- **Theorem 2 (Sufficient)**: if $r\le r_T+\rho_T-\rho_A$, then $\theta_D\le\theta_{\max}(\tau_{eng},\theta_{eng})$ is **also sufficient** — the defender reaches the engagement surface without being detected en route (rules out the premature-detection scenario of Fig. 4, where a defender cutting the chord gets sensed before reaching $x_{eng}$). [Proof gap: proof omitted due to space limitation.]
- **Remark 1 (the induction that makes the whole analysis close)**: if the defender starts the game on the capture circle, $r=r_T+2\gamma\rho_A<r_T+\rho_T-\rho_A$ by (2), so Theorem 2's radial hypothesis holds for **every** game in the sequence. Then: (a) $\theta_D\le\theta_{\max}$ ⇒ the defender reaches the engagement surface and the intruder is captured on the capture circle — so the hypothesis persists for the next game; (b) $\theta_D>\theta_{\max}$ ⇒ the defender loses the current game and must return to the target center, which guarantees winning the next game with probability 1 (Lemma 2 with $r=0$: full $\pi$ guarding angle); (2) ensures $\rho_T/\nu>r_T+2\gamma\rho_A$, i.e. the defender gets back to the center before the next game starts.
- **Proposition 1 (Defender Strategy, partial-information phase)**: if $\theta_D>\theta_{\max}(\tau_{eng},\theta_{eng})$ the optimal strategy is to go to the **target center**; otherwise go to $(\tau_{eng},\theta_{eng})\in\mathcal S_{\text{engage}}$ and start the full-information phase. *Proof (printed, in brief)*: when unreachable, attempting to engage ends with the AC intersecting the target ⇒ breach anyway, and the terminal defender position is at non-zero radius, degrading the next game (Corollary 1); the center maximizes the next game's win probability. When reachable, engaging is a win with probability 1 now plus a strictly positive capture probability next game, and "it can be verified (by considering the expected number of captures)" that engaging dominates repositioning. [Proof gap: the last verification is asserted, not derived; see §7.]

### 5.4 Full-information phase — Lemma 3, eq. (9) (p. 6598)

At engagement the intruder computes the instantaneous AC. If it intersects the target interior ⇒ breach (run to a breaching point, left panel of Fig. 2). Otherwise capture is inevitable and the intruder maximizes the next intruder's chance by being captured as far from the center as possible:
- **Lemma 3**: the doomed intruder runs in a straight line at maximum speed to
$$x_p= x_A(\tau_{eng})-\beta\rho_A\hat u(\theta_{eng})+\gamma\rho_A\hat u(\phi),\qquad
\phi=\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{\beta\rho_A\sin(\theta_{eng})}{\beta\rho_A\cos(\theta_{eng})-(r_T+\rho_T-\tau_{eng}\nu)}\right)\ (9).$$
*Proof (printed)*: at engagement $x_C(\tau_{eng})=x_A(\tau_{eng})-\beta\rho_A\hat u(\theta_{eng})=(r_T+\gamma\rho_A)\hat u(\phi)$ — the radial direction $\phi$ of the AC center follows from the tangency (6); the farthest AC point from the target center is $x_p=x_C+\gamma\rho_A\hat u(\phi)$. Hence the capture point lies at radial distance $r_T+2\gamma\rho_A$ (the capture circle) at angle $\phi$. [Observation: the printed proof establishes *where* the capture must occur given the engagement geometry, but the pursuit dynamics between engagement and capture — why the defender cannot force an earlier, closer capture, and how the AC stays tangent while the intruder runs — are delegated to Lemma 1/[18] and not written out; see §7.]

### 5.5 Whole-sequence analysis — Algorithm 1, eqs. (10)–(14) (pp. 6599–6600)

- **Algorithm 1 (Defender's Strategy, p. 6599)**: initialize $x_D=[0,0]^\top$, $N_{capture}\leftarrow0$; for $n=1:N$: draw $\theta_A\sim\mathcal U(-\pi,\pi)$. Two defender states: (i) **at target center**: engage at $x_{eng}\leftarrow(r_T-\rho_A/(1+\nu))\hat u(\theta_A)$ — capture happens — $N_{capture}\mathrel{+}=1$, and $x_D\leftarrow(r_T+2\gamma\rho_A)\hat u(\theta_A)$ (after capture, on the capture circle). (ii) **on the capture circle** at $(r_T+2\gamma\rho_A)\hat u(\theta_D)$: if $|\theta_D-\theta_A|\le\theta_{\max}$ — engage at $x_{eng}\leftarrow x_A(\tau_{eng})+\rho_A\hat u(\theta_{eng})$, capture, $N_{capture}\mathrel{+}=1$, $x_D\leftarrow x_p$ of Lemma 3 (still on the capture circle, angle $\phi$); else — breach — defender returns to the target center, $x_D\leftarrow[0,0]^\top$.
  [My independent verification ✔ of the center-start engagement point]: placing the defender at radius $r_T-\rho_A/(1+\nu)$ on the arrival radial, the radially inbound intruder senses it when the separation is $\rho_A$, i.e. at intruder radius $r_T+\nu\rho_A/(1+\nu)$; the resulting head-on AC has center radius $a+\beta\rho_A$ with $a=r_T+\nu\rho_A/(1+\nu)$, and $a+\beta\rho_A-\gamma\rho_A=r_T$ exactly — i.e., the AC is **exactly tangent** to the target, so (6) holds and capture at $(r_T+2\gamma\rho_A)\hat u(\theta_A)$ is guaranteed. Numerically with the base parameters ($r_T=5,\rho_A=1,\nu=0.8$): defender engages at $4.4444$, intruder at $5.4444$, AC center at $7.2222=r_T+\gamma\rho_A$, tangency at $r=5$, capture circle at $9.4444=r_T+2\gamma\rho_A$; also $(\theta,\tau)=(\pi,11.944)$ satisfies (7) with RHS $=1.0000$. The center-start line of Algorithm 1 is thus the $\theta_{eng}=\pi$ point of the engagement surface.
- **Per-game capture probability (10)**: from the target center capture is guaranteed for any $\theta_A$ (Lemma 2, $r=0$); from the capture circle only arrivals with $|\theta_D-\theta_A|\le\theta_{\max}$ are capturable, so under i.i.d. uniform arrivals
$$p^*\triangleq\frac{\theta_{\max}}{\pi}\qquad(10).$$
- **Renewal structure**: every breach (defender back to center) is a *reset*; the capture run between two resets is a *travel* of length $L_i$. Since each game is won independently with probability $p^*$ while on the capture circle (and the first game, from the center, is always won),
$$P(L_i=k)=(p^*)^{k-1}(1-p^*),\qquad k=1,2,\dots\qquad(11)$$
— $L_i$ is geometric (mean $1/(1-p^*)$); the number of captures over $m$ travels is negative binomial: $P(\sum_{i=1}^m L_i=n)=\binom{n-1}{m-1}(p^*)^{n-m}(1-p^*)^m$.
- **Reset count (12)**: with $\bar S_N$ the number of resets after $N$ games, $N\ge\sum_{i=1}^{\bar S_N}L_i+\bar S_N$ a.s., and using $\{\bar S_N\le m\}\Leftrightarrow\{\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}L_i+m\ge N\}$,
$$P(\bar S_N>m)=\sum_{j=m+1}^{N-m-1}\binom{j-1}{m}(p^*)^{j-m-1}(1-p^*)^{m+1}\qquad(12)$$
(the CDF of $\bar S_N$; negative binomial with $m+1$ "failures"). [Internal consistency ✔.]
- **Finite-horizon capture percentage (13)**: the capture fraction after $N$ games is $(N-\bar S_N)/N$, hence
$$\text{percentage}(N)=\frac{N-\mathbb E[\bar S_N]}{N}\times100\qquad(13)$$
with $\mathbb E[\bar S_N]$ obtained numerically from the CDF (12); no closed form for $\mathbb E[\bar S_N]$ is printed.
- **Infinite-horizon capture percentage (14)**: after the $k$-th travel the capture fraction is $c(k)=\sum_{i=1}^kL_i/(\sum_{i=1}^kL_i+k)$; by the (strong) law of large numbers $(1/k)\sum_i L_i\to\mathbb E[L_i]=1/(1-p^*)$ a.s., giving $c_\infty=1/(2-p^*)$ and, using (10),
$$\text{percentage}(\infty)=\frac{100}{2-p^*}=\frac{100\pi}{2\pi-\theta_{\max}}\qquad(14).$$
Physical meaning: even with infinitely many intruders the defender captures a strictly positive fraction — bounded below by 50% (as $p^*\to0$) and approaching 100% as $\theta_{\max}\to\pi$ — because every breach resets the defender to the invincible center configuration. *Character*: fully analytic once $\theta_{\max}$ (from (8), maximized over $\mathcal S_{\text{engage}}$) is available; global in the arrival sequence, conditional on the geometric machinery of §5.1–5.4 and assumption (2).

- **Scenario**: Monte-Carlo-type random trials of the full sequential game run exactly under Algorithm 1; intruders drawn uniform on the TSR boundary; the empirical capture percentage is compared against the analytic predictions (13)–(14).

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues

**Author-stated**:
1. The intruder's radial-motion prescription in the partial-information phase is assumed, not proven — footnote 1 (p. 6596): "A formal proof of the last statement is beyond the scope of this paper."
2. Parametric restriction (2) excludes part of the parameter space (deadlock/escape regimes; the blank top-right region of Fig. 7); the analysis is silent there.
3. Future work (Sec. VII, p. 6600): different arrival patterns (periodic, non-uniform arrival-location probabilities, multiple simultaneous arrivals); heterogeneous intruder teams (different speeds/sensing); a defender with its own sensing region (removing the target-as-sensor teaming).

**Critical observations (with page/eq.)**:
4. **All four core geometric results are stated without proof**: Lemma 2, Corollary 1, Theorem 1, Theorem 2 each carry "A proof … is omitted due to space limitation" (pp. 6596–6598). In particular $\theta_{\max}$ — the single quantity that determines $p^*$, (13) and (14) — is never derived inside this paper, and its maximizer over the engagement surface is defined implicitly ("we will denote $(\tau_{eng},\theta_{eng})$ to be the one that maximizes $\theta_{\max}$", p. 6597) with no closed form; reproducibility depends on a numerical optimization the paper does not specify.
5. **No formal equilibrium result**: despite the abstract's "we derive equilibrium strategies for both the players", no saddle-point/Nash theorem is stated or proven for the one-vs-one game, and none for the sequential game (no value function or dynamic program over the state {center} ∪ {capture circle × angle}); the strategies are justified by dominance-style arguments (Remark 1, Proposition 1). The optimality of *engaging* vs. *repositioning* in Proposition 1 rests on "it can be verified (by considering the expected number of captures)" (p. 6598) — asserted, not derived; the objective traded off is the expected percentage over the whole future, so the claim requires precisely the renewal accounting of Sec. V, which is done only afterwards and only for the resulting policy.
6. **Capture-location claim leans on [18]**: Lemma 3 fixes the intruder's straight-line target $x_p$ and the paper asserts "all the captures of the intruders will happen on this circle" (p. 6597), but the pursuit dynamics between engagement and capture (whether the defender can hold the initial AC tangent while the intruder darts to $x_p$, and why capture cannot be forced earlier/closer) are not written out — they are inherited from Lemma 1, i.e. [18, Theorem 1], whose hypotheses (e.g., capture "arbitrarily close to" the AC, not *on* it) are absorbed without an $\epsilon$-analysis.
7. **Between-game timing is implicit**: Algorithm 1 treats "defender goes to the target center / to $x_{eng}$" as instantaneous decisions validated by (2)'s second inequality; the model has no explicit inter-arrival gap, so if capture took longer than the next appearance the renewal structure would break — the feasibility is guaranteed only within regime (2).
8. **$\mathbb E[\bar S_N]$ has no printed closed form** — (13) is defined through it and evaluated numerically from (12); the finite-$N$ curves are therefore semi-analytic.
9. **Empirical breadth**: one base parameter set for the Monte-Carlo validation; no baseline policies, no $\rho_A=0$ ablation (the headline "evasive intruder changes the game" claim is supported by construction, not by comparison), no noise/estimation errors in the sensing model, platform unspecified.
10. **Minor**: Fig. 1's caption uses color-swatch glyphs where the target/TSR symbols would be (rendered p. 6595); the "percentage of capture" of the very first game is 1 by construction, which slightly inflates small-$N$ percentages (visible as the high start of the curves in Fig. 5).

## 8. Citation Value

**Paragraph role**: P071 is the reference point for *sequential (one-at-a-time) intruder target defense with cross-game state carry-over under partial information* — the direct extension of the partial-information TAD game [12] from a single intruder to an arrival process, and one of the few perimeter-defense papers where the intruder is strategic (sensing-enabled, evasive) rather than hard-wired straight-line (contrast [14]–[17]). Its durable technical assets are: (i) the **engagement surface** — the tangency locus (7) selecting the full-information-phase starting configuration that is simultaneously capture-guaranteeing and next-game-optimal; (ii) the **capture circle** $r_T+2\gamma\rho_A$ as the invariant set of terminal defender states that closes the induction across games; and (iii) the renewal/probabilistic layer — geometric travel lengths (11), negative-binomial reset counts (12), and the clean asymptotic capture percentage $100/(2-p^*)$ with $p^*=\theta_{max}/\pi$ (10), (14) — a template for turning geometric game solutions into mission-level statistics. It should be cited whenever a paper needs the "sequence of one-vs-one games with terminal-state inheritance" formulation, a tractable partial-information defense benchmark with closed-form performance, or the linear-level-set empirical law relating $\rho_A$ and $\rho_T$; it pairs naturally with [12] (predecessor) and [18] (Apollonius-circle machinery it leans on).

**Suggested citation sentences**:
- "Sequential target-defense games in which the terminal configuration of each one-vs-one encounter initializes the next admit equilibrium strategies built on an engagement surface and a capture circle, and yield a closed-form asymptotic capture percentage $100/(2-p^*)$ with per-game capture probability $p^*=\theta_{max}/\pi$ [Pourghorban et al., CDC 2022]."
- "Unlike simultaneous-arrival perimeter-defense formulations with fixed straight-line intruders [14]–[17], Pourghorban et al. (CDC 2022) show that equipping sequentially arriving intruders with a limited-range sensor lets each doomed intruder steer its own capture location, shaping the defender's initial condition — and hence the breach probability — for every subsequent game."
