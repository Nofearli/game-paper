## 2. Research Background
Pursuit-evasion (PE) problems formalize applications in surveillance, navigation, biological behavior analysis, and combat operations (p. 1049). The common engineering practice of imposing assumed adversary behaviors [refs [1], [2] in the paper] is fragile against an intelligent opponent that does not abide by a restricted action set; this motivated differential game theory [Isaacs, ref [3]], in which saddle-point strategies guarantee performance "regardless of the actual strategies implemented by the adversary" (p. 1049). The paper positions itself as an organized introduction to two-team PE differential games with emphasis on multi-player games (three or more players), where team cooperation is "implicitly required" — theoretically because the saddle-point solution of a multi-player game "requires the strictest level of cooperation," and practically because cooperation improves team performance (p. 1049). The historical backdrop (Section II, pp. 1049–1050) credits Isaacs (the "father of Differential Games"), Bellman (dynamic programming yielding state-feedback strategies directly), Breakwell, and Pontryagin (Minimum Principle; the paper deliberately calls it Pontryagin's Minimum Principle, PMP), plus RAND scientists (Berkovitz, Blackwell, Dresher, Fleming, Nash) and Soviet schools (Krasovskii, Melikyan, Subbotin), and recalls the First International Conference on the Theory and Applications of Differential Games (Amherst, MA, Sept. 29–Oct. 1, 1969, organized by Ho and Leitmann).

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
This is a tutorial/survey paper, not a research contribution in the usual sense. **What it surveys**: the PE differential-game literature organized by a taxonomy on player counts (Section III, pp. 1050–1053): (A) 1 pursuer–1 evader (Homicidal Chauffeur, Two Cars, constrained environments, incomplete information, aerial engagements, other); (B) N pursuers–1 evader; (C) 1 pursuer–M evaders; (D) N pursuers–M evaders. **What it adds beyond surveying**: (i) a complete, self-contained proof of the verification theorem (HJI sufficiency) applied to Isaacs' two-cutters and fugitive ship game, extending Garcia et al. [60] "by offering a complete proof of the verification theorem … it also identifies the game's singular surface which is of dispersal type, and it illustrates the applicability of the solution … to … multiple pursuers and multiple evaders" [authors' claim, p. 1054]; and (ii) a distilled treatment of the Active Target Defense Differential Game (ATDDG) game-of-kind/game-of-degree solution, summarizing [111] (p. 1061). **Gap addressed** [authors' framing]: no single organized entry point existed that connects Isaacs' seminal constructs (barrier, universal, equivocal, dispersal surfaces) to recent multi-player results and demonstrates on two fully worked games both "the tools available to synthesize and verify optimal strategies" (PMP, HJI verification, Apollonius-circle geometry) and the cooperation aspect (p. 1049). Claims in Sections II–III are literature summaries [verified as faithful one-to-two-sentence digestions of the cited titles]; the case-study mathematics in Sections IV–V is reproduced and re-derived by the authors themselves and was verified line-by-line against the rendered PDF for this archive.

## 5. Key Mathematical Results (MOST IMPORTANT)

**Setup and information structure.** Both case studies use Isaacs' simple motion (holonomic, constant speed, heading-only controls), full-state information (perfect information games; the incomplete-information literature is only surveyed), zero-sum payoff, and point capture. General verification framework: dynamics `xdot = f(t, x(t), u(t), v(t))` (eq. (1)), payoff `J = q(x(tf)) + ∫_0^{tf} g(t, x(t), u(t), v(t)) dt` (eq. (2)), state feedback `u(t)=mu(t,x(t))`, `v(t)=nu(t,x(t))`.

**Theorem 1 (p. 1054, eq. (3); cited to [88] — Hespanha's book).** Assume there exists a C¹ function `V(t, x(t))` satisfying the HJI PDE
`-∂V/∂t = ∂V/∂x · f(t, x, u*, v*) + g(t, x, u*, v*)` (3), with `V(tf, x) = q(x)`.
Then `u* = mu*(t, x(t))`, `v* = nu*(t, x(t))` is a saddle-point equilibrium in state feedback policies. No proof given (cited). Meaning: the HJI equation is a *sufficient* condition for a (Nash/saddle) equilibrium; the authors deliberately use it for *verification* of a Value obtained geometrically, noting that ∇V replaces the PMP co-states so HJI could also synthesize strategies (p. 1054).

### 5.1 Two Cutters and Fugitive Ship (Two-Pursuer-One-Evader Differential Game, 2P1EDG), Sec. IV, pp. 1054–1059

**Dynamics and termination.** Evader E and pursuers P1, P2 with simple motion and constant speeds `v_E, v_{P1}, v_{P2}`; normalized speeds `beta_1 = v_{P1}/v_E > 1`, `beta_2 = v_{P2}/v_E > 1` (not necessarily equal); state `x = (x_E, y_E, x_{P1}, y_{P1}, x_{P2}, y_{P2}) ∈ R^6`; controls `u_E = {phi}`, `u_P = {psi_1, psi_2}` (instantaneous headings). Dynamics (eq. (4)): `xdot_E = cos phi`, `ydot_E = sin phi`; `xdot_{Pi} = beta_i cos psi_i`, `ydot_{Pi} = beta_i sin psi_i`. Point capture terminates the game when (eq. (5)) a) `x_{P1}=x_E, y_{P1}=y_E`, or b) `x_{P2}=x_E, y_{P2}=y_E`, or c) both. Payoff (eq. (6)): `min_{psi_1,psi_2} max_phi ∫_0^{tf} dt` (capture time).

**Hamiltonian and co-states.** `H = 1 + lambda_{x_E} cos phi + lambda_{y_E} sin phi + beta_1 lambda_{x_{P1}} cos psi_1 + beta_1 lambda_{y_{P1}} sin psi_1 + beta_2 lambda_{x_{P2}} cos psi_2 + beta_2 lambda_{y_{P2}} sin psi_2` (eq. (7)); Isaacs' Main Equation `min_{psi_1,psi_2} max_phi H ≡ 0` (eq. (8)). Optimal controls (eqs. (9)–(11)): `cos phi* = lambda_{x_E}/sqrt(lambda_{x_E}^2 + lambda_{y_E}^2)`, `sin phi* = lambda_{y_E}/sqrt(...)`; `cos psi_i* = -lambda_{x_{Pi}}/sqrt(lambda_{x_{Pi}}^2 + lambda_{y_{Pi}}^2)`, `sin psi_i* = -lambda_{y_{Pi}}/sqrt(...)` (pursuers anti-align with their co-states). Co-state dynamics `lambdadot = -∂H/∂x` give all constant co-states (p. 1055; **printed typo verified on the page image**: the chain reads `lambdadot_{x_E} = lambdadot_{y_E} = lambdadot_{x_{P1}} = lambdadot_{y_{P1}} = lambdadot_{x_{P2}} = lambdadot_{y_{P1}} = 0`, the last term should be `lambdadot_{y_{P2}}`), hence constant optimal headings and **straight-line optimal trajectories**.

**1v1 capture-time building block (eqs. (12)–(15), p. 1055).** For case a) capture by P1: `x_{P1f} = x_{P10} + beta_1 tf_1 cos psi_1* = x_{E0} + tf_1 cos phi* = x_{Ef}` and likewise in y (eq. (12)), giving
`tf_1 = c_1 cos(phi* - lambda_1) + sqrt( c_1^2 cos^2(phi* - lambda_1) + c_1 r_10 )` (13),
with `lambda_1 = arctan((y_{E0}-y_{P10})/(x_{E0}-x_{P10}))`, `r_10 = sqrt((x_{E0}-x_{P10})^2 + (y_{E0}-y_{P10})^2)`, `c_1 = (1/(beta_1^2 - 1)) r_10`. Case b) is identical with index 2 (eqs. (14)–(15)). These are the canonical Apollonius capture-time formulas for a faster pursuer vs. slower evader under simple motion. `tf_i = 0` only if `r_i = 0` (already captured).

**Evader's optimal heading (eqs. (16)–(17), p. 1055).** If condition a) is active, E solves `max_phi tf_1` s.t. eq. (12). The stationary condition
`dtf_1/dphi = -c_1 sin(phi - lambda_1) - c_1^2 cos(phi* - lambda_1) sin(phi* - lambda_1)/sqrt(c_1^2 cos^2(phi* - lambda_1) + c_1 r_10) = 0 ⇒ sin(phi - lambda_1) = 0 ⇒ phi* = lambda_1` (17),
i.e., the evader runs directly away from the pursuer (heading `phi = lambda_1 + pi` minimizes `tf_1`); "as expected." If a) or b) is active the 2P1EDG collapses to the 1v1 game with E running away from the capturing pursuer.

**Active-case test (eqs. (18)–(19), p. 1055).** Compute `tf_1(lambda_1)` from (13), substitute `phi = lambda_1` into (15) to get `tf_2(lambda_1)`: if `tf_1(lambda_1) <= tf_2(lambda_1)` (18) then `phi* = lambda_1` and "there is nothing Pursuer 2 can do to decrease the capture time." Symmetrically `tf_2(lambda_2) <= tf_1(lambda_2)` (19) ⇒ `phi* = lambda_2`.

**Apollonius-circle geometry (pp. 1055–1056).** For each pair (E, Pi): the Apollonius circle has center at distance `c_i = (1/(beta_i^2 - 1)) r_{i0}` from E along the `lambda_i` bearing and radius `rho_i = (beta_i/(beta_i^2 - 1)) r_{i0}`; center coordinates `a_i = x_E + rho_i cos lambda_i`, `b_i = y_E + rho_i sin lambda_i`; circle equation `(x - a_i)^2 + (y - b_i)^2 = rho_i^2` (20). For case c) (simultaneous capture), `tf_1 = tf_2` with the same `phi*` is solved by the **intersection of the two Apollonius circles** (20); E chooses the intersection point *farthest* from its own position as the aimpoint `I = (x_I, y_I)`, and `phi_s* = arctan((y_I - y_E)/(x_I - x_E))`, `psi_{1s}* = arctan((y_I - y_{P1})/(x_I - x_{P1}))`, `psi_{2s}* = arctan((y_I - y_{P2})/(x_I - x_{P2}))` guarantee simultaneous interception. In simultaneous capture `lambda_m <= phi_s* <= lambda_M`, `lambda_m = min{lambda_1, lambda_2}`, `lambda_M = max{lambda_1, lambda_2}`.

**Proposition 1 (p. 1056, eq. (21)) — state-feedback solution of the 2P1EDG (4)–(6) with `beta_1 > 1`, `beta_2 > 1`.**
`phi* = lambda_1, psi_1* = lambda_1` if `tf_1(lambda_1) <= tf_2(lambda_1)`;
`phi* = lambda_2, psi_2* = lambda_2` if `tf_2(lambda_2) <= tf_1(lambda_2)`;
`phi* = phi_s*, psi_1* = psi_{1s}*, psi_2* = psi_{2s}*` otherwise.
(Note: in the first two branches the winning pursuer simply heads straight at the evader — heading `lambda_i`.) Proof approach: construction from (12)–(19) plus Apollonius intersections; verification deferred to Theorem 2.

**Value function (eq. (22)–(24), p. 1056).** The capture set (whole state space) partitions into `R_1` (E captured only by P1), `R_2` (only by P2), `R_s` (simultaneous); boundaries are the equality cases of (18)/(19) (Fig. 4 example: `P1 = (0,3)`, `P2 = (0,-3)`, `beta_1 = 1.3`, `beta_2 = 1.25`).
`V(x) = sqrt((x_E - x_{P1})^2 + (y_E - y_{P1})^2)/(beta_1 - 1)` for `x ∈ R_1`;
`V(x) = sqrt((x_E - x_{P2})^2 + (y_E - y_{P2})^2)/(beta_2 - 1)` for `x ∈ R_2`;
`V(x) = [F_1(x) tf_2(x) - F_2(x) tf_1(x)] / [F_1(x) - F_2(x)]` for `x ∈ R_s` (22),
where
`tf_i(x) = (1/(beta_i^2 - 1)) [ (x_E - x_{Pi}) cos phi* + (y_E - y_{Pi}) sin phi* + ( [(x_E - x_{Pi}) cos phi* + (y_E - y_{Pi}) sin phi*]^2 + (beta_i^2 - 1)[(x_E - x_{Pi})^2 + (y_E - y_{Pi})^2] )^{1/2} ]` (23),
`F_i(x) = [ (y_E - y_{Pi}) cos phi* - (x_E - x_{Pi}) sin phi* ] × ( [(x_E - x_{Pi}) cos phi* + (y_E - y_{Pi}) sin phi*]^2 + (beta_i^2 - 1)[(x_E - x_{Pi})^2 + (y_E - y_{Pi})^2] )^{-1/2}` (24),
with `beta_i > 1` and `phi*(x, beta_1, beta_2)` obtained from the Apollonius-circle intersection points; `cos phi*, sin phi*` are left implicit "due to the complexity of an explicit expression" (p. 1056). The `R_s` form is a convex combination of the two 1v1 capture times; when `x ∈ R_s` simultaneous capture means `tf_1(phi*) = tf_2(phi*) =: tf(phi*)`. **Physical meaning**: V is the capture time under optimal play (in speeds normalized by `v_E`; if `v_E ≠ 1` the real time is `V/v_E`, Remark p. 1059).

**Dispersal surface.** `D ⊂ R_s` exists where the two Apollonius-circle intersections are equidistant from E — two optimal evader strategies (p. 1056). Define `R_s^- = R_s - D`, regular region `R = R_1 ∪ R_2 ∪ R_s^-`.

**Theorem 2 (p. 1057) — verification.** For the 2P1EDG (4)–(6) with the solution of Proposition 1 and Value (22): the Value function is continuous and continuously differentiable over `R` and satisfies the HJI equation for any `x ∈ R`. Proof (pp. 1057–1058), the paper's centerpiece:
1. *Inside `R_1`*: `∂V/∂x_E = (1/(beta_1 - 1)) cos lambda_1`, `∂V/∂y_E = (1/(beta_1 - 1)) sin lambda_1`, `∂V/∂x_{P1} = -∂V/∂x_E`, `∂V/∂y_{P1} = -∂V/∂y_E` (25), with `cos lambda_1 = (x_E - x_{P1})/r_1`, `sin lambda_1 = (y_E - y_{P1})/r_1`; analogous in `R_2`. Hence V is C¹ inside `R_1`, `R_2`.
2. *Inside `R_s`*: full gradient (26) contains a `∂V/∂phi* · dphi*/dx` term plus `tf_1 ∂/∂x(-F_2/(F_1 - F_2)) + tf_2 ∂/∂x(F_1/(F_1 - F_2))`. Since `tf = tf_1 = tf_2`, the latter equals `tf ∂/∂x((F_1 - F_2)/(F_1 - F_2)) = 0`; and since `∂tf_i/∂phi* = F_i tf_i` (decisive intermediate identity), `∂V/∂phi* = (-F_2 ∂tf_1/∂phi* + F_1 ∂tf_2/∂phi*)/(F_1 - F_2) = (-F_1 F_2 tf_1 + F_1 F_2 tf_2)/(F_1 - F_2) = 0`. So the gradient reduces to `∂V/∂x = (-F_2/(F_1 - F_2)) ∂tf_1/∂x + (F_1/(F_1 - F_2)) ∂tf_2/∂x` (28). The partials `∂tf_i/∂x_E, ∂tf_i/∂y_E` are explicit (27) with `Q_i = ([(x_E - x_{Pi}) cos phi* + (y_E - y_{Pi}) sin phi*]^2 + (beta_i^2 - 1)[(x_E - x_{Pi})^2 + (y_E - y_{Pi})^2])^{1/2} > 0`, and `∂tf_i/∂x_{Pj} = -∂tf_i/∂x_E` if `i = j`, else 0. Rewriting `F_i(x) = sin(lambda_i - phi*)/sqrt(cos^2(lambda_i - phi*) + beta_i^2 - 1)` (29) and using `lambda_m <= phi* <= lambda_M`, F1 and F2 have **opposite signs**, so `F_1 - F_2 ≠ 0`: V is C¹ inside `R_s`.
3. *Across the `R_1`/`R_s` boundary* (the `R_2`/`R_s` case is symmetric): there `phi* = lambda_1` (by (17)) so `F_1(phi* = lambda_1) = 0` and `V|_{R_s} = (-F_2/(-F_2)) tf_1 = tf_1`. Evaluating (23) at `phi* = lambda_1` gives `tf_{1s} = (1/(beta_1^2 - 1))[ r_1 + (r_1^2 + (beta_1^2 - 1) r_1^2)^{1/2} ] = ((beta_1 + 1) r_1)/(beta_1^2 - 1) = r_1/(beta_1 - 1) = tf_1` (30) — **continuity**. For the gradient, `∂V/∂x_{P2} = ∂V/∂y_{P2} = 0` on the boundary (since `F_1 = 0`, `∂tf_1/∂x_{P2} = 0`), and `∂V/∂x_E = (-F_2/(-F_2)) ∂tf_1/∂x_E |_{phi* = lambda_1} = (1/(beta_1^2 - 1))[cos lambda_1 + (r_1 cos lambda_1 + (beta_1^2 - 1)(x_E - x_{P1}))/(beta_1 r_1)] = (1/(beta_1^2 - 1)) cos lambda_1 (1 + (1 + (beta_1^2 - 1))/beta_1) = ((beta_1 + 1)/(beta_1^2 - 1)) cos lambda_1 = (1/(beta_1 - 1)) cos lambda_1` (31), matching (25) — **C¹ across the boundary**.
4. *HJI satisfaction*: with `V(x, t) = V(x)` (autonomous) the HJI check is `0 = {∂V/∂x · f(x, u_P*, u_E*) + 1}` (eqs. (32)–(33)). In `R_1`, `phi* = psi_1* = lambda_1` and `∂V/∂x · f = (1/((beta_1 - 1) r_1))[(x_E - x_{P1}) cos lambda_1 + (y_E - y_{P1}) sin lambda_1 - beta_1 (x_E - x_{P1}) cos lambda_1 - beta_1 (y_E - y_{P1}) sin lambda_1] = ((1 - beta_1)/((beta_1 - 1) r_1)) r_1 = -1` (34), hence `1 + ∂V/∂x · f = 0` (35); same in `R_2`. In `R_s`, the pursuers' optimal headings are expressed through the evader's: `cos psi_i* = cos phi*/beta_i + (x_E - x_{Pi})/(beta_i tf)`, `sin psi_i* = sin phi*/beta_i + (y_E - y_{Pi})/(beta_i tf)` (37), "obtained from eqs. (12) and (14)". Substituting into `∂V/∂x · f` (36) yields (38), and the per-pursuer identity `(1/tf)(∂tf_i/∂x_E (x_{Pi} - x_E) + ∂tf_i/∂y_E (y_{Pi} - y_E)) = -(1/(tf(beta_i^2 - 1)))[(x_E - x_{Pi}) cos phi* + (y_E - y_{Pi}) sin phi* + Q_i] = -(1/tf) tf = -1` (39) (using (23) in the form `tf = (1/(beta_i^2 - 1))[(x_E - x_{Pi}) cos phi* + (y_E - y_{Pi}) sin phi* + Q_i]`). Therefore `∂V/∂x · f = (-F_2/(F_1 - F_2))(-1) + (F_1/(F_1 - F_2))(-1) = -1` and `1 + ∂V/∂x · f = 0` (40). QED.
The closing summary (p. 1059) adds that "plus the uniqueness property of the Value function" certifies that the candidate solution *is* the solution. **Proof gaps / quirks (verified against page images)**: (i) the repeated `lambdadot_{y_{P1}}` typo on p. 1055 (should be `y_{P2}`); (ii) in eq. **Dispersal-surface example (p. 1059, Fig. 5).** `E = (5, 0)`, `P1 = (0, 0)`, `P2 = (24, -4)`; speeds `v_E = 1`, `v_{P1} = 1.25`, `v_{P2} = 1.3125`. ### 5.2 Active Target Defense Differential Game (ATDDG), Sec. V, pp. 1060–1064

**Scenario and metrics (pp. 1060–1061).** Target T is pursued by Attacker A; Defender D pursues A. If D intercepts A before A captures T, defense succeeds; otherwise A wins. When T maneuvers, T and D cooperate against A — the ATDDG. Popular metrics [authors' survey]: games of kind — does the defense succeed; games of degree — the A–T range at the instant A is intercepted by D (if T escapes), or the D–A distance at the instant A captures T (if T is captured); time-to-capture also used. Literature digest (pp. 1060–1061): early two-player one-target game [93]; Boyell ship-vs-torpedo counterweapon [94], [95]; defensive missile launches [96], [97]; Rubinsky–Gutman end-game LQ linearization [98], [99]; non-maneuverable aircraft [100], [101]; limiting strategies as the defender's acceleration weight → 0 [102]; Ratnoo–Shima LOS guidance [103], [104]; Shima's cooperative strategies allowing a maneuverability-disadvantaged Defender, optimal target maneuver constant or arbitrary [105]; MMAE guidance identification [106]; ZEM-based three-agent game [107]; the authors' own line [51], [52], [91], [92], [108]–[110] (two defenders [108]; non-zero capture radius [110]; PN/pure-pursuit restricted-information setting [91], [109]; EKF sensor models [92]).

**Formulation (p. 1061).** Simple motion in the Euclidean plane; controls are headings `phi, chi, psi ∈ [-pi, pi]`; state `x = (x_T, y_T, x_A, y_A, x_D, y_D) ∈ R^6`; speeds normalized `V_A = V_D = 1`, `V_T = alpha`, speed ratio `alpha = V_T/V_A < 1` (Target slower than Attacker; Attacker and Defender have similar capabilities). Dynamics (eq. (41)): `xdot_A = cos chi`, `ydot_A = sin chi`; `xdot_D = cos psi`, `ydot_D = sin psi`; `xdot_T = alpha cos phi`, `ydot_T = alpha sin phi`, with initial conditions. Termination set `S = S_e ∪ S_c` (42) with `S_e = {x | sqrt((x_A - x_D)^2 + (y_A - y_D)^2) = 0}` (43) (Defender intercepts Attacker; Target escapes) and `S_c = {x | sqrt((x_A - x_T)^2 + (y_A - y_T)^2) = 0}` (44) (Attacker captures Target). Focus: **Game of Degree when (43) applies**, with cost (terminal Attacker-Target separation)
`J_c(u_A(t), u_D(t), x_tilde_0) := (1/2)[(x_{Af} - x_{Tf})^2 + (y_{Af} - y_{Tf})^2]` (45),
subject to (43). Reduced state space (WLOG): `x_D = -x_A`, `y_A = y_D = 0`, `y_T >= 0`; reduced state `x = (x_A, x_T, y_T)` (A at `(x_A, 0)`, `x_A > 0`; D at `(-x_A, 0)`; T at `(x_T, y_T)`).

**Game of Kind — Proposition 2 (pp. 1061–1062; attributed to [111]).** When `x_T < 0` the Target can escape outright. For `x_T > 0` and a given speed ratio `0 < alpha < 1`, the boundary is rendered by
`alpha = [ sqrt((x_A + x_T)^2 + y_T^2) - sqrt((x_A - x_T)^2 + y_T^2) ] / (2 x_A)` (46),
i.e., the locus where T is equidistant-in-time from the two interception points; it "constitutes the solution to the Game of Kind." Precisely:
Attacker's win region `R_c = {(x_A, x_T, y_T) | x_A > 0, x_T > 0, y_T >= 0, x_A^2 + y_T^2/(1 - alpha^2) - x_T^2/alpha^2 < 0}`;
boundary manifold `B = {... = 0}`;
T&D win region `R_e = {(x_A, x_T, y_T) | x_A >= 0, x_T >= 0, y_T >= 0, x_A^2 + y_T^2/(1 - alpha^2) - x_T^2/alpha^2 > 0} ∪ {(x_A, x_T, y_T) | x_A >= 0, x_T <= 0}`.
For fixed `x_A > 0`, the cross-section of B is the right branch (`x_T > 0`) of the hyperbola
`x_T^2/(alpha^2 x_A^2) - y_T^2/((1 - alpha^2) x_A^2) = 1` (47).
The ATDDG game of degree is played in `R_e`.

**Theorem 3 (p. 1062) — Game of Degree in the escape region, via "two-sided" PMP.** Parameter `0 <= alpha < 1`; reduced state `(x_A, x_T, y_T)`, `x_A > 0`, `y_T >= 0`. In the part of the state space where T can escape, the optimal state-feedback headings are
`cos phi* = ± x_T / sqrt(x_T^2 + (y_T - y)^2)`, `sin phi* = ± (y_T - y)/sqrt(x_T^2 + (y_T - y)^2)` for `x_T ≠ 0`;
`cos chi* = -x_A / sqrt(x_A^2 + y^2)`, `sin chi* = y / sqrt(x_A^2 + y^2)`;
`cos psi* = x_A / sqrt(x_A^2 + y^2)`, `sin psi* = y / sqrt(x_A^2 + y^2)` (48),
where `y` is a real solution of the quartic
`(1 - alpha^2) y^4 - 2 (1 - alpha^2) y_T y^3 + [ (1 - alpha^2) y_T^2 + x_A^2 - alpha^2 x_T^2 ] y^2 - 2 x_A^2 y_T y + x_A^2 y_T^2 = 0` (49),
parameterized by `alpha`. The quartic has two real solutions `y_1`, `y_2` with `y_1 <= y_T <= y_2`; **root selection**: `x_T <= 0` → use `y_1`; `x_T > 0` → use `y_2`. **Sign selection** in the Target heading (48): `x_T < 0` → positive sign; `x_T > 0` → negative sign. When `x_T = 0`: `phi* = varphi* + pi/2` (50) with `tan varphi* = sqrt(x_A^2 + (1 - alpha^2) y_T^2)/(alpha y_T)` (51) (derivation referred to [111]). Geometric meaning: A and D (equal speeds) both head to the point `(0, y)` on the **orthogonal bisector of AD**, where D intercepts A at time `tf = sqrt(x_A^2 + y^2)`; the Target's heading is set by the optimal aimpoint `y*` on that bisector.

**Proof (pp. 1062–1064), decisive steps.** (i) Isaacs' Main Equation 1 `min_{phi,psi} max_chi H = 0` gives co-state-aligned controls: `cos chi* = +lambda_{x_A}/|lambda_A|` (Attacker aligns — the maximizing side of H as printed), `cos psi* = -lambda_{x_D}/|lambda_D|`, `cos phi* = -lambda_{x_T}/|lambda_T|` (unnumbered display, p. 1062). Co-state dynamics vanish ⇒ constant co-states ⇒ straight-line optimal trajectories. (ii) Augmented Mayer function `Phi_a(x_f) := (1/2)[(x_A(tf) - x_T(tf))^2 + (y_A(tf) - y_T(tf))^2] + nu_1 (x_A(tf) - x_D(tf)) + nu_2 (y_A(tf) - y_D(tf))`; transversality `lambda(tf) = -∂Phi_a/∂x` gives (52)–(57) (e.g., `lambda_{x_A} = x_T(tf) - x_A(tf) - nu_1` (52), `lambda_{x_T} = x_A(tf) - x_T(tf)` (56)); eliminating `nu_1, nu_2` using terminal constraints `x_A = x_D`, `y_A = y_D` (58) yields `lambda_{x_A} + lambda_{x_D} = x_T(tf) - x_A(tf)` (59), `lambda_{y_A} + lambda_{y_D} = y_T(tf) - y_A(tf)` (60); `tf` fixed by `H|_{tf} ≡ 0`. (iii) Straight lines + `V_D = V_A` force `x_A(tf) = 0` (61), `x_D(tf) = 0` (62), `y_A(tf) = y_D(tf) =: y` (63); from (41), `tf = sqrt(x_A^2 + y^2)` (75) (interception point I on the bisector; triangle △ADy). (iv) The cost reduces to a **one-variable optimization** ("using Isaacs' method … reduce the solution of the zero-sum differential game of degree to the optimization of a cost/payoff function of one variable", p. 1063): `J(y; x_A, x_T, y_T) = TT_f ± TI` with `TT_f = alpha · tf`; case a) `x_T < 0`: `min_y J_1 = alpha sqrt(x_A^2 + y^2) + sqrt((y_T - y)^2 + x_T^2)` (76) (Target heads away from I; heading (78)); case b) `x_T > 0`: `max_y J_2 = alpha sqrt(x_A^2 + y^2) - sqrt((y - y_T)^2 + x_T^2)` (77) (heading (79)). A/D headings (80)–(81). (v) Matching co-state-characterized controls with (80)–(81) gives four equations (59), (60), (82), (83) in `lambda_{x_A}, lambda_{y_A}, lambda_{x_D}, lambda_{y_D}` with solution (84)–(87); terminal Target positions (88) (case a, `+` sign) and (89) (case b, `-` sign) give co-states in terms of y (90). (vi) The terminal Hamiltonian condition `H|_{tf} ≡ 0` with (78)–(81) and (90) simplifies to `(1 - y_T/y)^2 (x_A^2 + y^2) = alpha^2 [ x_T^2 + (y_T - y)^2 ]` (p. 1064), and "grouping terms in y" yields exactly the quartic (49): the optimal aimpoint `y*` on the orthogonal bisector of AD. QED, with the `x_T = 0` case delegated to [111].

**Verification status [authors' statement, p. 1064].** Saddle-point verification of the ATDDG escape-region strategies was "recently provided" in [112] (Garcia, Casbeer, Pachter, IEEE TAC 64(2), 2019); a candidate solution in the **capture region** was presented in [113] and "verification of these strategies is a topic of current research."

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
**Author-stated:**
- Verification of ATDDG strategies in the capture region is open: "Preliminary results and a candidate solution … have been presented in [113]; verification of these strategies is a topic of current research" (p. 1064). Escape-region verification is outsourced to [112].
- The `x_T = 0` Target strategy derivation is not included ("please refer to [111]", p. 1064).
- Dispersal dilemma is intrinsic: pursuers "will pay a price" when the state leaves D; only the improbable perpetual dilemma avoids it (p. 1059).
**Critical observations (with page/eq.):**
- Both case studies assume **point capture** (eqs. (5), (43)–(44)) and **simple motion** (no inertia, no heading-rate limits) — the surveyed literature on bounded controls [61], heading-rate constraints [109], and non-zero capture radii [110] is not incorporated into the worked games.
- Section IV requires both pursuers strictly faster (`beta_i > 1`, eq. (4)); Section V assumes `V_A = V_D` (p. 1061) — heterogeneous attacker/defender speeds are excluded from the presented solution (the fast-Defender variant is only cited [52]).
- The Value (22) is verified C¹ only on `R = R_1 ∪ R_2 ∪ R_s^-`; behavior on the dispersal surface D and the C¹-selection uniqueness of `phi*` inside `R_s` are not analyzed (pp. 1056–1057); no viscosity/weak-solution treatment is given, so the verification is classical and local to the regular region.
- Theorem 3's proof ends at the necessary conditions (`H|_{tf} ≡ 0` → quartic); sufficiency/saddle-point property in the paper itself rests on the external verification [112]; the min/max attribution in the one-variable reductions (76)–(77) is stated without an explicit argument of who moves first along y, and the printed Main Equation order `min_{phi,psi} max_chi` (p. 1062) should be read together with the co-state sign pattern rather than with the prose "T and D cooperate to maximize the terminal range" (p. 1062).
- The 5v3 assignment result (Table I) is an exhaustive enumeration over 2-2-1 teams only — "We do not consider cases where three Pursuers may be assigned to a single Evader" (p. 1059); global optimality beyond the enumerated set and against non-2-2-1 partitions is not proven.
- Minor errata verified on page images: repeated `lambdadot_{y_{P1}}` (p. 1055, should be `y_{P2}`); missing subscript in `(x_E - x_P)` in eq. (31) (p. 1058); superscript "(5)" in Table I row (4,5) is the pursuer index of the solo capturer, consistent with the caption only after interpretation (p. 1060).

## 8. Citation Value
**Paragraph role.** This is the field's modern "front-door" citation: a peer-reviewed (ACC 2020) tutorial by three of the most prolific AFRL/AFIT PE-game researchers that (i) canonizes the taxonomy 1v1 / Nv1 / 1vM / NvM used in much of the subsequent multi-player PE literature, (ii) condenses the Isaacs→Bellman→Breakwell→Pontryagin lineage and the Homicidal Chauffeur/Two Cars classics into two paragraphs, and (iii) provides fully worked, verified templates of the two standard solution toolchains — geometric Apollonius-circle synthesis + HJI verification (2P1EDG) and two-sided PMP with a quartic-characterized aimpoint + hyperbolic game-of-kind boundary (ATDDG). Its Sections IV–V are faithful self-contained restatements of [60] and [111], so it can be cited in place of (or as a bridge to) those longer sources for standard derivations: the 1v1 capture-time formula (13)/(15), the Apollonius circle center/radius `c_i = r_{i0}/(beta_i^2 - 1)`, `rho_i = beta_i r_{i0}/(beta_i^2 - 1)` (p. 1056), the HJI verification theorem, the dispersal-surface/perpetual-dilemma discussion, and the ATDDG hyperbola boundary (47) and quartic (49).
**Suggested citation sentences.** In an Introduction: "Pursuit-evasion differential games are conventionally categorized by the number of players — one-versus-one, many-versus-one, one-versus-many, and many-versus-many — following the organized taxonomy of [P044], which also surveys the seminal contributions of Isaacs, Bellman, Breakwell, and Pontryagin." In a methodology or preliminaries section: "We adopt the standard simple-motion pursuit-evasion setup in which a faster pursuer's capture time against a heading-controlled evader is expressed through the Apollonius circle, and optimal strategies are verified a posteriori via the Hamilton-Jacobi-Isaacs equation, as canonicalized in [P044]." For attack-defense work: "The active target defense differential game, in which a Defender cooperates with a maneuvering Target against an Attacker, is introduced and surveyed in [P044], with the game-of-kind boundary given by a hyperbola in the reduced state space and the game-of-degree aimpoint characterized as a root of a quartic equation."
