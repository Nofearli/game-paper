## 2. Research Background

The Target–Attacker–Defender (TAD) game, originated by Boyell (1976) and formalized in the active-defense differential-game line of Garcia, Casbeer, Pachter and co-workers (IEEE TAES 2018; IEEE TAC 2019; JOTA 2021; Automatica 2021), models an Attacker (A) pursuing a Target (T) while a Defender (D) escorts T by intercepting A. Practically it corresponds to an aircraft launching a missile against an incoming missile (Prokopov & Shima 2013; Shaferman & Shima 2010; Shima 2011) or a torpedo defending a ship (Boyell 1976). The related reach-avoid literature (Yan et al. 2017, 2019, 2024a,b, 2025; Garcia et al. 2020) handles pursuers preventing evaders from entering a target region, but — as the authors stress — reach-avoid formulations assume a **static** target, whereas TAD requires co-designing the mobile target's strategy with the defender's. Virtually the whole TAD state-feedback line (Garcia et al. 2018, 2019, 2021; Liang et al. 2019) assumes **point capture** (zero capture radius for A and/or D); the exceptions that admit a non-zero Attacker radius (Liang et al. 2021; Garcia 2021) treat the *superior*-attacker case V_A > V_D via reachable-set constructs (Limaçon of Pascal, circle of protection) that, per Remark 1 (p. 3), cease to exist when V_A ≤ V_D. This paper closes that gap for the equal-speed case V_A = V_D > V_T with **both** capture radii non-zero.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap

**Problem.** For the TAD game with simple motion (Isaacs 1965), V_A = V_D = 1 (normalized), V_T = γV_A, 0 < γ < 1, A's capture radius R > 0 and D's capture radius r > 0, solve: (i) **Problem 1 — Game of Kind** (p. 3): determine for every initial state x₀ whether it belongs to A's winning region R_c or the Target–Defender team's winning region R_t, i.e., whether x reaches M₁ = {x | ‖x_A − x_T‖₂ ≤ R} (eq. (3), p. 3) or M₂ = {x | ‖x_A − x_D‖₂ ≤ r} (eq. (4)) first, termination set M = M₁ ∪ M₂ (eq. (2)); and (ii) **Problem 2 — Game of Degree** (p. 3): on R_t, find the Nash equilibrium (β*, δ*, ζ*) of the max–min problem V(x₀) := min_{β(·)} max_{δ(·),ζ(·)} ‖x_{Af} − x_{Tf}‖₂ (eq. (5)) — A minimizes the final A–T distance, the D–T team maximizes it. Information structure: perfect information (all players know dynamics (1), current time, state and speeds; adopted from Garcia et al. 2018, 2019, Liang et al. 2019).

## 4. Core Contributions

1. **Modeling** (theory): first TAD game formulation — within the Garcia lineage — in which **both** A and D have non-zero capture radii (R > 0, r > 0) under equal speeds V_A = V_D > V_T; terminal sets (2)–(4) with two distinct radii.
2. **Game of Degree** (theory/algorithm): two-sided Pontryagin Maximum Principle solutions for three initial placements of T relative to the D–A interception hyperbola (left / right / on it), giving constant-heading state-feedback strategies (Theorems 1–3) whose intercept point solves the algebraic system (11)–(12) and is radius-dependent (Remark 4); C¹ value functions (16), (46) verified against the HJI equation (17), (44), establishing Nash equilibrium.
3. **Game of Kind** (theory): a new geometric object — the **Attacker's limit interception curve** 𝒜 (Definition 2, eq. (64), p. 8) — and an explicit implicit barrier function B (Theorem 4, eqs. (72)–(74)) obtained from the **tangency** between the T–A Apollonius oval and 𝒜; proved via monotonicity of the horizontal-gap function U(y); Algorithm 1 (p. 11) constructs B numerically (plus an alternative traversing method, Remark 6).
4. **Experiment** (simulation): four numerical examples (Section 5, Figs. 5–8) validating the three Game-of-Degree theorems and comparing the new barrier with the Garcia et al. (2018, 2019) barriers under identical parameters, demonstrating that r > 0 shrinks A's winning region and R > 0 enlarges it.

## 5. Key Mathematical Results (exhaustive)

### 5.0 Dynamics, information, cost, termination

- **Dynamics (eq. (1), p. 2)**: Isaacs "simple motion" (holonomic) on the Euclidean plane, states x_T, x_A, x_D ∈ ℝ², full state x = (x_A, x_D, x_T) ∈ ℝ⁶; controls are heading angles ζ (T), β (A), δ (D) ∈ [−π, π]:
  ẋ_A = (cos β, sin β), ẋ_D = (cos δ, sin δ), ẋ_T = γ(cos ζ, sin ζ); speeds normalized V_A = V_D = 1, V_T = γ ∈ (0,1) (assumption V_A = V_D > V_T, same as Garcia et al. 2018 TAES / 2019 TAC).
- **Termination (eqs. (2)–(4), p. 2–3)**: M = M₁ ∪ M₂, M₁ = {‖x_A − x_T‖₂ ≤ R} (A wins), M₂ = {‖x_A − x_D‖₂ ≤ r} (D–T win); t_f = first entry into M₁ or M₂; a third outcome (simultaneous entry into both) is the tie case realized on the barrier.
- **Information**: perfect-information differential game (p. 3).
- **Cost (eq. (5), p. 3)**: V(x₀) := min_β max_{δ,ζ} ‖x_{Af} − x_{Tf}‖₂ (zero-sum, terminal payoff; solved only for the D–T-victory premise).
- **Dominant-region preliminaries**:
  - **Definition 1 (p. 3)**: dominant point/region of an evader (points the evader reaches before capture regardless of the pursuer).
  - **Lemma 1 [Yan et al. 2021] (p. 3, eqs. (6)–(7))**: if ‖x_A − x_T‖₂ > R, the T–A capture curve and T's dominant region are the **Apollonius oval** 𝒜^R = {z | ‖z − x_T‖₂ = γ(‖z − x_A‖₂ − R)} and R_TE = {z | ‖z − x_T‖₂ < γ(‖z − x_A‖₂ − R)}. Remark 2 (p. 3): for R = 0 the oval degenerates to the Apollonius circle; R > 0 sharply shrinks T's dominant region (Fig. 2, p. 4, with x_A = (3,4), x_T = (7,10), R = 0.8, γ = 0.8).
  - **Lemma 2 [Garcia et al. 2018] (p. 3, eqs. (8)–(9))**: if ‖x_A − x_D‖₂ > r, the D–A **interception hyperbola** and A's dominant region are 𝒜^r = {z | ‖z − x_A‖₂ = ‖z − x_D‖₂ − r} and R_AE = {z | ‖z − x_A‖₂ < ‖z − x_D‖₂ − r} (branch closer to A; foci A, D).

### 5.1 Game of Degree: Hamiltonian and intercept-point system

- **Hamiltonian (eq. (10), p. 4)**: ℋ = γλ_{xT} cos ζ + γλ_{yT} sin ζ + λ_{xA} cos β + λ_{yA} sin β + λ_{xD} cos δ + λ_{yD} sin δ, costate λ ∈ ℝ⁶.
- **Optimal intercept-point system (eqs. (11)–(12), p. 4)**: the interception point Q(x, y) must satisfy
  √((x−x_A)² + (y−y_A)²) = √((x−x_D)² + (y−y_D)²) − r  (11)  [Q on 𝒜^r: A's and D's arrival times, with D's radius], and
  √((x−x_A)²+(y−y_A)²)[(x−x_D)(y−y_T) − (x−x_T)(y−y_D)] − γ√((x−x_T)²+(y−y_T)²)[(x−x_A)(y−y_D) − (x−xD)(y−y_A)] − √((x−x_D)²+(y−y_D)²)[(x−x_A)(y−y_T) − (x−x_T)(y−y_A)] = 0  (12),
  which is the PMP unknown-terminal-time condition (see Theorem 1 proof, eqs. (38)–(39)). Note (11) explicitly contains r — the resulting strategies are D-radius-dependent, distinguishing them from Garcia et al. (2019) (**Remark 4, p. 6**).

### 5.2 Theorem 1 (p. 4, eqs. (13)–(17)) — T initially **left of** the interception hyperbola

- **Statement**: for the TAD game (1)–(5), under the condition ‖x_T − x_A‖₂ > ‖x_T − x_D‖₂ − r (T on D's side of 𝒜^r), the optimal state-feedback strategies are
  cos ζ* = (x_T − x*)/√((x_T−x*)² + (y_T−y*)²), sin ζ* = (y_T − y*)/√(…)**(13)** [T heads *away from* the intercept point z*];
  cos β* = (x* − x_A)/√((x_A−x*)² + (y_A−y*)²), sin β* = (y* − y_A)/√(…) **(14)**; cos δ* = (x* − x_D)/√((x_D−x*)² + (y_D−y*)²), sin δ* = (y* − y_D)/√(…) **(15)** [A and D head *toward* z*];
  where z* = (x*, y*) is the solution of (11)–(12) that **minimizes** the C¹ value function
  V(x) = γ‖z* − x_A‖₂ + ‖z* − x_T‖₂ **(16)**,
  and V satisfies the HJI equation −∂V/∂t = ∂V/∂x · f(x, β*, δ*, ζ*) **(17)**.
- **Assumptions**: V_A = V_D = 1, V_T = γ < 1, ‖x_A − x_T‖ > R, ‖x_A − x_D‖ > r, condition above; D–T victory premise (x reaches M₂ first).
- **Proof approach** (pp. 4–6): (1) stationarity of Isaacs' main equation min_β max_{ζ,δ} ℋ = 0 gives optimal controls as normalized costates (18)–(20); (2) λ̇ = −∂ℋ/∂x = 0 (ℋ state-independent) ⇒ all costates constant ⇒ all optimal headings constant (straight-line trajectories); (3) two-point BVP in ℝ¹² solved via the augmented terminal value Ψ_a(x_f) := ‖x_{Af} − x_{Tf}‖₂ + ν(‖x_{Af} − x_{Df}‖₂ − r) (21) with Lagrange multiplier ν for the interception constraint, using x ≡ x_{Af}, y ≡ y_{Af} (22); (4) transversality λ(t_f) = ∂Ψ_a/∂x (23) yields costates (24)–(27); (5) T's heading in terms of terminal state (28), restated in current state (29); A's and D's headings toward Q (30)–(31), matched with (18)–(19) in (32)–(33); (6) eliminating ν gives λ_{xA} + λ_{xD} = (x − x_{Tf})/‖·‖, λ_{yA} + λ_{yD} = (y − y_{Tf})/‖·‖ (34); (7) terminal time from speed normalization: t_f − t = AQ = ‖(x_A − x, y_A − y)‖ (35); T's terminal position from constant heading (36); (8) **decisive intermediate result** — the costate–state map (37) expressing all six costates through the current state and Q(x,y); substituting (29)–(31), (37) into the unknown-terminal-time PMP identity λ_{xA}cos β* + λ_{yA}sin β* + λ_{xD}cos δ* + λ_{yD}sin δ* + γλ_{xT}cos ζ* + γλ_{yT}sin ζ* ≡ 0 (38) produces eq. (39), which simplifies exactly to (12); (9) per Garcia et al. (2018) and Pachter et al. (2014), candidates from (11)–(12) are ranked by (16) and the minimizer is z*; (10) **Nash/HJI verification**: dV/dx* is computed in Box I (eq. (40)) using dy*/dx* = −(cos δ* − cos β*)/(sin δ* − sin β*) (41, implicit function theorem, Walter 1976); the numerator of (40) is shown in (42) to equal (up to positive factors) the left-hand side of (12), hence dV/dx* = 0; the state gradient is ∂V/∂x_A = −γ cos β*, ∂V/∂y_A = −γ sin β*, ∂V/∂x_D = ∂V/∂y_D = 0, ∂V/∂x_T = cos ζ*, ∂V/∂y_T = sin ζ* (43); consequently ∂V/∂x · f = −γcos²β* − γsin²β* + 0 + 0 + γcos²ζ* + γsin²ζ* = 0 (44), i.e., V satisfies HJI and (β*, δ*, ζ*) is a Nash equilibrium.
- **Physical meaning**: T is safe behind D's hyperbola; the D–T team forces interception at z* while T flees the intercept point; the terminal payoff γ‖z*−x_A‖ + ‖z*−x_T‖ is exactly T's displacement (γ × A's travel time) plus T's standoff at interception. Local character: saddle-point equilibrium verified through HJI (stationary, state-feedback); intercept point selected globally among candidates of (11)–(12).
- **Extensibility**: the PMP template (constant headings + terminal constraint with multiplier) extends to other radius pairs; feedback is *implicit* — z* must be re-solved from (11)–(12) as the state evolves.

### 5.5 Attacker's limit interception curve (pp. 8–9, eqs. (61)–(71)) — the new geometric object

- **Definition 2 (p. 8)**: C(x′, y′) is a *limit point of A's firepower* if A captures T at C while being intercepted by D at I(x_I, y_I) on the hyperbola (8); the locus of all such C is the **Attacker's limit interception curve 𝒜**.
- **Construction (61)–(64)**: with slope k = y_I/(x_I − x_A) (61), |IC| = R (62), and collinearity of A, I, C (Lemma 1 of Waszy et al. 2019, eq. (63)) — i.e., C = I + R·(I − A)/‖I − A‖ (A's firepower extends exactly R beyond the interception point along the aiming line) — the parametric curve is (64):
  x = a√(1 + y_I²/b²) − R/√(1 + (y_I/(a√(1+y_I²/b²) − x_A))²) for |y_I| < Γ;  x = a√(1 + y_I²/b²) for |y_I| = Γ;  x = a√(1 + y_I²/b²) + R/√(1 + (…))² for |y_I| > Γ;  y = y_I + y_I R/√((a√(1+y_I²/b²) − x_A)² + y_I²), y_I ∈ ℝ, where Γ = b√(x_A²/a² − 1) is the ordinate at x = x_A. **Key property**: for R = 0, (64) reduces to the parametric form of the D–A interception hyperbola (p. 8) — the construction strictly generalizes the classical barrier ingredient.
- **Monotonicity (65)–(71), pp. 8–9**: for y_I > 0, dx/dy_I (65), with Ξ = a√(1+y_I²/b²) − x_A, is shown positive in three regimes: (i) y_I < Γ: x_I = a√(1+y_I²/b²) < x_A (66) ⇒ Ξ < 0 ⇒ −ay_I²/(b²√(1+y_I²/b²)·Ξ) > 0 (67) ⇒ both summands of (65) positive; (ii) y_I > Γ: chain rule (68) gives a factor (1 + R(4b²x_Ix_A/r² − b²)/(((x_I−x_A)² + b²(x_I²/a²−1))^{3/2})), and the decisive inequality 4b²x_Ix_A/r² > 4b²(r/2)(r/2)/r² = b² (using x_I, x_A > r/2 = a) makes the bracket ≥ 1, so dx/dy_I > ay_I/(b²√(1+y_I²/b²)) > 0; (iii) y_I = Γ: (69). With dy/dy_I > 0 and symmetry about the x-axis, y_I ↔ y is invertible, giving x = f(y) = {f₁(y), y ∈ [0,∞); f₂(y), y ∈ (−∞,0)} (70) with dx/dy > 0 for y > 0 and < 0 for y < 0 (71): f₁ strictly increasing, f₂ strictly decreasing. [The bound x_Ix_A > a² = r²/4 is the decisive intermediate inequality.]

### 5.6 Theorem 4 (p. 9, eqs. (72)–(74)) — the barrier function (Game of Kind)

- **Statement**: given initial positions, capture radii R, r, and ratio γ, the barrier curve separating A's winning region R_c from the D–T winning region R_t is
  **x + 𝒩(σ*)cos σ* − f(y + 𝒩(σ*)sin σ*) = 0  (72)**,
  where σ*(x, y) ∈ (σ_min, σ_max) is the unique solution of
  [𝒩̇(σ)cos σ − sin σ 𝒩(σ)]/[𝒩̇(σ)sin σ + cos σ 𝒩(σ)] = ḟ(y + 𝒩(σ)sin σ)  (73),
  and (74): 𝒩(σ) = γ/(1−γ²)( γ(z − x_A)·e − R + √((γ(z−x_A)·e − R)² + (1−γ²)(‖z − x_A‖₂² − R²)) ), 𝒩̇(σ) = (γ(z − x_A)·e − R)𝒩(σ)/√((γ(z−x_A)·e − R)² + (1−γ²)(‖z−x_A‖₂² − R²)), e = (cos σ, sin σ)ᵀ — the polar form of the Apollonius oval (90) (credited to Yan et al. 2021), centered at the candidate T position z = (x, y).
- **Relation to value/winning regions (precise)**: 𝒜 (fixed by x_A, x_D, r, R) partitions the plane into R₁ (A's firepower reaches before D intercepts) and R₂ (it cannot). If x_T ∈ R₂, D–T wins outright. For x_T ∈ R₁, compare the T–A capture oval 𝒜^R with 𝒜: **intersection** (oval's left segment crosses left of 𝒜) ⇒ T can escape into R₂ ⇒ D–T winning region; **separation** (oval entirely right of 𝒜) ⇒ A destroys T wherever T can get before D intercepts ⇒ A's winning region; **tangency** ⇒ tie ⇒ x_T on the barrier. Hence the barrier is the locus of T positions whose oval is *tangent* to 𝒜 — a purely geometric Game-of-Kind solution, with no HJI barrier solving.
- **Proof** (pp. 9–11): (1) potential 𝒢(z) = ‖z − x_A‖₂ − ‖z − x_T‖₂/γ − R (75) with 𝒢 > 0 ⟺ z ∈ R_TE, 𝒢 = 0 ⟺ z ∈ 𝒜^R (76) and gradient (77); (2) extreme points of the oval: leftmost L via 𝒢 = 0, 𝒢_y = 0, 𝒢_x > 0 (78); rightmost N (79: 𝒢_y = 0, 𝒢_x < 0); highest M (80: 𝒢_x = 0, 𝒢_y < 0); lowest O (81: 𝒢_x = 0, 𝒢_y > 0); (3) the oval's left segment ÔLM is written x = g(x_T, y_T, y) = {g₁ on [y_min, y_L], g₂ on [y_L, y_max]} (82), g₁ decreasing, g₂ increasing (83); (4) define U(y) = g(x_T, y_T, y) − f(y); **Case 1 (y_L > 0)**: U̇ < 0 on [y_min, y_L] (84), U̇(y_L) < 0 (85), U̇(y_max) > 0 (86, "from Eq. (81) and (82)" — apparent cross-reference typo for (80)), hence a unique y* ∈ (y_L, y_max] with U̇(y*) = 0 (87) attaining min U; **Case 2 (y_L < 0)**: U̇ > 0 on [y_L, y_max], unique minimum at y* ∈ [y_min, y_L) (88); **Case 3 (y_L = 0)**: minimum at y* = y_L = 0 (89); (5) polar re-parametrization of the oval (90)–(91) x = x_T + 𝒩(σ)cos σ, y = y_T + 𝒩(σ)sin σ with dx/dσ = 𝒩̇cos σ − 𝒩 sin σ, dy/dσ = 𝒩̇ sin σ + 𝒩 cos σ (92)–(93); domain endpoints σ_min (94: dy/dx = 0, dx/dy > 0), σ₀ (95), σ_max (96: dy/dx = 0, dx/dy < 0); (6) U-minimization becomes F(σ) = U(y_T + 𝒩(σ)sin σ) = x_T + 𝒩(σ)cos σ − f(y_T + 𝒩(σ)sin σ) (98) with stationarity Ḟ(σ) = 0 ⟺ (73) (99); the unique σ* gives U_min = F(σ*) (100); (7) substituting x_T → x, y_T → y in (90), (100) yields the Cartesian barrier (72)/(101). ∎
- **Algorithm 1 (p. 11)**: (1) inputs x_A, x_D, γ, R, r; (2) build 𝒜 above the x-axis from (70); (3) treat x, y in (73) as known and solve σ(x, y) using (70), (74); (4) substitute into (72) to trace B.
- **Physical meaning**: the barrier is exactly the set of T positions where A's *extended* lethal reach (radius R beyond its impending interception) simultaneously closes all of T's escape boundary — i.e., where "A captures T while being intercepted by D" (Fig. 3, p. 7). It depends on R, r, γ, and both positions; it coincides with the classical hyperbola-based barrier when R = 0.
- **Character**: analytic-implicit (f and σ* computed numerically); global partition of the plane for the equal-speed case; proof is global (monotonicity + IVT), not local.
- **Caveat on (74), second line**: as printed, 𝒩̇(σ) = (γ(z−x_A)·e − R)𝒩(σ)/√(…). Direct differentiation of 𝒩 gives instead 𝒩̇(σ) = γ𝒩(σ)(z − x_A)·e_⊥/√(…), with e_⊥ = (−sin σ, cos σ)ᵀ, since d/dσ[γ(z−x_A)·e] = γ(z−x_A)·e_⊥ and (using √(u²+(1−γ²)s²) + u = 𝒩(1−γ²)/γ with u = γ(z−x_A)·e − R) the chain rule collapses to 𝒩̇ = u′𝒩/√(u²+(1−γ²)s²). The printed numerator (γ(z−x_A)·e − R) therefore does not equal the σ-derivative factor γ(z−x_A)·e_⊥; this is flagged as a likely typographical error in (74) (proof gap: the printed formula is inconsistent with (73)/(92)–(93), which themselves are internally consistent).
- **Uniqueness caveat**: existence of the minimizer follows from the sign change (85)–(86) via IVT, but strict monotonicity of U̇ on (y_L, y_max) (both ḡ₂ and ḟ are increasing there) is not established, so the asserted *uniqueness* of y*/σ* (87)–(89) is not fully proven; likewise Remark 5 concedes f "lacks a specific expression" and rests invertibility on monotonicity alone.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues

**Author-stated:**
- Game of Degree is solved only under the D–T-victory premise; "leaving A's winning scenario for future study" (p. 3, after Problem 2) — no value/strategies inside R_c.
- 2-D only; 3-D extension to "more general TAD games" is future work (Remark 7, p. 11); future work also lists obstacles, limited observation, and multi-TAD scenarios (Conclusion, p. 12).
- Focus on V_A = V_D "to allow direct comparison with Garcia et al. (2018, 2019)"; Remark 1 (p. 3) asserts the approach "applies to both equal-speed and slower attacker scenarios", but no such extension is proven in the paper.

**Critical observations (with page/eq.):**
1. **Eq. (74) derivative inconsistency** (p. 9): the printed 𝒩̇(σ) is not the σ-derivative of 𝒩(σ); correct differentiation gives 𝒩̇ ∝ γ(z−x_A)·(−sin σ, cos σ)ᵀ𝒩(σ)/√(…). Since Algorithm 1 (step 3) and (73) both consume 𝒩̇, this typo would corrupt a literal implementation (the geometry via (73)–(93) suggests the intended quantity is the genuine derivative).
2. **Uniqueness of the barrier minimizer** (p. 10, eqs. (87)–(89)): uniqueness of y*/σ* is asserted from endpoint signs of U̇; strict monotonicity of U̇ = ḡ − ḟ on (y_L, y_max) is not proven (both terms increase), so (73) could in principle admit multiple roots; the barrier's well-definedness as a *function* relies on this unproven uniqueness.
3. **Cross-reference slip** (p. 10, eq. (86)): "From Eq. (81) and (82)" is cited to obtain U̇(y_max) > 0, but (81) characterizes the *lowest* point O; (80) (highest point M) appears intended.
4. **Omitted proofs**: Theorem 2 (p. 6) and Theorem 3 (p. 7) proofs are omitted "for brevity"; in Theorem 2 the reversal of the candidate-selection rule (z* *maximizes* (46)) versus Theorem 1 (z* minimizes (16)) is the delicate saddle-point step and is not re-derived.
5. **Example 3 value inconsistency** (p. 12): with Q* = (1, 5.1230) = x_T (T on the hyperbola, ‖x_T−x_A‖ = ‖x_T−x_D‖ − r = 5.4996 checked), eq. (16) evaluates to γ‖Q*−x_A‖ + ‖Q*−x_T‖ = 0.7(5.4996) + 0 ≈ 3.85, not the printed V* = 7.9918; the printed value corresponds to an intercept point further up the hyperbola, so the sentence "T's optimal heading and the optimal interception point coincide at Q*" is ambiguous at best. (Examples 1–2 were numerically re-verified and are consistent.)
6. **Implicit feedback**: strategies (13)–(15) are "state feedback" only through z*(x), which requires solving the nonlinear system (11)–(12) (equivalently the scalar F(ω) = 0) online; no closed form, no complexity/real-time analysis, and C¹ of V across candidate-switching surfaces (where the arg-min/max over (11)–(12) solutions jumps) is not discussed — the HJI verification (40)–(44) is local to a fixed candidate.
7. **Only existence, not uniqueness, of the intercept point** is proven (58)–(60); selection among candidates leans on Garcia et al. (2018) and Pachter et al. (2014) rather than a self-contained argument.
8. **Tie set**: the measure-zero simultaneous-capture set M₁ ∩ M₂ (the barrier itself) is declared a "tie" but the dynamics/strategies on it are not analyzed (no Envelope/Barriér–Isaacs regularity argument).
9. **Reference defect**: the collinearity lemma used in (63) is attributed to "Waszy, P., Pachter, M., & Pham, K. (2019)" (MED 2019, *Two-on-one pursuit with a non-zero capture radius*) — the author string appears garbled in the reference list (p. 12); also Liang et al. (2019) is printed without a journal name ("102, 58–71"), as is Yan, Duan, et al. (2024) ("167, 111770", Automatica).
10. **Modeling scope**: simple motion only (no turning-rate/Dubins constraints), perfect information, deterministic speeds; the "non-suicidal attacker" nuance of Liang et al. (2021) (D can only win by rendezvous with T) is not addressed — here D wins by intercepting A within r, per (4).

## 8. Citation Value

This paper is the equal-speed (V_A = V_D > V_T) member of the TAD-with-finite-capture-radii line, complementing Garcia (2021, Automatica) and Liang et al. (2021, TAC), which require a superior attacker; it is the natural extension of Garcia et al. (2018 TAES, 2019 TAC) to R > 0 and r > 0 simultaneously, and its Game-of-Kind machinery (Attacker's limit interception curve + tangency with the Apollonius oval) is the reusable novelty — a template transferable to any pursuit-evasion problem where the pursuer's lethal radius extends beyond an interception manifold. Cite it when: (i) formulating TAD/active-defense games with non-zero capture (blast) radii for both attacker and defender; (ii) constructing barrier surfaces geometrically via curve-tangency instead of HJI/viscosity solutions; (iii) needing PMP-derived, HJI-verified state-feedback Nash strategies with radius-dependent value functions (16)/(46) for the defender–target team; or (iv) benchmarking barrier curves against Garcia et al. (2018, 2019) under identical kinematics. Suitable citation sentence: "Shi et al. (2026) solved the equal-speed TAD game with non-zero capture radii for both the attacker and the defender, deriving Pontryagin-based optimal feedback strategies that satisfy the HJI equation and constructing, via the tangency between the Attacker's limit interception curve and the T–A Apollonius oval, an implicit barrier function that partitions the plane into the attacker's and the defender–target's winning regions."
