## 2. Research Background
Target-Attacker-Defender (TAD) games extend pursuit-evasion by adding an active target that cooperates with defenders to nullify attackers; classical TAD results partition the state space geometrically (Apollonius/barrier-type analyses) or use HJI/zero-sum reductions, mostly with one defender, one attacker and one target [refs 14, 18, 20–22, 26]. This paper works in the linear-quadratic (LQ) nonzero-sum tradition of Starr & Ho [ref 41]: single-integrator agents, quadratic value functions, feedback Nash equilibrium (SFNE) via coupled Riccati differential equations (CRDE). Two realism gaps motivate the work: (i) classical TAD games assume perfect, unlimited observation, whereas real robots/vehicles have finite sensing radii, so a time-varying visibility network governs what each agent actually knows; (ii) all defenders are assumed cooperative and rational, while in practice some defenders may behave anomalously — "greedy" (over-aggressive) or "fearful" (under-motivated), e.g., a compromised insider in competitive-intelligence scenarios [ref 34]. The paper merges both aspects into one multi-agent TAD framework and quantifies how anomalies affect victory conditions and capture time.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
**Problem**: decide optimal feedback strategies for n defenders (n_c of them anomalous), m attackers (m ≤ n) and one active target with single-integrator dynamics, under two information regimes: (i) attackers have unlimited observation while defenders/target have limited visibility (asymmetric information), and (ii) all agents have limited visibility (symmetric imperfect information); then characterize the defender team's victory conditions and interception time as a function of the anomaly profile.

**Gaps, with claim verification**:

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Modeling**: a multi-agent TAD nonzero-sum LQ differential game with n defenders, m attackers (m ≤ n), one active target, single-integrator dynamics in relative coordinates, and a novel two-parameter anomaly model: defender $D^I_l$'s interception drive $\mu_{lj}$ scales its quadratic penalty on defender–attacker distances ($\mu_{lj}>1$ greedy, $0\le\mu_{lj}<1$ fearful, $=1$ normal) — Definition 2 (p. 4).
2. **Information structure**: binary-visibility adjacency matrices $\mathcal{A}(t)$ (16) and $\mathcal{A}^*(t)$ (17) inducing a time-varying directed visibility network; masking matrices $\mathcal{I}_h(t)$ (20) that project Riccati feedback onto each agent's observable subspace; adaptive state-feedback strategy classes (19) (Definition 4).
3. **Theory — inverse game theory**: Theorem 1 (p. 8): for ARBITRARY gain matrices $\mathcal{M}_\Xi(t)$, there exist parametric performance indices (22) under which the visibility-adapted strategies plus the attackers' SFNE form a Nash equilibrium (existence by construction, Remark 6). Theorem 2 (p. 10): the fully symmetric case where attackers also adapt, with four parametric indices and generalized cost (28).
4. **Algorithm**: the "optimal performance criterion" — minimize the Frobenius-norm mismatch functional $\Gamma(t)$ (23)–(24) (and $\Gamma'(t)$ (28)) by gradient descent with closed-form gradients (p. 9) to pick the gains $\mathcal{M}^*_\Xi(t)$; gains are independent of initial states, and strategies are "consistent" with the SFNE at the full-visibility instant $t_m$ (Definitions 5–6).
5. **Victory conditions and capture time**: Theorem 3 (p. 10): with anomalies present the defender team still wins iff the number of greedy defenders satisfies $0<n_{c1}\le n$ (or fearful defenders $0<n_{c2}\le n-1$, i.e., at least one normal defender must remain). Definition 7 + Lemma 2: closed-form lower bound of the "minimal interception value" $\eta_{\min}$ (30)–(31). Lemma 3 + Theorem 4: greedy defenders do not increase capture time ($t_g\le t_n$) and fearful defenders do not decrease it ($t_s\ge t_n$), under matrix-inequality conditions on the aggregated value weights.
6. **Experiment**: 7-agent simulation (4 defenders with imperfect visibility, 2 attackers with perfect information, 1 target) quantifying $t_n=0.259$, $t_g=0.224$, $t_s=0.303$ (Sec. VI).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results (exhaustive)

### 5.0 Setup: dynamics, information, costs, termination
- **Dynamics (eq. (1), p. 3)**: every agent is a 2-D single integrator, $\dot x_{D_i}=u_{D_i x},\ \dot y_{D_i}=u_{D_i y}$ (likewise attackers $A_j$ and target $T$). Relative-state vector $Z(t)=\mathrm{col}\{Z_{D_1A_1},\dots,Z_{D_nA_m},Z_{TA_1},\dots,Z_{TA_m}\}\in\mathbb{R}^{2m(n+1)}$ with $\dot Z(t)=\sum_i B_{D_i}u_{D_i}(t)+B_Tu_T(t)+\sum_j B_{A_j}u_{A_j}(t)$ (eq. (2)); $B_{A_j}$ carries the $-1$ block (column $e^j_{n+1}$) so attacker controls subtract. After splitting anomalous/normal defenders (eq. (3)): $\dot Z=B^I_Du^I_D+B^{II}_Du^{II}_D+B_Tu_T+B_Au_A$, with $B_A=-1_{m(n+1)\times m}\otimes I_2$ (p. 4).
- **Visibility (Definition 1, p. 3)**: agent $h$ perceives $\ell$ at time $t$ iff $\|x_h(t)-x_\ell(t)\|_2=\|Z_{hA_j}(t)-Z_{\ell A_j}(t)\|_2\le R_h$; this defines the directed graph $Q(t)=(\mathcal{H},E(t))$. Assumption: capture radii $r_{D_i}<R_{D_i}$, $r_{A_j}<R_{A_j}$.
- **Termination/winning conditions (p. 3)**: game ends when (a) every attacker $A_j$ is captured by some defender ($\|x_{D_i}-x_{A_j}\|_2\le r_{D_i}$) — a capturing defender then freezes permanently ("will remain stationary and will not continue to seek out new attackers"), or (b) any attacker captures the target ($\|x_{A_j}-x_T\|_2\le r_{A_j}$). The interaction horizon is assumed long enough ($[0,t_f]$) for termination.
- **Anomalous-defender modeling (Definition 2, p. 4)**: anomaly is purely a reward-coefficient phenomenon. The $l$-th anomalous defender's individual metric is
$$J^I_{D_l}=\tfrac12\int_0^{t_f}\Big(\sum_{A_j}\mu_{lj}\|Z_{D_lA_j}(t)\|^2_{Q^I_{lA_j}}+\|u^I_{D_l}(t)\|^2_{W^I_{D_l}}\Big)dt+\tfrac12\sum_{A_j}\mu_{lj}\|Z_{D_lA_j}(t_f)\|^2_{F^I_{lA_j}}\quad(4)$$
with $F^I_{lA_j}=f^I_{lA_j}I_2$, $Q^I_{lA_j}=q^I_{lA_j}I_2$, $W^I_{D_l}=w^I_{D_l}I_2$, $f,q\ge0$, $w>0$. **Greedy**: $\mu_{lj}>1$ (over-weighted interception desire); **fearful**: $0\le\mu_{lj}<1$ (under-weighted); normal: $\mu_{lj}=1$. Team costs: anomalous defenders jointly minimize (5) with $F^I_D,Q^I_D$ block-diagonal carrying the $\mu$-scaled entries and $0_{(\kappa+1)\times(\kappa+1)}$ blocks ($\kappa=n-n_c$) for normal-defender and target columns; normal defenders minimize (6)–(7) ($F^{II}_{iA_j}=f^{II}_{iA_j}I_2$ etc.); the target minimizes (8) with **negative** weights $F_T=\mathrm{diag}\{0_{2n\times2n},-f_{TA_1}I_2,\dots\}$, $Q_T$ likewise (minimizing $J_T$ pushes $\|Z_{TA_j}\|$ up ⇒ escape); the attacker team minimizes (9) with $F_A=\mathrm{diag}\{-f_{A_1D_1},\dots,-f_{A_1D_n},f_{A_1T},\dots\}\otimes I_2$ (positive weight on closing on the target, negative weight on defender–attacker distance ⇒ penalize proximity of defenders).
- **Strategy classes (eq. (10))**: feedback strategies are linear in the full relative state, $u_h(t)=S_h(t,Z(t))=C_h(t)Z(t)$, $C_h\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times2m(n+1)}$.
- **Nash equilibrium (Definition 3, eq. (11), p. 5)**: standard SFNE inequalities over the four controller groups $(S^I_D,S^{II}_D,S_T,S_A)$.

### 5.1 Lemma 1 (p. 5, eqs. (12)–(13)) — perfect-information SFNE via CRDE
- **Statement**: For the $(n+m+1)$-agent nonzero-sum TAD game of (3)–(9), the SFNE controls are
$$u^*_{D^I}(t)=-W^{-1}_{D^I}B'_{D^I}P_{D^I}(t)Z(t),\quad u^*_{D^{II}}(t)=-W^{-1}_{D^{II}}B'_{D^{II}}P_{D^{II}}(t)Z(t),\quad u^*_T(t)=-W^{-1}_TB'_TP_T(t)Z(t),\quad u^*_A(t)=-W^{-1}_AB'_AP_A(t)Z(t)\tag{12}$$
where $P_{D^I},P_{D^{II}},P_T,P_A$ solve the coupled Riccati differential equations on $[0,t_f]$:
$$\dot P_{D^I}=-Q_{D^I}+P_{D^I}S_{D^I}P_{D^I}+P_{D^I}(S_{D^{II}}P_{D^{II}}+S_TP_T+S_AP_A)+(P_{D^{II}}S_{D^{II}}+P_TS_T+P_AS_A)P_{D^I}\tag{13}$$
(and three symmetric equations cyclically permuting the roles), with terminal conditions $P_{D^I}(t_f)=F_{D^I}$, $P_{D^{II}}(t_f)=F_{D^{II}}$, $P_T(t_f)=F_T$, $P_A(t_f)=F_A$.
- **Assumptions**: finite horizon; weights as in (4)–(9); $m\le n$. The matrices $S_\Xi$ are **never explicitly defined** in the paper — implicitly $S_\Xi=B_\Xi W_\Xi^{-1}B'_\Xi$ (standard Starr–Ho form, ref [41]); note this implicit definition when reproducing.
- **Proof approach** (3 steps): (i) this is the classical nonzero-sum LQ result of Starr & Ho applied to the TAD cost stack (4)–(9) — the paper cites [41] and does not re-prove; (ii) each $\dot P_\Xi$ equation follows from completing the square of $\tfrac{d}{dt}(\tfrac12Z'P_\Xi Z)$ along $\dot Z=\sum B_\Xi u_\Xi$; (iii) the coupling terms $P_\Xi S_\Theta P_\Theta+P_\Theta S_\Theta P_\Xi$ come from the other players' best-response substitutions. Decisive structure: the affine-in-$u$ quadratic value with terminal condition $P_\Xi(t_f)=F_\Xi$ makes (12) minimize each $\hat J_\Xi$ simultaneously.
- **Meaning/extensibility**: with perfect information the anomaly coefficients $\mu_{lj}$ enter only through $Q_{D^I},F_{D^I}$, so greedy defenders simply weight interception errors more heavily in their own Riccati solution. Analytic, finite-horizon, global (no proof of global existence of CRDE solutions is given — inherited from [41]).

### 5.2 Imperfect information structure (Sec. III-B, p. 6, eqs. (14)–(17))
Binary visibility functions $p_{ij}(t)=1$ iff $i\to A_j\in E(t)$ (14); defender-team adjacency $[\mathcal{C}(t)]_{il},[\mathcal{C}(t)]_{i(n+1)},[\mathcal{C}(t)]_{(n+1)i}\in\{0,1\}$; augmented matrices $\mathcal{A}(t)=[\,\mathcal{C}(t)\ \ \mathcal{H}(t)\,]$ (16) with $\mathcal{H}=[p_{D_iA_j}]$, and for the all-limited case $\mathcal{A}^*(t)=[\,\mathcal{C}\ \ \mathcal{H}\ \ \phi_{DT}\ \ \phi_A\,]$ (17) adding attacker rows ($\phi_{DT}$: attackers' view of defenders/target; $\phi_A$: attacker-to-attacker visibility, zero diagonal).

### 5.3 Adaptive strategies (eqs. (18)–(21), pp. 6–7)
- **Eq. (18)** rewrites the SFNE (12a)–(12c) in a decomposed form whose terms are grouped by which relative positions the agent can actually observe, e.g. for anomalous defender $D^I_l$:
$$u^*_{D^I_l}(t)=w^{-1}_{D^I_l}\Big[\sum_{\alpha=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}P^{\alpha l j}_{D^I}(t)\Big]Z_{D^I_l}(t)+w^{-1}_{D^I_l}\Big[\sum_{\alpha=1}^{m}\sum_{k=1}^{n_c}P^{\alpha l k}_{D^I}(t)\big](Z_{D^I_k}(t)-Z_{D^I_l}(t)\big)+w^{-1}_{D^I_l}\Big[\sum_{\alpha=1}^{m}\sum_{i=n_c+1}^{n}P^{\alpha l i}_{D^I}(t)\big](Z_{D^{II}_i}(t)-Z_{D^I_l}(t)\big)+w^{-1}_{D^I_l}\Big[\sum_{\alpha=1}^{m}P^{\alpha l(n+1)}_{D^I}(t)\big](Z_T(t)-Z_{D^I_l}(t))\tag{18a}$$
where $P^{\alpha lj}_{D^I}$ is the $(\alpha l, j)$-entry of $P_{D^I}$; (18b), (18c) analogously for normal defenders and the target. Once the visibility edge to a teammate/target is absent, **the coefficient of the corresponding term becomes zero** (Remark 3) — so (18) is not itself an equilibrium any more.
- **Definition 4 (p. 7, eq. (19))**: the implementable (network-adapted) strategy sets $\sigma^s_{D^I_l}=\{S^s_{D^I_l}(t,\mathcal{M}_{D^I_l}(t),Z(t))=\mathcal{M}_{D^I_l}(t)\mathcal{I}_{D^I_l}(t)Z(t)\}$, similarly $\sigma^s_{D^{II}_i}$, $\sigma^s_T$, with gain matrices $\mathcal{M}_\Xi(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times2(n+m+1)}$ assembled from $2\times2$ blocks ($\mathcal{M}^{D_l}_{D^I_l}=0$ self-block, etc.).
- **Masking matrix (eq. (20))**: $\mathcal{I}_h(t):=\mathrm{diag}\{[\mathcal{A}(t)]_{h\bullet}\}\Big[\begin{smallmatrix}L&0_{n+1}&\cdots&0_{n+1}\\ K_1&&&\\ K_2&&&\\ \vdots&&&\\ K_m&&&\end{smallmatrix}\Big]\otimes I_2$, $h\in D^I\cup D^{II}$, $L=I_{n+1}-e^h{}'_{n+1}\otimes(1_{n+1}-e^h_{n+1})$, $K_1=\dots=K_m=(-e^h{}'_n,\,1)_{1\times(n+1)}$; for the target ($h=n+1$) use $K_i=(0,\dots,0,1)$. $\mathcal{I}_h(t)$ zeroes every unobservable coordinate of $Z$. Team strategy $S^s_{D^ID^{II}T}$ stacks the three (21).
- **Assumption 1 (p. 7)** (anti-stagnation initial visibility): every defender/target sees someone at $t=0$; some defender sees $T$; every attacker is seen by some defender. Assumption 2 (p. 9) adds symmetric conditions for attackers in Scenario 2.
- **Remark 4**: no defender–target communication; behavioral rule — pursue any observed attacker, else move toward visible teammates (information gathering), else stay put.

### 5.4 Theorem 1 (p. 8, eqs. (22a)–(22d)) — inverse-game Nash equilibrium for Scenario 1 (asymmetric information)
- **Statement**: Consider the $(n+m+1)$-agent finite-horizon TAD game (3)–(9). For ARBITRARY matrices $\mathcal{M}_{D^I}(t),\mathcal{M}_T(t),\mathcal{M}_{D^{II}}(t)$, $t\in[0,t_f]$, the strategies $S^s_\Xi(t,Z(t),\mathcal{M}_\Xi(t))=\mathcal{M}_\Xi(t)\mathcal{I}_\Xi(t)Z(t)$ ($\Xi\in D^I\cup D^{II}\cup\{T\}$) together with the attackers' unmodified SFNE $S^*_A(t)=-W_A^{-1}B'_AP_A(t)Z(t)$ form a Nash equilibrium with respect to the parametric performance indices
$$J^s_{D^I}=\tfrac12\|Z(t_f)\|^2_{F_{D^I}}+\tfrac12\int_0^{t_f}\big(\|u_{D^I}\|^2_{W_{D^I}}+\|Z\|^2_{Q^s_{D^I}(t)}-u'_{D^I}(t)L_1(t)Z(t)-Z'(t)L_1'(t)u_{D^I}(t)\big)dt\tag{22a}$$
(and (22b)–(22c) with $L_2,L_3$ for $D^{II},T$; the attacker keeps a purely quadratic (22d)). Here
$$L_1(t)=W_{D^I}\mathcal{M}_{D^I}(t)\mathcal{I}_{D^I}(t)+B'_{D^I}P_{D^I}(t),\quad L_2=W_{D^{II}}\mathcal{M}_{D^{II}}\mathcal{I}_{D^{II}}+B'_{D^{II}}P_{D^{II}},\quad L_3=W_T\mathcal{M}_T\mathcal{I}_T+B'_TP_T,$$
$$Q^s_{D^I}(t)=\mathcal{I}'_{D^I}\mathcal{M}'_{D^I}W_{D^I}\mathcal{M}_{D^I}\mathcal{I}_{D^I}-2P_{D^I}B_{D^{II}}\mathcal{M}_{D^{II}}\mathcal{I}_{D^{II}}-2P_{D^I}B_T\mathcal{M}_T\mathcal{I}_T-\textstyle\sum_\Xi 2P_{D^I}B_\Xi W_\Xi^{-1}B'_\Xi P_\Xi+P_{D^I}B_{D^I}W^{-1}_{D^I}B'_{D^I}P_{D^I}+Q_{D^I}(t),$$
with analogous $Q^s_{D^{II}},Q^s_T$, and $Q^s_A(t)=Q_A(t)-\sum_\Xi 2P_AB_\Xi\mathcal{M}_\Xi\mathcal{I}_\Xi-\sum_\Xi 2P_AB_\Xi W_\Xi^{-1}B'_\Xi P_\Xi$; $\Xi$ ranges over $D^I\cup D^{II}\cup\{T\}$; $P_A,P_\Xi$ are the symmetric CRDE solutions (13).
- **Assumptions**: perfect-information attackers that are unaware of the defenders' limited perception (they keep (12d)); costs (5)–(9) apply only under full perception, hence the replacement indices (22).
- **Proof approach** (Appendix, pp. 13–14, 5 steps): (1) take Lyapunov/value functions $V_f=\tfrac12Z'P_fZ$ (39) with $P_f$ from CRDE (13); (2) differentiate $V_{D^I}$ along the closed-loop dynamics and substitute the CRDE to get (40); (3) complete squares — the decisive identity is (41): $\dot V_{D^I}=\tfrac12\big(\|u_{D^I}-\mathcal{M}_{D^I}\mathcal{I}_{D^I}Z\|^2_{W_{D^I}}-\|u_{D^I}\|^2_{W_{D^I}}-\|Z\|^2_{Q^s_{D^I}}+u'_{D^I}L_1Z+Z'L_1'u_{D^I}\big)+Z'P_{D^I}B_{D^{II}}[u_{D^{II}}-\mathcal{M}_{D^{II}}\mathcal{I}_{D^{II}}Z]+Z'P_{D^I}B_T[u_T-\mathcal{M}_T\mathcal{I}_TZ]+Z'P_{D^I}B_A[u_A+W_A^{-1}B'_AP_AZ]$; (4) integrate $0\to t_f$, use $P_{D^I}(t_f)=F_{D^I}$, obtaining the "completion" representation (42): $J^s_{D^I}=\tfrac12\int_0^{t_f}\big(\|u_{D^I}-S^s_{D^I}\|^2_{W_{D^I}}+2Z'P_{D^I}B_{D^{II}}[u_{D^{II}}-S^s_{D^{II}}]+2Z'P_{D^I}B_T[u_T-S^s_T]+2Z'P_{D^I}B_A[u_A-S^*_A]\big)dt+V_{D^I}|_{t=0}$, and three analogous representations; (5) since every term is nonnegative and each is individually minimized at the adopted strategy holding the others fixed, the profile is a Nash equilibrium for (22). Local/global: the NE property is global in state (quadratic forms), finite-horizon, analytic given CRDE solutions; existence is by construction (Remark 6, p. 10) — this is the paper's answer to the usual CRDE-existence difficulty.
- **Physical meaning**: the cross terms $-u'L_kZ$ and the extra terms in $Q^s_\Xi$ are exactly the "price" of perception limits — the indices are reverse-engineered so that best response to (22) reproduces the visibility-feasible strategy $\mathcal{M}_\Xi\mathcal{I}_\Xi Z$. Bayesian-game interpretation for the asymmetric-information scenario (Remark 5, p. 8).
- **Extensibility**: any gain choice works; the design freedom is used next for performance matching. Minor typo to be aware of: in the printed $Q^{s*}_A$ (p. 10) the sum term reads $\sum_\Theta 2P_T(t)B_\Theta W_\Theta^{-1}B'_\Theta P_\Theta(t)$ — the leading $P_T$ is inconsistent with the surrounding $P_A$ terms (likely should be $P_A$ or $P_\Theta$); recorded as printed.
- **Proof gap (minor)**: the paper does not verify that the constructed $Q^s_\Xi$, $L_k$ correspond to sensible (e.g., sign-definite) weights for arbitrary $\mathcal{M}_\Xi$; the "equilibrium" is w.r.t. the surrogate indices, not the original (5)–(9).

### 5.5 Definitions 5–6 and gain optimization (pp. 8–9, eqs. (23)–(24))
- **Definition 5** ("optimal performance criterion"): choose $\mathcal{M}^*_\Xi(t)$ minimizing the weighted combination of $\|Q^s_\Xi-Q_\Xi\|$, $\|Q^s_A-Q_A\|$, $\|L_k\|$ so that (22) best matches the original (5)–(9).
- **Definition 6**: $t_m\in[0,t_f]$ = a moment when defenders/target observe all agents; a gain-parameterized strategy is **consistent** at $t_m$ if $u^s_\Xi(t_m)=\mathcal{M}_\Xi(t_m)\mathcal{I}_\Xi(t_m)Z(t_m)=S_\Xi(t_m,Z(t_m))$ (i.e., it coincides with the SFNE under full visibility).
- **Optimization (23)–(24)**: $\Gamma(t)=\sum_{i=1}^4\omega_i\|Q^s_{(\cdot)}(t)-Q_{(\cdot)}(t)\|_F^2+\omega_5\|L_1\|_F^2+\omega_6\|L_2\|_F^2+\omega_7\|L_3\|_F^2$, $\omega_i\in[0,1]$, minimized by $\Gamma(\mathcal{M}^*_{D^I},\mathcal{M}^*_{D^{II}},\mathcal{M}^*_T)\le\Gamma(\mathcal{M}_{D^I},\mathcal{M}_{D^{II}},\mathcal{M}_T)$ (24). Closed-form gradients are derived (p. 9, unnumbered), e.g. $\nabla_{\mathcal{M}_{D^I_l}(t)}\Gamma(t)=(e^l{}'_{n_c}\otimes I_2)\big[4\omega_1W_{D^I}\mathcal{M}_{D^I}\mathcal{I}_{D^I}(Q^s_{D^I}-Q_{D^I})-4\omega_2P_{D^{II}}B_{D^I}(Q^s_{D^{II}}-Q_{D^{II}})-4\omega_3P_TB_{D^I}(Q^s_T-Q_T)-4\omega_4B'_{D^I}P_A(Q^s_A-Q_A)+2\omega_5W_{D^I}L_1\big]\mathcal{I}'_{D^I_l}$ (and cyclic analogues), solved by gradient-based iteration. **Key stated property**: $\mathcal{M}^*_\Xi$ is independent of initial states, so it is a genuine feedback gain; tuning $\omega_i$ yields non-inferior (Pareto) solutions.

### 5.6 Theorem 2 (p. 10, eqs. (25)–(29)) — Scenario 2, all agents imperfect
- **Statement**: with attackers also visibility-limited, using $\mathcal{A}^*(t)$ (17), attacker-adapted strategies $S^s_{A_j}(t,\mathcal{M}_{A_j}(t),Z(t))=\mathcal{M}_{A_j}(t)\mathcal{I}_{A_j}(t)Z(t)$ (25)–(26) (with masking $\mathcal{I}_j(t)$ built from the $(n+1+j)$-th row of $\mathcal{A}^*$, self-block $K_j=0'_{n+1}$), the four adapted strategies form a Nash equilibrium w.r.t. parametric indices $J^{s*}_{D^I},J^{s*}_{D^{II}},J^{s*}_T,J^{s*}_A$ which augment (22) by the attacker cross term $L_4(t)=W_A\mathcal{M}_A\mathcal{I}_A+B'_AP_A$ and corrections $Q^{s*}_{D^I}=Q^s_{D^I}-2P_{D^I}B_A\mathcal{M}_A\mathcal{I}_A-2P_{D^I}B_AW_A^{-1}B'_AP_A$ (and analogues; $Q^{s*}_A$ gains the $\mathcal{M}_A$ and $P_A B_\Xi\mathcal{M}_\Xi\mathcal{I}_\Xi$ terms as printed on p. 10).
- **Proof**: "the same as that of Theorem 1" (p. 10) — i.e., identical completion-of-squares; not restated.
- **Generalized criterion (28)–(29)**: $\Gamma'(t)=\sum_{i=1}^4\omega_i\|Q^{s*}_{(\cdot)}-Q_{(\cdot)}\|_F^2+\sum_{k=1}^8\omega_{k+4}\|L_k\|_F^2$ with the new $\|L_4\|_F^2$ term; solution method identical, omitted.
- **Assumption 2** (p. 9) prevents attacker stagnation in this scenario. **Remark 6**: Nash existence in BOTH scenarios is inherently guaranteed by the inverse-game construction (strategies fixed first, metrics reverse-engineered).

### 5.7 Theorem 3 (p. 10) — victory conditions with anomalous defenders
- **Statement**: In the imperfect TAD game (3) under optimal strategies (12d) and (21), if anomalous defenders exist, the defender team's victory conditions are: the number of greedy defenders satisfies $0<n_{c1}\le n$ (or the number of fearful defenders satisfies $0<n_{c2}\le n-1$). That is, the team may consist entirely of greedy defenders, but with fearful defenders at least one normal defender must remain to guide them.
- **Assumptions**: Scenario 1 information structure; Definition 2 anomaly semantics; strategies consistent at $t_m$.
- **Proof approach** (Appendix, p. 14, 6 steps): (1) team value $V_D=\tfrac12\|Z\|^2_{P_{D^I}}+\tfrac12\|Z\|^2_{P_{D^{II}}}$; by Definition 2, $V_D\ge0$; (2) differentiate using (41) to get (43): $\dot V_D=\tfrac12(-\|Z\|^2_{Q^s_D}-\|u_{D^I}\|^2_{W_{D^I}}-\|u_{D^{II}}\|^2_{W_{D^{II}}}+u'_{D^I}L_1Z+Z'L_1'u_{D^I}+u'_{D^{II}}L_2Z+Z'L_2'u_{D^{II}})$ with $Q^s_D=Q^s_{D^I}+Q^s_{D^{II}}$; (3) at the consistency time $t_m$ the parametric metrics approach the originals, so cross terms vanish and (44) holds: $\dot V_D=\tfrac12(-\|Z\|^2_{Q_D}-\|u_{D^I}\|^2-\|u_{D^{II}}\|^2)$, $Q_D=Q_{D^I}+Q_{D^{II}}$; (4) $\mu_{lj}>1\Rightarrow Q_{D^I}\succeq0$, normal $\Rightarrow Q_{D^{II}}\succeq0$, so $Q_D\succeq0$ (the paper calls it "a Laplacian matrix" — terminology is loose; it is not a graph Laplacian), and $W_{D^I},W_{D^{II}}\succ0$ give $\dot V_D\le-\tfrac12Z'Q_DZ\le0$; (5) integrate: $\int_0^{t_f}Z'Q_DZ\,dt\le V_D(0)-V_D(t_f)$; $\dot V_D$ negative semidefinite $\Rightarrow V_D$ converges as $t_f\to\infty$, hence $\lim\int Z'Q_DZ\,dt$ exists and is bounded, so $Z'Q_DZ\to0$ — "then, we have obtained the victory conditions"; (6) fearful case: $0\le\mu_{lj}<1$ diminishes capture willingness, so require $0<n_{c2}\le n-1$ to keep at least one normal defender; proof "follows a similar approach … omitted".
- **Proof gaps**: (i) the implication "$Z'Q_DZ\to0$ ⇒ capture of all attackers" is asserted, not proven — convergence of a weighted quadratic form to zero does not by itself establish $\|Z_{D_iA_j}\|\le r_{D_i}$ for every $j$ (weights could degenerate; no observability/positive-definiteness of $Q_D$ on the capture-relevant subspace is invoked); (ii) the vanishing of the cross terms between (43) and (44) at $t_m$ is stated, not derived (it holds only on the range where $\mathcal{M}^*_\Xi\mathcal{I}_\Xi$ reproduces $-W_\Xi^{-1}B'_\Xi P_\Xi$); (iii) a typo on p. 14: "the count of fearful **attackers** is confined to the range $0<n_{c2}\le n-1$" should read fearful *defenders*. The result is qualitative/analytic; no rate or quantitative margin is given.

### 5.8 Definition 7 + Lemma 2 (p. 11, eqs. (30)–(37)) — minimal interception value
- **Definition 7 (30)**: $\eta_{\min}=\min_Z\big[-\dot V(Z)/V(Z)\big]$, $\forall V(Z)\ne0$, where $V(Z)=\tfrac12\|Z\|^2_{P_{D^I}}+\tfrac12\|Z\|^2_{P_{D^{II}}}+\tfrac12\|Z\|^2_{P_T}+\tfrac12\|Z\|^2_{P_A}$ is the aggregated value function. Larger $\eta_{\min}$ ⇒ shorter capture time (attenuation-coefficient heuristic borrowed from [42] Zheng, *Linear System Theory*). Notation: $Q^s=Q^s_{D^I}+Q^s_{D^{II}}+Q^s_T+Q^s_A$, $P=P_{D^I}+P_{D^{II}}+P_T+P_A$, terminal $\bar Q^s=F_{D^I}+F_{D^{II}}+F_T+F_A$.
- **Assumption 3**: $Q^s\succeq0$, $\bar Q^s\succeq0$. **Remark 7**: the compensation term $Q^s_{D^I}$ is indispensable with fearful defenders; constructing $Q^s_{D^{II}}$ lets normal defenders offset negative elements of $Q^s_{D^I}$ ("benefits of teamwork").
- **Assumption 4**: $\mathrm{rank}(P)=\mathrm{rank}(P,Q^s)$ — control matching the objective; equivalent to existence of $\Phi$ with $C=\Phi G$ where $P=GG'=G^2$, $Q^s=CC'$, $\Gamma=FF'$ (symmetric square roots); also $\Psi$ with $F=\Psi G$.
- **Lemma 2 (31)** [adapted from ref [7]]: under the above, the minimal interception value admits
$$\eta_{\min}=\lambda_{\min}(\Phi'\Phi)+\lambda_{\min}(\Psi'\Psi)+\frac{\epsilon_{\min}}{n+m+1}\lambda_{\min}(G'G),\tag{31}$$
where $\epsilon_{\min}=\min\{\lambda_{\min}(S_{D^I_l}),\lambda_{\min}(S_{D^{II}_i}),\lambda_{\min}(S_T),\lambda_{\min}(S_{A_j})\}$ and (per the Notations, p. 3) $\lambda_{\min}$ = smallest **nonzero** eigenvalue.
- **Proof approach** (p. 11, 5 steps): (1) with strategies (12d)+(21), $\dot V=-\tfrac12Z'Q^sZ-\tfrac12Z'\Gamma Z-\tfrac12Z'\mathcal{B}Z$ (32), $\mathcal{B}=P_{D^I}S_{D^I}P_{D^I}+P_{D^{II}}S_{D^{II}}P_{D^{II}}+P_TS_TP_T+P_AS_AP_A$; define $\eta_1=Z'Q^sZ/Z'PZ$, $\eta_2=Z'\Gamma Z/Z'PZ$, $\eta_3=Z'\mathcal{B}Z/Z'PZ$; (2) Rayleigh-quotient bounds via the square-root factorizations: $\eta_1=Z'G'\Phi'\Phi GZ/Z'G'GZ\ge\lambda_{\min}(\Phi'\Phi)$ (33), $\eta_2\ge\lambda_{\min}(\Psi'\Psi)$ (34); (3) for $\eta_3$ prove $P^2_{D^I}+P^2_{D^{II}}+P^2_T+P^2_A\ge\frac{1}{n+m+1}P^2$ (35): introduce $\bar P_{D^I},\bar P_A\ge0$ with $P^2_{D^I}+P^2_{D^{II}}+P^2_T+P^2_A=\bar P^2_{D^I}+P^2_{D^{II}}+P^2_T+\bar P^2_A$, then $\bar P^2_{D^I}+P^2_{D^{II}}+P^2_T+\bar P^2_A\ge\frac{1}{n+m+1}(\bar P_{D^I}+P_{D^{II}}+P_T+\bar P_A)^2\ge\frac{1}{n+m+1}(P_{D^I}+P_{D^{II}}+P_T+P_A)^2=\frac{1}{n+m+1}P^2$ (36) — the decisive inequality is a Cauchy–Schwarz/Jensen-type bound $(\sum X_i)^2\le(n+m+1)\sum X_i^2$; (4) hence $\eta_3\ge\frac{\epsilon}{n+m+1}\lambda_{\min}(G'G)$ (37); (5) sum (33), (34), (37) to get (31). Local/global: analytic, global in $Z$; hinges on the "diffusion property" (all of $Q^s,\Gamma,P$ PSD with 0 as smallest eigenvalue, so $\lambda_{\min}$ means smallest nonzero).
- **Proof gap**: step (3) asserts the existence of the $\bar P$ decomposition and the intermediate step "after some mathematical computations" without derivation; the PSD-ness of each $P_\Xi$ is used implicitly (each team's value can be sign-indefinite in principle, e.g., the target's and attackers' costs have negative weights).

### 5.9 Lemma 3 (p. 11, eq. (38)) — CRDE monotonic comparison
- **Statement**: Let $\hat P$ and $P$ be CRDE solutions under conditions $\hat Q^s+\hat\Omega+\hat\Gamma\succeq Q^s+\Omega+\Gamma$ and $\hat{\bar Q}^s\succeq\bar Q^s$. Then $\hat P-P\succeq0$.
- **Proof** (3 steps): define $\tilde P=\hat P-P$; $\dot{\tilde P}=\tilde PS\tilde P+PS\tilde P+\tilde PSP-(\hat Q^s+\hat\Omega+\hat\Gamma-Q^s-\Omega-\Gamma)$; hence $\dot{\tilde P}\preceq(\tfrac12\tilde PS+PS)\tilde P+\tilde P(\tfrac12S\tilde P+SP)$ (38), so $\dot{\tilde P}\preceq0$, and since $\tilde P(t_f)=\hat{\bar Q}^s-\bar Q^s\succeq0$, integration backwards gives $\tilde P\succeq0$.
- **Proof gap (notation)**: **$\Omega$ is never defined anywhere in the paper** — it appears first in Lemma 3's hypothesis and later only through its $(1,1)$ block in the proof of Theorem 4 (p. 15: $\Omega=\begin{bmatrix}\Omega_{11}&\Omega_{12}\\\Omega_{21}&\Omega_{22}\end{bmatrix}$). $\Gamma$ is introduced via the square-root factorization $\Gamma=FF'$ and the value-derivative decomposition (32). Readers reproducing Theorem 4 must reverse-engineer $\Omega$ (apparently the remaining quadratic-form component of $\dot V$ complementary to $Q^s$ and $\Gamma$).

### 5.10 Theorem 4 (p. 11) — capture-time monotonicity in anomaly type
- **Statement**: In imperfect TAD games (3) under optimal adaptive feedback strategies (12d)+(21), Assumptions 3–4 and the conditions of Theorem 3, with $t_n$ = capture time when all defenders are normal, $t_g$ with greedy, $t_s$ with fearful:
  - **Case 1**: $t_g\le t_n$ if $Q^s_g+\Gamma_g+\Omega_g\succeq Q^s_n+\Gamma_n+\Omega_n$ and $\bar Q^s_g\succeq\bar Q^s_n$;
  - **Case 2**: $t_s\ge t_n$ if $Q^s_s+\Gamma_s+\Omega_s\preceq Q^s_n+\Gamma_n+\Omega_n$ and $\bar Q^s_s\preceq\bar Q^s_n$.
- **Proof approach** (Appendix, p. 15, 6 steps): (1) must show $\{\eta_{\min}\}_{greedy}\ge\{\eta_{\min}\}_{normal}$, i.e. the eigenvalue inequality $\lambda_{\min}(\Phi'_g\Phi_g)+\lambda_{\min}(\Psi'_g\Psi_g)+\frac{\epsilon}{n+m+1}\lambda_{\min}(G'_gG_g)\ge$ (normal counterpart); (2) from the matrix dominance and Lemma 3, $G_g\succeq G_n$, so $\lambda_{\min}(G'_gG_g)\ge\lambda_{\min}(G'_nG_n)$; (3) reduce to proving $\lambda_{\min}(\Phi'_g\Phi_g)+\lambda_{\min}(\Psi'_g\Psi_g)\ge\lambda_{\min}(\Phi'_n\Phi_n)+\lambda_{\min}(\Psi'_n\Psi_n)$ (45); (4) block-partition $\Phi=\begin{bmatrix}\Phi_*&0\\0&0\end{bmatrix}$, $\Psi=\begin{bmatrix}\Psi_*&0\\0&0\end{bmatrix}$, $G=\begin{bmatrix}G_*&0\\0&0\end{bmatrix}$ with $\Phi_*,\Psi_*,G_*\succ0$, $\mathrm{rank}(\Phi_*)=\mathrm{rank}(\Psi_*)=\mathrm{rank}(G_*)=C$; the decisive identity is $-2G'_*\dot G_*=G'_*\Phi'_*\Phi_*G_*+G'_*\Psi'_*\Psi_*G_*-G'_*G_*S_{11}G'_*G_*+\Gamma_{11}+\Omega_{11}$, which after "some mathematical calculations" gives $G^{-1}_*\Phi'_*\Phi_*G^{-1}_*+G^{-1}_*\Psi'_*\Psi_*G^{-1}_*\le S_{11}-2G^{-1}_*\dot G_*G^{-1}_*G^{-1}_*+\epsilon_{\min}(n+m-1+\frac{1}{n+m+1})I_{C\times C}$; (5) applying Lemma 3 to the pair $(\Phi_g,\Psi_g)$ vs $(\Phi_n,\Psi_n)$ gives $\dot\Phi_{*g}+\dot\Psi_{*g}\le\dot\Phi_{*n}+\dot\Psi_{*n}$, hence $G^{-1}_{g}\Phi'_{*n}\Phi_{*n}G^{-1}_{g}\dots\le G^{-1}_{g}\Phi'_{*g}\Phi_{*g}G^{-1}_{g}+\dots$, whence $\Phi'_ {*n}\Phi_{*n}+\Psi'_{*n}\Psi_{*n}\preceq\Phi'_{*g}\Phi_{*g}+\Psi'_{*g}\Psi_{*g}$ and (45) follows; (6) Case 2 by the same method. Local/global: analytic but **sketchy** — multiple steps are "after some mathematical calculations".
- **Proof gaps**: (i) the step "According to Lemma 3, $\dot\Phi_{*g}+\dot\Psi_{*g}\le\dot\Phi_{*n}+\dot\Psi_{*n}$" applies Lemma 3 to objects ($\Phi,\Psi$) for which a CRDE-like dynamics was never written; (ii) the unstated premise is that greedy defenders' $\mu>1$ indeed makes $Q^s_g+\Gamma_g+\Omega_g\succeq Q^s_n+\Gamma_n+\Omega_n$ — plausible since $\mu$ scales the positive interception weights, but not verified; (iii) $\Omega$ undefined (see 5.9).
- **Remark 8 (author-stated limitation)**: Theorem 4 covers only ONE anomaly type at a time; mixed greedy+fearful teams give an "indeterminate" overall effect and are left to future work.
- **Physical meaning**: greed accelerates team capture; fear slows it — quantified through the decay-rate proxy $\eta_{\min}$ rather than a direct capture-time formula. The capture time itself is computed numerically in simulation, not in closed form.

### 5.11 Remarks on multi-agent assignment
There is **no explicit attacker–defender assignment algorithm**: role allocation emerges implicitly from the visibility network and the Nash strategies — "Defenders will pursue any observed attacker directly" (Remark 4), and "Once a defender successfully captures an attacker, the defender will remain stationary and will not continue to seek out new attackers" (p. 3), so the effective matching is first-capture-first-frozen. The team-level results (Theorems 3–4) depend only on the anomaly counts $n_{c1},n_{c2}$, not on which defender is anomalous.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
**Author-stated**:
- Mixed greedy+fearful defender teams are not analyzed; combined effect on interception time "becomes indeterminate" (Remark 8, p. 11).
- Only one specific anomaly scenario per theorem; no coexistence analysis (Remark 8).
- Attacker-winning conditions are not characterized; only enabling factors listed qualitatively (Remark 9, p. 13).
- No inter-agent communication is modeled (Remark 4) — cooperation is implicit through shared strategy structure.
**Critical observations (with page/eq.)**:
1. **Undefined matrix $\Omega$** in Lemma 3 and Theorem 4 hypotheses (p. 11) — only its $2\times2$ block partition appears (p. 15); reproducibility of Theorem 4's hypotheses is compromised.
2. **$S_\Xi$ matrices in CRDE (13) never explicitly defined** (p. 5) — implicitly $B_\Xi W_\Xi^{-1}B'_\Xi$.
3. **Theorem 3's proof is informal**: "$Z'Q_DZ\to0$ ⇒ victory conditions" (p. 14) does not establish $\|Z_{D_iA_j}\|\le r_{D_i}$ for all $j$; "Laplacian matrix" terminology for $Q_D\succeq0$ is inaccurate; the fearful case proof is omitted ("similar approach"); typo "fearful attackers" for defenders (p. 14).
4. **Theorem 1's equilibrium is w.r.t. surrogate costs (22), not the original (5)–(9)**: the inverse-game trick guarantees existence by construction (Remark 6), but the agents are then optimal for metrics they arguably do not "own"; the gap between (22) and (5)–(9) is only minimized heuristically via (23)–(24) (no optimality/identifiability guarantee for the gradient scheme, no convergence proof).
5. **Sign-definiteness of $Q^s_\Xi$ for arbitrary gains is not established**, so Assumption 3 ($Q^s\succeq0$) is an additional unstated constraint on the gain solution, patched by Remark 7's "compensation" argument (p. 11).
6. **Capture-time result is indirect**: Theorem 4 compares the decay-rate proxy $\eta_{\min}$, not capture time itself; the inference "increasing $\eta_{\min}$ shortens capture time" (Definition 7 discussion, p. 11) is a heuristic from linear-system attenuation [42], valid when $V$ is a valid Lyapunov-like measure of capture progress — not proven here.
7. **Dynamic/termination subtleties**: frozen-after-capture defenders create hybrid dynamics (rank changes in the effective control set) that the CRDE framework (13) does not model post-capture; the paper sidesteps this by simulating only cases where captures nearly coincide ($t_1\approx t_2$).
8. Single integrator dynamics only; no speed superiority analysis (unlike the geometric TAD literature [15], [22], [26]); no obstacles/occlusion although invoked in Remark 9; scalability ( Curse of dimensionality of the $2m(n+1)$-dimensional CRDE with $m,n$ large) not discussed.
9. Early-access version: volume/issue/pages missing; minor typographical inconsistencies recorded above ($P_T$ vs $P_A$ in $Q^{s*}_A$, p. 10).

## 8. Citation Value
**Paragraph role**: P090 is the bridge paper between geometric TAD games and networked/partially-observable nonzero-sum LQ games. It contributes (a) a minimal, transferable formalization of "anomalous teammates" via a scalar drive coefficient $\mu$ in the team cost — usable in any Stackelberg/Nash LQ multiplayer setting; (b) an inverse-game-theory construction (Theorems 1–2) that converts arbitrary visibility-feasible feedback laws into Nash equilibria of reverse-engineered quadratic costs, with a Frobenius-matching gradient algorithm for gain selection; and (c) count-based victory conditions and a decay-rate ($\eta_{\min}$) analysis of capture time under greed/fear. In a paper on pursuit-evasion, reach-avoid, or target defense, cite it in the related-work paragraph on imperfect-information/multi-agent TAD games and on non-fully-rational or heterogeneous teammates; its $\mu$-parameterization is a convenient citation anchor for "teammate reliability/behavioral anomalies" in defense games, and its visibility-network masking $\mathcal{I}_h(t)$ is a citable device for limited-sensing LQ games generally. Suggested citation sentence: "Wang et al. [P090] model heterogeneous defender reliability in multi-agent TAD games by greed/fear coefficients $\mu_{lj}$ scaling each defender's interception penalty, and use inverse game theory to certify visibility-adapted feedback strategies as Nash equilibria, showing that greedy (fearful) defenders can only shorten (lengthen) the team's capture time." Note when comparing: the equilibrium is w.r.t. reverse-engineered costs (Sec. 7, item 4), and the capture-time claims ride on the $\eta_{\min}$ proxy rather than a direct time bound.
