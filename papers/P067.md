## 2. Research Background
Reach-avoid differential games model adversarial scenarios in which an evader tries to enter a target set while a pursuer tries to capture it first, with applications to robot competition, path planning, safety verification and reachability analysis (cited refs [1]–[5], incl. automation-assisted capture-the-flag, Huang et al., IEEE TCST 2015). The vast majority of reach-avoid analyses assume **complete information**: both players observe the full state. This paper studies the practically motivated case where the pursuer carries a **finite sensing radius** $r$ and therefore cannot see the evader outside its sensing disc, while the evader processes global information — so the pursuer must first *deploy* to a guard position and can only start pursuing after *detecting* the evader. Partial information both invalidates the classical non-anticipative information structure (hence complicates HJI-based solution of the game) and creates a "fuzzy region" of the plane in which the pursuer cannot certify victory. The paper answers with a geometric, barrier-based deployment design, validated on a quadrotor testbed (NOKOV motion capture, 24 cameras).

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
**Problem**: one pursuer P (faster, $\alpha = v_P/v_E > 1$, sensing radius $r$) versus one evader E (global information, unaware of $r$) in $\mathbb{R}^2$ with a straight target line $\mathcal{T}$ of length $l$ (endpoints $\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}$); P wins by capture ($\|\mathbf{x}_P-\mathbf{x}_E\|_2=0$), E wins by entering $\mathcal{T}$ (p. 2, Sec. II-A). Under Assumption 1 the pursuer has an open-loop deployment phase ($t<0$, position pre-specifiable under complete information; initial positions random under partial information, $\mathbf{x}_E^0\in\Omega_{play}-\mathcal{S}$); the defense strategy splits into an open-loop deployment strategy $\Gamma_P^d(\mathbf{x}_P,\mathbf{v}_P,\mathbf{v}_E)$ and a closed-loop capture strategy $\Gamma_P^c(\mathbf{x}_P,\mathbf{x}_E,\mathbf{v}_P,\mathbf{v}_E)$ (p. 2, eq. (2)).
**Gap, as stated by the authors** (p. 1–2): (i) complete-information defense strategies exist ([6] Yan et al., Automatica 2022; [7] Selvakumar & Bakolas, TCYB 2021; [12]–[14]) "but the open-loop deployment strategy was not further studied"; (ii) partial-information target-defense works ([8] Shishika et al., ICRA 2021; [9] Chen et al., Complexity 2022) "did not fully analyze the changes of the winning region with varying sensing radius" and were "confined to simple circular target domains in [8] or limited escape time in [9]"; (iii) HJI-based resolutions ([10] Garcia et al., RA-L 2020; [11] Lee & Bakolas, CDC 2021) do not extend because "the consideration of the partial information complicates the solution to the HJI equation".
**Verification against cited text**: [8] (Shishika, Maity, Dorothy, "Partial information target defense game", ICRA 2021) indeed studies a target-defense game with limited pursuer information; [9] (Chen et al., "Reach-avoid games with a time limit and detection range: a geometric approach") indeed adds detection range and a time limit; both are conference/journal companions held in the same corpus and the authors' characterizations are plausible; the specific criticisms ("winning-region variation with sensing radius not analyzed") are the authors' positioning claims and are not independently re-verified here. The claim that [6],[7],[12]–[14] only treat closed-loop capture and not open-loop deployment is [authors' claim]; the cited works do contain capture strategies for multiplayer reach-avoid games, and deployment/assignment is at most implicit there — plausible but not re-verified line-by-line.

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Theory (complete information)**: closed-form barrier $B(\mathbf{x}_P)=F_1\cup F_2\cup F_3$ (eq. (5)) for a straight target line, and **Theorem 1**: the deployment position minimizing the area of the evasion winning region is the target-line midpoint $\mathbf{x}_{opt}^c=(l/2,0)$, proved analytically via convexity (Hessian, eq. (9)), Lagrange multipliers, the mean value theorem for integrals (eq. (14)) and monotonicity arguments (eqs. (10)–(17)).
2. **Modeling (partial information)**: introduction of two new concepts — the **pursuit sensing winning region** $\mathcal{W}_P^\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{W}_P^\mathcal{B}\cap\mathcal{S}$ and the **fuzzy region** $\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{W}_P^\mathcal{B}-\mathcal{S}$ (Definition 2, p. 5) — and of the partial-information winning regions $\mathcal{W}_P^*$ (eq. (18)) with $\mathcal{W}_E^*=\mathcal{W}_E^\mathcal{B}$; **Lemma 2** characterizes the minimal sensing radius $d(\mathbf{x}_P^0)$ needed to cover the whole barrier (eqs. (19)–(20)).
3. **Theory/Algorithm (partial information)**: **Theorem 2** (eq. (21)): the optimal deployment is $\mathbf{x}_{opt}=(l/2,0)$ if $r>R^\alpha=(1+\alpha^{-1})l/2$, and $(l/2,\,r/(\alpha^{-1}-1))$ (sensing disc tangent to the barrier, hence the *entire* sensing disc becomes a certified capture zone) if $r\le R^\alpha$; packaged as **Algorithm 1** (deploy → stand still until detection → Apollonius-based capture strategy $\Gamma_{P}^{c\delta}$ of eq. (4)).
4. **Experiment**: hardware validation on a NOKOV optical-motion-capture quadrotor platform (24 cameras, 12 m × 5 m arena, target line 2 m) with two cases covering both regimes ($r\le R^\alpha$ and $r>R^\alpha$), demonstrating three-stage strategy execution and simulation–experiment consistency (Figs. 7–9).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results

### 5.1 Dynamics, information structure, termination (Sec. II, p. 2)
- **Dynamics (eq. (1))**: Isaacs "simple motion" mass points, $\dot{\mathbf{x}}_P=v_P\mathbf{u}_P$, $\dot{\mathbf{x}}_E=v_E\mathbf{u}_E$, controls $\mathbf{u}_P,\mathbf{u}_E\in\mathcal{U}=\{\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^2\,|\,\|\mathbf{u}\|_2=1 \text{ or } \mathbf{u}=\mathbf{0}\}$ (instantaneous heading change; stopping allowed).
- **Domain**: $\mathcal{T}$ a segment with $\|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{n}\|_2=l$; the affine hull splits $\mathbb{R}^2$ into target region $\Omega_{tar}$ and play region $\Omega_{play}$; origin at endpoint $\mathbf{m}$. Printed typo: "the target line belongs to the target region $\Omega_{play}$" (p. 2) — the highlighted $\Omega_{play}$ must be $\Omega_{tar}$; the *extension* of the line belongs to $\Omega_{play}$ (crossing the extension does not win).
- **Information (p. 2, below eq. (2))**: $I_P(t)=\{\mathbf{x}_P,\mathbf{v}_P,\mathbf{v}_E\}\cup\{\mathbf{x}_E\,|\,\mathbf{x}_E\in\mathcal{S}\}$, $\mathcal{S}=\{\mathbf{z}\,|\,\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}_P\|_2\le r\}$; $I_E(t)=\{\mathbf{x}_P,\mathbf{x}_E,\mathbf{v}_P,\mathbf{v}_E\}$ — the evader sees everything **except the pursuer's sensing radius $r$**. The classical non-anticipated structure is declared "not appropriate… under partial information"; a state-feedback structure is adopted instead (eq. (2): $\mathbf{u}_P=\Gamma_P(I_P(t))$, $\mathbf{u}_E=\Gamma_E(I_E(t))$). No value function / equilibrium concept is defined under partial information (see §7).
- **Termination**: capture ($\|\mathbf{x}_P-\mathbf{x}_E\|_2=0$) or evader entry into $\mathcal{T}$.
- **Assumption 1**: complete-information pursuer start pre-specifiable; partial-information starts random with $\mathbf{x}_E^0\in\Omega_{play}-\mathcal{S}$. **Assumption 2**: $\alpha=v_P/v_E>1$.

### 5.2 Evasion region and capture strategy (Sec. III, p. 3, eqs. (3)–(4))
- **Evasion region / boundary (eq. (3))**: with $\mathbf{x}_A=(\alpha^2\mathbf{x}_E-\mathbf{x}_P)/(\alpha^2-1)$ and $R_A=\alpha\|\mathbf{x}_P-\mathbf{x}_E\|_2/(\alpha^2-1)$, $R_E=\{\mathbf{z}\,|\,\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}_A^0\|_2<R_A^0\}$, $R_E^\partial=\{\mathbf{z}\,|\,\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}_A^0\|_2=R_A^0\}$: the Apollonius circle; its interior is the evader's dominance region (points E reaches before P).
- **Capture strategy $\Gamma_P^{c\delta}$ (eq. (4))**: at detection time $t_c$ fix the disc $A_\delta=\{\mathbf{z}\,|\,\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}_A^0\|_2\le R_{A\delta}\}$ with $R_{A\delta}=R_A(t_c)+\delta$, $\delta>0$; then $\mathbf{u}_P=\hat{\mathbf{z}}_P$ with $\mathbf{z}_P=(R_{A\delta}-R_A)\hat{\mathbf{d}}+\alpha^{-1}\mathbf{y}$, $\mathbf{d}=\mathbf{x}_E-\mathbf{x}_P$, $\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{x}_A-\mathbf{x}_A(t_c)$. Printed guarantees: capture within a finite time and continuous monitoring once the evader is in range, $\forall t>t_c,\ \|\mathbf{d}(t)\|_2<\|\mathbf{d}(t_c)\|_2$. Attributed to nonlinear control "rather than a traditional pursuit-evasion game theory (see, [20])" — i.e., the Dorothy–Maity–Shishika–Von Moll single-Apollonius-circle machinery (Automatica 2024). Proof not reproduced in this paper (imported from [20]).

### 5.3 Barrier and winning regions under complete information (Sec. IV-A, p. 3, eq. (5))
- **Barrier (eq. (5), as printed)**: $B(\mathbf{x}_P)$ divides $\Omega_{play}$ into the barrier-based pursuit winning region $\mathcal{W}_P^\mathcal{B}$ and evasion winning region $\mathcal{W}_E^\mathcal{B}$ (Isaacs [15]; functional form cited to [16] Yan, Shi, Zhong, T-RO 2020):
  - $F_1(\mathbf{x}_P)=\{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^2\,|\,\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{m}\|_2=\alpha^2\|\mathbf{x}_P-\mathbf{m}\|_2,\ x\le\sigma_1,\ y<0\}$;
  - $F_2(\mathbf{x}_P)=\{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^2\,|\,(x-x_P)^2+(1-\alpha^2)y^2+(1-\alpha^{-2})y_P^2=0,\ x\in(\sigma_1,\sigma_2),\ y<0\}$;
  - $F_3(\mathbf{x}_P)=\{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^2\,|\,\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{n}\|_2=\alpha^2\|\mathbf{x}_P-\mathbf{n}\|_2,\ x\ge\sigma_2,\ y<0\}$;
  - $\sigma_1=\alpha^{-2}x_P^0$, $\sigma_2=(1-\alpha^{-2})l+\alpha^{-2}x_P^0$.
  - **Proof gap / printed typo (p. 3, eq. (5))**: the coefficient $\alpha^2$ in $F_1,F_3$ is inconsistent with the rest of the paper. Internal consistency requires the $F_1,F_3$ radii to be $\alpha^{-1}\|\mathbf{x}_P-\mathbf{m}\|_2$ and $\alpha^{-1}\|\mathbf{x}_P-\mathbf{n}\|_2$: (a) at $x=\sigma_1=\alpha^{-2}x_P$ the hyperbola $F_2$ has $y^2=\frac{(\alpha^2-1)x_P^2+\alpha^2y_P^2}{\alpha^4}$, which equals the circle value only for radius $\alpha^{-1}\|\mathbf{x}_P-\mathbf{m}\|_2$; (b) eq. (19) uses $x_{left}$ with offset $\alpha^{-1}\sqrt{x_P^2+y_P^2}$; (c) Theorem 2's threshold $R^\alpha=(1+\alpha^{-1})l/2$ follows from the $\alpha^{-1}$ radius. Also $F_2$ is equivalently the hyperbola $y=-\sqrt{(x-x_P)^2/(\alpha^2-1)+y_P^2/\alpha^2}$ (transformation shown on p. 6). The barrier depends **only on the pursuer state and $\alpha$** (p. 3) — this is what makes a deployment-only design possible.

### 5.4 Theorem 1 (p. 3, with eqs. (6)–(17), proof pp. 3–4)
- **Definition 1 (payoff, p. 3)**: deployment payoff $J=S_{\mathcal{W}_E}$, the area of the evasion winning region. Since "[t]he winning region and the barrier remain unchanged regardless of the breakout strategy adopted by the evader" (asserted, cited to [15],[16]), deployment reduces to the pursuer-side program $\min_{\mathbf{x}_P^0\in\Omega_{play}}J$ (eq. (6)).
- **Theorem 1 (p. 3)**: *the optimal deployment position under complete information is $\mathbf{x}_{opt}^c=(l/2,0)$* — the midpoint of the target line. Global, exact, analytic.
- **Proof approach** (8 steps):
  1. Decompose $S_{\mathcal{W}_E}=S_{F1}+S_{F2}+S_{F3}$ (Fig. 2: $S_{F1},S_{F3}$ as sector+triangle sums; $S_{F2}$ as an integral) — eq. (7): $S_{F1}=\alpha^{-2}(x_P^2+y_P^2)\bigl(\pi-\arccos\tfrac{x_P}{\alpha\sqrt{x_P^2+y_P^2}}\bigr)/2+\alpha^{-3}x_P\sqrt{(1-\alpha^{-2})x_P^2+y_P^2}/2$; $S_{F2}=\int_{\sigma_1}^{\sigma_2}\sqrt{(x-x_P)^2/(\alpha^2-1)+\tfrac{1}{\alpha^2}y_P^2}\,dx$; $S_{F3}$ symmetric with $x_Q=l-x_P$.
  2. Fix $y_P$; minimize $S_{F1}+S_{F3}$ s.t. $x_P+x_Q=l$, $x_P,x_Q\ge0$ (eq. (8)).
  3. **Decisive convexity step**: Hessian diagonal entries (eq. (9)) $\partial^2S_{F1+F3}/\partial x_P^2=\alpha^{-2}\pi-\alpha^{-2}\arccos\bigl(\tfrac{x_P}{\alpha\sqrt{x_P^2+y_P^2}}\bigr)+\tfrac{(1-\alpha^{-2})x_P^3+(2-\alpha^{-2})x_Py_P^2}{\alpha^3(x_P^2+y_P^2)\sqrt{(1-\alpha^{-2})x_P^2+y_P^2}}>0$ (same in $x_Q$), cross-partials $=0$ ⇒ $S_{F1+F3}$ convex; Lagrange multiplier method gives $x_P=x_Q=l/2$.
  4. Outside the segment ($x_P>l$): closed form (eq. (10)) and derivative (eq. (11)) $dS_{F1+F3}/dx_P=\alpha^{-2}x_P(\pi-\arccos\cdot)+\alpha^{-2}(x_P-l)(\pi-\arccos\cdot)+\alpha^{-3}\bigl[\sqrt{(1-\alpha^{-2})x_P^2+y_P^2}-\sqrt{(1-\alpha^{-2})(x_P-l)^2+y_P^2}\bigr]>0$ ⇒ monotone increasing on $(l,+\infty)$; symmetric for $x_P<0$.
  5. $S_{F2}$: the integration-interval length $\sigma_2-\sigma_1=(1-\alpha^{-2})l$ is **constant**; substituting $\tau=x-x_P$ splits eq. (12) into two integrals of $f(\tau)=\sqrt{\tau^2/(\alpha^2-1)+y_P^2}$ over $[0,(1-\alpha^{-2})x_P]$ and $[0,(1-\alpha^{-2})(l-x_P)]$; at $x_P=l/2$, eq. (13).
  6. **Decisive inequality (mean value theorem for integrals)**: for $x_P>l/2$, $S_{F2}-S_{F2}^*=(1-\alpha^{-2})(x_P-l/2)\,(f(\eta_1)-f(\eta_2))>0$ (eq. (14)) with $\eta_1\in((1-\alpha^{-2})l/2,(1-\alpha^{-2})x_P)$, $\eta_2\in((1-\alpha^{-2})(l-x_P),(1-\alpha^{-2})l/2)$ and $f$ monotonically increasing; by symmetry also for $x_P<l/2$.
  7. Fix $x_P=l/2$, vary $y_P$: eq. (15); $S_{F2}$ "obviously" minimal at $y_P=0$, and $dS_{F1+F3}/dy_P=\alpha^{-2}y_P\bigl(\pi-\arccos\tfrac{l}{2\alpha\sqrt{l^2/4+y_P^2}}\bigr)<0$ (eq. (16), printed for $y_P<0$; the parenthesized factor is positive so the sign is the sign of $y_P$ — i.e., the area decreases as the pursuer approaches the line from the play region).
  8. Chain of inequalities (eq. (17)): $S_{\mathcal{W}_E}(x_P,y_P)\ge S_{\mathcal{W}_E}(l/2,y_P)\ge S_{\mathcal{W}_E}(l/2,0)$. ∎
- **Physical meaning**: guarding the midpoint of the segment is area-optimal: it equalizes the two endpoint Apollonius arcs and centers the hyperbolic middle arc. Extensibility: the proof relies only on the additive three-part structure; the same scheme would extend to piecewise-straight targets, at the price of bookkeeping (not done here). Minor rigor notes: the program (6) minimizes over $\Omega_{play}$ while the optimum $(l/2,0)$ lies on $\mathcal{T}$ (Sec. V's eq. (22) correctly uses $\Omega_{play}\cup\mathcal{T}$); the invariance of $\mathcal{W}_E$ to the evader strategy is asserted, not proven.

### 5.5 Partial-information winning regions (Sec. V-A, pp. 4–5, eq. (18))
- **Invariance claim (p. 5)**: $\mathcal{W}_E^*=\mathcal{W}_E^\mathcal{B}$ — "The evader can always adopt its winning strategy when the evader is within $\mathcal{W}_E^\mathcal{B}$" (its global information suffices to execute the barrier-winning strategy). The open question is whether capture can be guaranteed when the evader is inside $\mathcal{W}_P^\mathcal{B}$ **and inside $\mathcal{S}$**.
- **Lemma 1 (p. 5)**: *If E is located in $\mathcal{W}_P^\mathcal{B}\cap\mathcal{S}$, P can always adopt a capture strategy guaranteeing victory.* Proof (3 steps, terse): (i) $\mathbf{x}_E^0\in\mathcal{W}_P^\mathcal{B}$ implies the Apollonius boundary stays away from the target: $\mathrm{dist}(R_E^\partial,\mathcal{T})=d_{ET}>0$; (ii) choose $\delta=k\,d_{ET}$, $k\in(0,1)$; (iii) the capture strategy $\Gamma_P^{c\delta}$ of eq. (4) captures within $A_\delta$ in finite time (imported from [20]). Meaning: a detection occurring inside $\mathcal{W}_P^\mathcal{B}$ is a *certified* capture. **How missing information is compensated**: no estimation/no observer — the compensation is purely *worst-case geometric*: only detections inside $\mathcal{W}_P^\mathcal{B}$ are trusted, and the deployment is chosen so that the sensing disc is as valuable as possible (Thm 2).
- **Definition 2 (p. 5)**: **pursuit sensing winning region** $\mathcal{W}_P^\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{W}_P^\mathcal{B}\cap\mathcal{S}$; **fuzzy region** $\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{W}_P^\mathcal{B}-\mathcal{S}$ (states of $\mathcal{W}_P^\mathcal{B}$ invisible to P; in the worst case the evader crosses the barrier undetected from $\mathcal{F}$, so $\mathcal{F}$ must be treated as evader-favorable, Fig. 4).
- **Barrier-covered case and eq. (18)**: if the sensing disc contains the whole barrier, $\mathcal{F}$ is irrelevant (every entry into $\mathcal{W}_P^\mathcal{B}$ is detected); the partial-information pursuit winning region is
  $$\mathcal{W}_P^*=\begin{cases}\mathcal{W}_P^\mathcal{B}, & \text{if } B(\mathbf{x}_P)\subset\mathrm{int}\,\mathcal{S}\\ \mathcal{W}_P^\mathcal{S}, & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}\qquad\text{(eq. (18))}$$
- **Lemma 2 (p. 5, eqs. (19)–(20), proof pp. 5–6)**: $\forall\mathbf{x}_P^0\in\Omega_{play}$, with $\mathbf{x}_{left}=F_1(\mathbf{x}_P^0)|_{y=0}$ and $\mathbf{x}_{right}=F_3(\mathbf{x}_P^0)|_{y=0}$ (barrier–axis intersections; note $\mathbf{x}_{left}$ lies on the extension line left of $\mathbf{m}$), there exists $d(\mathbf{x}_P^0)=\max\{\|\mathbf{x}_P^0-\mathbf{x}_{left}\|_2,\|\mathbf{x}_P^0-\mathbf{x}_{right}\|_2\}$ s.t. $\forall r>d(\mathbf{x}_P^0)$: $B(\mathbf{x}_P^0)\subset\mathrm{int}\,\mathcal{S}$, and $\forall r\le d(\mathbf{x}_P^0)$: $B(\mathbf{x}_P^0)\not\subset\mathrm{int}\,\mathcal{S}$.
  Proof: (i) by symmetry $\|\mathbf{x}_P^0-\mathbf{x}_{left}\|$ is maximal for $x_P>l/2$ and vice versa (eq. (19): $\|\mathbf{x}_P^0-\mathbf{x}_{left}\|_2=\sqrt{(x_P+\alpha^{-1}\sqrt{x_P^2+y_P^2})^2+y_P^2}$, $\|\mathbf{x}_P^0-\mathbf{x}_{right}\|_2=\sqrt{(l-x_P+\alpha^{-1}\sqrt{(l-x_P)^2+y_P^2})^2+y_P^2}$); (ii) on $F_1$ the law of cosines $|PB_i|=|OB_i|^2+|OP|^2-2|OB_i||OP|\cos\theta_i$ (eq. (20) — **printed without the square on the LHS; should be $|PB_i|^2$**) shows the farthest point is the endpoint $B_1=\mathbf{x}_{left}$; same for $F_3$; (iii) on $F_2$, parametrize $B_4=(x_4,y_4)$ by $y$ (using $y=-\sqrt{(x-x_P)^2/(\alpha^2-1)+y_P^2/\alpha^2}$): the squared distance is $d_{PB_4}=\alpha^2y_4^2-2y_Py_4+\alpha^{-2}y_P^2$, convex in $y_4$; $F_2$'s topmost point is $y_4=y_P/\alpha$ (for $y_P<0$), and the distances at the tangency points of $F_2$ with $F_1,F_3$ are below $\|\mathbf{x}_P^0-\mathbf{x}_{left}\|_2$. ∎ **Proof-gap note**: the printed wording is garbled — "its maximum value is attained on the domain's boundary" and "The maximum value $y_P/\alpha$ can take on $y$ in $F_2$ is less than $y_P/\alpha^2$, and $d_{PB_4}$ is minimized" (p. 6). Recovered logic: the unconstrained minimizer of the convex quadratic is $y_4=y_P/\alpha^2$, which is *infeasible* (outside $F_2$'s $y$-range $y_4\le y_P/\alpha$); hence the constrained minimum over $F_2$ is attained at the boundary $y_4=y_P/\alpha$, where $d_{PB_4}^*=(\alpha^{-1}-1)y_P$ (positive for $y_P<0$). The claim itself is correct; the write-up is not.

### 5.6 Theorem 2 and Algorithm 1 (Sec. V-B, pp. 6–7, eqs. (21)–(24))
- **Theorem 2 (p. 6, eq. (21))**: *in the one-versus-one game under partial information the optimal deployment position is*
  $$\mathbf{x}_{opt}=\begin{cases}\bigl(\tfrac{l}{2},0\bigr), & r>R^\alpha\\[2pt] \Bigl(\tfrac{l}{2},\tfrac{r}{\alpha^{-1}-1}\Bigr), & r\le R^\alpha\end{cases}\qquad R^\alpha=(1+\alpha^{-1})l/2$$
  (with the play region at $y<0$, the second coordinate $r/(\alpha^{-1}-1)<0$ places P *below* the line, inside $\Omega_{play}$). Global, analytic.
- **Proof approach** (5 steps):
  1. Minimal barrier-covering radius: $\min d(\mathbf{x}_P^0)$ s.t. $\mathbf{x}_P^0\in\Omega_{play}\cup\mathcal{T}$ (eq. (22)); by eq. (19), $d(x_P,y_P)>d(x_P,0)$ for any $y_P\ne0$, and eq. (23): $d(x_P,0)=(1+\alpha^{-1})x_P$ for $x_P>l/2$, $(1+\alpha^{-1})(l-x_P)$ for $x_P\le l/2$ — minimized at $(l/2,0)$ with value $R^\alpha=(1+\alpha^{-1})l/2$. Note the coincidence: this position is also the complete-information optimum of Theorem 1.
  2. If $r>R^\alpha$: deploying at $(l/2,0)$ covers the whole barrier ⇒ $\mathcal{W}_P^*=\mathcal{W}_P^\mathcal{B}$; "both players' winning region are identical" to the complete-information case, so Theorem 1's position remains optimal.
  3. If $r\le R^\alpha$: a fuzzy region necessarily exists; in the worst case "the evader could reach the barrier undetected", and deploying at $(l/2,0)$ "would waste more than half of the sensing region".
  4. **Surrogate program (eq. (24))**: $\min S_{\mathcal{W}_E}$ s.t. $\mathcal{W}_P^*=\mathcal{S}$ — use the sensing disc *fully*, i.e., make every point of $\mathcal{S}$ a certified capture zone (equivalently $\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{W}_P^\mathcal{B}$, the disc tangent to the barrier from the $\mathcal{W}_P^\mathcal{B}$ side).
  5. By eq. (17) fix $x_P=l/2$; the distance from P to the barrier $F_2$ is minimized at the topmost hyperbola point $y_4=y_P/\alpha$ with $d_{PB_4}^*=(\alpha^{-1}-1)y_P$; setting $d_{PB_4}^*=r$ (tangency) yields $y_P=r/(\alpha^{-1}-1)$, the claimed second case. ∎ **Proof-gap note**: step 4's claim that the tangency position minimizes $S_{\mathcal{W}_E}$ is argued only along the line $x_P=l/2$ (via eq. (17)) and "even without a specific strategy for the fuzzy region" (p. 6); global optimality over $x_P$ is inherited from eq. (17) but the trade-off between shrinking $S_{\mathcal{W}_E}$ and the tangency constraint is resolved by assertion ("This position represents the optimal solution to the optimization problem (24)"), not by a KKT argument.
- **Physical meaning / compensation mechanism**: when $r$ is small the pursuer cannot see the whole barrier, so it *retreats* from the line to depth $r/(1-\alpha^{-1})=r\alpha/(\alpha-1)$ below the midpoint, where its sensing disc is internally tangent to the hyperbolic barrier: every detection inside $\mathcal{S}$ then occurs inside $\mathcal{W}_P^\mathcal{B}$, activating Lemma 1's certified capture; the region between the barrier and the line that is conceded is the price of ignorance of the evader's position. There is **no estimation and no barrier adaptation online** — the compensation is a static, worst-case deployment; the barrier is "adapted" only at design time through the pursuer's chosen position (the barrier depends on $\mathbf{x}_P$).
- **Algorithm 1 (p. 7)**: Input $\mathbf{x}_P^0,\mathbf{x}_E^0,\alpha,r$. Step 1: determine $\mathbf{x}_{opt}$ by Theorem 2. Step 2: P moves in a straight line to $\mathbf{x}_{opt}$ and stands still until $\mathbf{x}_E\in\mathcal{S}$. Step 3: adopt capture strategy $\Gamma_P^{c\delta}$; terminate at $\|\mathbf{x}_P-\mathbf{x}_E\|_2=0$ or $\mathbf{x}_E\in\mathcal{T}$.
- **What is NOT proven**: no value function, no saddle-point strategy, no HJI verification under partial information; "optimality" is optimality of the *deployment position* w.r.t. the surrogates (6)/(24) plus the qualitative guarantee chain Lemma 1 ⇒ capture upon detection inside $\mathcal{W}_P^\mathcal{B}$; the evader strategy $\Gamma_E$ is left unspecified and all winning-region statements are worst-case over $\Gamma_E$.

## 6. Simulation and Experiments
**Platform (p. 7)**: NOKOV optical motion-capture system, 24 cameras, sub-millimeter position/velocity accuracy; each quadrotor carries a microcontroller for attitude stabilization and a communication link; a central computer runs the strategy and issues commands; quadrotors hold constant altitude and descend upon capture. Game space limited to **12 m × 5 m** (vs the infinite-plane theory) in both simulation and experiment. Strategy software, simulator, and control bandwidth: UNREPORTED. The evader "aims to reach the target line in the shortest possible time"; scenarios with the evader initially *on the barrier* or other critical conditions are deliberately excluded.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
**Author-stated**: (i) experiments exclude critical initial conditions (evader starting on/near the barrier) because of quadrotor control accuracy (p. 7) — so the sharp boundary cases where the theory is most delicate are untested; (ii) the infinite-plane theory is validated only in a 12 m × 5 m room (p. 7); (iii) sensing/capture instants differ between experiment and simulation due to model simplification (p. 8); (iv) analysis is confined to one-versus-one with a straight target line.

**Critical observations**:
1. **Printed typo in the central object (p. 3, eq. (5))**: $F_1,F_3$ print radius $\alpha^2\|\mathbf{x}_P-\mathbf{m}\|_2$ but $\alpha^{-1}$ is required by $\sigma_1,\sigma_2$, by eq. (7)'s areas, by eq. (19), and by $R^\alpha=(1+\alpha^{-1})l/2$; as printed, $F_1\cup F_2\cup F_3$ would not even be a connected barrier. Readers reproducing the barrier must correct this.
2. **No game solution under partial information**: the paper explicitly abandons the non-anticipative structure (p. 2) but never defines a value or equilibrium for the partial-information game; "optimal defense strategy" means the optimal *deployment position* w.r.t. surrogates (6)/(24) plus worst-case guarantees (Lemma 1, Definition 2). Optimality over $x_P$ in eq. (24) is asserted along $x_P=l/2$ via eq. (17) without a KKT-style argument (p. 6).
3. **Information asymmetry doing silent work**: the evader is assumed global-information but *unaware of $r$* (p. 2). Since Algorithm 1's behavior (transit to a deterministic $\mathbf{x}_{opt}$, then stand still) is predictable, a rational evader that knew $r$ (or the deployment policy) could time its dash; the paper provides no equilibrium analysis of this asymmetry and no evader-side optimality claim.
4. **Deployment transient unanalyzed**: the barrier depends on the pursuer's *current* position, which moves during Step 2 of Algorithm 1; the winning-region analysis is quasi-static at the final position, and the window in which the evader might exploit the moving barrier is not modeled (Assumption 1 / Algorithm 1, pp. 2, 7).
5. **Write-up defects in proofs**: Lemma 2's convexity argument is garbled ("maximum value… attained on the domain's boundary", "$y_P/\alpha$… is less than $y_P/\alpha^2$", p. 6 — the correct statement is that the constrained minimum of the convex quadratic lies at the feasible boundary $y_4=y_P/\alpha$); eq. (20) misses the square on $|PB_i|$; eq. (16)'s sign is stated only for $y_P<0$; eq. (6) minimizes over $\Omega_{play}$ yet the optimum lies on $\mathcal{T}$. None of these invalidate the results, but they must be repaired before reuse.
6. **Terse imported guarantees**: finite-time capture of $\Gamma_P^{c\delta}$ within $A_\delta$ is imported from [20] without restatement of its hypotheses; Lemma 1's proof is three sentences (p. 5).
7. **Editorial noise (review-copy artifacts)**: "target line belongs to the target region $\Omega_{play}$" (p. 2; should be $\Omega_{tar}$); figures label the target region $\Omega_{goal}$ (Figs. 3–4) vs $\Omega_{tar}$ in the text; duplicated sentence on p. 5 ("Note that the pursuer's location determines the barrier's position…"); "evasion wining region" (p. 6); "purser" (p. 7); "not unstable" (p. 8).
8. **Open**: multiplayer/generalization to convex or circular targets (cf. refs [17]–[19]); evader-side uncertainty of the deployment position; online replanning of $\mathbf{x}_{opt}$ when $r$ or $\alpha$ is uncertain; quantitative performance metrics and baseline comparisons.

## 8. Citation Value
**Role**: this is the corpus's most direct treatment of *how a faster defender should choose its guarding position when it cannot see the whole game*: it couples the Yan-et al.-style analytical barrier for a straight target with a finite sensing radius, contributes the fuzzy-region / pursuit-sensing-winning-region vocabulary, and gives a closed-form deployment rule with a phase transition at $R^\alpha=(1+\alpha^{-1})l/2$ (guard the midpoint vs retreat to the barrier-tangency depth $r/(\alpha^{-1}-1)$), plus rare hardware validation (quadrotors, NOKOV). In a paper introduction it belongs in the paragraph on reach-avoid defense under imperfect/limited information, next to Shishika et al.'s partial-information target defense [8] and Chen et al.'s detection-range game [9], and as a deployment-level consumer of Dorothy et al.'s Apollonius capture strategy [20].
**Ready-to-adapt citation sentences**: "Ma et al. derive the optimal deployment position of a faster pursuer with a finite sensing radius in a one-versus-one reach-avoid game with a straight target line, showing that the defender should occupy the target midpoint once its sensing radius exceeds $R^\alpha=(1+\alpha^{-1})l/2$ and otherwise retreat to the unique position where its sensing disc is tangent to the Apollonius barrier, thereby converting its entire sensing region into a certified capture zone [P067]." Alternatively: "Under partial information, the barrier-based pursuit winning region shrinks to the intersection with the sensing disc, and the residual 'fuzzy region' must be treated worst-case in the defender's deployment design [P067]."
