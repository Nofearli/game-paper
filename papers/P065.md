## 2. Research Background
Pursuit–evasion games (PEGs) have been studied extensively either through environment geometry [1] or through multiple pursuers/evaders [2], [3], but nearly always under perfect state information and complete knowledge of the payoff and of the opponent's capability (p. 1). Extensions to limited perception exist for limited sensing range [4], limited field of view [5], and noisy measurements [6], yet "the challenges associated with the lack of continuous sensing remains unsolved" (p. 1). The authors' motivating scenario (also the umbrella of their larger project, funded by ARL DCIST) is a Pursuer P with two sensing modes: **passive sensing** (E detected only within a finite range) and **costly active sensing** (E detected anywhere when P pays to activate a measurement device). Solving that full game requires deciding *when* to activate sensing and *where to head* while blind. This paper solves a precisely carved subproblem: the time interval between two consecutive active-sensing instants, during which P — holding no information — moves in a straight line, and E, fully informed, chooses its heading optimally.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
**Problem** (pp. 1–2): P moves along the positive $x$-axis at constant speed $v_P$ with capture radius $r_{cap}$ (the "Proximity Circle"); E starts outside the Proximity Circle with speed $v_E < v_P$ and controls its heading $\psi(t)$. Given a fixed final time $T$, E maximizes its final distance from P, $J=\Phi(x_f)=\|x_f\|^2$ (eq. (4), p. 2 — the printed "$\|x_f\|_2$" in (4) is the squared norm, as certified by the terminal adjoint $\lambda_f^T=[2x_f\ 2y_f]$ in eq. (12), p. 3); if capture is unavoidable, E maximizes its survival time $t_{cap}$ (p. 2). Two variations are posed (p. 1): (1) an **optimal control problem** — best response of E for fixed $T$; (2) a **Nash game** — if P can select the duration $T$ (the inter-sensing interval), find the equilibrium pair in $(\psi(\cdot),T)$.

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **(Modeling)** Extraction of a clean subproblem of the intermittent-sensing PEG: fixed-heading pursuer, evader best response over a fixed window, in a pursuer-fixed frame with a state constraint $S(x)=\|x\|-1\ge 0$ (eqs. (1)–(3), p. 1), plus the two-layer problem stack (fixed-$T$ OCP; Nash game over $T$, p. 1).
2. **(Theory, geometry)** Complete characterization of the fixed-$T$ optimal evasion policy: either a single constant-heading straight line (Lemma 4, eq. (19), p. 3) or a three-phase trajectory — tangent entry, boundary riding, tangential exit (Theorems 1–2, Lemma 6, pp. 4–6), with the riding time given by an elliptic integral (eq. (25), p. 5) and the optimal final distance in closed form $d_f^*=\mu/\cos\theta_{exit}$ (eq. (34), p. 5).
3. **(Theory, reachability)** Necessary and sufficient conditions for inevitable capture — the no-escape zone $\Omega_{capture}$ and the exact maximum survival time $T_{survive}(x_0)$ (Lemma 3, eqs. (9)–(10), p. 2; Appendix A, pp. 7–8).
4. **(Game theory)** A Stackelberg/Nash equilibrium pair over $(\psi(\cdot),T)$: the pursuer's optimal announced inter-sensing time $T_{NE}=\max\{x-\tfrac{\mu}{\sqrt{1-\mu^2}}y,0\}$ and the evader's non-exploitable heading $\psi_{NE}=\cos^{-1}\mu$, independent of the state (Lemma 7, Theorem 3, eqs. (36)–(40), pp. 6–7; Appendix C, p. 8).
5. **(Phenomenology)** Identification of a "risky" optimal maneuver (Abstract, p. 1; Figs. 6–7, p. 6): in constrained regimes the evader deliberately grazes the Proximity Circle before swinging behind the pursuer, and for the degenerate initial condition $y_0=0$ the best response is *discontinuous* in $T$ with a continuum of optima at $T=x_0$.

## 5. Key Mathematical Results (exhaustive)

### 5.1 Dynamics, information structure, payoff, termination (p. 1–2)
- **Dynamics (eq. (1), p. 1)**: inertial frame, $\dot x_P = v_P[1\ 0]^T$ (P moves along $+x$ at constant speed, no control), $\dot x_E = v_E[\cos\psi\ \sin\psi]^T$; $v_E<v_P$, $\psi(t)$ is E's instantaneous heading. Normalization (footnote 1, p. 1): $\bar x_i(t)=\frac{1}{r_{cap}}x_i(\frac{r_{cap}}{v_P}t)$, $\mu=v_E/v_P<1$, capture radius $\to 1$; i.e., WLOG $v_P=1$, $v_E=\mu$, $r_{cap}=1$ (the footnote prints $\dot{\bar x}_P=[-1\ 0]^T$, a sign slip for P's own scaled velocity; the $-1$ drift is correct only in the relative frame).
- **Pursuer-fixed frame (eq. (2), p. 1)**: $x=x_E-x_P=[x\ y]^T$, $\dot x = [\mu\cos\psi-1,\ \mu\sin\psi]^T$, $x(0)=x_0$. E's relative velocity always has non-positive $x$-component.
- **State constraint (eq. (3), p. 1)**: $S(x)=\|x\|-1\ge 0$ for all $t\in[0,T]$ (E must stay on/outside the Proximity Circle of radius 1 centered at the origin of the relative frame). By symmetry $y\ge 0$ WLOG.
- **Payoff / terminal manifold (eqs. (4)–(5), p. 2)**: $J=\Phi(x_f)=\|x_f\|^2$, terminal manifold $F(x_f,t_f)=t_f-T=0$. If capture unavoidable ($\exists t\le T$ with $S(x(t))<0$ for every strategy), E maximizes $t_{cap}$, the earliest time with $S(x(t_{cap}))=0$.
- **Information structure (pp. 1, 7)**: within the analyzed window P is blind (this is the gap between two active-sensing instants) and moves in a straight line — hence open-loop by default (Remark 4, p. 7: "P would obtain no additional information at any time"); E is fully informed and may use any admissible $\psi(t)$ (no straight-line restriction). Termination at $t=T$ (announced) or at capture.
- **Relative-frame velocity geometry (eqs. (6)–(8), p. 2; Fig. 1, p. 2)**: with $v,\phi$ the speed/heading of E in the pursuer-fixed frame, $v\sin\phi=\mu\sin\psi$, $v\cos\phi=-1+\mu\cos\psi$ (6); hence $\cos\phi<0$ and $\phi\in[\pi-\sin^{-1}\mu,\ \pi+\sin^{-1}\mu]$, $v\in[1-\mu,\ 1+\mu]$; forward map $\phi(\psi)=\mathrm{atan2}(\mu\sin\psi,\mu\cos\psi-1)$, $v(\psi)=\sqrt{1+\mu^2-2\mu\cos\psi}$ (7); inverse has *two* branches $(\psi(\phi),v(\phi))$ (8) — the same effective relative heading $\phi$ is achievable by two inertial headings, with the first branch ($\psi=\phi-\sin^{-1}(\sin\phi/\mu)$) strictly faster: $v=-\cos\phi+\sqrt{\mu^2-\sin^2\phi}$ vs. $v=-\cos\phi-\sqrt{\mu^2-\sin^2\phi}$. This two-branch structure is used repeatedly (fast tangent approach in Sec. VI-A, exit speed in Sec. VI-D).

### 5.2 Lemma 1 (p. 2) — safe region
**Statement**: for all states $\|x\|>1$ with $x\le 0$ or $y\ge 1$ (pursuer-fixed frame), the optimal trajectory starting at $x$ avoids the constraint. **Proof**: "by inspection" — E maximizes final distance from the relative-frame origin. Physical meaning: being behind/beside-and-above the pursuer is intrinsically safe; only the quadrant-ish region $x>0, y<1$ matters. Extensibility: defines the domain of interest for everything downstream. (Proof is informal — a rigorous version would exhibit a feasible monotonically-$\|x\|$-increasing maneuver; none is spelled out.)

### 5.3 Lemma 2 (p. 2) — fatal arc of the Proximity Circle
**Statement**: if E is on the Proximity Circle at angle $|\theta|<\cos^{-1}\mu$ ($\theta=\mathrm{atan2}(y,x)$), then $\|x(t^+)\|<1$ and capture happens. **Proof**: $\dot d = x^T\dot x/\|x\| = v\cos(\phi-\theta)$ with $d=\|x\|$; since $\phi\in[\pi-\sin^{-1}\mu,\pi+\sin^{-1}\mu]$ and $\theta\in(-\cos^{-1}\mu,\cos^{-1}\mu)$, $|\phi-\theta|>\pi-\sin^{-1}\mu-\cos^{-1}\mu=\pi/2$, so $\dot d<0$. **Decisive inequality**: $|\phi-\theta|>\pi/2$ (p. 2). Physical meaning: on the forward-facing arc (within $\cos^{-1}\mu$ of the $+x$ axis) E cannot even *stay* on the circle — the drift pushes it inside. Corollary used later: riding the boundary requires $\theta\ge\cos^{-1}\mu$.

### 5.4 Lemma 3 (p. 2, eqs. (9)–(10); Appendix A, pp. 7–8) — no-escape zone and exact survival time
**Statement**: capture is guaranteed **iff** E's initial location $x_0=[x_0\ y_0]^T$ and duration $T$ satisfy
$$\mu x_0+\sqrt{1-\mu^2}\,y_0<1,\qquad x_0>\mu \quad (9),\qquad T>T_{survive}(x_0)\quad(10),$$
where $T_{survive}(x_0)=\frac{1}{1-\mu^2}\Big[(x_0-\mu)-\sqrt{(1-\mu x_0)^2-(1-\mu^2)y_0^2}\Big]$.
**Proof approach (Appendix A)**: E's reachable set at time $t$ is the disc $R(t,x_0)=\{x_f:(x_f-(x_0-t))^2+(y_f-y_0)^2\le\mu^2t^2\}$; its farthest point from the relative origin is $x^*=(1+\frac{\mu t}{\sqrt{(x_0-t)^2+y_0^2}})[x_0-t\ \ y_0]^T$ (eq. (41), p. 7) at distance $\bar d_f(t,x_0)=\sqrt{(x_0-t)^2+y_0^2}+\mu t$. **Sufficiency**: $\bar d_f<1$ yields the quadratic (1−μ²)t² − 2(x₀−μ)t + ‖x₀‖² − 1 < 0 (eq. (42), p. 8); given (9), $T_{survive}$ is its smaller root, so any $T>T_{survive}$ forces $\|x(t)\|<1$ for some $t$. **Necessity**: under the heading $\psi=\cos^{-1}\mu$, $\|\mathbf x(t)\|^2=(\mu x_0+\sqrt{1-\mu^2}y_0)^2+(1-\mu^2)(t-c)^2$ with $c=x_0-\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{1-\mu^2}}y_0$ — so capture under this heading requires exactly (i) $\mu x_0+\sqrt{1-\mu^2}y_0<1$ and (ii) $c>0$; the paper then prints $c$'s defining bracket as $[1\ \ \mu/\sqrt{1-\mu^2}]\,\mathbf x_0$ (p. 8) — **erratum: the second component must carry a minus sign** ($c=x_0-\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{1-\mu^2}}y_0$), as forced by the direct expansion and consistent with Lemma 7's (37). The step "(i) and (ii) ⟹ $x_0>\mu$" implicitly uses the standing assumption $\|x_0\|>1$: (ii) gives $y_0<x_0\sqrt{1-\mu^2}/\mu$, hence $\|x_0\|<x_0/\mu$, hence $x_0>\mu$. Necessity of (10) uses the identity $\big(\frac{x_0-T_{survive}}{1-\mu T_{survive}}\big)^2+\big(\frac{y_0}{1-\mu T_{survive}}\big)^2=1$ (verified numerically: $\mu=0.6,x_0=1.5,y_0=0.1\Rightarrow$ LHS $=1.000$), a heading $\psi$ with $\cos\psi=\frac{x_0-T_{survive}}{1-\mu T_{survive}}$, $\sin\psi=\frac{y_0}{1-\mu T_{survive}}$ (the printed "$\sin\psi=y_0/T_{survive}$" and eq. (43)'s denominators "$1-T_{survive}$", "$T_{survive}$" are **typesetting errata**; the corrected (43) is $\mathbf x(t)=[x_0+t\frac{\mu x_0-1}{1-\mu T_{survive}},\ y_0+t\frac{\mu y_0}{1-\mu T_{survive}}]^T$, for which $\|\mathbf x(T_{survive})\|=1$ and the first circle-crossing time equals $T_{survive}$).
**Downstream objects**: $\Omega(T)=\{x_0\,|\,(x_0-T)^2+y_0^2<(1-\mu T)^2 \text{ and (9) satisfied}\}$; $\Omega_{capture}=\cup_{T\ge0}\Omega(T)$, the "no-escape zone" (p. 2; Fig. 2, p. 3 shows level curves of $T_{survive}$ for $\mu=0.4$). For $x_0\in\Omega_{capture}$ the max survival time is $T_{survive}(x_0)$.
**Remark 1 (p. 2)**: if $x_0\notin\Omega_{capture}$, the constant heading $\psi=\cos^{-1}\mu$ keeps $\|x(t)\|\ge1$ for all $t$ (explicit solution $\mathbf x(t)=x_0+t[(\mu^2-1),\ \mu\sqrt{1-\mu^2}]^T$, giving $\|x(t)\|^2\ge(\mu x_0+\sqrt{1-\mu^2}y_0)^2\ge1$); "such a heading angle is not necessarily optimal" — it is the workhorse of the necessity proofs and, later, of the equilibrium (39).

### 5.5 Lemma 4 (p. 3, eqs. (11)–(15)) — constant optimal heading (unconstrained arc)
**Statement**: the optimal Evader heading $\psi^*$ is constant backwards in time until either the constraint $S=0$ is reached or the initial condition is met. **Proof (Pontryagin; Bryson & Ho [12])**: Hamiltonian $H=\dot x^T\lambda=\lambda_x(\mu\cos\psi-1)+\lambda_y\mu\sin\psi$ (11); terminal adjoints $\lambda_f^T=\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial x_f}+\gamma\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_f}=[2x_f\ 2y_f]$ (12); adjoint dynamics $\dot\lambda=-\partial H/\partial x=0$ (13) (state does not appear in $H$), so $\lambda(t)=\lambda_f$; terminal Hamiltonian $H_f=2x_f(\mu\cos\psi_f-1)+2y_f\mu\sin\psi_f$ (14) is maximized by $\cos\psi_f^*=x_f/\sqrt{x_f^2+y_f^2}$, $\sin\psi_f^*=y_f/\sqrt{x_f^2+y_f^2}$ (15) — terminal heading aligned with the evader's terminal position vector; since $\lambda$ is constant and $H$ is the same function of $\psi$ at all times, $\psi^*\equiv\psi_f^*$. **Closed form (eqs. (16)–(19), p. 3)**: for constant $\psi$, $x(t)=[x_1+t(\mu\cos\psi-1),\ y_1+t\mu\sin\psi]^T$ (16)–(17); combining with (15): $\psi^*(t,x)=\mathrm{atan2}(y,\ x-(T-t))$ (19). **Geometric interpretation (Fig. 3, p. 3)**: apply P's drift for the remaining $T-t$ to get a virtual point $[x-(T-t), y]^T$; E's reachable set from there is a disc of radius $\mu(T-t)$; its farthest point from the origin lies on the ray through the virtual point — "pursuit of a drifting ghost". Local/global: global for the unconstrained arc. Physical meaning: the evader heads toward where the pursuer *will have drifted from*.

### 5.6 Lemma 5 (p. 4, eqs. (20)–(21); Figs. 4–5, p. 4) — when the constraint is hit
**Statement**: for given $x_0$, a straight-line trajectory with heading $\psi$ intersects the Proximity Circle **iff** $\phi_{tan}+\mathrm{atan2}(\mu\sin\psi,1-\mu\cos\psi)<\pi$ **and** $T>t_c$, where $\phi_{tan}=\pi-\sin^{-1}\!\frac{1}{\|x_0\|}+\mathrm{atan2}(y_0,x_0)$ (20) and $t_c=\frac{1}{v(\psi)}\big[-(x_0\cos\phi+y_0\sin\phi)-\sqrt{1-(x_0\sin\phi-y_0\cos\phi)^2}\big]$ (21), $v(\psi),\phi$ from (7). **Proof**: geometric — (20) is the relative heading tangent to the circle at the entry point (Fig. 4); a heading whose effective $\phi$ points "outside" misses; if it points "inside", entry occurs only if the game lasts past the collision time, obtained by dividing the tangent-length distance along the line by the speed $v(\psi)$ (Law of Cosines). Consequence: if the unconstrained optimum $\psi^*$ from (19) fails either test, the constraint becomes active. Fig. 5 (p. 4) partitions initial positions into: no-escape zone $\Omega_{capture}$ (black), constraint-activated (brown), unconstrained (light), for $T=2$, $\mu=0.7$.

### 5.7 Phase I and Theorem 1 (p. 4, eqs. (22)–(23)) — tangent entry
From (8), two inertial headings reach the same effective heading $\phi_{tan}$: $\psi_{tan}=\phi_{tan}-\sin^{-1}(\sin\phi_{tan}/\mu)$ (fast branch) or $\psi_{tan}=\phi_{tan}+\sin^{-1}(\sin\phi_{tan}/\mu)-\pi$; the former is faster (higher $v$). Hence the shortest time to the tangent point is
$$t_{tan}=\frac{\sqrt{\|x_0\|^2-1}}{-\cos\phi_{tan}+\sqrt{\mu^2-\sin^2\phi_{tan}}}\quad(22),$$
i.e., tangent length $\sqrt{\|x_0\|^2-1}$ divided by the fast-branch speed (verified: the extraction ambiguity "−cos φtan in numerator" was checked against the 400-dpi rendering — the numerator is clean). The entry angle is $\theta_{tan}=\cos^{-1}\!\frac{1}{\|x_0\|}+\mathrm{atan2}(y_0,x_0)$ (23). **Remark 2 (p. 4)**: $t_{tan},\phi_{tan},\theta_{tan}$ depend on $x_0$ "and not on $T$ or $\mu$" — **erratum**: $\phi_{tan}$ (20) and $\theta_{tan}$ (23) are $\mu$-independent, but $t_{tan}$ (22) explicitly contains $\mu$; also "all constrained trajectories have $\theta_{tan}\in[0,\pi/2)$" (from Lemma 1). **Theorem 1 (p. 4)**: the optimal Evader trajectory to reach any location $\theta\ge\theta_{tan}$ on the constraint is: straight line to $\theta_{tan}$, then along the constraint to $\theta$. **Proof**: any straight line to $\theta>\theta_{tan}$ first hits the circle at $\theta'<\theta_{tan}$, forcing a longer ride through $\theta_{tan}$ anyway; entering below $\theta_{tan}$ and riding up to it is suboptimal because the straight line to $\theta_{tan}$ is feasible [13 — Zermelo's navigation result, cited without restatement]. Physical meaning: the fastest way to gain angular position on the circle is to arrive at the earliest possible tangency, as late-drifted as possible.

### 5.8 Phase II — riding the constraint (p. 4–5, eqs. (24)–(25))
On the circle at angle $\theta$, the effective heading that keeps $S=0$ active satisfies $\cos\phi=-\sin\theta$, $\sin\phi=\cos\theta$ (i.e., $\phi=\theta+\pi/2$, tangent, counterclockwise); substituting the fast branch of (8) gives the angular rate
$$\dot\theta=v(\phi)=\sqrt{\mu^2-\cos^2\theta}+\sin\theta\quad(24).$$
Integrating, the time on the arc between $\theta_1$ and $\theta_2$ ($\pi>|\theta_1|\ge\cos^{-1}\mu$):
$$t_{arc}(\theta_1,\theta_2)=\frac{1}{1-\mu^2}(\cos\theta_1-\cos\theta_2)\ -\ \frac{\mu}{1-\mu^2}\int_{\theta_1}^{\theta_2}\sqrt{1-\frac{\cos^2\theta}{\mu^2}}\,d\theta \quad(25),$$
whose second term is an **elliptic integral of the second kind with no closed form** (p. 5). Physical meaning: riding speed vanishes as $\theta\downarrow\cos^{-1}\mu$ ($\dot\theta=\sqrt{1-\mu^2}\cdot 0 + \sin(\cos^{-1}\mu)$... precisely $\dot\theta\to\sqrt{1-\mu^2}>0$ at $\theta=\cos^{-1}\mu$; at $\theta=\pi/2$, $\dot\theta=\sqrt{\mu^2-0}+1$... $\dot\theta=\mu+1$) — the evader climbs from the fatal-arc boundary toward the rear.

### 5.9 Phase III and Theorem 2 (p. 5, eqs. (26)–(30)) — tangential exit
Definitions: remaining time $t_r(\theta)=T-t_s(\theta)$ (26) with $t_s(\theta)=t_{tan}+t_{arc}(\theta_{tan},\theta)$ (27); if E exits at $\theta$ with remaining $t_r$, the (modified) unconstrained optimum is $\psi=\mathrm{atan2}(\sin\theta,\ \cos\theta-t_r(\theta))$ (28) (eq. (19) restarted at the exit point). **Theorem 2 (p. 5)**: the optimal exit angle $\theta_{exit}$ satisfies
$$\mathrm{atan2}(\mu\sin\psi_{exit},\ \mu\cos\psi_{exit}-1)-\theta_{exit}=\frac{\pi}{2}\quad(29),$$
with $\psi_{exit}$ from (28) at $\theta=\theta_{exit}$ — i.e., *the unconstrained optimal trajectory leaving the circle is tangent to the circle* ($\phi_{exit}=\theta_{exit}+\pi/2$). **Proof**: restart the Section V analysis at $x_{exit}=[\cos\theta_{exit}\ \sin\theta_{exit}]^T$ with remaining time $t_r(\theta_{exit})$; the resulting relative heading $\phi_{exit}$ from (7) equals $\theta_{exit}+\pi/2$ by (29), so the straight line does not re-enter the circle. Substituting (28) simplifies (29) to
$$\big(\mu t_r+\sqrt{1+t_r^2-2t_r\cos\theta_{exit}}\big)\cos\theta_{exit}-\mu=0\quad(30).$$
**Proposition 1 (p. 5; Appendix B, p. 8)**: for any $t_r>0$, (30) has a **unique** solution $\theta_{exit}\in[\cos^{-1}\mu,\ \pi/2]$, and $\theta_{exit}\to\pi/2$ as $t_r\to\infty$. **Proof**: rearrange to $\cos\theta_{exit}\sqrt{1+t_r^2-2t_r\cos\theta_{exit}}=\mu(1-t_r\cos\theta_{exit})$ (44); square to the cubic $f(s)=2t_rs^3-(1+t_r^2(1-\mu^2))s^2-2\mu^2t_rs+\mu^2$ in $s=\cos\theta_{exit}$; sign pattern $f(-1)<0$, $f(\mu)<0$, $f(0)>0$ puts the three roots in $(-1,0)$, $(0,\mu)$, $(\mu,\infty)$ — the root in $(0,\mu)$ is the unique admissible one (note: squaring can create extraneous roots; the argument is by sign pattern on $f$ rather than an explicit check of (44) at each root — a small proof gap, mitigated by the physical tangency interpretation). Vieta: $r_1r_2+r_2r_3+r_3r_1=-\mu^2$, $r_1r_2r_3=-\mu^2/(2t_r)$, $r_1+r_2+r_3=\frac{1+t_r^2(1-\mu^2)}{2t_r}$, giving $r_2\to0, r_1\to0$-type limits, hence $\theta_{exit}\to\pi/2$ as $t_r\to\infty$.

### 5.10 Full constrained solution (p. 5, eqs. (31)–(34)) — the headline formula
Solving (30) for $t_r$:
$$t_r=\frac{\sin\theta_{exit}\sqrt{\mu^2-\cos^2\theta_{exit}}-(\mu^2-\cos^2\theta_{exit})}{(1-\mu^2)\cos\theta_{exit}}\quad(31),$$
and combining with (26)–(27) yields the scalar equation for $\theta_{exit}$:
$$\frac{\sin\theta_{exit}\sqrt{\mu^2-\cos^2\theta_{exit}}-(\mu^2-\cos^2\theta_{exit})}{(1-\mu^2)\cos\theta_{exit}}+t_{arc}(\theta_{tan},\theta_{exit})=T-t_{tan}\quad(32).$$
The LHS $f(\theta_{exit})$ has derivative $\frac{df}{d\theta_{exit}}=\frac{\mu^2\sin\theta_{exit}}{\cos^2\theta_{exit}\sqrt{\mu^2-\cos^2\theta_{exit}}\,(\sin\theta_{exit}+\sqrt{\mu^2-\cos^2\theta_{exit}})}>0$ on $[\cos^{-1}\mu,\pi/2]$ (printed p. 5), so (32) is solved by bisection. The final location (33) is the exit point plus $t_r$ along the tangent direction $\phi_{exit}=\theta_{exit}+\pi/2$:
$$\begin{bmatrix}x_f\\y_f\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_{exit}-v(\tfrac{\pi}{2}+\theta_{exit})t_r(\theta_{exit})\sin\theta_{exit}\\ \sin\theta_{exit}+v(\tfrac{\pi}{2}+\theta_{exit})t_r(\theta_{exit})\cos\theta_{exit}\end{bmatrix},\qquad v(\tfrac{\pi}{2}+\theta_{exit})=\sin\theta_{exit}+\sqrt{\mu^2-\cos^2\theta_{exit}},$$
and, using the exact identity $t_r\,v(\tfrac{\pi}{2}+\theta_{exit})=\frac{\sqrt{\mu^2-\cos^2\theta_{exit}}}{\cos\theta_{exit}}$ (re-derived symbolically: with $s=\sin\theta,c=\cos\theta,w=\sqrt{\mu^2-c^2}$, $(sw-w^2)(s+w)=w(s^2-w^2)=w(1-\mu^2)$),
$$\boxed{\ d_f^*(T,x_0)=\|x_f\|=\sqrt{1+t_r^2v^2}=\frac{\mu}{\cos\theta_{exit}}\ }\quad(34),$$
where $\theta_{exit}$ is the unique solution of (32) and depends on $T$ and $x_0$ only through $t_{tan},\theta_{tan}$. **Remark 3 (p. 6)**: $d_f^*>1$ since $\theta_{exit}\in(\cos^{-1}\mu,\pi/2)$; $d_f^*\to\infty$ as $T\to\infty$. Physical meaning: the optimal final distance equals the speed ratio divided by the cosine of the exit angle — a remarkably compact certificate; longer games push the exit toward the rear ($\theta\to\pi/2$) and the payoff to infinity.

### 5.11 Lemma 6 (p. 6, eq. (35)) — critical final time
**Statement**: the critical $T$ partitioning unconstrained ($T\le T_c$) from constrained ($T>T_c$) optimal trajectories is
$$T_c(x_0)=t_{tan}+\cos\theta_{tan}-\frac{\sin\theta_{tan}}{\tan\psi_{tan}}\quad(35),$$
with $t_{tan},\theta_{tan},\psi_{tan}$ from (22), (23), and (20)-into-(8). **Proof**: the critical trajectory grazes the circle, i.e., the unconstrained optimum (19) evaluated at the tangent point equals $\psi_{tan}$: $\mathrm{atan2}(\sin\theta_{tan},\cos\theta_{tan}-t_r)=\psi_{tan}$; taking $\tan$ gives $t_r=\cos\theta_{tan}-\sin\theta_{tan}/\tan\psi_{tan}$; add $t_{tan}$. For $T>T_c$, $\psi^*$ grows toward $\pi$ and the line cuts into the circle; for $T<T_c$ it decreases toward 0 and stays feasible. **Numerical validation**: for Fig. 6's $x_0=[2,0.3]^T$, $\mu=0.6$: $\|x_0\|=2.0224$, $\theta_{tan}=1.2034$ rad, $\phi_{tan}=2.7735$ rad, $\psi_{tan}=2.1304$ rad, $t_{tan}=\sqrt{3.09}/1.4132=1.2439$, $t_r=0.9434$, $T_c=2.187$ — matches the printed $T_c\approx2.19$ (Fig. 6, p. 6).

### 5.12 Degenerate case $y_0=0$ and the discontinuous best response (p. 6, Figs. 6–7)
For $x_0=[x_0\ 0]^T$: for all $T<x_0$ the optimal heading is $\psi^*=0$ (unconstrained); for all $T>x_0$ the three-phase constrained solution applies; at $T=x_0$, *any* constant $\psi$ not satisfying Lemma 5's intersection test is optimal, and the set of optimal final locations is a continuum (the "orange arc" of Fig. 7). The final location $x_f$ changes smoothly in $T$ except at $T=T_c$ (Fig. 6), and no optimal trajectory ever terminates on the Proximity Circle — final locations are bounded below by the dashed circle of radius $\min_T d_f^*(T,x_0)$ (Figs. 6–7).

### 5.13 Nash/Stackelberg layer (pp. 6–7, eqs. (36)–(40); Appendix C, p. 8)
**Game**: E picks $\psi(t)$, P picks the final time $T$ (the sensing interval length); a Stackelberg game with P as leader (announces $T$) is natural, and the Stackelberg solution turns out to be a Nash equilibrium (p. 6). E's best-response value $d_f^*(T,x_0)$ from the previous sections; P's problem $T_{min}(x_0)=\arg\min_{T>0}d_f^*(T,x_0)$ (36). **Lemma 7 (p. 6; Appendix C)**: for any $x_0\notin\Omega_{capture}$,
$$T_{min}(x_0)=\begin{cases}x_0-\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{1-\mu^2}}y_0, & x_0-\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{1-\mu^2}}y_0>0\\ 0, & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}\quad(37),\qquad
d_f^*(T_{min},x_0)=\begin{cases}\mu x_0+\sqrt{1-\mu^2}y_0, & T_{min}>0\\ \|x_0\|, & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}\quad(38).$$
**Proof**: $R(T,x_0)$'s farthest point gives the upper bound $\bar d_f(T,x_0)=\sqrt{(x_0-T)^2+y_0^2}+\mu T$, minimized at $T_{min}$ of (37) (set $\frac{d}{dT}=0$: $x_0-T=\mu\sqrt{(x_0-T)^2+y_0^2}$). Since $R(T,x_0)$ over-approximates E's reachable set, $d_f^*\le\bar d_f$. Lower bound: the straight line $\psi=\cos^{-1}\mu$ is feasible for *any* $T$ (Lemma 5, Remark 1), yielding $d_{f}^{subopt}(T,x_0)=\sqrt{\|x_0\|^2+(1-\mu^2)(T^2-2(x_0-\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{1-\mu^2}}y_0)T)}$ (45); chain $d_f^*(T)\ge d_f^{subopt}(T)\ge d_f^{subopt}(T_{min})=\bar d_f(T_{min})$, so $T_{min}$ is the *unique* minimizer of $d_f^*$. At $T=T_{min}$ the best response (19) collapses to
$$\psi(t,x)=\cos^{-1}\mu\quad(39),$$
**independent of $t$, $x_0$, and $T$** (algebraically: with $T_{min}=x_0-\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{1-\mu^2}}y_0$ the drift-corrected point $x-(T_{min}-t)$ factors as $(y_0+\mu\sqrt{1-\mu^2}t)\,[\mu/\sqrt{1-\mu^2},\ 1]^T$, whose angle is $\cos^{-1}\mu$); this is the maximum admissible $\phi=\pi-\sin^{-1}\mu$, never intersecting the constraint, so the whole trajectory is one straight line. **Theorem 3 (p. 7)**: an equilibrium pair of policies is
$$(\psi_{NE},T_{NE})=\Big(\cos^{-1}\mu,\ \max\big\{x-\tfrac{\mu}{\sqrt{1-\mu^2}}y,\ 0\big\}\Big)\quad(40)$$
("for a given $x$" — notation slip for the initial condition $x_0=[x\ y]^T$). **Proof**: $\psi_{NE}$ is the best response to $T_{NE}$ by (39); conversely, under $\psi_{NE}$, $\mathbf x(t)=[x+(\mu^2-1)t,\ y+\mu\sqrt{1-\mu^2}t]^T$ and $d(t)=\sqrt{x^2+y^2+(1-\mu^2)(t^2-2t(x-\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{1-\mu^2}}y))}$, whose minimum over $t$ is attained exactly at $t=T_{NE}$ — so neither player gains by unilateral deviation. **Special case $y=0$** (p. 7): $T_{NE}=x$; any constraint-avoiding constant heading is a best response to $T_{NE}$, but only (39) is *non-exploitable*; the best response jumps discontinuously across $T_{NE}$ ($\psi=0$ for $T<x$ vs. three-phase ride for $T>x$). **Remark 4 (p. 7)**: policies are pre-selected; P has no basis for a feedback policy, but E's NE policy is an open-loop commitment until $T_{NE}$ — if P plays suboptimally with $T>T_{NE}$, how E should respond after $T_{NE}$ in light of the new information "remains an open question"; for $y=0$, E may have "significant regret" for having committed (cf. Section IV-C of [11]).

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
**Author-stated**:
- The analysis is a *subproblem*: P is restricted to straight-line motion between two active-sensing instants; the full intermittent-sensing PEG (timing of costly active sensing + heading choice while blind) "forms a basis ... in future work" (pp. 1, 7).
- Remark 4 (p. 7): the Nash equilibrium demands that E commit to an open-loop policy until $T_{NE}$; E "cannot gain any information that would allow him to exploit any suboptimal behavior by P during the time $t\le T_{NE}$"; how E should respond *after* $T_{NE}$ if P announces/plays $T>T_{NE}$ is explicitly open; and for $y=0$ the discontinuous best response can make commitment costly (regret), cf. [11], Sec. IV-C.
- The riding time requires numerical evaluation of an elliptic integral and bisection on (32) (p. 5) — no closed form, though monotonicity is proven.
**Critical observations (with page/eq.)**:
1. **The "sensing-limited" aspect never enters the math** (Abstract, p. 1 vs. Sec. II): the circle of radius 1 is the *capture* radius $r_{cap}$; the detection/sensing radius, the active-sensing cost, and the timing decision are all abstracted away. The paper's actual result is optimal evasion from a *constant-velocity, capture-radius-1* pursuer over a fixed window. The abstract's "sensing boundary" conflates the two.
2. **Asymmetric information is assumed, not modeled**: E is fully informed of $x_P$ (eq. (2) is deterministic in the relative frame) while P is blind; no noise, no estimation error, no E-side uncertainty about P's straight-line assumption.
3. **Open-loop OCP, no feedback synthesis**: Lemma 4's constant heading and the three-phase policy are open-loop; no value function/HJI verification, no robustness to P deviating from straight-line motion within the window.
4. **Lemma 1's "proof by inspection"** (p. 2) and Theorem 1's reliance on [13] (p. 4) leave the geometric optimality arguments informal (see Sec. 5.14, items 6–7).
5. **Typesetting errata** in Appendix A (items 1–2, Sec. 5.14) do not change the results but impede verification; Remark 2's $\mu$-independence claim (p. 4) is wrong as stated.
6. **Scope**: 2D, single pursuer/evader, simple motion, unconstrained evader turn rate, no P control; Lemma 7's $T_{min}=0$ branch means for favorable $x_0$ the pursuer's best is to end the game immediately — the game value $\|x_0\|$ then equals the initial separation, i.e., the "game" is trivial there; extensions to 3D or multiple intervals are not discussed.

## 8. Citation Value
**Paragraph role**: this is the canonical *building-block* reference for intermittent-sensing pursuit–evasion: whenever a paper models a pursuer that senses only intermittently (event-/self-triggered sensing, costly communication, limited-range passive detection plus scheduled active looks), the inter-observation window reduces to exactly this fixed-horizon, state-constrained evasion problem, and P065 supplies the complete best-response catalog — no-escape zone $\Omega_{capture}$ with exact survival time $T_{survive}$ (Lemma 3), unconstrained constant heading $\psi^*=\mathrm{atan2}(y,x-(T-t))$ (Lemma 4), and the three-phase tangent-entry / boundary-ride (elliptic integral) / tangential-exit solution with the compact value $d_f^*=\mu/\cos\theta_{exit}$ (eq. (34)) and critical time $T_c$ (eq. (35)). Its second layer — the pursuer choosing the window length, with the equilibrium $(\cos^{-1}\mu,\ \max\{x-\mu y/\sqrt{1-\mu^2},0\})$ — is a rare example of a Stackelberg game *over the horizon* in a PEG and pairs naturally with reach-avoid/target-defense papers that fix engagement times. **Suggested citation sentences**: "The evader's optimal response to a pursuer moving at constant velocity between sensing instants consists of a tangent entry, a boundary-riding arc governed by an elliptic integral of the second kind, and a tangential exit at the unique angle $\theta_{exit}$ for which the departing trajectory grazes the Proximity Circle, yielding the final distance $d_f^*=\mu/\cos\theta_{exit}$ [P065]." and "For a pursuer that selects the inter-sensing interval, the unique Stackelberg/Nash equilibrium pairs the state-independent evader heading $\psi=\cos^{-1}\mu$ with the announced horizon $T=\max\{x-\mu y/\sqrt{1-\mu^2},0\}$ [P065]." In the P065-corpus lineage it bridges [1] (Dorothy et al., one-Apollonius-circle PEGs, Automatica 2024) and the authors' costly-information LQ line [8]–[11], and it is the natural comparator for any limited-FOV/limited-range or trigger-scheduled pursuit paper.
