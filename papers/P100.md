## 2. Research Background
The paper studies a two-team, many-player reach–avoid (RA) differential game of kind played in a bounded convex planar domain Ω partitioned by a straight target line T into a target region Ωtar and a play region Ωplay. The evasion team wants to move as many of its members (evaders) as possible into Ωtar, while the pursuit team — initially distributed in Ωplay ∪ T (and, in the relaxed setting, also inside Ωtar) — tries to capture evaders in Ωplay before they reach T. Classical HJI-based treatments of RA games are either limited to two/three players by the curse of dimensionality or require numerical grid solutions; geometric tools (Apollonius circles, Voronoi diagrams, shortest paths) give constructive strategies only in restricted settings. Existing multiplayer RA results ([9] Chen–Zhou–Tomlin TAC 2017, [28] Margellos–Lygeros, [29] Fisac et al.) construct barriers only for one-pursuer–one-evader pairs, so cooperative capture (two pursuers jointly catching an evader that neither can catch alone) is invisible to their matching stage, making their maximum matching suboptimal for the pursuit team. This article builds the complete analytical machinery — barrier, winning regions, and a maximum interception matching — for arbitrary numbers of players in general convex domains.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
**Problem** (p. 109–112): Given Np pursuers and Ne evaders with simple motion (1) and speed ratio α = vE/vP ∈ (0,1) in a convex Ω with target line T, determine the task assignment (matching of pursuit coalitions to evaders) that maximizes the number of evaders captured in Ωplay before reaching T when both teams play optimally — a game of kind [24].

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Theory — one-pursuer barrier (Theorem 1)**: closed-form, three-piece barrier B1 for one pursuer vs. one evader in any convex Ωplay, composed of two circular arcs (centered at the target-line endpoints m, n) and one middle hyperbola-type arc (p. 113–114).
2. **Theory — two-pursuer barrier (Theorem 2)**: five-piece analytical barrier for two pursuers vs. one evader, with the active/inactive pursuer dichotomy (Definition 3) and the unique equal-arrival point pc on T (p. 115–116).
3. **Theory — general-coalition barrier (Theorem 3 + Lemma 3 + Algorithm 1 + Theorem 4)**: analytical barrier for a full-active pursuit coalition of arbitrary size nk, constructed inductively along an x-rank index set; every barrier segment depends on at most two pursuers; any general coalition degenerates to its unique largest full-active subcoalition with an identical barrier (p. 116–118). First direct barrier construction for more than three players (authors' claim).
4. **Modeling — relaxed initial deployment (Definition 7, Assumption 5, Lemma 4, Corollary 1)**: pursuers starting inside Ωtar are replaced by "virtual pursuers" mirrored across T (mirror property), reducing Assumption-3 games to Assumption-2 games (p. 119).
5. **Algorithm — task assignment (Lemma 5, Theorem 5, Algorithms 1–2)**: since any capturable evader is capturable by some ≤2-pursuer coalition, maximum matching over all 2^Np − 1 coalitions (a constrained bipartite matching, stated to be NP-hard class "NP-problem") reduces to a 0-1 integer program with Nv = Ne·Np(Np+1)/2 binary variables and Nv + Ne + Np inequality constraints, whose optimum q is the guaranteed number of intercepted evaders (p. 120–121).
6. **Experiment — simulations** validating the barriers, the matching, the optimal-match-size claim, and superiority over [9], [28], [29], [38] in coverage (p. 121–122).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results

### 5.1 Setup: dynamics, information, cost, termination
- **Dynamics (eq. (1), p. 109)**: decoupled simple motion, ẋPi = vPi·uPi, ẋEj = vEj·uEj, xi(0) = x0i; controls u ∈ U = {u ∈ R² : ‖u‖₂ = 1}. Point-mass players, point capture (positions coincide) inside Ωplay; once an evader enters Ωtar it can no longer be captured (p. 109).
- **Domain (p. 109–110)**: Ω ⊂ R² bounded, convex; target line T ⊂ Ω with endpoints m, n, ‖m − n‖₂ = l; Ωtar compact with T ⊂ Ωtar; Ωplay = Ω \ Ωtar. Coordinates: origin at m, x-axis along T, y-axis toward Ωtar; hence evaders satisfy y0Ej < 0.
- **Assumptions (p. 110)**: Ass. 1 distinct initial positions; Ass. 2 (constrained) pursuers in Ωplay ∪ T, evaders in Ωplay; Ass. 3 (relaxed) pursuers anywhere in Ω; Ass. 4 homogeneous speeds, α = vE/vP ∈ (0,1) (slower evaders).
- **Information structure (p. 110)**: nonanticipative — the pursuit team chooses its input knowing the evaders' current inputs; the evader knows the pursuers' declared strategy. Stated to be an instantiation of a Stackelberg game [1], citing [27], [53] (Elliott–Kalton). Crucially this lets a pursuer "mirror" the evader's chosen crossing point, which underpins all barrier derivations.
- **Payoff / value (eq. (11), p. 112)**: for coalition k (with index set Ik) vs. evader Ej, J = min_{i∈Ik} ‖xPi(t1) − xEj(t1)‖₂, V = min_{Pk∈U^nk} max_{uEj∈U} J, where t1 is Ej's first arrival time at T. This "safe distance on arrival" (evader maximizes the distance to the closest pursuer at the moment it reaches T; pursuers minimize it) makes the game a game of kind: V = 0 characterizes the barrier, V > 0 the evasion winning region.
- **Winning regions (Problems 1–2, p. 112)**: W_P^nk = capturable region of coalition k; W_E^nk = uncapturable region; barrier B^nk(X0k) is the separating curve with B^nk ∪ W_P^nk ∪ W_E^nk = Ωplay; W_E^nk is the subregion adjacent to T. Notation: B̆, W̆P, W̆E denote the same objects ignoring the boundary of Ωplay (p. 112).

### 5.3 Lemma 1 (Monotony of Function) — p. 111, eqs. (5)–(6)
**Statement**: Given x0Pi, x0Ej with y0Ej < 0 and x0Pi ≤ x0Ej, if A ∩ {y = 0} = {(c1,0), (c2,0)}, c1 < c2, then G1(xp) = ‖p − x0Pi‖₂ − ‖p − x0Ej‖₂/α, p = (xp, 0) (eq. (5)), is strictly increasing on [c1, x*p] and strictly decreasing on [x*p, c2], where x*p is the unique solution in [c1, c2] of the quartic-type stationarity equation (6): (x*p − x0Pi)/‖p* − x0Pi‖₂ = (x*p − x0Ej)/(α‖p* − x0Ej‖₂), p* = (x*p, 0).
**Proof approach**: (i) interpret G1(xp) for xp ∈ [c1, c2] as the Pi–Ej distance at the moment Ej arrives at p when both head straight to p; (ii) circle geometry gives G1(c1) = G1(c2) = 0, G1 > 0 on (c1, c2); (iii) existence of the maximizer by continuity; (iv) stationarity G1′(x*p) = 0 ⇔ (6); (v) for x0Pi < x0Ej, (7) ‖p* − x0Pi‖₂ ≥ ‖p* − x0Ej‖₂/α > α‖p* − x0Ej‖₂ combined with (6) gives (8) |x*p − x0Pi| > |x*p − x0Ej|, hence x*p > x0Ej > x0Pi; (vi) define G2 (eq. (9)) = [‖p*−x0Pi‖₂²(x*p−x0Ej)²]/[‖p*−x0Ej‖₂²(x*p−x0Pi)²], so (6) ⇔ G2 = α² < 1, which implies inequality (10): (y0Ej)²(x*p−x0Pi)² − (y0Pi)²(x*p−x0Ej)² > 0; (vii) G2 is strictly monotonic under (10) and x*p > x0Ej > x0Pi, so G2 = α² has the unique root x*p; (viii) case x0Pi = x0Ej forces x*p = x0Ej by (7). **Decisive relation**: G2(x*p) = α² with (9)–(10). **Meaning**: the evader's optimal target point (OTP) on T is unique and interior maxima of the arrival-distance function are well-posed — the analytic backbone for Theorems 1–3. **Proof gap (minor, p. 111)**: the strict monotonicity of G2 is asserted ("by computing the derivative of G2(x*p) … it can be verified") without printing the derivative computation.

### 5.4 Lemma 2 (Optimal Trajectories) — p. 113, eq. (15)
**Statement**: for the payoff (11) restricted to a single pursuer, optimal trajectories of Pi and Ej are straight lines. **Proof**: Hamiltonian H = vP uPiλ1ᵀ + vE uEjλ2ᵀ; Isaacs' method gives u*Pi = −λ1/‖λ1‖₂, u*Ej = λ2/‖λ2‖₂, λ̇1 = λ̇2 = 0 (15): constant costates ⇒ time-invariant optimal controls ⇒ straight lines. **Meaning**: legitimates reducing the game to a one-dimensional optimization of the arrival-distance over T, and (with convexity of Ωplay) legitimating B = B̆ ∩ Ωplay in every theorem.

### 5.5 Theorem 1 (Barrier for One Pursuer) — p. 113–114, eqs. (12), (16)–(26)
**Statement**: under Assumptions 1, 2, 4, if coalition k = {Pi}, then B1(X0k) = B̆1 ∩ Ωplay with B̆1 = ∪³s=1 B̆1s, B̆1s = F1s(x0Pi), where (eq. (12) / base curves of Definition 5):
- F11(h1) = {z = (x,y) : ‖z − m‖₂ = α‖h1 − m‖₂, x ≤ k1, y < 0}, k1 = α²x1 — circular arc centered at the left endpoint m;
- F12(h1) = {z : (x − x1)² + (1 − 1/α²)y² + (1 − α²)y1² = 0, x ∈ (k1, k2), y < 0}, k2 = (1 − α²)l + α²x1 — hyperbola-type arc (coefficient 1 − 1/α² < 0);
- F13(h1) = {z : ‖z − n‖₂ = α‖h1 − n‖₂, x ≥ k2, y < 0} — circular arc centered at the right endpoint n;
(h1 = (x1, y1) = pursuer position; all y < 0 restricts to Ωplay side).
**Proof approach** (7 steps): (i) assume Ej reaches T and let its OTP be p* = (x*p, 0) maximizing (16) J = ‖xPi(t1) − xEj(t1)‖₂; (ii) by Lemma 2 both optimal paths are straight lines and by the nonanticipative structure Pi heads to the same point of T as Ej; (iii) the payoff reduces to G1(xp) of (17) (= (5) with p ∈ T); (iv) barrier condition x0Ej ∈ B̆1 ⇔ capture exactly at arrival ⇔ V = 0 ⇔ (19) G1(x*p) = 0, i.e., ‖p* − x0Ej‖₂ = α‖p* − x0Pi‖₂ — capture and arrival simultaneously; (v) interior OTP case x*p ∈ (0, l): stationarity (18) plus (19) yield (20) x*p = (x0Ej − α²x0Pi)/(1 − α²); substituting back gives the **decisive relation (21)**: (x0Ej − x0Pi)² + (1 − 1/α²)(y0Ej)² + (1 − α²)(y0Pi)² = 0, with x-range (22) x0Ej ∈ (α²x0Pi, (1 − α²)l + α²x0Pi); collecting all such z gives B̆12 = F12 (23); (vi) endpoint OTP x*p = 0 (p* = m): (24) ‖x0Ej − m‖₂ = α‖x0Pi − m‖₂ with x0Ej ≤ α²x0Pi (from (22) + Lemma 1 monotonicity) gives B̆11 (25); symmetrically x*p = l gives B̆13 (26); (vii) B1 = B̆1 ∩ Ωplay because optimal trajectories are straight lines and Ωplay is convex; the EWR is the subregion bordering T. **Local/global, analytic**: global, fully analytic. **Meaning**: on B1 the evader is caught exactly upon touching T; the closed (barrier-bounded) region containing T is the EWR. **Extensibility**: template for all subsequent constructions; convexity is the only property of Ωplay used.

### 5.6 Theorem 2 (Barrier for Two Pursuers) — p. 115–116, eqs. (13), (27)–(35)

(a) If only one pursuer is active (Definition 3: Pi is active in k iff ∃ p ∈ T that Pi reaches before all others in k), then B2(X0k) = B1(x0Pi), i = i1 or i2. (b) If both are active, B2(X0k) = B̆2 ∩ Ωplay, B̆2 = ∪⁵s=1 B̆2s, B̆2s = F2s(x0Pi1, x0Pi2) (eq. ### 5.8 Lemma 3 (Barrier Equivalence) + Algorithm 1 + Theorem 4 — p. 118, eqs. (43)–(47)
**Lemma 3**: for any coalition k, B^nk(X0k) = B^nk̄(X̄0k), where X̄0k is the initial position set of the unique largest full-active pursuit subcoalition. **Proof approach**: enough to show W̆E^n̄k ⊆ W̆E^nk. For p ∈ W̆E^n̄k there is p1 ∈ T with (43) ‖p − p1‖₂ < α‖x0Pi − p1‖₂ for all pursuers in X̄0k — this inequality is the paper's operational EWR criterion (evader can reach p1 strictly earlier than any pursuer can reach p1, with speed ratio α). If an inactive Pj violated it, i.e., (44) α‖x0Pj − p1‖₂ ≤ ‖p − p1‖₂ held, then (45) ‖x0Pj − p1‖₂ < ‖x0Pi − p1‖₂ for all Pi ∈ X̄0k, so Pj would reach p1 before everyone — contradicting maximality of the full-active subcoalition; hence (46) holds for all pursuers of X0k and p ∈ W̆E^nk. Uniqueness of X̄0k by a contradiction argument using Definition 6. **Algorithm 1 (p. 118)**: for each pursuer Pmi in k: T1 ← T; intersect T1 with RD(x0Pmi, x0Pmj) ∩ T1 over all j ≠ i (RD per (47)); if T1 ≠ ∅, Pmi is active — O(nk²) half-plane/segment tests. **Theorem 4**: B^nk(X0k) = B^n̄k(X̄0k), with X̄0k from Algorithm 1 and B^n̄k from Theorem 3. **Meaning**: inactive pursuers never change the barrier; the coalition's capture power is exactly that of its active core. Note the EWR criterion (43)/(48) is the analytic characterization of "guaranteed arrival" used throughout (also in Lemma 4's proof).

### 5.9 Mirror property: Definition 7, Assumption 5, Lemma 4, Corollary 1 — p. 119, eqs. (48)–(49)
**Definition 7 (Virtual pursuer)**: for x0Pi = (x0Pi, y0Pi) ∈ Ωtar, define x̃0Pi = (x0Pi, −y0Pi) — reflection across T. **Assumption 5**: no virtual pursuer coincides with another pursuer (coincidence with its own original allowed, when x0Pi ∈ T). **Lemma 4 (Mirror Property)**: B^nk(X0k) = B^nk(X0k(−i) ∪ x̃0Pi). **Proof**: p ∈ W_E^nk(X0k(−i) ∪ x̃0Pi) gives p1 ∈ T with (48) ‖p − p1‖₂ < α‖x0Pj − p1‖₂ for all j in the replaced set; reflection gives (49) ‖x0Pi − p1‖₂ = ‖x̃0Pi − p1‖₂, so the same p1 works for X0k; converse identical; equality of EWRs plus "B is the separating curve" finishes it. The discussion (Fig. 8) notes the virtual pursuer may lie outside Ω without harm since optimal trajectories are straight lines in the convex Ω. **Corollary 1 (Barrier for Relaxed Initial Deployment)**: under Assumptions 1, 3, 4, with X0k,1 ⊂ Ωplay and X0k,2 ⊂ Ωtar, B^nk(X0k) = B^nk(X0k,1 ∪ X̃0k,2), computable by Theorem 4. **Meaning**: pursuers resting inside the protected target zone protect exactly as their mirror images; the Assumption-3 game is reduced to the Assumption-2 theory at zero cost.

### 5.10 Lemma 5 (Degeneration of Pursuit Coalition) — p. 120
**Statement**: for any coalition k with nk ≥ 3, if x0Ej ∈ W_P^nk then some two-pursuer subcoalition k1 ⊂ k has x0Ej ∈ W_P^{nk1}. **Proof**: by Theorem 3 every barrier segment depends on ≤ 2 pursuers (F3 splits into two two-pursuer parts); hence capture by a big coalition is already certified by a pair. **Remark 4 (p. 120)**: any maximum matching can be reduced to one using only execution pursuit coalitions (Definition 8: nk ∈ {1, 2}) without losing global optimality in the number of intercepted evaders. **Remark 5**: general-coalition analysis remains useful for deployability design (shaping capturable regions) and is what reveals the degeneration.

### 5.11 Theorem 5 (Maximum Matching) — p. 120–121, eq. (50), Algorithms 1–2
**Setup**: bipartite graph G = (P, E, E) with coalition nodes P = {1, …, 2^Np − 1} and evader nodes E = {1, …, Ne}; edge eij exists iff coalition i guarantees capture of Ej in Ωplay or T (obtained by checking x0Ej ∈ W_P for each coalition's barrier). Prior information vector (Definition 9): r = [r1_1, …, r1_Np, r2_1,2, …, r2_Np−1,Np] ∈ R^{Nv}, Nv = Ne(Np + Np(Np−1)/2) = Ne·Np(Np+1)/2, with r1_i(j) = 0 iff x0Ej ∈ W1_E(x0Pi) (Pi cannot guarantee capture) and r2_i1,i2(j) defined analogously — so r(j-slot) = 1 encodes feasibility. Strategy vector z = [s1, s2]ᵀ ∈ {0,1}^{Nv}; Σj s1_i(j) ≤ 1 and Σj s2_i1,i2(j) ≤ 1 (each coalition intercepts at most one evader).
**Statement**: under Assumptions 1, 3, 4, the number q of evaders the pursuit team can guarantee to keep out of Ωtar is
q = maximize cᵀz s.t. A1z ≤ b1, A2z ≤ b2, A3z ≤ b3, z(i) ∈ {0,1} (eq. (50)),
with c = ones(Nv, 1), b1 = rᵀ, A1 = I_{Nv} (assignment must be feasible per prior information), b2 = ones(Ne, 1), A2 = ones(1, Nv/Ne) ⊗ I_Ne (each evader matched at most once), b3 = ones(Np, 1), and A3 built by Algorithm 2 so that A3z ≤ b3 enforces each pursuer appearing in at most one chosen coalition (rows tagged by the index-arithmetic conditions j == i − k + (k − 1)(Np − k/2), k < i, and (i−1)(Np − i/2) < j ≤ i(Np − (i+1)/2) for two-pursuer coalitions containing Pi; then A3 ← A3 ⊗ ones(1, Ne) and A3 ← [I_{Np} ⊗ ones(1, Ne), A3] for the one-pursuer part). The optimal assignment is z* = argmax_z cᵀz.
**Proof approach (short, constructive)**: the objective counts assigned (hence captured) evaders; constraint 1 restricts to coalitions that truly guarantee capture (per Sections III–IV); constraint 2 forbids double-assigning an evader; constraint 3 forbids re-using a pursuer across coalitions; Algorithm 2 enumerates, for each pursuer, all execution coalitions containing it, making the IP well-posed. **Complexity remark (Remark 7)**: the original problem is a (2^Np − 1) × Ne constrained bipartite matching (an "NP-problem" as printed); the IP has only Nv = Ne·Np(Np+1)/2 variables and Nv + Ne + Np constraints, and its first constraint collapses when r is sparse. q is unique; z* need not be. **Definition 10**: matching pairs M1(z*) = {(i, j) : s1*_i(j) = 1} (one-to-one) and M2(z*) = {(i1, i2, j) : s2*_{i1,i2}(j) = 1} (two-to-one). **Meaning**: q is the exact game value for the pursuit team in the game-of-kind sense ("the most evaders captured"), because (i) any feasible interception scheme uses disjoint coalitions, (ii) by Lemma 5 only ≤2-pursuer coalitions are ever needed, and (iii) matched pairs play their Theorem 1–3 optimal strategies. **Extensibility**: r is the only problem-dependent input; re-planning = recomputing barriers + re-solving a small IP (real-time, per simulations).

### 5.12 Winning/termination summary
Evader Ej wins (escapes) iff it reaches T; capture = position coincidence in Ωplay; on the barrier V = 0 (simultaneity); inside W_E the EWR criterion "∃p1 ∈ T : ‖p − p1‖₂ < α‖x0Pi − p1‖₂ ∀ pursuers" (eqs. (43), (48)) certifies guaranteed arrival regardless of pursuit strategies; inside W_P the mirrored straight-line strategies of Lemma 2 certify capture before T. The whole analysis is of game of kind (winning-set determination), not a quantitative HJ value.

**Case 1 (p. 121–122, Fig. 9): Np = 5, Ne = 6.**
- Scenario: 31 pursuit coalitions (2⁵ − 1); barriers and winning regions computed for all; Fig. 9(a) all five one-pursuer coalitions; (b)–(d) two-, three-, four-pursuer coalitions (two depicted each); (e) five-pursuer coalition plus the resulting maximum matching.

**Case 2 (p. 122, Fig. 10): Np = 3, Ne = 2.**
- Purpose: show the matching is nontrivial and differs from "assign the two closest pursuers to each evader". Fig. 10(a): barriers for P1 (brown), P2 (orange), P1–P2 pair (red); the blue region marks evader initial positions capturable only by the P1–P2 cooperation. Fig. 10(b): same for P1 and P3; P3 dominates P2 (P2's barrier lies entirely inside P3's PWR), so the triple P1–P2–P3 has the same barrier as the pair P1–P3 — a live illustration of Lemma 3/Theorem 4. Although P1 and P2 are both closer to E1 than P3, and although E2 is closer to P3 than E1, the optimum assigns P3 (with P1) to the farther E1 — non-intuitive, cooperation-driven assignment.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
**Author-stated (p. 122–123)**: (i) all players use simple motion with instantaneous turns — Isaacs–Dubins-car-type dynamics are future work; (ii) convex domains only; nonconvex domains via conservative convex approximation is "straightforward" but left open; (iii) centralized computation and full information — distributed multiplayer RA games with limited communication/computation are the announced next focus. Also implicit: point capture, homogeneous speeds within each team, slower evaders (α < 1), straight target line (T must be a line segment for the mirror property and endpoint arcs).

**Critical observations**:
1. **Information structure asymmetry (p. 110)**: the nonanticipative/Stackelberg structure lets pursuers react to the evaders' current inputs ("Pi knows Ej's current input", used in the Theorem 1 proof, p. 113–114). All winning-region claims are for this structure; under plain simultaneous-play state feedback the barrier could differ, and the paper does not prove robustness of W_P/W_E to that change (the authors note the evader "is at a slight disadvantage" — the resulting PWR may overstate capture capability).
2. **No HJI verification**: the barrier is derived from straight-line optimal play and dominance-region logic; there is no formal proof (e.g., via a viscosity-solution value or Isaacs' condition) that the constructed B coincides with the HJI barrier of the game; rigor rests on Lemma 1–2 plus the EWR criterion (43)/(48). In particular the identification "green region = PWR" in Theorems 1–3 is argued geometrically (Figs. 5–7) rather than proven against all deviating strategies.
3. **Minor proof gaps**: Lemma 1's monotonicity of G2 asserted, not computed (p. 111); Theorem 2's Part (b) middle case reuses Theorem 1's derivation without re-deriving the evader-side bound for the interval [k4, k5]; eq. (36) prints "×" between union groups (typo, verified from p. 116 render).
4. **Combinatorial cost**: barrier count still scales as 2^Np − 1 coalitions (31 barriers for Np = 5); the IP reduces the matching, not the barrier enumeration. For large Np, coalition enumeration remains exponential; the paper's complexity claim is relative to the NP constrained-bipartite-matching formulation ("NP-problem", p. 121 — the term is used loosely; constrained matching is polynomial in general, the hardness here coming from the pursuer-disjointness side constraints and the coalition structure).
5. **Independence assumption across pairs**: Theorem 5 counts captures pairwise; interactions between simultaneously executing matches (one pursuer's presence affecting another pair's barrier, multiple evaders' joint play) are excluded by construction of the payoff (11) and the matching semantics; the upper-bound argument (that unmatched evaders indeed escape) implicitly relies on the teams' optimal play within the fixed assignment.
6. **Simulation scope**: no ground-truth comparison (e.g., grid-based HJI), no tracking of the actual closed-loop trajectories after assignment inside the printed pages, single instance per case; timings are machine-specific.
7. The quartic/stationarity equation (6) has no closed-form root printed; the "analytical" claim holds for the barrier curves themselves, while Lemma 1's x*p (not needed for B) would be solved numerically.

## 8. Citation Value
**Paragraph role**: this is the reference work for *analytical* (grid-free) barrier and winning-region construction in multiplayer reach–avoid games of kind with simple motion, slower evaders, and straight-line target sets in convex domains, and for the reduction of the resulting cooperative task-assignment problem to a small 0-1 integer program. Its two structural lemmas — (a) every multiplayer barrier segment depends on at most two pursuers, hence cooperative capture never needs more than two pursuers (Lemma 5), and (b) pursuers inside the target set act as their mirror images (Lemma 4) — are the results most worth quoting, together with the demonstration that distance-greedy assignment is strictly suboptimal (Case 2). Cite it as: "Yan, Shi and Zhong [P100] constructed analytical barriers for pursuit coalitions of arbitrary size in convex reach–avoid games and obtained the optimal pursuer–evader matching via a 0-1 integer program over two-pursuer coalitions, showing that at most two pursuers ever need to cooperate to intercept one evader and that the resulting maximum matching, computable in real time, strictly dominates pairwise-outcome assignments."; also usable as the convex-domain generalization of square-domain analytical barriers [38] and as the analytical contrast to numerical HJI multiplayer RA treatments [9], [28], [29].
