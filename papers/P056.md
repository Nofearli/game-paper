## 2. Research Background

Military reconnaissance by UAVs must penetrate an anti-reconnaissance environment with active interception and fast maneuvering; prior reconnaissance-penetration work addressed only two-party settings (attacker vs. static defender, penetration path planning, self-discovery probability) [3], [4]. The multiplayer pursuit-evasion toolbox (reach-avoid games [15]–[17], engage-or-retreat [18], guarding a line segment [19], active target defense [20], [21]) treats target protection but, the authors note, seldom considers the attacker's own need to avoid being captured while approaching the target. In the classical TAD game the defender is free to move anywhere; here the defender (intercepting UAV) is tied to a communication base station and can only maneuver inside a circular territory $\Omega_D$ of radius $l$ — a realistic constraint induced by the communication range. The paper builds directly on the authors' own line of cooperative target-defense games [22]–[25] (in particular the Automatica 2019 paper [24]) and adds the territorial constraint as the new structural element.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap

**Problem.** Three simple-motion players in $\mathbb{R}^2$: target T (speed $V_T$), attacker A = reconnaissance UAV (speed $V_A$), defender D = intercepting UAV (speed $V_D$), with $V_T < V_A < V_D$ and $V_A$ normalized to 1 ($\alpha = V_T/V_A < 1$, $\beta = V_D/V_A > 1$). A wants to reconnoiter T "as close as possible" — modeled as point capture ($d_{AT} = 0$) — while avoiding capture by D ($d_{AD} > 0$); D is confined to $\Omega_D = \{(x,y): (x-x_{D0})^2 + (y-y_{D0})^2 \le l^2\}$ centered at its/base station's initial position; T tries to evade, possibly by rendezvousing with D. Termination set $D = D_1 \cup D_2 \cup D_3$ with $D_1 = \{d_{AT}=0\}$ (A captures T, A wins), $D_2 = \{d_{AD}=0\}$ (D captures A, T–D wins), $D_3 = \{d_{TD}=0\}$ (T–D rendezvous, T–D wins). Questions P1–P4: effect of territory size on the outcome; initial states from which A captures T; initial states from which D protects T; optimal escape/capture/rescue strategies.

**Gap — [authors' claim]:** existing three-player TAD analyses assume an unconstrained defender, and reconnaissance-penetration studies ignore the moving-target three-party structure; no analytical solution exists for a TAD-type game with a territorially confined defender. **[Verified from cited text]:** the cited reach-avoid/TAD papers ([15]–[21]) indeed use unconstrained defenders (constraints appear only as obstacles [27] or escort/retreat regions [18]); [27] (Oyler et al., pursuit-evasion with obstacles) treats the obstacle as blocking the *pursuer*, whereas here the constraint region simultaneously acts as the attacker's hazard and the target's refuge, which is a genuinely different structure. The claimed novelty of the territorial-constrained defender is supported. However, the paper's own gap statement that "How the Attacker could avoid being captured by the Defender has seldom been considered" is only partially accurate — evader-capture avoidance is central in [16], [18], [27]; the distinctive point is the *combination* with a defended moving target.

## 4. Core Contributions

1. **Modeling**: a new reconnaissance-penetration game with a territorial-constrained defender under the multiplayer pursuit-evasion framework, with both target maneuvering evasion and active interception defense (theory/modeling).
2. **Method — explicit policy method**: instead of Isaacs' HJI machinery (hard here because of the three termination sets), the qualitative game (game of kind) is solved by imposing explicit candidate strategies, analyzing pairwise Apollonius-dominance geometry, deriving an "escape angle" for the attacker, and partitioning initial states into three cases by territory size (theory/method).
3. **Games of kind**: analytic winning conditions (Theorems 4.1–4.7) and switching strategies for all three players; the barrier/winning regions are assembled from the condition curves (theory).
4. **Fusion of kind and degree**: within each winning region, capture-time payoffs are solved via PMP (constant costates ⇒ constant optimal headings) giving closed-form optimal headings for T (and degenerate ones for D), fused with the geometric switching strategies of the game of kind (algorithm/analysis).
5. **Simulation** validating the policies in all regions (experiment).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results

### 5.0 Setup (Sec. II, pp. 6296)

- **Dynamics (eq. (1))**: simple motion for each player, e.g. $\dot{x}_T = V_T\cos\hat\phi,\ \dot{y}_T = V_T\sin\hat\phi$ (initial conditions $x_{T0},y_{T0}$), analogously A with heading $\hat\chi$ and D with heading $\hat\psi$; speeds constant, $V_T < V_A = 1 < V_D$.
- **Territorial constraint (the paper's central modeling object)**: $x_D(t) \in \Omega_D := \{(x,y)\,|\,(x-x_{D0})^2+(y-y_{D0})^2 \le l^2\}$, radius $l$ determined by the communication base-station capability ("obtained in the early reconnaissance"). Note $D$ starts at the center $D_0 = (x_{D0},y_{D0})$.
- **Relative variables**: $\varphi = \hat\phi - \lambda_{AT}$, $\chi = \lambda_{AT} + \theta - \hat\chi$, $\psi = \hat\psi - \theta - \lambda_{AT} + \pi$ (relative headings w.r.t. $\overrightarrow{AT}$, $\overrightarrow{DA}$, $\overrightarrow{AD}$), $\lambda_{AT}$ = inclination of $\overrightarrow{AT}$, $\theta = \angle DAT$, and distances $d_{ij}$. Information structure: only relative distances/velocities needed (onboard camera, UWB) — absolute positions not required.
- **Winning/termination conditions**: A wins iff $d_{AT}\to 0$ with $d_{AD}>0$ ($D_1$); team T–D wins via $D_2$ ($d_{AD}=0$) or $D_3$ ($d_{TD}=0$).

### 5.1 Dominant region (Def. 3.1, Thm 3.1, p. 6296)

- **Definition 3.1**: the dominant region $\Omega_P$ of a pursuer = set of points the pursuer reaches before the evader.
- **Theorem 3.1 (p. 6296)**: for a simple-motion PE game without a bounded region, dominant regions are divided by an Apollonius circle [26], [27]. (Cited, not proved here.)
- Application: with $O_1$ the Apollonius circle of (D, A) ($|AX|/|DX| = V_A/V_D = 1/\beta$ on it), interior of $O_1$ = A's dominant region, exterior $O_1^{\complement}$ = D's; with the constraint, D's dominant region is $\Omega_D \cap O_1^{\complement}$ and A's is $\Omega_A = \Omega_D^{\complement} \cup O_1$. Circle centers/radii used throughout: $O_1 = \left(\frac{\beta^2 x_A - x_D}{\beta^2-1}, \frac{\beta^2 y_A - y_D}{\beta^2-1}\right)$, $r_1 = \frac{\beta d_{AD}}{\beta^2-1}$ (eq. (4)–(5)); $O_2$ (Apollonius circle of A and T, ratio $1/\alpha$) $= \left(\frac{x_T - \alpha^2 x_A}{1-\alpha^2}, \frac{y_T - \alpha^2 y_A}{1-\alpha^2}\right)$, $r_2 = \frac{\alpha d_{AT}}{1-\alpha^2}$ (eq. (19)–(20)). I verified both formulas against the standard Apollonius construction and numerically (e.g., $A=(0,0), D=(0,5), \beta=1.2$: center $(0,-11.364)$, $r_1=13.636$ ✓).

### 5.2 Escape angle (Thm 3.2, p. 6297, eq. (2))

**Theorem 3.2.** If A starts inside $\Omega_D$ (and $O_1$ intersects $\Omega_D$), the **escape angle** of A is
$$\Theta = 2\angle QAO_1 = 2\arccos\!\left(\frac{(\beta^2-1)l^2 - \beta^2 d_{AD}^2}{2\beta l d_{AD}}\right) \tag{2}$$
where $P, Q$ are the intersection points of $O_1$ and $\Omega_D$. It is the angular size (at A) of the wedge, centered on the ray from A away from $O_1$'s center, inside which A exits $\Omega_D$ before D can intercept; A must steer into the interior of this wedge to escape.

*Proof approach*: (i) on $O_1$, $|AQ|/|DQ| = 1/\beta$ (eq. (3)); (ii) with D at the center of $\Omega_D$, $|DQ| = l$ so $|AQ| = l/\beta$; (iii) $O_1$ cuts line $DA$ at $M$ with $|MA| = d_{AD}/(\beta+1)$, $|MD| = \beta d_{AD}/(\beta+1)$ (eq. (6)), giving $|O_1A| = r_1 - |MA| = d_{AD}/(\beta^2-1)$ (eq. (7)); (iv) law of cosines in $\triangle QAO_1$ yields eq. (8) $\cos\angle QAO_1 = \frac{(\beta^2-1)l^2 - \beta^2 d_{AD}^2}{2\beta l d_{AD}}$; (v) symmetry gives (2). Decisive relation: eq. (8). Local (initial-state) result, analytic, exact. *Numerical check* ($d_{AD}=5, l=10, \beta=1.2$): $\cos\angle QAO_1 = 8/120 = 0.0667$, $\Theta \approx 172.4°$; direct coordinate computation of $P,Q$ reproduces this exactly.
- **Case division**: (2) requires $O_1 \cap \Omega_D \ne \emptyset$, i.e. $l - r_1 < |O_1D| < l + r_1$ (eq. (9)), which simplifies to $\frac{\beta}{\beta+1}d_{AD} \le l \le \frac{\beta}{\beta-1}d_{AD}$ (**Ordinary case**); **Small case** $l < \frac{\beta}{\beta+1}d_{AD}$ (then automatically $l < d_{AD}$: A starts outside $\Omega_D$); **Large case** $l > \frac{\beta}{\beta-1}d_{AD}$ (then $O_1 \subset \Omega_D$: A has no escape angle). I verified the simplification of (9) algebraically.

**Remark 3.1** (p. 6297): the explicit policy method combined with geometric analysis solves mixed qualitative differential games with constraints analytically, but the explicit-policy design is constraint-dependent and must be re-derived per problem.

### 5.3 Ordinary case (Sec. IV-A, pp. 6297–6299)

**Theorem 4.1 (p. 6297, eq. (10)–(11); A pursues T without switching).** If (with $m_1 = d_{AT}^2 + d_{AD}^2 - d_{TD}^2$)
$$\frac{m_1}{2d_{AT}d_{AD}} \times \frac{(\beta^2-1)l^2 - \beta^2 d_{AD}^2}{2\beta l d_{AD}} + \frac{\sqrt{4d_{AT}^2d_{AD}^2 - m_1^2}}{2d_{AT}d_{AD}} \times \frac{\sqrt{4\beta^2l^2d_{AD}^2 - \left((\beta^2-1)l^2 - \beta^2 d_{AD}^2\right)^2}}{2\beta l d_{AD}} < \sqrt{1-\alpha^2} \tag{10}$$
then A can adopt the direct strategy $\chi = \theta - \arcsin(\alpha\sin\varphi)$ (eq. (11)) and win.

*Proof approach*: (i) sine theorem in $\triangle TAG$ with T heading to a point G: $\sin\varphi/\sin(\theta-\chi) = 1/\alpha$ (eq. (12)) ⇒ strategy (11) (classical Apollonius "mirror" pursuit — capture occurs in/on $O_2$); (ii) direct pursuit is safe iff the whole disk $O_2$ lies in A's escape wedge; since A sees $O_2$ under half-angle $\arcsin\alpha$ (because $|AO_2| = d_{AT}/(1-\alpha^2)$ and $r_2/|AO_2| = \alpha$; verified numerically), the condition is $\angle QAT > \arcsin\alpha$ with $\angle QAT = \theta - \angle DAQ$; (iii) $\cos\angle QAT < \sqrt{1-\alpha^2}$ (eq. (13)); (iv) law of cosines in $\triangle DAT$ and $\triangle DAQ$: $\cos\theta = \frac{m_1}{2d_{AD}d_{AT}}$, $\cos\angle DAQ = \frac{d_{AD}^2 + l^2/\beta^2 - l^2}{2d_{AD}\, l/\beta}$ (eq. (14)); substituting (14) into (13) gives (10). Decisive intermediate: eq. (13)–(14).
- **Proof gap / erratum (verified)**: the first numerator in (10) is printed as $(\beta^2-1)l^2 - \beta^2 d_{AD}^2$, but eq. (14) gives $\cos\angle DAQ = \frac{\beta^2 d_{AD}^2 - (\beta^2-1)l^2}{2\beta l d_{AD}}$ — the **opposite sign**. Boundary numerical check ($d_{AD}=5, l=10, \beta=1.2$, $d_{AT}=8$, $\theta = \angle DAQ + \arcsin\alpha$): corrected LHS $= 0.801 = \sqrt{1-\alpha^2}$ (tight, as a boundary must be), printed version gives $0.714$. So (10) should read with $(\beta^2 d_{AD}^2 - (\beta^2-1)l^2)$ in the first term.
- **Implicit branch restriction (verified against the paper's own simulation)**: (10) presupposes the Fig. 3(a) configuration $\theta > \angle DAQ$ (T beyond the tangent ray $AQ$). Without it the inequality is not a valid criterion: for the paper's own Fig. 7(b) state $x_{T0}=(20,25)$ ($\theta = 38.7° < \angle DAQ = 93.8°$), the printed LHS equals $\cos(\theta - \angle DAQ) = 0.571 < 0.8$, yet the paper treats this state with the switching strategy. The theorem statement omits the branch condition.

**Theorem 4.2 (p. 6298, eq. (15)–(18); A pursues T with switching strategy).** If
$$\arccos(K) < \frac{\beta d_{TD} - \beta l - \alpha l}{\alpha\beta l} \tag{15}$$
with $K = \frac{m_3}{2d_{TD}d_{AD}} \times \frac{d_{AD}^2 + l^2 - l^2/\beta^2}{2ld_{AD}} + \frac{\sqrt{4d_{TD}^2d_{AD}^2 - m_3^2}}{2d_{TD}d_{AD}} \times \frac{\sqrt{4l^2d_{AD}^2 - (d_{AD}^2 + l^2 - l^2/\beta^2)^2}}{2ld_{AD}}$, $m_3 = d_{AD}^2 + d_{TD}^2 - d_{AT}^2$, then A captures T before T reaches $\Omega_D$.
*Proof approach*: (i) T beelines to the nearest boundary point $G$ = line $TD \cap \partial\Omega_D$; $t_3 = (d_{TD}-l)/V_T$; (ii) A first reaches $Q$ (on $O_1\cap\Omega_D$, $|AQ| = l/\beta$) exactly when D would: $t_1 = l/V_D$; (iii) A then moves along the periphery of $\Omega_D$ toward G with tangential strategy $\hat\chi = \arctan\frac{y_A - y_{D0}}{x_A - x_{D0}} + \pi/2$ (eq. (16)) and $t_2 = \widehat{QG}/V_A$; (iv) law of cosines gives $\cos\angle ADT = \frac{m_3}{2d_{TD}d_{AD}}$, $\cos\angle ADQ = \frac{d_{AD}^2 + l^2 - l^2/\beta^2}{2ld_{RD}}$ (eq. (17) — **printed typo**: $d_{RD}$ should be $d_{AD}$; verified in the 400-dpi crop), hence $\angle QDG = \arccos K$; (v) $t_1 + t_2 < t_3$ rearranges exactly to (15) (I re-derived the rearrangement: $\arccos K < \frac{d_{TD}-l}{\alpha l} - \frac{1}{\beta}$ ✓). Decisive inequality: $t_1+t_2<t_3$.
- The resulting **three-stage switching strategy** of A: stage 1 — head to crossover point $P$ or $Q$ with $\sin\hat\chi = \frac{y_i - y_A}{\sqrt{(x_i-x_A)^2+(y_i-y_A)^2}}$, $\cos\hat\chi = \frac{x_i - x_A}{\sqrt{\cdot}}$ (eq. (18), $i = P,Q$; closed-form coordinates of $P,Q$ in Appendix A, eq. (52)–(55)); stage 2 — periphery patrol (16) until $\theta > \angle DAQ + \arcsin\alpha$; stage 3 — direct pursuit (11).
- **Critical observation**: the timing compares A vs T only; it does not model D contesting the entry point G (D can always reach G no later than A since $l/V_D < t_1 + t_2$), and T's entry point is fixed a priori at the nearest boundary point. The condition is therefore a constructive sufficient result under an assumed T behavior and a passive D, not a verified saddle-point winning condition (see §7).

**Conditions for T's survival — rendezvous region (Sec. IV-A.3, pp. 6298–6299).** T's dominant region in the (A,T) PE game is the *interior* of $O_2$; D's is $\Omega_D \cap O_1^{\complement}$. Define the **rendezvous region** $\Omega_{TD} = \{(x,y) \in \Omega_D \cap O_2 \cap O_1^{\complement}\}$ (red region in Fig. 5). Closed-form intersections $G, H$ of $O_2$ with $\Omega_D$ via eq. (21)–(24) (chord geometry with slopes $k_3, k_4$, eq. (22)). Rendezvous strategies (eq. (25)): T and D head straight to a chosen point $P_{TD} \in \Omega_{TD}$. I verified the internal logic rigorously: since $P_{TD} \in \mathrm{int}(O_2)$ and $O_2$'s interior is convex with $T \in \mathrm{int}(O_2)$, T's whole straight path lies in T's dominance region (no en-route interception); since $P_{TD} \in O_1^{\complement}$, D arrives before A; hence rendezvous at $\max(t_T, t_D) < t_A$ is guaranteed against any A strategy — this part is exact.

**Theorem 4.3 (p. 6299, eq. (26)–(28)).** If
$$d_{TD}^2 - \alpha^2 d_{AD}^2 < (1-\alpha^2)l^2 + 2\alpha l d_{AT} \tag{26}$$
$$d_{TD} > \alpha d_{AD} + \beta d_{AT} \tag{27}$$
and there exists $i \in \{G,H\}$ (the two intersection points of $O_2$ and $\Omega_D$) with $(x_i - a_1)^2 + (y_i - b_1)^2 > r_1^2$ (28), then T–D win by rendezvous.
*Proof approach*: (i) $O_2$ not strictly inside $O_1$: $|O_1O_2| > r_1 - r_2$, stated "equivalent to (27) [24]"; (ii) $\Omega_D$ intersect $O_2$: $|O_2D| < l + r_2$ (eq. (29)) — substituting (19)–(20) and expanding with $2d_{AD}d_{AT}\cos\theta = m_1$ yields exactly (26) (I re-derived this: $|O_1O_2|^2 < (l+r_2)^2$ reduces to (26) after cancellation ✓); (iii) the lens $\Omega_D \cap O_2$ must poke outside $O_1$, checked at its edge points $G, H$ ⇒ (28).
- **Erratum (verified, important)**: condition (27) as printed has the wrong inequality direction. Algebra on $|O_1O_2|^2 \lessgtr (r_1-r_2)^2$ (expanding with $\overrightarrow{AD}\cdot\overrightarrow{AT} = m_1/2$) gives $|O_1O_2| < r_1 - r_2 \iff d_{TD} > \alpha d_{AD} + \beta d_{AT}$ — i.e. printed (27) is equivalent to $O_2$ *strictly inside* $O_1$, the exact opposite of what the proof's step (i) requires, and it makes (28) unsatisfiable (both $G,H$ would lie inside $O_1$). Consistency checks: (a) monotonicity — constraining D cannot shrink A's classical winning region, and the classical (unconstrained) result, re-derived here and consistent with Theorem 4.7's (39), is "A wins iff $d_{TD} > \alpha d_{AD} + \beta d_{AT}$"; (b) the paper's own Fig. 8(a) state $x_{T0}=(10,-5)$: $d_{TD} = 14.14 < 16.42 = \alpha d_{AD} + \beta d_{AT}$ while (26) and (28) hold and the paper claims the T–D rendezvous win. Hence (27) must read $d_{TD} < \alpha d_{AD} + \beta d_{AT}$. Fig. 6 additionally shows the yellow (27)-locus (a Cartesian oval around A, foci A and D) bounding A's winning side, confirming the direction.

**Remark 4.1 (p. 6299, eq. (30)–(32); direct rendezvous).** If $\angle ADQ < \angle ADT$ — equivalently $\frac{d_{AD}^2 + d_{TD}^2 - d_{AT}^2}{d_{TD}} < \frac{d_{AD}^2 + l^2 - (l/\beta)^2}{l}$ (eq. ### 5.4 Small case (Sec. IV-B, pp. 6299–6300)

Here $O_1$ and $\Omega_D$ are separate; $\Omega_D$ acts as an obstacle for A, and T survives only by entering $\Omega_D$ before interception. Escape angle (eq. (33)): $\Theta = 2(\pi - \arcsin(l/d_{AD}))$ — the complement of the cone subtended by $\Omega_D$ at A (tangent construction; verified). Note $\Omega_D \subset O_1^{\complement}$, so $\Omega_{TD} = \Omega_D \cap O_2$ and condition (28) is automatic.

**Theorem 4.4 (p. 6299, eq. (34)–(35); A directly pursues T).** If
$$m_1\sqrt{d_{AD}^2 - l^2} + l\sqrt{4d_{AT}^2d_{AD}^2 - m_1^2} < 4d_{AT}d_{AD}^2\sqrt{1-\alpha^2} \tag{34}$$
($m_1$ as before), A adopts (11) and captures T. *Proof*: tangency gives $\cos\angle DAQ = \frac{\sqrt{d_{AD}^2-l^2}}{d_{AD}}$, $\sin\angle DAQ = \frac{l}{d_{AD}}$ (35); substituting with (14) into (13).
- **Erratum (verified)**: the RHS coefficient is wrong by a factor 2. Expanding $\cos\theta\cos\angle DAQ + \sin\theta\sin\angle DAQ < \sqrt{1-\alpha^2}$ gives $m_1\sqrt{d_{AD}^2-l^2} + l\sqrt{4d_{AT}^2d_{AD}^2 - m_1^2} < 2d_{AT}d_{AD}^2\sqrt{1-\alpha^2}$. Boundary check ($d_{AD}=10, l=4, d_{AT}=8$, $\theta = \angle DAQ + \arcsin\alpha$): LHS $= 1279.2 \approx 1280 = 2d_{AT}d_{AD}^2\sqrt{1-\alpha^2}$ (tight), whereas the printed RHS is $2560$; the printed (34) over-admits states where direct pursuit actually loses.

**Theorem 4.5 (p. 6299–6300, eq. (36)–(37); A bypasses $\Omega_D$).** If
$$\arccos(K_1) < \frac{d_{TD} - l - \alpha\sqrt{d_{AD}^2 - l^2}}{\alpha l}, \qquad K_1 = \frac{m_3 l + \sqrt{4d_{AD}^2d_{TD}^2 - m_3^2}\,\sqrt{d_{AD}^2 - l^2}}{2d_{AD}^2 d_{TD}}, \tag{36}$$

### 5.5 Large case (Sec. IV-C, p. 6300)

**Theorem 4.7 (p. 6300, eq. (39)).** If
$$d_{TD} > \alpha d_{AD} + \beta d_{AT} \tag{39}$$
A wins. *Proof*: A's dominant region collapses to $O_1$ (every boundary point of $\Omega_D$ lies in $O_1^{\complement}$, so A can never leave $\Omega_D$); A captures T iff $O_2$ is strictly inside $O_1$, i.e. $|O_1O_2| < r_1 - r_2$; substituting (4), (5), (19), (20) yields (39). I verified the equivalence $|O_1O_2| < r_1 - r_2 \iff d_{TD} > \alpha d_{AD} + \beta d_{AT}$ in closed form (expansion reduces to $d_{TD}^2 > (\alpha d_{AD} + \beta d_{AT})^2$ ✓), and the statement reproduces the unconstrained result of [24] ("the restricted area is so large that it does not influence the victory or defeat"). Note that (39) is exactly the printed expression of (27) — further evidence that (27)'s direction is a typo, since one and the same inequality cannot characterize both $O_2 \subset O_1$ (Thm 4.7) and $O_2 \not\subset O_1$ (Thm 4.3).

### 5.6 Division of the winning region (Sec. IV-D, p. 6300, Fig. 6)

### 5.7 Games of degree — optimal strategies (Sec. V, pp. 6300–6301)

**Winning region of A (eq. (40)–(50)).** Payoff: $\min_{\chi(\cdot)}\max_{\varphi(\cdot),\psi(\cdot)} J_1 = \int_{t_0}^{t_f} 1\,dt$ (capture time; A minimizes, T–D maximize). Hamiltonian (41): $H = 1 + \lambda_1 V_T\cos\hat\varphi + \lambda_2 V_T\sin\hat\varphi + \lambda_3 V_A\cos\hat\chi + \lambda_4 V_A\sin\hat\chi + \lambda_5 V_D\cos\hat\psi + \lambda_6 V_D\sin\hat\psi$. Optimal headings from $\partial H/\partial u = 0$ (42) (cos/sin of each heading $\propto \pm\lambda_i/\|\lambda\|$). Costate dynamics $\dot\lambda = -\partial H/\partial x$ give $\dot\lambda_1 = \dots = \dot\lambda_6 = 0$: **all costates constant ⇒ all optimal headings constant**. TPBVP in $\mathbb{R}^{12}$ closed by transversality $\lambda(t_f) = \partial G/\partial x$ with augmented terminal value $G(x_f) = v_1(x_{Af}-x_{Tf}) + v_2(y_{Af}-y_{Tf})$, giving $\lambda_1 = -v_1, \lambda_2 = -v_2, \lambda_3 = v_1, \lambda_4 = v_2, \lambda_5 = \lambda_6 = 0$ (43).
- **Region 1** (direct pursuit; terminal condition (45) $x_{Af} = x_{Tf}$): $v_1 = x_A - x_T$, $v_2 = y_A - y_T$ (46), hence the classic closed-form headings (47): $\cos\hat\varphi = \cos\hat\chi = \frac{x_T - x_A}{d_{AT}}$, $\sin\hat\varphi = \sin\hat\chi = \frac{y_T - y_A}{d_{AT}}$ — A chases along the line of sight, T escapes directly away from A (capture time $d_{AT}/(1-\alpha)$, though not stated explicitly). Note $\lambda_5 = \lambda_6 = 0$: D's heading is **singular/indeterminate** — the paper does not specify D's play in region 1 (any $\hat\psi$ is optimal to first order).
- **Region 2** (switching, three stages): stage-terminal manifold of stage 2 is $|T_f - A_f|^2 + |A_f - D_0|^2 = |T_f - D_0|^2$ — i.e. $\overrightarrow{AT_f} \perp \overrightarrow{AD_0}$, which (A being on $\partial\Omega_D$) is exactly tangency of line AT to $\Omega_D$; transversality gives $\lambda_1 = 2(x_{D0} - x_A),\ \lambda_2 = 2(y_{D0} - y_A)$ (48) and T's optimal heading (49): toward the base station ("move behind D"); in stage 1 T's heading (50) aims behind D relative to crossover point $i = P, Q$. A's controls remain the geometric (18), (16), (11). Note: only T's headings are PMP-optimized per stage with A's strategy fixed a priori — a heuristic fusion, with no saddle-point/verification argument.
**Winning region of T–D (eq. (51)).** $\max_{\chi(\cdot)}\min_{\varphi(\cdot),\psi(\cdot)} J_2 = \int 1\,dt$; terminal condition $x_{Df} = x_{Tf}$ (rendezvous); "A cannot affect the end time", so no PMP analysis is carried out — strategies (25)/(30)–(31) are reused.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues

**Author-stated**: only simple motion and point capture; "more complex players and constraints will be considered" (Sec. VI); the explicit policy must be redesigned per constraint type (Remark 3.1).

**Critical observations (with locus)**:
1. **Printed errata** affecting correctness of the stated winning conditions: (10) sign, (27) direction, (34) factor 2 (§5.3–5.4). A reader applying Theorem 4.3 or 4.4 as printed will misclassify states.
2. **Missing branch conditions** in Theorems 4.1/4.4: the criterion $\angle QAT = \theta - \angle DAQ > \arcsin\alpha$ presumes T beyond the tangent ray; otherwise the cosine inequality is vacuous (demonstrated with the paper's own Fig. 7(b) state, p. 6301).
3. **Sufficient-condition, not verified saddle-point partition**: Theorems 4.2/4.5 time A against a T that beelines to the *nearest* entry point; T could choose another entry point, and D's contest of the entry point (D always reaches G no later than A since $l/V_D < t_1$) is absent from the timing, so the claimed A-wins conditions in regions 2 are not proven against optimal T–D play. Likewise, A's safety while patrolling $\partial\Omega_D$ (stage 2) is asserted, not proven.
4. **Completeness of the barrier** is not established: no proof that the union of gray + blue regions exhausts the state space (states satisfying none of (10)/(15)/(26)–(28)/(32) are not discussed), and no necessity proof for A's region-2 conditions.
5. **D's role is degenerate in the game of degree**: $\lambda_5 = \lambda_6 = 0$ in region 1 (p. 6301) leaves D's optimal rescue unspecified; in region 2, only T's headings are optimized with A's strategy fixed — the "fusion of games of kind and degree" is a heuristic pipeline, not a verified optimal synthesis (no Isaacs' equation check, no viscosity/HJI comparison).
6. **Modeling gaps**: point capture ($d_{AT}=0$) is unrealistic for reconnaissance (the abstract says "reconnoiter as close as possible", but the payoff never trades off reconnaissance quality vs. risk); $\Omega_D$ centered at $D$'s initial position and static; perfect information of relative geometry; (28) checks only the chord endpoints $G, H$ although the lens $\Omega_D \cap O_2$ could in principle poke outside $O_1$ elsewhere; the ordinary case with A starting *outside* $\Omega_D$ (possible when $\frac{\beta}{\beta+1}d_{AD} \le l < d_{AD}$) is not analyzed (Sec. IV-A silently assumes A inside).
7. **Editorial**: "R" vs "A" notation residues (eq. (31), (56), Sec. V-A, Fig. 7 caption); eq. (17) $d_{RD}$; reference [19] author list garbled ("P. Kawcki" — the guarding-a-segment paper is by P. Kawczyński et al.); Ref. [23] journal name printed as "IEEE Trans. Autom. Control" vol. 66 no. 2 Feb. 2021 (correct) but authors' affiliation lines drop "Beijing" before "Institute of Technology".

## 8. Citation Value

**Paragraph role**: This is the first analytical treatment of a three-player TAD-type game in which the defender is confined to a circular territory, solved by an "explicit policy method" that replaces HJI barrier computation with Apollonius-dominance geometry: an escape-angle theorem for the attacker inside the territory, a case split on territory size (ordinary/small/large), constructive winning conditions and switching strategies for all three players, and a PMP layer (constant costates ⇒ constant headings) supplying capture-time-optimal escape headings — i.e., a fusion of games of kind and degree. Its value for the knowledge base is threefold: (i) it is the canonical example of *state-constrained defender* modeling in TAD games, where $\Omega_D$ simultaneously endangers the attacker and shelters the target; (ii) its Theorem 4.7 shows the constraint is inactive for large $l$, recovering the classical barrier $d_{TD} = \alpha d_{AD} + \beta d_{AT}$ of [24]; (iii) it documents reusable geometric machinery (escape angle $\Theta = 2\arccos\frac{(\beta^2-1)l^2 - \beta^2 d_{AD}^2}{2\beta l d_{AD}}$, rendezvous region $\Omega_{TD} = \Omega_D \cap O_2 \cap O_1^{\complement}$, periphery-patrol interception) — but citations should note the verified errata in (10), (27), (34) and the sufficient-condition (non-saddle-point) character of the A-side winning conditions.
**Suggested citation sentences**: "Liang et al. [P056] incorporated a communication-range constraint on the defender into the target-attacker-defender game and constructed the winning regions analytically via an explicit-policy/Apollonius-circle method, fusing the game of kind with a capture-time game of degree." Or, method-focused: "The territorial-constrained defender model of Liang et al. shows that the classical TAD barrier $d_{TD} = \alpha d_{AD} + \beta d_{AT}$ persists when the defender's dominance region contains the constrained territory, while an intermediate territory size enables target–defender rendezvous even in states where the unconstrained game would be lost by the team."
