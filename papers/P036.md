## 2. Research Background
The paper treats the **blocking, or "football," pursuit-evasion differential game**: two cooperating pursuers try to capture a ball-carrying evader **as far as possible from the goal line**, while the evader wants to be as close as possible to the goal line at the instant of capture and, if possible, to reach the line. Determining *which side wins* ahead of time — Isaacs' **game of kind** — is singled out (p. 1) as one of the most important problems in pursuit-evasion games, distinct from the **game of degree** which supplies the actual optimal strategies for a prescribed outcome. The authors' own prior work [22] (Garcia, Casbeer, Von Moll, Pachter, ACC 2019) solved only the game of degree of this football game, assuming the game starts in the pursuers' region of win, so capture evaluation was out of reach. The game of kind was recently addressed by Yan–Shi–Zhong [25] (IEEE T-RO), but only for faster pursuers with mutually equal speeds and via implicit "base curves." The stated application pull is border defense with autonomous vehicles [23], [24]. No passing mechanics appear: the "football" setup is one ball-carrier against a cooperating two-defender team.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
**Problem**: construct, in closed form, the **Barrier surface** $\mathcal B=\{\mathbf x \mid B(\mathbf x)=0\}$ that partitions the six-dimensional state space $\mathbb R^6$ of the two-pursuer/one-evader football differential game into the pursuers' winning set $\mathcal R_p=\{\mathbf x \mid B(\mathbf x)<0\}$ and the evader's winning set $\mathcal R_e=\{\mathbf x \mid B(\mathbf x)>0\}$ (eqs. (5)–(6), p. 2); under optimal play the winning team is read off by evaluating $B(\mathbf x)$ at the current state, and when the pursuer team wins, the **mode of capture** (single-pursuer vs. dual cooperative capture) is identified by which active segment of the piecewise $B$ contains the state. Two regimes are covered: (i) all players at the same speed (Section III); (ii) faster pursuers, in general with **different** speeds (Section IV).

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Theory (game of kind)**: complete solution of the game of kind of the cooperative 2P1E football differential game — an explicit Barrier surface $B(\mathbf x)=0$ in $\mathbb R^6$, piecewise defined over segments indexed by the evader's abscissa $x_E$: three segments for equal-speed players (**Theorem 1**, eqs. (7)–(8)) and five segments for faster pursuers with possibly different speeds (**Theorem 2**, eqs. (15)–(17)).
2. **Modeling**: a clean problem statement of the football pursuit-evasion DG (dynamics (2), terminal set (4), non-anticipative perfect-information feedback setting), including the "active pursuer" concept and single vs. simultaneous capture.
3. **Geometric characterization**: closed-form **cross-sections** of the 6-D Barrier surface obtained by fixing the pursuer positions — arcs of three circles for equal speeds (**Corollary 1**, p. 3) and arcs of three circles plus two hyperbola segments for fast pursuers (**Corollary 2**, p. 4) — replacing [25]'s implicit "base curves" with explicit quadratic curves.
4. **Capture-mode determination**: within the pursuers' winning region the active segment of $B$ tells whether optimal capture is by $P_1$ alone, by $P_2$ alone, or by **simultaneous cooperative capture** (explicitly stated in the proofs, pp. 2–4, and advertised in the Introduction, p. 1).
5. **Extensibility remark** (Remark, p. 5): the construction extends to more than two pursuers vs. one evader "in a straightforward manner"; with multiple evaders, pursuer-to-evader assignment must be performed first or the constructed Barrier becomes invalid and the multi-player game intractable.

## 5. Key Mathematical Results

### 5.0 Game setup, information structure, termination, winning sets (pp. 1–2)
- **Players and state (eq. (1), p. 1)**: agents $E, P_1, P_2$ with max speeds $v_E, v_1, v_2$; states $\mathbf x_E=(x_E,y_E)$, $\mathbf x_1=(x_1,y_1)$, $\mathbf x_2=(x_2,y_2)$; complete state $\mathbf x := (x_E,y_E,x_1,y_1,x_1,y_1)\in\mathbb R^6$. **Printed typo in eq. (1)** (verified at 400 dpi): the last pair is printed as $(x_1,y_1)$ but must be $(x_2,y_2)$ — eqs. (2)–(3) confirm ($\mathbf x_0 := (x_{E0},y_{E0},x_{10},y_{10},x_{20},y_{20})$).
- **Football setup**: one ball-carrying evader, **no passing**; two pursuers form a team. Game played on $\Omega=\{x,y \mid x\in[0,\bar x],\, y\ge 0\}$; goal line $G=\{(x,y)\in\Omega \mid y=0\}$ (p. 2).
- **Dynamics (eq. (2))**: simple motion, $\dot x_E=v_E\cos\phi$, $\dot y_E=v_E\sin\phi$, $\dot x_1=v_1\cos\psi_1$, $\dot y_1=v_1\sin\psi_1$, $\dot x_2=v_2\cos\psi_2$, $\dot y_2=v_2\sin\psi_2$, with initial conditions and controls $\phi,\psi_1,\psi_2\in[-\pi,\pi]$; $E$'s control is $\mathbf u_A=\{\phi\}$, the team's is $\mathbf u_B=\{\psi_1,\psi_2\}$ (p. 1–2).
- **Information structure** (p. 2): every agent knows the dynamics; causal strategies; every agent has access to the current state $\mathbf x(t)$ — a **perfect information** differential game whose optimal strategies are state feedback; "most importantly," agents do not know the opponent's current decision — **non-anticipative strategies**.
- **Termination / winning conditions (eq. (4))**: terminal set $\mathcal T := \{\mathbf x \mid y_E=0\}\cup\{\mathbf x \mid x_1=x_E, y_1=y_E\}\cup\{\mathbf x \mid x_2=x_E, y_2=y_E\}$ — the evader wins by reaching $G$ before capture; the pursuers win by **point capture** of $E$ by either pursuer. The capturing pursuer is the *active* pursuer; if capture is simultaneous both are active (p. 2).
- **Winning sets (eqs. (5)–(6))**: $\mathcal R_p:=\{\mathbf x \mid B(\mathbf x)<0\}$, $\mathcal R_e:=\{\mathbf x \mid B(\mathbf x)>0\}$, separated by the Barrier surface $\mathcal B:=\{\mathbf x \mid B(\mathbf x)=0\}$; $B(\mathbf x)$ is obtained explicitly in Sections III–IV. (Sign convention check, this archive: $B<0$ = pursuers win is consistent throughout; e.g. in Theorem 1 case 1, $B_1<0 \Leftrightarrow x_E^2+y_E^2>x_1^2+y_1^2 \Leftrightarrow$ the OBS of $P_1E$ crosses the $x$-axis left of the origin $\Leftrightarrow P_1$ covers the whole reachable goal segment.)

### 5.1 Theorem 1 (p. 2, eqs. (7)–(8)): Barrier surface, equal speeds
- **Statement**: Consider the cooperative football DG with two pursuers and one evader, all at the same speed, WLOG $x_1<x_2$. The Barrier surface is $B(\mathbf x)=0$ with
  $$B(\mathbf x)=\begin{cases} B_1(\mathbf x), & x_E\le x_1\\ B_2(\mathbf x), & x_1<x_E<x_2\\ B_3(\mathbf x), & x_E\ge x_2\end{cases}\tag{7}$$
  $$B_1=x_1^2+y_1^2-x_E^2-y_E^2;\quad B_2=(x_1-x_I)^2+y_1^2-(x_E-x_I)^2-y_E^2;\quad B_3=(x_2-\bar x)^2+y_2^2-(x_E-\bar x)^2-y_E^2,\tag{8}$$
  where $x_I=\tfrac12\,\frac{x_2^2+y_2^2-x_1^2-y_1^2}{x_2-x_1}$.
- **Assumptions**: equal speeds; $x_1<x_2$ WLOG; point capture; goal segment $[0,\bar x]$; (implicitly) non-degenerate $y$-coordinates, see §7.
- **Proof approach** (pp. 2–3, constructive geometry, no HJI): dominance regions of equal-speed players are separated by the **orthogonal bisector (OBS)** of the connecting segment. *Case 1* ($x_E\le x_1$, only $P_1$ active): OBS of $P_1E$ is $y=m_1x+n_1$ with $m_1=-\frac{x_1-x_E}{y_1-y_E}$, $n_1=\frac12\frac{x_1^2+y_1^2-x_E^2-y_E^2}{y_1-y_E}$ (eq. (9)); the barrier is obtained when this line meets the $x$-axis at $x=0$, i.e. $0=m_1(0)+n_1\Rightarrow n_1=0$ (eq. (10)); substituting (9) into (10) gives $B_1=0$. *Case 2* ($x_1<x_E<x_2$, both pursuers active): OBS of $P_1P_2$ is $y=mx+n$ (eq. (11)); its intersection with the $x$-axis is $I=(x_I,0)$, $x_I=-n/m=\tfrac12\frac{x_2^2+y_2^2-x_1^2-y_1^2}{x_2-x_1}$ (eq. (12)); simultaneous capture occurs at the intersection of OBS($P_1E$) and OBS($P_2E$) (the latter $y=m_2x+n_2$, eq. (13)); **decisive concurrency fact** (stated p. 3, unproven): since all players have the same speed, the three OBS lines meet at one point (the circumcenter of $\triangle P_1P_2E$); the barrier is when that point is on the goal line: $0=m_1x_I+n_1=-2(x_1-x_E)x_I+x_1^2+y_1^2-x_E^2-y_E^2$ (eq. (14)); adding/subtracting $x_I^2$ gives $B_2=0$. *Case 3* ($x_E\ge x_2$, only $P_2$ active): the barrier is when OBS($P_2E$) passes through the right endpoint $(\bar x,0)$: $0=m_2\bar x+n_2=-2(x_2-x_E)\bar x+x_2^2+y_2^2-x_E^2-y_E^2$ (unnumbered, p. 3) $\Rightarrow B_3=0$.
- **Local/global, analytic/numerical**: global on $\mathbb R^6$ (within the stated case partition), fully analytic.
- **Physical meaning**: when the evader is left (right) of both pursuers, its only hope is the left (right) endpoint of the goal line, and the barrier states are exactly those where $P_1$ (resp. $P_2$) can just cover that endpoint; when the evader is between the pursuers, the critical event is the triangle's circumcenter dropping onto the goal line at $(x_I,0)$ — the point where a straight run by $E$ is met simultaneously by both defenders. Only in case 2 does $B$ depend on all six coordinates ($x_I$ is a function of both pursuers' states; p. 3).
- **Extensibility**: the same OBS logic is the $\gamma\to1$ limit of the Apollonius construction of Theorem 2; fixing the pursuer positions yields Corollary 1.

### 5.2 Corollary 1 (p. 3): 2-D cross-section, equal speeds
- **Statement**: for fixed positions of $P_1,P_2$, the cross-section of the Barrier surface consists of arc segments of three circles: $x^2+y^2=x_1^2+y_1^2$ if $x\le x_1$; $(x-x_I)^2+y^2=(x_1-x_I)^2+y_1^2$ if $x_1<x<x_2$; $(x-\bar x)^2+y^2=(x_2-\bar x)^2+y_2^2$ if $x\ge x_2$. No proof printed (immediate specialization of Theorem 1; □ printed after the statement).
- **Meaning** (p. 3, Fig. 1.a): with pursuers fixed, the barrier projects to a curve made of three circular arcs centered at $(0,0)$, $(x_I,0)$, $(\bar x,0)$; an evader initially in the projected $\mathcal R_p$ is captured under optimal play; in projected $\mathcal R_e$ it reaches the goal line.

### 5.3 Theorem 2 (pp. 3–4, eqs. (15)–(17)): Barrier surface, faster pursuers with different speeds
- **Statement**: Consider the cooperative football DG with two fast pursuers and one evader; speed ratios $\gamma_1=v_E/v_1<1$, $\gamma_2=v_E/v_2<1$, in general $\gamma_1\ne\gamma_2$; WLOG $P_2$ faster than $P_1$, i.e. $\gamma=v_1/v_2<1$. The Barrier surface is $B(\mathbf x)=0$ with five segments:
  $$B(\mathbf x)=\begin{cases}
  B_1(\mathbf x), & x_E\le \gamma_1^2 x_1\\
  B_2(\mathbf x), & \gamma_1^2x_1 < x_E\le (1-\gamma_1^2)x_I+\gamma_1^2x_1\\
  B_3(\mathbf x), & (1-\gamma_1^2)x_I+\gamma_1^2x_1 < x_E\le (1-\gamma_2^2)x_I+\gamma_2^2x_2\\
  B_4(\mathbf x), & (1-\gamma_2^2)x_I+\gamma_2^2x_2 < x_E\le (1-\gamma_2^2)\bar x+\gamma_2^2x_2\\
  B_5(\mathbf x), & x_E> (1-\gamma_2^2)\bar x+\gamma_2^2x_2
  \end{cases}\tag{15}$$
  $$\begin{aligned}
  B_1&=\gamma_1^2(x_1^2+y_1^2)-x_E^2-y_E^2\\
  B_2&=\gamma_1^2\!\left(x_1^2+(1-\gamma_1^2)y_1^2\right)+\gamma_1^2x_E^2-(1-\gamma_1^2)y_E^2-2\gamma_1^2x_1x_E\\
  B_3&=\gamma_1^2\!\left((x_1-x_I)^2+y_1^2\right)-(x_E-x_I)^2-y_E^2\\
  B_4&=\gamma_2^2\!\left(x_2^2+(1-\gamma_2^2)y_2^2\right)+\gamma_2^2x_E^2-(1-\gamma_2^2)y_E^2-2\gamma_2^2x_2x_E\\
  B_5&=\gamma_2^2\!\left((x_2-\bar x)^2+y_2^2\right)-(x_E-\bar x)^2-y_E^2
  \end{aligned}\tag{16}$$
  where now
  $$x_I=\frac{x_1-\gamma^2x_2+\sqrt{\gamma^2(x_1-x_2)^2-(1-\gamma^2)(y_1^2-\gamma^2y_2^2)}}{1-\gamma^2}.\tag{17}$$
- **Assumptions**: $0<\gamma_1,\gamma_2<1$ (evader slower than each pursuer), $\gamma=v_1/v_2<1$; point capture; real $x_I$ requires the discriminant of (17) to be nonnegative (implicit, not discussed); non-degenerate geometries (see §7).
- **Proof approach** (pp. 3–4, constructive geometry via Apollonius circles): dominance regions between each pair are now separated by an **Apollonius circle**. The $EP_1$ circle is $(x-a_1)^2+(y-b_1)^2=r_1^2$ (eq. (18)) with $a_1=\frac{1}{1-\gamma_1^2}(x_E-\gamma_1^2x_1)$, $b_1=\frac{1}{1-\gamma_1^2}(y_E-\gamma_1^2y_1)$, $r_1=\frac{\gamma_1}{1-\gamma_1^2}\sqrt{(x_E-x_1)^2+(y_E-y_1)^2}$; analogously the $EP_2$ circle (eq. (19)) with $\gamma_2,x_2,y_2$; the $P_1P_2$ circle (eq. (20)) with $a=\frac{1}{1-\gamma^2}(x_1-\gamma^2x_2)$, $b=\frac{1}{1-\gamma^2}(y_1-\gamma^2y_2)$, $r=\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma^2}\sqrt{(x_1-x_2)^2+(y_1-y_2)^2}$. (All three verified in this archive as the standard locus $\lVert\mathbf z-\mathbf p\rVert=\gamma\lVert\mathbf z-\mathbf q\rVert$ from the respective speed ratios.)
  - *Case 1* ($x_E\le\gamma_1^2x_1$, only $P_1$ active; note $a_1\le0$ here): barrier when the $EP_1$ circle intersects the $x$-axis at $x=0$: $a_1^2+b_1^2=r_1^2$, written out as $\gamma_1^2(1-\gamma_1^2)(x_1^2+y_1^2)-(1-\gamma_1^2)(x_E^2+y_E^2)=0$ (unnumbered, p. 4); cancelling $(1-\gamma_1^2)$ gives $B_1=0$.
  - *Case 2* ($\gamma_1^2x_1<x_E\le(1-\gamma_1^2)x_I+\gamma_1^2x_1$; $P_1$ still the only active pursuer): barrier when the $EP_1$ circle is **tangent** to the $x$-axis at $(a_1,0)$: $b_1-r_1=0$, i.e. $\frac{y_E-\gamma_1^2y_1-\gamma_1\sqrt{(x_E-x_1)^2+(y_E-y_1)^2}}{1-\gamma_1^2}=0$, squared to $y_E^2+\gamma_1^4y_1^2=\gamma_1^2(x_E^2+y_E^2+x_1^2+y_1^2-2x_1x_E)$ (unnumbered, p. 4); rearranging gives $B_2=0$. **Squaring is the decisive step** — see §7 on reversibility.
  - *Case 3* ($(1-\gamma_1^2)x_I+\gamma_1^2x_1<x_E\le(1-\gamma_2^2)x_I+\gamma_2^2x_2$): **simultaneous capture by both pursuers**; the barrier is when the intersection of the $EP_1$ and $EP_2$ circles lies on the goal line at $I=(x_I,0)$. **Decisive concurrency fact** (stated p. 4, unproven, no citation): "a third circle, the $P_1P_2$ Apollonius circle, intersects the other two at the same intersection points." Using (20) at $x=x_I$: $x^2-2ax+a^2+b^2-r^2=0$ (eq. (21)), whose "applicable solution" is $x_I=a+\sqrt{r^2-b^2}$ (eq. (22)) — the **right** intercept of the $P_1P_2$ circle with the goal line; substituting $a,b,r$ yields (17) (this archive re-derived (17) from (22) symbolically and confirmed it). Then the $EP_1$ circle condition $(x_I-a_1)^2+b_1^2=r_1^2$ is written as $\big((1-\gamma_1)x_I-(x_E-\gamma_1^2x_1)\big)^2+(y_E-\gamma_1^2y_1)^2=\gamma_1^2\big((x_E-x_1)^2+(y_E-y_1)^2\big)^2$ (unnumbered, p. 4 — **printed with two typos**, see §7), then as $\gamma_1^2(x_1^2+y_1^2)+2x_I(x_E-\gamma_1^2x_1)-(1-\gamma_1^2)x_I^2-x_E^2-y_E^2=0$ (unnumbered, p. 4 — verified correct), and rearranged to $B_3=0$.
  - *Case 4* ($(1-\gamma_2^2)x_I+\gamma_2^2x_2<x_E\le(1-\gamma_2^2)\bar x+\gamma_2^2x_2$; only $P_2$ active): $EP_2$ circle tangent to the $x$-axis; "similar to the second case" → $B_4=0$.
  - *Case 5* ($x_E>(1-\gamma_2^2)\bar x+\gamma_2^2x_2$; only $P_2$ active): $EP_2$ circle intersects the $x$-axis at $x=\bar x$; "similar to the first case" → $B_5=0$.
- **Local/global, analytic/numerical**: global on $\mathbb R^6$ over the five-segment partition; fully analytic; no HJI equation is written or verified anywhere in the paper — the barrier is justified purely by dominance-region geometry.
- **Physical meaning (this archive's synthesis of pp. 3–4)**: the five segments trace the critical goal-line interception point from left to right as the evader's abscissa grows: $P_1$ covering the left endpoint $(0,0)$ → $P_1$'s Apollonius circle just touching the goal line (tangency point $x=a_1$ sweeps from $0$ to $x_I$) → joint interception at $(x_I,0)$ where all three Apollonius circles meet the goal line → $P_2$'s tangency (from $x_I$ to $\bar x$) → $P_2$ covering the right endpoint $(\bar x,0)$. The segment boundaries are exactly where the Apollonius center $a_1$ (resp. $a_2$) equals $0$, $x_I$, $x_I$, $\bar x$: $a_1=\frac{x_E-\gamma_1^2x_1}{1-\gamma_1^2}=x_I \Leftrightarrow x_E=(1-\gamma_1^2)x_I+\gamma_1^2x_1$, etc. Sign consistency re-verified in this archive: $B_1<0\Leftrightarrow$ $P_1$ reaches the origin no later than $E$; $B_2<0\Leftrightarrow b_1>r_1\Leftrightarrow$ the $EP_1$ circle lies strictly above the goal line $\Leftrightarrow$ no goal point is in $E$'s dominance region of $P_1$; $B_3<0\Leftrightarrow d(E,I)>\gamma_1 d(P_1,I)$ at the joint point. Capture-mode readout: $B_1,B_2$ active → $P_1$ captures alone; $B_3$ → cooperative simultaneous capture; $B_4,B_5$ → $P_2$ captures alone.
- **Extensibility**: Remark (p. 5) — more than two pursuers vs. one evader extends "in a straight-forward manner"; multi-evader play requires pursuer-to-evader assignment, otherwise a pursuer chasing two evaders invalidates the Barrier and renders the multi-player game intractable.

### 5.4 Corollary 2 (p. 4): 2-D cross-section, fast pursuers
- **Statement**: for fixed positions of $P_1,P_2$, the cross-section of the Barrier surface consists of arc segments of three circles and arc segments of two hyperbolas, matched to the five segments of (15): $x^2+y^2=\gamma_1^2(x_1^2+y_1^2)$ if $x\le\gamma_1^2x_1$; $\gamma_1^2x^2-(1-\gamma_1^2)y^2-2\gamma_1^2x_1x+\gamma_1^2\big(x_1^2+(1-\gamma_1^2)y_1^2\big)=0$ if $\gamma_1^2x_1<x\le(1-\gamma_1^2)x_I+\gamma_1^2x_1$; $(x-x_I)^2+y^2=\gamma_1^2\big((x_1-x_I)^2+y_1^2\big)$ if $(1-\gamma_1^2)x_I+\gamma_1^2x_1<x\le(1-\gamma_2^2)x_I+\gamma_2^2x_2$; $\gamma_2^2x^2-(1-\gamma_2^2)y^2-2\gamma_2^2x_2x+\gamma_2^2\big(x_2^2+(1-\gamma_2^2)y_2^2\big)=0$ if $(1-\gamma_2^2)x_I+\gamma_2^2x_2<x\le(1-\gamma_2^2)\bar x+\gamma_2^2x_2$; $(x-\bar x)^2+y^2=\gamma_2^2\big((x_2-\bar x)^2+y_2^2\big)$ if $x>(1-\gamma_2^2)\bar x+\gamma_2^2x_2$. No proof printed (□ after statement; immediate specialization of Theorem 2). The two "hyperbola" segments are exactly $B_2=B_4=0$ with $x$ in place of $x_E$ — coefficients of $x^2$ and $y^2$ of opposite sign, hence genuinely hyperbolas; this is the paper's headline geometric improvement over [25].

### 5.5 Proof-status annotations and proof gaps (traceability)
- **Proof gap 1 (semipermeability not verified)**: nowhere does the paper prove that the constructed $B(\mathbf x)=0$ is *the* Isaacs barrier (no HJI equation, no retrogression/separatrix argument, no formal necessity/sufficiency proof that $B<0\Rightarrow$ capture and $B>0\Rightarrow$ escape). The identification rests on dominance-region geometry (OBS/Apollonius coverage of the goal line), which is convincing but presented only through the case-by-case constructions of pp. 2–4. The abstract's "Under optimal play, the winning team is determined by evaluating the associated Barrier function" is therefore an assertion backed by geometric reasoning, not by a printed verification.
- **Proof gap 2 (concurrency facts unproven)**: (a) p. 3: the three OBS lines of a triangle "intersect at the same point" (circumcenter property — standard, cited to no one); (b) p. 4: the $P_1P_2$ Apollonius circle "intersects the other two at the same intersection points." ## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
**Author-stated**:
- Multi-evader extension requires a pursuer-to-evader assignment first; without it a pursuer may chase two evaders, "which renders the herein constructed Barrier surface invalid and the solution of the attendant multi-player differential game intractable" (Remark, p. 5).
- Simple-motion dynamics and a straight goal line are fixed throughout; no extension to curved borders, obstacles, or richer dynamics is attempted (whole paper).
- The paper solves only the game of kind — no optimal strategies/payout (game of degree) inside $\mathcal R_p$ or $\mathcal R_e$ are given here; for the game of degree the reader is pointed to [22] (pp. 1–2).

**Critical observations (this archive, with page/eq. anchors)**:
- **No semipermeability/HJI verification** of the barrier (Proof gap 1, §5.5) — the strongest methodological limitation: the "solution of the game of kind" is argued geometrically, and edge regimes where the construction degenerates are silently excluded.
- **Degenerate configurations undiscussed**: OBS slopes divide by $y_1-y_E$, $y_2-y_1$, $y_2-y_E$ (eqs. (9), (11), (13)) — equal-height configurations are excluded implicitly; eq. (22)/(17) requires $r^2\ge b^2$ and a nonnegative discriminant; the tangency cases require $y_E-\gamma_1^2y_1\ge0$ for reversibility of squaring (p. 4). None is stated as an assumption of Theorems 1–2.
- **Faster-evader regime absent**: only $v_E\le v_1,v_2$ (equal speeds, Section III) or $v_E<v_1,v_2$ (Section IV) are treated; the case of an evader faster than a pursuer — where pairwise Apollonius circles do not exist and the winning-set answer changes qualitatively — is not mentioned (pp. 1, 3).
- **Printed typos** listed in §5.5 (eq. (1) state vector; the dimensionally inconsistent intermediate equation in the $B_3$ derivation, p. 4; Fig. 1.a/1.b cross-reference, p. 5) — none affects the final results (16)–(17), all of which were re-derived or consistency-checked in this archive.
- **Case-2 subtlety**: in the segment $\gamma_1^2x_1<x_E\le(1-\gamma_1^2)x_I+\gamma_1^2x_1$ the paper says "$P_1$ is still the only active pursuer" yet the segment boundary depends on $x_I$, i.e. on both pursuers' states (p. 4) — the claim that $P_2$'s presence is irrelevant *inside* the open segment but decisive for its boundary is asserted, not proven.
- **No quantitative validation whatsoever** (Section 6): the entire empirical support is one un-parameterized figure.
- **Publication status**: arXiv-only as printed; per skill rules, cite the formally published version if it exists (to verify) rather than the preprint.

## 8. Citation Value
This preprint is the game-of-kind companion to Garcia–Casbeer–Von Moll–Pachter's ACC 2019 blocking-game paper [22]: it completes the football differential game by delivering the explicit Barrier surface that [22] lacked, and it upgrades Yan–Shi–Zhong's analytical-barrier construction [25] from implicit "base curves" to closed-form piecewise-quadratic expressions whose 2-D cross-sections are recognizable conics (circles and hyperbolas), now valid for **heterogeneous** pursuer speeds. In the knowledge base it plays three roles: (i) the canonical reference for "barrier surface by dominance-region geometry" in a 2-vs-1 goal-defense game, ideally contrasted with numerical HJI reach-avoid machinery [8] and with assignment-based analytical barriers [25]; (ii) a source of ready-made geometric objects — the five-segment barrier, the joint-interception abscissa $x_I$ (17), and the circle/hyperbola cross-sections of Corollaries 1–2 — reusable for perimeter/border-defense and capture-the-flag variants (P037, P018-line, capture-the-flag archives); (iii) an instance of the pattern "cooperative capture mode read off from which barrier segment is active," which foreshadows the two-on-one cooperative metrics of P037. Ready-to-adapt citation sentence: "Garcia et al. [P036] solved the game of kind of the cooperative football differential game by constructing, in closed form, the Barrier surface that partitions the six-dimensional state space into pursuer- and evader-winning regions — three quadratic segments for equal-speed players and five for faster pursuers with different speeds, built from orthogonal bisectors or Apollonius circles, with cross-sections consisting of circular arcs and hyperbola segments that also reveal whether optimal capture is single-pursuer or cooperative." Caveat sentence for related-work paragraphs: "[P036] derives the barrier purely from dominance geometry (no HJI/semipermeability verification) and reports no simulations; it is available only as an arXiv preprint (arXiv:2006.03682), whose formal publication status should be verified before citation."
