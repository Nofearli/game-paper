## 2. Research Background
Multiplayer reach-avoid (M-RA) differential games pit a team of pursuers against a team of evaders: evaders try to reach a goal region Ωgoal, pursuers try to intercept them first. The dominant solution paradigm decomposes the game into coalition-vs-single-evader subgames, decides each subgame by geometric tools (Voronoi, Apollonius circles, evasion spaces) or HJI computations, and then solves a constrained maximum matching over a bipartite graph to maximize the number of captured evaders (pp. 1–3). This matching problem is NP-hard [40], so existing analytical algorithms (e.g., the sequential matching algorithm, SMA) are heuristic approximations, and classical assignment algorithms (Hungarian/Auction) do not respect the "pursuer in at most one coalition" conflict constraint. In parallel, LLMs have become prominent for task planning and assignment via prompt engineering (zero-shot, few-shot, CoT, CoT-SC, ToT/GoT, self-refinement), but suffer from hallucination and lack of domain knowledge about differential games. This article is, to the authors' knowledge, the first LLM-based method for M-RA games, injecting analytical M-RA theory into the LLM's prompts and wrapping the LLM with analytic correction layers.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
**Problem**: assign pursuit coalitions (of size 1 or 2) to evaders so that the number of guaranteed captures is maximized, i.e., solve the constrained maximum matching BIP (2) over the coalition–evader bipartite graph, and output explicit pursuit strategies (p. 3–4).
- **Gap 1** [authors' claim, p. 1–2]: conventional matching algorithms (Hungarian, Auction) are not applicable to M-RA games because a pursuer may appear in many candidate coalitions, and the conflict ("a pursuer in at most one chosen coalition") creates an NP-hard matching problem [40]; existing algorithms remain heuristic. [Verified as consistent with the cited framing: the NP-hardness of (2) is attributed to [40] (Yan et al., Automatica 2022); the paper itself re-proves nothing about complexity — the sentence on p. 4 justifying this is garbled in the original ("analytical algorithms face the curse of dimensionality is a special case of integer programming"), a printing/editing error.]
- **Gap 2** [authors' claim, p. 2]: existing LLM-based assignment models "struggle with the inherent complexity of M-RA games where special geometric constraints and multiplayer coordination intertwine"; LLMs have no training knowledge of M-RA games, hallucinate infeasible/contradictory assignments, and cannot be trusted to synthesize continuous-time control laws. [Verified as the authors' positioning; the paper substantiates it empirically (Tables I–II, Fig. 8: IO/FS/CoT baselines lose >0.3 CER at large scale) rather than by any theorem.]

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Knowledge construction (modeling)**: textual knowledge extracted from analytical M-RA theory — pursuit winning knowledge Kw (Def. 3, from Lemma 2/Def. 2), coalition reduction knowledge Kr (Def. 4, from Lemma 3), constraint knowledge Kc (Def. 5, from BIP (2)) — packaged as LLM prompts (Sec. III, p. 4–5).
2. **Structured prompt engineering / planning module (algorithm)**: a two-chain reasoning process f^plan: (P_bd, P_sr, P¹_rt, P²_rt, P_or) → M0 — chain 1 evaluates one-to-one subgames, chain 2 evaluates two-to-one subgames (separated by Kr), outcomes analyzed by Kw, merged at the end under Kc (Sec. IV-A, p. 5–6, Fig. 5–6).
3. **Revision module (theory + algorithm)**: three analytic corrections Cu, Cr, Cp (Algorithms 2–4) that remove unreasonable pairs, redundant pursuers, and physical conflicts, then the SMA completes the matching on residual players; **Theorem 1** proves the final matching M is a feasible solution of the BIP (2) (Sec. IV-B, p. 7).
4. **Implementation module + guarantee (theory + experiment)**: a pursuit strategy base (ES-based strategy of Lemma 1 + pure pursuit) mapped to pursuers by Algorithm 5 (f^ime); **Theorem 2** bounds the guaranteed number of captures by |M| ≤ Nm ≤ Ne (p. 8). Validation: 1000-round Monte-Carlo matching comparisons vs IO/FS/CoT/SMA across Np = Ne ∈ {5,…,50} (Sec. V, Fig. 8, Tables I–II), 5-player trajectory simulations (Fig. 9(a)(b)), and a 6-AAV hardware experiment (Sec. VI, Figs. 10–11, Table III).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results

### 5.1 Game model and information structure (pp. 2–3)
- **Dynamics (1)** (p. 2–3): simple motion (Isaacs) mass points in the plane: ẋ_Pi = v_Pi u_Pi, ẋ_Ej = v_Ej u_Ej, with u_Pi, u_Ej ∈ U = {u: [0,∞) → S², piecewise smooth}, S² the unit disk; speed ratio α_ij = v_Pi/v_Ej > 1 (all pursuers faster than all evaders).
- **Domain**: Ωgoal = {y | g(y) ≤ 0}, T = {y | g(y) = 0}, Ωplay = {y | g(y) > 0}, with g: R² → R differentiable and Ωgoal nonempty, compact, **convex** (p. 3). Convexity is essential: Lemma 3's reduction is stated for convex goal regions.
- **Capture/termination**: E_j is captured by P_i iff distance ≤ r_i (r_i ≥ 0); a pursuer that captures one evader stays still afterward (p. 3). Evasively, E maximizes the number of evaders reaching Ωgoal; P prevents this. No payoff function is written — the game is a game of kind with a team-level counting objective.
- **Information**: state feedback — both teams act on the current positions of all players; both know speeds, radii, Ωgoal, T, Ωplay (p. 3, citing [5], [17]).
- **Subgame**: for coalition c ∈ [{1,…,Np}]⁺, P_c = {P_i | i ∈ c}; state X_cj = (X_c, x_Ej); admissible state set S_cj = {X_cj | x_Ej ∈ Ωplay, ‖x_Pi − x_Ej‖₂ > r_i ∀i ∈ c} (p. 3).
- **Definition 1 (Pursuit winning, [43]) (p. 3)**: P_c guarantees to win against E_j at X_cj ∈ S_cj if there exists a feedback strategy U_c such that, regardless of E_j's strategy, either (1) E_j is captured in Ωplay, or (2) E_j is delayed from reaching Ωplay forever unless captured.
- **Matching formulation**: bipartite graph G = (V_P ∪ V_E, E) with an edge e_cj iff P_c can ensure pursuit winning against E_j; conflict graph C = (E, Ē) with Ē = {(e_cj, e_pq) | e_cj ∈ E, e_pq ∈ E, c ≠ p, c ∩ p ≠ ∅} (p. 3). **BIP (2)** (p. 3): maximize Σ_{e_cj ∈ E} a_cj subject to Σ_{P_c∈V_P} a_cj ≤ 1 ∀E_j ∈ V_E (evader matched once); Σ_{E_j∈V_E} a_cj ≤ 1 ∀P_c ∈ V_P (coalition matched once); a_cj + a_pq ≤ 1 ∀(e_cj, e_pq) ∈ Ē (each pursuer in ≤ 1 chosen coalition). M* = optimal solution of (2); NP-hard per [40] (p. 4).

### 5.2 Knowledge-generation lemmas (imported from [40], [41], [43]) (Sec. III, pp. 4–5)
- **Definition 2 (Evasion space, [43]) (p. 4, eq. (3)–(4))**: E(c, j) = {x ∈ R² | f_ij(x) > 0 ∀i ∈ c}, where f_ij(x) = ‖x − x_Pi‖₂ − α_ij‖x − x_Ej‖₂ − r_i. Closure Ē(c, j) = {x | f_ij(x) ≥ 0 ∀i ∈ c}; stated to be strictly convex [40], degenerating to the classical Apollonius circle when r_i = 0. Physical meaning: the region E_j can occupy without being captured, no matter what P_c does — a semipermeable barrier intersection of Apollonius-type half-spaces.
- **Lemma 1 (ES-based pursuit strategy, [40]) (p. 4)**: *Assumption*: Ē(c, j) ∩ Ωgoal = ∅ at X_cj ∈ S_cj. *Claim*: d/dt d(Ē(c,j), Ωgoal) ≥ 0 if every P_i ∈ P_c adopts u*_Pi = (x_I − x_Pi)/‖x_I − x_Pi‖₂, where (x_I, y_G) ∈ R⁴ is the unique solution of the convex program **(5)**: minimize_{x,y∈R²} d(x, y) subject to f_ij(x) ≥ 0 ∀i ∈ c, g(y) ≤ 0. *Proof approach (cited, not restated)*: (i) the closest-pair structure between the convex set Ē(c,j) and convex Ωgoal is captured by the projection pair (x_I, y_G); (ii) convexity of both sets gives uniqueness; (iii) each pursuer heads to the barrier point x_I, maintaining the separating hyperplane; (iv) nonsmooth/convex-analytic arguments in [40] establish the non-approach inequality. *Decisive relation*: d/dt d(Ē(c,j), Ωgoal) ≥ 0 (eq. (5) context). *Local/global*: global, analytic given the initial condition.
- **Remark 1 (p. 4)**: equality d/dt d(Ē(c,j), Ωgoal) = 0 holds iff E_j plays u*_Ej = (x_I − x_Ej)/‖x_I − x_Ej‖₂; any other evader input makes the distance increase. Computationally, **Ē(c,j) ∩ Ωgoal = ∅ ⟺ optimal value of (5) > 0** — this is the implementable winning test used throughout the pipeline (so deciding a subgame costs one small convex program, not an HJI computation).
- **Lemma 2 (Game of kind, [40]) (p. 4)**: if Ē(c, j) ∩ Ωgoal = ∅ at X_cj ∈ S_cj, then P_c ensures pursuit winning against E_j regardless of E_j's strategy. Proof route: E_j ∈ Ē(c,j) always (Def. 2); Ē(c,j) never approaches Ωgoal under u* (Lemma 1); hence E_j can never reach Ωgoal. This converts the HJI-style game of kind into a static set-separation test.
- **Definition 3 (Pursuit winning knowledge Kw) (p. 4)**: the *textual* version of Lemma 2 for LLM consumption: for any point x in the goal region, if {distance between the pursuer and x} − {max speed of pursuer}/{max speed of evader} ∗ {distance between the evader and x} − {capture radius of pursuer} is less than 0 for the pursuer in a coalition, then the coalition can protect the goal region against the evader — i.e., the literal translation of f_ij(x) < 0 ∀x ∈ Ωgoal ∀i ∈ c. Remark 2 (p. 4) motivates this: LLMs "struggle with direct mathematical analysis" of set intersection but can execute basic arithmetic per point.
- **Lemma 3 (Coalition reduction, [41]) (p. 5)**: *Assumptions*: Ē(c, j) ∩ Ωgoal = ∅ at X_cj ∈ S_cj and |c| > 2. *Claim*: there exists a subcoalition c̄ ⊂ c with |c̄| ≤ 2 and Ē(c̄, j) ∩ Ωgoal = ∅. Consequently the optimal value of (2) is unchanged if only coalitions of size ≤ 2 are considered (p. 4–5). The paper notes the lemma is proved for convex goal regions in [41] (the text says "in the work [40]" on p. 5 while attributing the lemma to [41] — a minor citation slip) and thus applies to Ωgoal here. This collapses the 2^Np − 1 coalitions to Np + Np(Np−1)/2, which is what makes both the SMA and the LLM prompting tractable.
- **Definition 4 (Coalition reduction knowledge Kr) (p. 5)**: text form of Lemma 3 — "only consider all pursuit coalitions containing one pursuer and two pursuers, respectively."
- **Definition 5 (Constraint knowledge Kc) (p. 5)**: text form of the BIP constraints — each pursuer assigned to at most one evader, each evader to at most one coalition (printed with the typo "at to one").
- With Kw and Kr, the working graph is instantiated as V_P = [P]², V_E = E, E = {e_cj | Ē(c,j) ∩ Ωgoal = ∅} (p. 5).
- **Algorithm 1 (SMA, [40]) (p. 5)**: greedy two-phase baseline — phase 1: maximum matching M̄1 over all one-pursuer edges by maximum network flow; phase 2: on residuals U2 = [P\A1]², V2 = E\B1, an approximate matching M̄2 by local search with size-2 swaps [10]; return M̄ = M̄1 ∪ M̄2. It is a 1/2-approximation for (2) (invoked on p. 9).

### 5.3 TARA-LLM pipeline (Sec. IV, pp. 5–8)
- **Planning module** f^plan: (P_bd, P_sr, P¹_rt, P²_rt, P_or) → M0 (p. 6), with four prompt generators: R_bd: (Ωgoal, Ωplay) → P_bd (background: environment + objectives); R_sr: ({x_Pi}, {x_Ej}, {v_Pi}, {v_Ej}, {r_i}) → P_sr (JSON state); R_rt: (Kr, Kw) → (P¹_rt, P²_rt) (two reasoning chains: Kr first elicits candidate one-to-one and two-to-one subgames, then Kw checks whether Ē(c,j) intersects Ωgoal per subgame); R_or: Kc → P_or (output constraints + JSON schema). No optimality claim is made for M0 — it is an LLM guess; its empirical quality is the subject of Sec. V.
- **Revision module** f^rev: M0 → M (p. 6–7), three corrections:
  - **Algorithm 2 (Cu)**: delete e_cj ∈ M0 whenever Ē(c,j) ∩ Ωgoal ≠ ∅ (unreasonable pairs, contradicts Kw) → M0^u.
  - **Algorithm 3 (Cr)**: for |c| = 2, if some single i ∈ c already has Ē(i,j) ∩ Ωgoal = ∅, replace e_cj by e_ij (remove the redundant pursuer, Fig. 7(c)); output M0^r with |M0^r| = |M0^u| and no unreasonable/redundant pairs.
  - **Algorithm 4 (Cp)**: greedy conflict resolution with bookkeeping vectors m ∈ 0_{Np}, n ∈ 0_{Ne}: accept e_cj only if m[i] = 0 ∀i ∈ c and n[j] = 0; otherwise delete e_cj → M1, feasible and Kc-aligned.
  - Then the SMA is run on the players not yet in M1, giving M2; output M = M1 ∪ M2, M1 ∩ M2 = ∅.
- **Theorem 1 (Feasibility) (p. 7)**: The matching M computed by f^rev from M0 is a feasible solution to (2) and is aligned with Kw, Kr, Kc. *Proof (3 steps, as printed)*: (i) M1 is constructed feasible for (2) by Algorithms 2–4 (every kept pair satisfies Kw since Ē(c,j) ∩ Ωgoal = ∅ was re-checked; sizes ≤ 2 by Kr; Cp enforces Kc); (ii) M2 is feasible by the SMA's guarantee; (iii) M1 ∩ M2 = ∅ by construction (SMA runs only on leftover players), so the union M is feasible and knowledge-aligned. ∎ *Assessment*: the proof is a 3-line sketch; it inherits correctness of the analytic test Ē(c,j) ∩ Ωgoal = ∅ (via convex program (5)) and of the SMA; the only potentially fragile step is that M1's pairs were originally *proposed* by the LLM and are only verified (not re-derived) — verification suffices for feasibility, so the argument is sound but trivial-structural.
- **Implementation module** f^ime: M → {u_Pi} (p. 8, Algorithm 5): strategy base = {ES-based strategy u*_Pi (Lemma 1), pure pursuit u^p_Pi = (x_Ej − x_Pi)/‖x_Ej − x_Pi‖₂}. Rules: (1) if P_i belongs to a coalition in M, play u*_Pi against its assigned evader (winning guaranteed by Kw); (2) otherwise pure-pursue the closest evader E_j not in M; if at any time Ē(i,j) ∩ Ωgoal = ∅ becomes true (possible under suboptimal evader play), switch to u*_Pi. Rationale (p. 8): LLMs "generate patterns and may mix incompatible models", so no control law is ever synthesized by the LLM — the LLM only selects among hand-verified analytic strategies.
- **Theorem 2 (Number of evaders captured) (p. 8)**: The number Nm ∈ Z+ of evaders captured by TARA-LLM satisfies **|M| ≤ Nm ≤ Ne**. *Proof (as printed)*: lower bound — for each e_cj ∈ M, P_c guarantees the capture by the ES-based strategy u*_Pi (via Lemma 1/Lemma 2); pursuers outside M "seldom" guarantee winning but may still capture if evaders behave suboptimally, so Nm ≥ |M|; upper bound — Nm ≤ Ne trivially; also |M| ≤ |M*| ≤ Ne where M* solves (2). ∎ *Assessment*: (a) the notation Z+ is defined on p. 2 as "the set of nonpositive integers" — a printed typo for nonnegative integers; (b) **proof gap (mild)**: the step "P_c guarantees the capture" silently upgrades Definition 1's alternative (2) — "delay forever unless captured" — into finite-time capture; finite-time capture for α_ij > 1 under u* is implicit in [40] but is not re-established here; (c) no approximation-ratio theorem is proven for TARA-LLM: on p. 9 the authors only "infer" that |M| ≥ |M*|/2 "since the SMA is a 1/2-approximation algorithm" — this inference is not proven, because the SMA's 1/2 guarantee applies to the full instance, not to the post-deletion residual instance (removing M1's players can only reduce the achievable matching on the residual).

### 5.4 Summary of analytic content
The paper's mathematical novelty is thin by design: Lemmas 1–3 and the BIP are imported from [40], [41], [43]; the new formal results (Theorems 1–2) are feasibility and a capture-count guarantee for the LLM-in-the-loop pipeline. The real contribution is architectural: the LLM is confined to (i) proposing coalition–evader pairs via text-level arithmetic (Kw) and (ii) never touching dynamics — all correctness flows from the analytic verification layer (convex program (5) test, Algorithms 2–4, SMA).

## 6. Simulation and Experiments

### 6.1 Evaluation metrics (Sec. V-A, p. 8–9)
Five metrics on the generated matching: (1) **CER** (captured evader rate): cardinality of the matching after deleting unreasonable pairs and physical conflicts, over Ne — the least proportion of evaders captured; (2) **TC** (time cost) to generate a matching; (3) **UR** (unreasonable rate): winning-not-guaranteed pairs over matching cardinality; (4) **PCR** (physical conflict rate): pursuers incorrectly assigned to ≥2 evaders over Np; (5) **PUR** (pursuer utilization rate): number of evaders over number of pursuers in the matching.

### 6.2 Matching performance (Sec. V-B, p. 9, Fig. 8, Tables I–II)
- **Setup (as printed)**: methods = IO, FS [4], CoT [36], f^plan alone (PM), sequential f^plan + f^rev (PM+RM); benchmark = SMA [40]. LLM: **GPT-4** via OpenAI API, temperature = 0.0, top_p = 0.1, max_tokens = 2048, others default; TC = inference time from the API response **excluding API call latency**. For each Np = Ne ∈ {5, 10, …, 45, 50}: **1000 simulation rounds**; per round: initial positions drawn in a 40 × 40 area; v_Pi ∈ [2.1, 3.0], v_Ej ∈ [1.1, 2.0] (α_ij > 1); r_i ∈ [1.1, 3.0]; Ωgoal = disk of radius 5 centered at O = [5, 5]ᵀ.
- **Fig. 8 findings** (validates contributions 1–2 and the efficiency claim): proposed prompts beat IO/FS/CoT; CER gap between f^plan and SMA ≈ 0.2; f^rev lifts CER close to SMA's; UR and PCR of f^plan fluctuate around 0.2 (only a small fraction needs correction); PUR ≈ 1 for SMA (nearly only one-pursuer coalitions) vs PUR < 1 for TARA-LLM (nonnegligible two-pursuer coalitions); TC of SMA grows sharply with player count while TARA-LLM's growth rate is lower, so TARA-LLM eventually wins in time efficiency (though slower at small Np). The authors infer |M| ≥ |M*|/2 from the SMA's 1/2-approximation (see caveat in §5.3).
- **Prompt robustness**: 10 semantically equivalent but lexically/grammatically different prompt variants; 200 rounds each at Np = Ne = 35; CER standard deviation **0.08**; removing knowledge (IO/FS/CoT) costs > 0.3 CER for large player counts — validates the necessity of Kw/Kr/Kc.

### 6.3 Game simulations (Sec. V-C, p. 10, Fig. 9(a)(b))
- **Scenario (as printed)**: Np = Ne = 5; x0_P1 = [21.5, −8.4]ᵀ, x0_P2 = [24.6, 0.5]ᵀ, x0_P3 = [15.8, 17.1]ᵀ, x0_P4 = [−5.7, 14.8]ᵀ, x0_P5 = [−7.5, 5.4]ᵀ; x0_E1 = [−3.3, −8.6]ᵀ, x0_E2 = [5.2, −2.7]ᵀ, x0_E3 = [17.4, −2.6]ᵀ, x0_E4 = [9.2, 12.4]ᵀ, x0_E5 = [25.4, 9.4]ᵀ; v_P1 = v_P3 = v_P4 = v_P5 = 2.1, v_P2 = 2.0; r1 = 1.5, r2 = 2.3, r3 = 1.4, r4 = 1.1, r5 = 2.2; v_E1 = v_E3 = 1.5, v_E2 = 1.6, v_E4 = 1.9, v_E5 = 1.7. Evaders in M play u*_Ej; others steer straight to the goal center O.
- **Result**: TARA-LLM assigns P3→E5, P5→E1, {P1,P2}→E3 (all captured); SMA assigns P1→E5, P5→E1, leaving E3 unguarded — E3 escapes. Mechanism: f^plan evaluates one- and two-pursuer coalitions *simultaneously* (two chains), whereas SMA's greedy one-pursuer phase breaks up the winning coalition {P1,P2} first. This is the paper's concrete demonstration that LLM reasoning can outperform the SMA in capture count (validates the PUR observation from Fig. 8).

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
- **Author-stated (p. 11, Conclusion)**: LLM response latency blocks high-frequency decision-making (matching computed once at game start, p. 11); future work: distributed/onboard LLM-based assignment, richer dynamics (Dubins-car [41], damped double integrator [21]), multiphase reach-avoid games [11], obstacles [42], nonconvex goal regions, high-frequency decision scenarios.
- **Approximation claim not proven**: the |M| ≥ |M*|/2 statement (p. 9) is explicitly "inferred" from the SMA's 1/2-approximation; no theorem covers TARA-LLM's optimality gap, and the greedy SMA-on-residual argument does not by itself yield a global ratio (see §5.3).
- **Theorem 2's lower bound relies on finite-time capture**, which Definition 1 does not directly assert (its second alternative is only "delay forever unless captured"); the finite-time capture property is delegated to [40] and not re-proven (proof gap, p. 8).
- **Open-loop matching under state feedback information**: although the information structure is state feedback (p. 3), the assignment is computed once; capture guarantees hold because the ES-based strategy is feedback, but no re-planning mechanism is given if the LLM's initial matching is suboptimal for the realized play.
- **TC metric excludes API call latency** (p. 9), so reported time costs understate deployment latency; still, TARA-LLM beats SMA's TC only for large player counts (crossover visible in Fig. 8).
- **Evader model in evaluation**: simulations fix evaders in M to u*_Ej and others to straight-to-goal (pp. 10–11); CER numbers are conditional on this evader population, not worst-case adversarial evader teams.
- **Editorial defects (verified against rendered pages)**: p. 2 "Z+ represents the set of nonpositive integers" (should be nonnegative); p. 4 garbled NP-hardness sentence ("analytical algorithms face the curse of dimensionality is a special case of integer programming…"); p. 5 "assigned with at to one pursuit coalition" (Def. 5); p. 5 note cites [40] where Lemma 3's convex-region provenance is meant to be [41]; p. 9 "CEP gap" for CER.
- **Conceptual**: the LLM's value over the SMA is only demonstrated in specific scenarios (two-pursuer-coalition preservation, Sec. ## 8. Citation Value
Role in the literature: this is the first bridge between LLM-based decision-making and analytical multiplayer reach-avoid game theory, and a representative instance of the "knowledge-driven LLM + analytic verification" pattern for guaranteed multirobot task assignment; it connects the Yan et al. matching-based M-RA line ([40] Automatica 2022, [41] Automatica 2024, [43] TRO 2020) with the prompt-engineering literature (CoT, CoT-SC, ToT) and provides a rare hardware validation (6-AAV motion-capture platform). Cite it when: (i) surveying LLM/learning-based methods for differential games or multi-robot task assignment; (ii) motivating hybrid neuro-symbolic pipelines where the LLM proposes and analytic certificates (Apollonius/evasion-space winning conditions, convex-program subgame tests) dispose; (iii) discussing NP-hard coalition matching and approximation algorithms (SMA as 1/2-approximation baseline); or (iv) illustrating LLM hallucination mitigation via model-based revision layers. Example citation sentence: "TARA-LLM [P059] embeds analytical winning conditions of M-RA games into structured LLM prompts and restores feasibility through analytic revision, provably producing a feasible solution of the constrained matching problem with at least |M| guaranteed captures, and empirically outperforming the greedy sequential matching baseline in capture count and large-scale time efficiency."
