## 2. Research Background
Cooperative multi-agent interception in adversarial environments (national security, asset protection, surveillance) is classically modeled as a pursuit-evasion differential game, where differential game theory supplies state-feedback optimal strategies; the authors survey three streams: (i) game-theoretic optimal-strategy analyses (largely Target-Attacker-Defender and barrier constructions from Garcia–Casbeer–Pachter and the first author's own earlier TAD work), (ii) geometric/control-theoretic containment methods (encirclement via robust MPC, cooperative blocking, barrier construction), and (iii) approaches adding real-world complexities (obstacles, partial/asymmetric information, sequential intruder arrivals). Within stream (ii), the direct predecessor is the area-based control framework of Shah and Schwager (2019), in which pursuers cooperatively contain and capture an evader by shrinking the area of the evader's reachable set — but only for pursuers all moving at the evader's speed, where the safe set is an intersection of half-planes. This paper generalizes that framework to pursuers with heterogeneous, unequal speeds $V_i > V_e$, where the safe set becomes an intersection of Apollonius discs whose intersection area has no usable closed form for $N>2$.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
**Problem**: $N$ faster pursuers with distinct maximum speeds $V_i$ and one slower evader $V_e$, simple kinematic point masses with heading control in $\mathbb{R}^2$, deterministic and full information ([authors' statement, p. 2: "Our work focuses on the fundamental deterministic, full-information game"]). The pursuer team cooperatively minimizes the area $A_e(t)$ of the evader's safe-reachable set $S_e(t)$ (intersection of the $N$ Apollonius discs); the evader maximizes it. The paper derives closed-form instantaneous Nash heading controls for this zero-sum game on $\dot A_e(t)$.

**Gap as claimed by the authors** (p. 2, Related Work): (i) [authors' claim] the area-based framework of Shah and Schwager (2019) "was limited to the case where all pursuers have the same speed as the evader, resulting in a safe set defined by the intersection of half-planes"; (ii) [authors' claim] with heterogeneous speeds the safe set is an intersection of Apollonius discs, and "deriving an analytical formula for the intersection area of an arbitrary number of discs is an extremely sophisticated geometric problem … Consequently, differentiating such functions to find the gradients becomes essentially intractable for $N > 2$" (p. 4); (iii) hence a gap: area-optimal controls for the heterogeneous-speed case without closed-form area expressions.

**Verified / cross-checked**: the equal-speed degeneration claim is mathematically consistent: as $\alpha_i = V_e/V_i \to 1$, the Apollonius disc center $c_i \to \infty$ and radius $r_i \to \infty$ while the disc converges to the half-plane bounded by the perpendicular bisector of segment $[p_i, e]$, exactly Shah–Schwager's geometry. The intractability claim is standard (intersection area of $N$ discs requires arrangement-level case analysis, exponential in $N$ in the worst case). The paper's stated novelty — "the generalization of the area-based control framework to the challenging case of pursuers with heterogeneous speeds" with "a complete problem formulation and a proof for the area-optimal control strategy" (p. 1, boxed) — is credible against the cited literature list: no cited reference treats area-minimization over Apollonius-disc intersections (the cited Von Moll et al. 2019 treats the multi-pursuer single-evader game geometrically but with the dominant-region/minimum-time viewpoint, explicitly contrasted in footnote 1, p. 2). Note the claim is about the *instantaneous* game (greedy, myopic); the abstract's stronger reading ("area-optimal", "guaranteed capture") is simulation-supported only (see Sections 6–7).

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Modeling (zero-sum game on the area rate)**: formulation of multi-pursuer cooperative capture as the instantaneous min–max game $\min_{\{u_i\}} \max_v \dot A_e(t)$ (eq. (8), p. 3) over the area of the evader's safe-reachable set $S_e(t) = \bigcap_{i=1}^N B_i(t)$ of Apollonius discs (eqs. (3), (11)), with the value $\dot A_e = V_e\|F_e\| - \sum_i V_i\|F_{p_i}\|$ (eq. (10)) — a "direct competition between the weighted magnitudes of the area gradients" (p. 3).
2. **Theory (main contribution)**: analytical derivation of the area gradients $\nabla_{p_i} A_e$ and $\nabla_e A_e$ for the intersection of $N$ Apollonius discs **without any closed-form area formula**, by a coordinate-independent Leibniz integral rule on level-set functions (pp. 4–6): level-set gradients (Proposition 1.3, eqs. (19)–(22)), arc-restricted integral forms (eqs. (23)–(25)), and the geometric centroid form (Theorem 1.4, eqs. (26)–(29)).
3. **Strategy interpretation (theory)**: geometric characterization of the Nash equilibrium headings — each active pursuer moves directly toward the centroid $C_{arc,i}$ of the boundary arc it contributes; the evader moves along a positively weighted sum of vectors from its position to all active arc centroids (Theorem 1.4 + Lemma 1.1).
4. **Algorithm**: a complete implementable pipeline — active-set filtering $I(t)$ (containment test), arc angular-interval computation (Lemma 1.2, eqs. (12)–(14)), closed-form arc centroids (eq. (29)) — giving $O(N^2)$-per-step, decentralized computation, advertised as real-time capable.
5. **Experiment (illustrative only)**: one $N=5$ scenario with speeds $V_e=4$, $V=[6,6,12,10,9]$ demonstrating systematic shrinking of $S_e$ to capture (Figs. 1–2, pp. 5, 7); no quantitative metrics, baselines, or parameter table.

### 5.0 Dynamics, assumption, and geometric state (p. 2, eqs. (1)–(4))
- **Dynamics (eq. (1a–b), p. 2)**: $\dot p_i(t) = V_i u_i(t)$, $\|u_i(t)\| \le 1$; $\dot e(t) = V_e v(t)$, $\|v(t)\| \le 1$ — simple kinematic point masses, control = instantaneous heading (unit-norm). Constant maximum speeds $V_i$, $V_e$.
- **Key assumption**: $V_i > V_e$ for all $i \in \{1,\dots,N\}$ ("a critical assumption for capture to be feasible", p. 2). Speed ratio $\alpha_i := V_e/V_i \in (0,1)$.
- **Information structure**: deterministic, full information; the controls below are state-feedback maps of all agent positions (the arcs/centroids require global geometry).
- **Apollonius disc (eq. (2a–b), p. 2)**: $c_i(t) = \frac{V_i^2}{V_i^2 - V_e^2} e(t) + \frac{V_e^2}{V_e^2 - V_i^2} p_i(t)$, $\; r_i(t) = \frac{V_i V_e}{|V_i^2 - V_e^2|} \|e(t) - p_i(t)\|$ — the locus of points the evader reaches no later than pursuer $i$; interior = points reached strictly before. Equivalent forms printed in Theorem 1.4's preamble (p. 6): $c_i = \frac{e - \alpha_i^2 p_i}{1 - \alpha_i^2}$, $r_i = \frac{\alpha_i}{1-\alpha_i^2}\|e - p_i\|$. *Verification (mine)*: expanding $\|q-e\|^2 = \alpha_i^2\|q-p_i\|^2$ reproduces both; the absolute value in (2b) is redundant under $V_i > V_e$. The evader is strictly inside every disc: $\|e - c_i\| = \alpha_i r_i < r_i$.
- **Safe-reachable set (eq. (3), p. 2) and objective (eq. (4))**: $S_e(t) = \bigcap_{i=1}^N \{q \in \mathbb{R}^2 : \|q - c_i(t)\| \le r_i(t)\}$; $A_e(t) = \mathrm{Area}(S_e(t))$. $S_e$ is compact and convex (intersection of compact convex discs, p. 4). **Termination condition**: "If this set shrinks to a single point or becomes empty, capture is imminent" (p. 2); "As $A_e(t) \to 0$ … capture becomes inevitable" (p. 3). No explicit capture radius or finite-time bound is given.
- Footnote 1 (p. 2): the safe-reachable-set objective "defines the optimal pursuit strategy, as opposed to minimum capture time (Von Moll et al. 2019)" — area-optimality is explicitly *not* time-optimality.

### 5.1 Lemma 1.1 (p. 3, eqs. (5)–(10)): Nash headings of the instantaneous area game
- **Statement**: given the area gradients $F_{p_i}(t) := \nabla_{p_i} A_e(t)$ (eq. (5a)) and $F_e(t) := \nabla_e A_e(t)$ (eq. (5b)), the optimal headings solving the min–max game $\min_{\{u_i\}} \max_v \dot A_e(t)$ (eq. (8)) are
$$u_i^\star(t) = -\frac{F_{p_i}(t)}{\|F_{p_i}(t)\|}, \ i = 1,\dots,N \quad (9\mathrm{a}), \qquad v^\star(t) = \frac{F_e(t)}{\|F_e(t)\|} \quad (9\mathrm{b}).$$
- **Derivation chain**: chain rule $\dot A_e = \sum_i F_{p_i}^\top \dot p_i + F_e^\top \dot e$ (eq. (6)); substituting (1): $\dot A_e = \sum_i V_i F_{p_i}^\top u_i + V_e F_e^\top v$ (eq. (7)).
- **Proof approach**: separability — each control appears only in its own additive term of (7), so the min and max decouple; each scalar inner product $F^\top u$ with $\|u\| \le 1$ is minimized/maximized by anti-alignment/alignment with $F$ (Cauchy–Schwarz equality condition). Because of separability, $\min\max = \max\min$ here: (9) is a saddle point (two-team zero-sum: pursuer team = single minimizer) of the *instantaneous* game — the paper is explicit that this is an "instantaneous, greedy approach" (p. 3), not a solution of the dynamic differential game.
- **Decisive consequence (eq. (10), p. 3)**: substituting (9) into (7),
$$\dot A_e(t)\big|_{\star} = V_e \|F_e(t)\| - \sum_{i=1}^N V_i \|F_{p_i}(t)\|,$$
  i.e., under equilibrium play the area shrinks iff $\sum_i V_i \|F_{p_i}\| > V_e\|F_e\|$ — a competition of speed-weighted gradient magnitudes ("aggregate 'force' on the area's boundary", p. 3). Physical meaning: the evader's escape power is $V_e \|F_e\|$ (its ability to push its boundary outward in the most effective direction); each pursuer contributes $V_i\|F_{p_i}\|$ of shrinking power. Local/global: global saddle point of the one-shot game on $\dot A_e$; no claim (or proof) that $\dot A_e < 0$ holds along actual play or that capture follows — that is left to simulation.
- **Extensibility**: any controllable speed profile plugs into (7); the lemma is blind to the geometry (all geometric content lives in $F_{p_i}, F_e$).

### 5.2 Active-set reduction and Lemma 1.2 (p. 4, eqs. (11)–(14)): boundary arc computation
- **Setup (eq. (11), p. 4)**: $S_e(t) = \bigcap_{i=1}^N B_i(t)$, $B_i(t) = \{q : \|q - c_i(t)\| \le r_i(t)\}$.
- **Active index set (p. 4, unnumbered)**: $I(t) = \{ i \in \{1,\dots,N\} : \|c_i(t) - c_j(t)\| \ge r_i(t) - r_j(t) \ \forall j \ne i \}$ — the prose (p. 4) states the equivalent test $\|c_i - c_j\| + r_j \ge r_i$ "ensures $B_i$ does not fully contain any other disc $B_j$"; a containing disc's boundary cannot appear on $\partial S_e$ and is discarded. (Note the printed prose says the containing disc's "boundary lies entirely outside $B_j$", which is only true up to the tangency case — harmless.)
- **Lemma 1.2 (Boundary Arc Computation, p. 4)**: for each active circle $i \in I(t)$, the angular set $\Theta_i(t)$ of the arc of $\partial B_i$ that forms part of $\partial S_e(t)$ is a **single compact interval** $[\theta_i^{\min}, \theta_i^{\max}]$ or empty, computed by: (1) for each other active $j \ne i$, the pairwise constraint interval $\Gamma_{ij}(t) = [\varphi_{ij} - \alpha_{ij}, \varphi_{ij} + \alpha_{ij}] \pmod{2\pi}$, where $\varphi_{ij}$ is the polar angle of $c_j - c_i$ (eq. (12)) and
$$\alpha_{ij} = \arccos\!\left( \frac{\|c_j - c_i\|^2 + r_i^2 - r_j^2}{2 r_i \|c_j - c_i\|} \right) \quad (13)$$
  — the law-of-cosines half-angle subtended at $c_i$ by the chord in which disc $j$ cuts circle $i$; (2) $\Theta_i(t) = \bigcap_{j \in I(t), j \ne i} \Gamma_{ij}(t)$ (eq. (14)).
- **Proof**: "contained in the Technical Appendix" (p. 5) — **not part of the published paper** (8 pages include no appendix). *Verification (mine)*: (13) is the standard chord half-angle and is correct pairwise; the single-interval claim is plausible because each $\Gamma_{ij}$ has width $2\alpha_{ij} \le \pi$ and intersections of arcs of angular length $\le \pi$ on $S^1$ are again arcs (a Helly-type fact), but the wrap-around handling "(mod 2π)" and the empty/nonempty determination are not proven in the paper — **proof gap: the connectivity claim of Lemma 1.2 and the pairwise-intersection procedure at the $2\pi$ seam are unproven in the archived document**. A second subtlety: (14) intersects only over containment-active circles; the text then silently redefines $I(t)$ (p. 5) to those circles with $\Theta_i \ne \emptyset$ — operational but slightly circular.
- **Physical meaning**: this lemma is the discretization-free description of the "active Apollonius disc" geometry of Fig. 1 (p. 5): only 3 of 5 discs carry a boundary arc in the example.

### 5.3 Leibniz integral rule machinery (p. 5, eqs. (15)–(18))
- **Level-set form (eqs. (15)–(16), p. 5)**: $B_i(t) = \{q : F_i(q,t) \le 0\}$ with $F_i(q,t) = \|q - c_i(t)\|^2 - r_i(t)^2$; $\partial B_i$ is the zero level set.
- **Leibniz/Reynolds rule (eq. (17), p. 5)**: $\frac{\partial A_e(t)}{\partial \zeta} = \int_{\partial S_e(t)} V_n(q,\zeta)\, ds$ for any parameter $\zeta$ (a component of any agent position), with outward normal velocity on the arc of circle $i$
$$V_n(q,\zeta) = -\frac{\partial F_i / \partial \zeta}{\|\nabla_q F_i\|} \quad (18),$$
  attributed to Shah and Schwager (2019). This normalization-invariant formula is what removes the need for an area formula: only boundary data enter.

### 5.4 Proposition 1.3 (p. 5, eqs. (19)–(22)): level-set gradients
- **Statement**: $\nabla_q F_i = 2(q - c_i)$ (19); $\nabla_{p_i} F_i = \frac{2\alpha_i^2}{1-\alpha_i^2}(q - p_i)$ (20); $\nabla_e F_i = \frac{2}{1-\alpha_i^2}(e - q)$ (21); $\nabla_{p_k} F_i = 0$ for $k \ne i$ (22).
- **Proof**: "contained in the Appendix" (p. 5) — again absent from the PDF. *Independent verification (mine, full algebra)*: differentiating $c_i = (e-\alpha_i^2 p_i)/(1-\alpha_i^2)$ and $r_i = \alpha_i\|e-p_i\|/(1-\alpha_i^2)$: with $D = 1-\alpha_i^2$, $\partial F_i/\partial p_i = \frac{2\alpha_i^2}{D^2}[D q + (\alpha_i^2 - 1)p_i] = \frac{2\alpha_i^2}{D}(q - p_i)$ ✓, and $\partial F_i/\partial e = -\frac{2}{D^2}[Dq - De] = \frac{2}{D}(e - q)$ ✓. All four printed gradients are **correct**; the sparsity (22) is what later makes each pursuer's gradient depend only on its own arc.

### 5.5 Integral area gradients (p. 6, eqs. (23)–(25))
- **General form (eq. (23), pp. 5–6)**: $\nabla_z A_e(t) = \sum_{i \in I(t)} \int_{\theta_i^{\min}}^{\theta_i^{\max}} -\frac{\nabla_z F_i(q(\theta),t)}{\|\nabla_q F_i(q(\theta),t)\|} r_i\, d\theta$; on the arc $\|\nabla_q F_i\| = 2 r_i$.
- **Pursuer (eq. (24), p. 6)**: only the $i = k$ term survives by (22):
$$\nabla_{p_k} A_e(t) = -\frac{\alpha_k^2}{1-\alpha_k^2} \int_{\theta_k^{\min}}^{\theta_k^{\max}} \big(q(\theta) - p_k\big)\, d\theta.$$
- **Evader (eq. (25), p. 6)**: all active arcs contribute:
$$\nabla_e A_e(t) = -\sum_{i \in I(t)} \frac{1}{1-\alpha_i^2} \int_{\theta_i^{\min}}^{\theta_i^{\max}} \big(e - q(\theta)\big)\, d\theta.$$
- *Verification (mine)*: both follow from (17)–(18) + Prop. 1.3; signs are consistent (moving a pursuer toward its arc decreases the area; moving the evader outward — away from its interior position — increases it).

### 5.6 Theorem 1.4 (pp. 6–7, eqs. (26)–(29)): geometric centroid form — the paper's central result
- **Statement**: with the arc centroid (eq. (26), p. 6) $C_{arc,i}(t) \triangleq \frac{1}{L_i(t)} \int_{arc_i} q(s)\, ds$ ($L_i$ = arc length), the area gradients are:
$$\nabla_{p_i} A_e(t) = -(1 - \alpha_i^2)\, r_i(t) L_i(t)\, \big(C_{arc,i}(t) - p_i(t)\big) \quad (27)$$
$$\nabla_e A_e(t) = \sum_{i \in I(t)} \frac{1-\alpha_i^2}{\alpha_i^2}\, r_i(t) L_i(t)\, \big(C_{arc,i}(t) - e(t)\big) \quad (28)$$
  (both exactly as printed; fractions verified against the 400-dpi rendering of p. 6). Strategy construction: since $u_i^\star$ is anti-parallel to $\nabla_{p_i}A_e$ (Lemma 1.1), **each active pursuer moves directly toward the centroid of its own boundary arc**; since $v^\star$ is parallel to $\nabla_e A_e$, **the evader moves along a positively weighted sum of vectors from its position to all active arc centroids** — heterogeneous speeds enter only through $\alpha_i$.
- **Explicit centroid (eq. (29), p. 6)**: $C_{arc,i}(t) = c_i(t) + r_i(t)\big(\ell_i(t)/L_i(t)\big) \mathbf{u}_i(t)$, with $L_i = r_i \Delta\theta_i$, $\Delta\theta_i = \theta_i^{\max} - \theta_i^{\min}$, chord $\ell_i = 2 r_i \sin(\Delta\theta_i/2)$, and $\mathbf{u}_i = (\cos m_i, \sin m_i)^\top$ the bisector direction, $m_i = (\theta_i^{\max} + \theta_i^{\min})/2$. *Verification (mine)*: the standard arc-centroid distance $r \sin(\Delta\theta/2)/(\Delta\theta/2) = 2r\sin(\Delta\theta/2)/\Delta\theta = r\,\ell/L$ along the bisector — eq. (29) is **correct**.
- **Proof approach (p. 7)**: compare (24)/(25) with the centroid integral: $V_{target,i} = C_{arc,i} - p_i = \frac{1}{\Delta\theta_i}\int (q(\theta) - p_i)\,d\theta$, giving the intermediate proportionalities
$$\nabla_{p_i} A_e(t) = -\frac{\alpha_i^2 \Delta\theta_i}{1-\alpha_i^2} V_{target,i}(t), \qquad \nabla_e A_e(t) = \sum_{i \in I(t)} \frac{\Delta\theta_i}{1-\alpha_i^2} V_{target,e,i}(t),$$
  then "the precise formulation … found in (27) and (28) is obtained with $\Delta\theta_i = L_i/r_i$ and $r_i = \frac{\alpha_i}{1-\alpha_i^2}\|e - p_i\|$" (p. 7). The positive-scalar argument ($\alpha_i \in (0,1)$, $\Delta\theta_i > 0$) establishes the *directions* of the optimal headings.
- **Proof gap (scalar inconsistency in the printed theorem)**: the substitution actually yields $\nabla_{p_i}A_e = -\frac{\alpha_i^2 L_i}{(1-\alpha_i^2) r_i}(C_{arc,i} - p_i) = -\frac{\alpha_i}{\|e-p_i\|} L_i (C_{arc,i} - p_i)$ and $\nabla_e A_e = \sum_i \frac{L_i}{(1-\alpha_i^2) r_i}(C_{arc,i} - e) = \sum_i \frac{L_i}{\alpha_i \|e-p_i\|}(C_{arc,i} - e)$ — i.e., the printed prefactors of (27)/(28) equal the proof's prefactors times $\|e - p_i\|^2$ (per arc), and carry units of $m^2$ where an area gradient must have units of $m$. Consequences: (a) for pursuers the error is a positive per-pursuer scale factor, killed by the normalization in (9a) — the heading "toward the arc centroid" is unaffected; (b) for the **evader** the error is a *per-arc* weight distortion (different $\|e-p_i\|$ per pursuer), so implementing (28) as printed changes the *direction* of $\nabla_e A_e$, i.e., the evader's optimal heading, relative to the correct form implied by the paper's own eq. (25) and proof; (c) any quantitative use of gradient *magnitudes* — notably the equilibrium rate (10) and the capture condition $\sum_i V_i\|F_{p_i}\| > V_e\|F_e\|$ — is corrupted by the printed prefactors. The correct implementable form is the proof's version (or eqs. (24)–(25) directly). This is an erratum-level typo, not a flaw in the method's geometry, but it is the formula most likely to be copied by readers.
- **Local/global, analytic/numerical**: all quantities are analytic and closed-form per time instant (global geometry of the current configuration); the control is memoryless state feedback; the "optimality" is instantaneous (see 5.1).
- **Physical meaning / extensibility**: the safe set is a convex "shrink-wrap" around the evader made of arcs of the Apollonius circles of the faster pursuers; heterogeneous speeds only re-weight arcs. The Leibniz-rule derivation extends verbatim to 3D (surface-area control over Apollonius spheres) — the authors list 3D engagements as future work (p. 7).

### 5.7 Inactive agents and conventions
- If pursuer $i \notin I(t)$ or $\Theta_i = \emptyset$, its arc integral vanishes: $F_{p_i} = 0$, so (9a) is $0/0$; the paper's convention (Fig. 2 caption, p. 7): "Whenever a pursuer $i$ at time $t$ is inactive ($i \notin I(t)$), the pursuer is stationary". In Fig. 2, pursuers 1 and 5 never move.
- **Game value / termination**: no theorem states $\dot A_e < 0$ along play, monotone area decrease, or finite-time capture; capture is declared operationally when $S_e$ collapses (p. 2). The only analytic statement is (10).

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
**Author-stated** (p. 7, Conclusion; p. 2, Related Work): deterministic full-information game only; future work = environmental obstacles, 3D engagements, partial information/uncertainty; footnote 1 concedes the safe-set objective is not minimum-capture-time.

**Critical observations (with page/eq.)**:
1. **Erratum-level scalar inconsistency in the headline theorem**: printed prefactors of (27)/(28) (p. 6) disagree with the paper's own proof chain (24)–(25) and intermediate proportionalities (p. 7) by a factor $\|e - p_i\|^2$ per arc; dimensionally the printed gradients give $m^3$ instead of $m$. Pursuer headings survive (positive rescale + normalization (9a)); the evader heading direction does not, since (28)'s per-arc weights are distorted by heterogeneous $\|e-p_i\|$; equilibrium rate (10) is corrupted if fed the printed magnitudes. Readers must implement the proof form $-\frac{\alpha_i^2 \Delta\theta_i}{1-\alpha_i^2}V_{target,i}$ / $\frac{\Delta\theta_i}{1-\alpha_i^2}V_{target,e,i}$.
2. **Proofs live outside the paper**: Lemma 1.2, Proposition 1.3, and the centroid formula's derivation are "contained in the (Technical) Appendix" (pp. 5–7), which is not part of the 8-page PDF; the single-interval claim of Lemma 1.2 (intersection of mod-$2\pi$ arc intervals) and the boundary arc-algorithm's correctness at the seam are therefore unverifiable from the archived document.
3. **Instantaneous ≠ dynamic optimality**: the "Nash equilibrium" is the saddle point of the one-shot game on $\dot A_e$ (eq. (8), p. 3, "instantaneous, greedy approach"); no result ties it to the dynamic game's value function, to subgame-perfect play, or to feedback-stackelberg hierarchies; the abstract's "area-optimal" therefore means greedy-optimal rate, not globally optimal capture trajectory.
4. **No capture guarantee theorem**: $\dot A_e < 0$ (eq. (10)) is not proven to hold along equilibrium play (it requires $\sum_i V_i\|F_{p_i}\| > V_e\|F_e\|$ at every instant); "guaranteed capture" (p. 1) rests on one simulated run; behavior near $A_e \to 0$ (gradients vanish; set can become empty under discretization) is unanalyzed; no capture-time bound.
5. **"Decentralized" is generous**: each heading is computed per-agent, but $I(t)$, $\Theta_i$, and all centroids require every agent's position and speed — the information structure is centralized even if the computation is separable (cf. eqs. (12)–(14), (29)).
6. **Model scope**: all pursuers must be faster ($V_i > V_e$, p. 2); slower pursuers (complement-of-disc geometry) and speed-unequal evader teams are out of scope; agents are unconstrained point masses (no dynamics, turning-rate, or sensing limits); obstacles/uncertainty excluded by design.
7. **Experimental thinness**: one scenario, two figures, zero quantitative metrics or baselines (Section 6); the efficiency and robustness claims are unmeasured.
8. Minor: the active-set prose on p. 4 ("boundary lies entirely outside $B_j$") is loose under tangency; the double use of $I(t)$ (containment-active vs. arc-contributing, p. 5) is sloppy but harmless; eq. (2b)'s $|V_i^2 - V_e^2|$ is redundant under the standing assumption.

## 8. Citation Value
**Paragraph role**: this is the heterogeneous-speed extension of the area-based cooperative pursuit framework (Shah–Schwager 2019), published as an AAAI-26 main-track paper. In the knowledge base it serves as (i) the canonical reference for the **Apollonius-disc-intersection safe-reachable set** and its active-arc/centroid computation (Lemmas 1.2, Theorem 1.4), (ii) a clean worked example of deriving area gradients of implicitly defined sets via the **Leibniz integral rule / Reynolds transport theorem** without closed-form area formulas — a technique transferable to 3D safe-set (surface-area) control and to other level-set objectives, and (iii) an example of an **instantaneous zero-sum game formulation** whose Nash strategies have direct geometric meaning (pursue arc centroid / flee weighted centroids). Cite it whenever a paper needs area-minimization or spatial-containment objectives for cooperative pursuit with unequal pursuer speeds, or as the geometric baseline against which learning-based or minimum-time multi-pursuer strategies are compared; cite with caution for the evader-side formula (use the proof's coefficients, not the printed eq. (28)) and do not cite it as a capture-guarantee or capture-time result. Suggested citation sentence: "Mammadov and Ranasinghe (AAAI-26) derive closed-form Nash heading controls for heterogeneous-speed cooperative pursuit by minimizing the area of the evader's Apollonius-intersection safe-reachable set, proving via the Leibniz integral rule that each active pursuer should steer toward the centroid of its contributed boundary arc while the evader flees along a weighted sum of centroid directions." A contrasting sentence: "Unlike minimum-time formulations of the multi-pursuer single-evader game (Von Moll et al. 2019), the area-based objective yields decentralized, real-time-computable strategies at the price of only greedy (instantaneous) optimality."
