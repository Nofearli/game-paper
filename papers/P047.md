## 2. Research Background
Reach-avoid differential games (DGs), in which an attacking team tries to send attackers into a target set while a defending team captures them, have been studied extensively, but almost all analytic results (Chen–Zhou–Tomlin pairwise outcomes [6]; Yan et al.'s barrier computations for multiplayer games [9]–[11], [13]; Garcia et al.'s border-defense games [14]–[17]; perimeter-defense games [18]–[20]) assume **simple motion** — first-order dynamics with bounded speed and instantaneous heading changes (Isaacs [12]). Real wheeled robots cannot change heading instantaneously; they are closer to car (Dubins-type) models with bounded turn rate, for which analytic DG solutions are far harder (the classical counterpart being the Homicidal Chauffeur game [12]). This paper takes a pragmatic two-layer approach for the one-defender–one-attacker reach-avoid game with a **car-model defender** and a **simple-motion attacker**: compute a heading reference as if the defender were simple motion (using an existing evasion-space-based defense strategy), then design a finite-time tracking controller so that the physical car-like defender actually follows that reference despite the adversary. The work is accompanied by real-robot experiments with a vision-based re-localization pipeline solving the cross-robot coordinate-inconsistency problem.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
**Problem (Section II, pp. 1656–1657)**: Defender D with car kinematics (1) (below) and attacker A with simple motion (2) play on the plane split by a straight target line T into a goal region Ω_goal ⊂ R² and a play region Ω_play ⊂ R². The attacker wins by entering Ω_goal from Ω_play without capture; the defender wins by capturing the attacker in Ω_play. Capture: ‖x_A − x_D‖₂ ≤ r. Setting: r > 0 (non-zero capture radius) and α = v_D/v_A > 1 (faster defender). WLOG (for convenience) T is vertical to the y-axis with Ω_goal in the lower half-plane.

**Gap — [authors' claim]** (p. 1656): "Different from most of the current works on reach-avoid DGs [6], [9]–[11], the defender and the attacker here are modeled as a car model and simple-motion model, respectively"; the surveyed results "mainly focus on simple motion." **[Verified from cited text titles]**: the cited reach-avoid line [6], [9]–[11], [13]–[17] indeed consists of simple-motion analyses (their titles/abstracts as cited mention no bounded-turn-rate defenders), so the gap statement is accurate at the level of the survey. **[Authors' claim, not re-proven here]** (p. 1657): the ES-based strategy (4)–(5) "can guarantee that attacker's evasion space will not approach the goal region for the case of point capture" is attributed to [13], and the authors assert "the similar guarantee still holds under the defense strategy (4) for the case of non-zero capture radius considered here, **because the evasion space in this paper is a subset of that in the case of point capture**" — a one-line subset argument whose formal proof is deferred to [2], [9], [13]. This is the weakest link of the guarantee chain (see §7). What the paper itself proves is the **control-theoretic half**: that a bounded-turn defender can lock onto the ES heading reference in finite time against *any* attacker behavior.

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Modeling/approach (theory)**: A decomposition method for reach-avoid games with a car-model defender: (i) heading-reference design by pretending the defender has simple motion and applying the ES-based feedback strategy (4)–(5) inherited from [13]; (ii) a finite-time controller that forces the car defender's heading onto the reference. Lemma 1 gives the heading reference in closed form (6).
2. **Theory (tracking-time analysis)**: A "gradient-based method" that yields (a) the exact gradient of the heading reference w.r.t. the relative position (Lemma 2, eq. (12)), (b) the pointwise worst-case bound |θ̇_D^r(x_{D,A})| ≤ φ(x_{D,A}) over all attacker/defender controls (Lemma 3, eq. (13)), (c) a global uniform bound ϑ = α(v_A+v_D)/(r(α−1)) (Theorem 1, eq. (14)), and (d) a finite tracking time T (Theorem 2, eq. (15)) under the condition u_MD > ϑ — all under worst-case (non-anticipative) attacker behavior.
3. **Algorithm (control)**: Bang-bang steering law u_D = u_MD sgn(θ_D^r − θ_D) (9), i.e., always turning at the maximum rate toward the reference; Lyapunov proof of finite-time error extinction with V(t) = ½(θ_D − θ_D^r)² (16).
4. **Experiment (system)**: A complete robotic verification framework for reach-avoid DGs (Figs. 4–6): two modified TurtleBot2 differential-drive robots, RTAB-Map SLAM + ArUco-marker visual re-localization to put both players in one global map frame (solving the "initial pose problem"), ROS-based information sharing, and three game scenarios (static / optimal-of-[9] / random attacker) in which the defender always captures the attacker and experimental trajectories match simulations.

## 5. Key Mathematical Results

### 5.1 Dynamics, information structure, termination
- **Defender (car model) (1, p. 1656)**: ẋ_D = v_D cos θ_D, ẏ_D = v_D sin θ_D, θ̇_D = u_D, with initial conditions x_D(0) = x_{D0}, θ_D(0) = θ_{D0}; v_D > 0 constant, u_D ∈ R, |u_D| ≤ u_MD. This is a Dubins-type vehicle (constant speed, bounded angular rate).
- **Attacker (simple motion) (2, p. 1657)**: ẋ_A = v_A cos θ_A, ẏ_A = v_A sin θ_A, x_A(0) = x_{A0}; control u_A = (cos θ_A, sin θ_A)^⊤, θ_A ∈ [0, 2π) (the printed text has a typo "θ ∈ [0,2π)").
- **Winning/termination**: attacker wins iff it reaches Ω_goal from Ω_play with ‖x_A − x_D‖₂ > r throughout; defender wins iff capture ‖x_A − x_D‖₂ ≤ r occurs in Ω_play (p. 1657). No value function, HJI equation, or barrier is computed for the *car* game — the paper's analytic machinery is entirely about reference generation and tracking.
- **Information structure**: the defender's strategy is full-state feedback requiring (x_D, θ_D, x_A); the attacker's current control is **unknown** to the defender (p. 1658), which motivates worst-case bounding instead of exact prediction of θ̇_D^r. No delay/noise analysis.

### 5.2 Reference-generation layer: ES-based strategy and Lemma 1
- **ES-based defense strategy (4, p. 1657)**, under the *assumed* simple-motion defender kinematics (3) (identical to (2) but for D): u_D = (x_I − x_D)/‖x_I − x_D‖₂, where the interception point is (5, p. 1657):
  x_I = ( (α²x_A − x_D)/(α² − 1), (α²y_A − y_D − α‖x_A − x_D‖₂)/(α² − 1) )^⊤.
  This is the point on the Apollonius circle (Fig. 1) where the faster defender intercepts the attacker; the guarantee attached to it (evasion space never approaches the goal region) is [13]'s claim for point capture, extended to r > 0 by the authors' subset argument (p. 1657; see §7).
- **Lemma 1 (Heading Reference of the Defender) (p. 1657, eq. (6))**: under strategy (4) the defender's heading reference is
  θ_D^r = { atan( y_{D,A}/x_{D,A} + ‖x_{D,A}‖₂/(α x_{D,A}) ) + (π/2)[sgn(x_{D,A}) + 1],  x_{D,A} ≠ 0;
            −(π/2) sgn(y_{D,A}),  x_{D,A} = 0 },
  where x_{D,A} = (x_{D,A}, y_{D,A})^⊤ = x_D − x_A. **Proof**: substituting (4) into (3) gives the defender's velocity under ES pursuit,
  ẋ_D = −(α² v_D/((α²−1)‖x_I − x_D‖₂)) (x_{D,A}, y_{D,A} + (1/α)‖x_{D,A}‖₂)^⊤  (7, p. 1657),
  so the path slope is dy_D/dx_D = ẏ_D/ẋ_D = y_{D,A}/x_{D,A} + ‖x_{D,A}‖₂/(α x_{D,A})  (8, p. 1657); θ_D^r = atan(dy_D/dx_D) plus the quadrant correction (π/2)[sgn(x_{D,A})+1] because atan's range is only (−π/2, π/2). For x_{D,A} = 0, (7) reduces to a purely vertical velocity (0, −α y_{D,A} − |y_{D,A}|)^⊤ (p. 1658), giving θ_D^r = −π/2 for y_{D,A} > 0 and +π/2 for y_{D,A} < 0, i.e., −(π/2)sgn(y_{D,A}). The reference is undefined at x_{D,A} = 0 (meaningless under capture). **Geometric structure (Fig. 2, p. 1659)**: θ_D^r is invariant along rays k·x_{D,A}, k > 0 — "equal heading reference lines" are straight lines through the origin of the relative-position plane. Analytic, global (where defined), exact.

### 5.3 Tracking layer: gradient analysis and worst-case bound
- **Controller (9, p. 1658)**: u_D = u_MD sgn(θ_D^r − θ_D) — bang-bang maximum-rate steering, motivated by "the defender aims to track the heading reference as soon as possible."
- **Chain rule (10, p. 1658)**: θ̇_D^r = [∂θ_D^r/∂x_{D,A}, ∂θ_D^r/∂y_{D,A}] [dx_{D,A}/dt, dy_{D,A}/dt]^⊤ = ∇θ_D^r(x_{D,A})^⊤(v_D u_D − v_A u_A), using (2), (3) and x_{D,A} = x_D − x_A. Exact value "hard to be determined" because u_A is unknown.
- **Worst-case problem (11, p. 1658)**: φ(x_{D,A}) = max_{u_A, u_D} |θ̇_D^r(x_{D,A})| — the worst case over all attacker *and defender* controls at a frozen relative position. (Both controls are treated as free unit vectors; see §7 for the conservatism this implies.)
- **Lemma 2 (Gradient of the Heading Reference) (p. 1658, eq. (12))**: 
  ∇θ_D^r = { G/(1+F²) (−y_{D,A}, x_{D,A})^⊤,  x_{D,A} ≠ 0;
             ( −α/([α + sgn(y_{D,A})] y_{D,A}), 0 )^⊤,  x_{D,A} = 0 },
  with F = y_{D,A}/x_{D,A} + ‖x_{D,A}‖₂/(α x_{D,A}) and G = (1/x_{D,A}²)(1 + y_{D,A}/(α‖x_{D,A}‖₂)). Moreover ∇θ_D^r ⊥ x_{D,A} with the direction of (−y_{D,A}, x_{D,A})^⊤. **Proof**: direct differentiation of (6) giving ∂θ_D^r/∂x_{D,A} = (1/(1+F²))(−y_{D,A}/x_{D,A}² − y_{D,A}²/(α x_{D,A}²‖x_{D,A}‖₂)) and ∂θ_D^r/∂y_{D,A} = (1/(1+F²))(1/x_{D,A} + y_{D,A}/(α x_{D,A}‖x_{D,A}‖₂)); for the singular line x_{D,A} = 0 the partial derivative is computed via a difference quotient, L'Hôpital's rule, and evaluation of the (non-indeterminate) limit, yielding −α/((α−1)y_{D,A}) for y_{D,A} < 0 (consistent with (12) since sgn(y_{D,A}) = −1); the perpendicularity follows from G/(1+F²) > 0, proven by G = (α‖x_{D,A}‖₂ + y_{D,A})/(α x_{D,A}²‖x_{D,A}‖₂) ≥ (α‖x_{D,A}‖₂ − |y_{D,A}|)/(α x_{D,A}²‖x_{D,A}‖₂) > 0 (using α > 1 so α‖x‖ − |y| ≥ (α−1)‖x‖ > 0). The perpendicularity is the algebraic reason behind the equal-heading lines of Fig. 2. **Verified independently**: from (12), ‖∇θ_D^r‖ = α(α + sin φ)/((α² + 1 + 2α sin φ) R) for x_{D,A} = R(cos φ, sin φ) — this reproduces the paper's φ(R, φ) below, so the (12)→(13) chain is internally consistent.
- **Lemma 3 (Bounds for θ̇_D^r at x_{D,A}) (p. 1659, eq. (13))**: φ(x_{D,A}) = ‖∇θ_D^r(x_{D,A})‖₂ (v_A + v_D). **Proof**: θ̇_D^r = ∇θ_D^r^⊤(v_D u_D − v_A u_A) is an inner product; by Lemma 2 the gradient direction is fixed at the given state, so both terms are maximized simultaneously when u_D points along ∇θ_D^r and u_A along −∇θ_D^r (Fig. 3(a)), giving the upper bound ‖∇θ_D^r‖₂(v_A + v_D); reversing both directions gives the symmetric lower bound −‖∇θ_D^r‖₂(v_A + v_D) (Fig. 3(b)); equal magnitudes imply (13). (This is the Cauchy–Schwarz bound attained by collinear alignment; decisive inequality: |∇θ^⊤(v_D u_D − v_A u_A)| ≤ v_D‖∇θ‖ + v_A‖∇θ‖.) Local-in-state, exact worst case *given the frozen-state relaxation of (11)*.
- **Theorem 1 (Upper Bounds for |θ̇_D^r|) (p. 1659, eq. (14))**: the global uniform bound ϑ such that |θ̇_D^r(t)| ≤ ϑ, ∀t ≥ 0, is
  ϑ = α/(r(α − 1)) (v_A + v_D).
  **Assumptions**: capture radius r > 0, hence ‖x_{D,A}‖₂ ≥ r for all t until capture (the game is ongoing); α > 1. **Proof**: seek ϑ with φ(x_{D,A}) ≤ ϑ ∀‖x_{D,A}‖₂ ≥ r; parametrize x_{D,A} = [R cos φ, R sin φ]^⊤, R ≥ r, and substitute into (13):
  φ(R, φ) = α(α + sin φ)/((α² + 1 + 2α sin φ) R) · (v_A + v_D);
  the formula also covers x_{D,A} = 0 directions (φ = π/2 + kπ, k ∈ Z). For fixed φ, φ(R, φ) ∝ 1/R, so the maximum over R ≥ r is at R = r. Then f(φ) := φ(r, φ) has
  f′(φ) = α(1 − α²) cos φ/(α² + 1 + 2α sin φ)² · (v_A + v_D)/r,
  so f′ > 0 on (π/2, 3π/2) and f′ < 0 on (3π/2, 5π/2) (since 1 − α² < 0 for α > 1); being periodic, f peaks at φ = 3π/2, and f(3π/2) = α(α−1)/((α−1)² r)(v_A + v_D) = α(v_A+v_D)/(r(α−1)) = ϑ. **Verified independently** (algebra above). **Physical meaning**: the heading reference can spin fastest when the players are at the capture-distance limit with the attacker "below" the defender relative to the gradient direction; smaller capture radius r or speed ratio α closer to 1 makes the reference harder to follow (ϑ → ∞ as r → 0⁺ or α → 1⁺ — the method fundamentally requires r > 0 and a speed advantage). Analytic, global (uniform in time, over the admissible state set). Note the bound is a *pre-capture* statement: it holds while ‖x_{D,A}‖₂ ≥ r, i.e., while the game is unresolved.
- **Theorem 2 (Finite Tracking Time) (pp. 1659–1660, eq. (15))**: under controller (9), θ_D(t) = θ_D^r(t), t ≥ T, with
  T = √(2V(t₀))/(u_MD − ϑ) + t₀,  where V(t) = ½(θ_D(t) − θ_D^r(t))²  (16, p. 1660).
  **Assumption (made explicit in Remark 1)**: u_MD > ϑ. **Proof (Lyapunov)**: V̇(t) = (θ_D − θ_D^r)(θ̇_D − θ̇_D^r) = (θ_D − θ_D^r)[u_MD sgn(θ_D^r − θ_D) − θ̇_D^r] ≤ −u_MD|θ_D − θ_D^r| + |θ_D − θ_D^r|ϑ (using Theorem 1's bound) = −√2(u_MD − ϑ)√V. Then dV/√V ≤ −√2(u_MD − ϑ)dt integrates to √V(t) ≤ √V(t₀) − (√2/2)(u_MD − ϑ)(t − t₀)  (17, p. 1660) (the paper attributes this to "the Gronwall's lemma," though it is a direct separation-and-integration; see §7). Setting the right side of (17) to zero yields (15). **Interpretation**: the heading error is extinguished in finite time (not merely asymptotically) because the bang-bang control dominates the worst-case reference rotation; T is bounded by π/(u_MD − ϑ) in the worst case since √(2V(t₀)) = |θ_D(t₀) − θ_D^r(t₀)| ≤ π. Once V ≡ 0, the condition u_MD > ϑ also guarantees the defender keeps up with the reference thereafter (V̇ ≤ 0 whenever V > 0). Analytic, global in initial heading error.
- **Remark 1 (p. 1660)**: the paper focuses on u_MD > ϑ, "i.e., the turning capability of the defender is large compared to the capture radius and their speeds, like the situation considered in [21]" (Garcia et al., BVR tactics, ACC 2021). Once tracking succeeds, "the car-model defender can move along the same trajectory as the simple-motion defender, leading to the distance guarantee related to the evasion space and goal region as in [13]" — this is the *only* bridge back to the reach-avoid winning guarantee, and it is stated, not proven (see §7). **The full reach-avoid guarantee chain is therefore**: (i) ES strategy (4)–(5) keeps the attacker's evasion space away from Ω_goal [inherited from [13]; subset argument for r > 0]; (ii) Lemma 1 converts it into heading reference (6); (iii) Theorem 1 bounds its rotation rate by ϑ; (iv) Theorem 2 guarantees exact tracking after finite T if u_MD > ϑ; (v) Remark 1 asserts trajectory equivalence with the ideal simple-motion defender, transferring [13]'s distance guarantee. **No step of this chain covers the transient [t₀, T]**, during which the defender deviates from the ES strategy.

### 5.4 Proof gaps and unclear steps
- **Proof gap (guarantee inheritance, p. 1657)**: "the similar guarantee still holds … because the evasion space in this paper is a subset of that in the case of point capture" — no definition of the non-zero-capture-radius evasion space is given here, no derivation of the subset relation, and no statement of what exactly is guaranteed (invariance? monotone distance?) for r > 0; everything is deferred to [2], [9], [13].
- **Proof gap (transient, pp. 1659–1660)**: Theorem 2 only ensures θ_D = θ_D^r for t ≥ T; the effect of the initial untracked interval on the evasion-space guarantee is not analyzed (the guarantee of [13] presupposes the defender plays (4) from t = 0).
- **Relaxation in (11)**: φ(x_{D,A}) maximizes over u_A *and* u_D with x_{D,A} frozen, although u_D is actually fixed by (9) and both controls move x_{D,A}; this makes (13)–(14) valid upper bounds but conservative ones (the paper itself calls φ "the worst case for all possible control of the attacker and itself," p. 1658).
- **Minor**: (17) is attributed to "Gronwall's lemma" (p. 1660) but is a direct integration; in (2) the attacker's heading is written "θ ∈ [0,2π)" instead of θ_A; θ_D^r is undefined at x_{D,A} = 0 (moot in practice since capture at radius r occurs first, and the singular gradient branch ∝ 1/y_{D,A} in (12) is only reached at coincidence).

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
**Author-stated**:
- The heading reference is undefined at x_{D,A} = 0 (p. 1658) and the analysis presumes r > 0; the point-capture case is excluded from the tracking guarantee (ϑ → ∞ as r → 0⁺).
- The attacker is only *approximately* simple motion (u_MA chosen large so turning time is "neglected," p. 1661).
- Experiment–simulation trajectories do not converge (p. 1661) — inherent to heading-only tracking with recomputed references.
- Future work: multiplayer reach-avoid DGs (Section VI, p. 1661).

**Critical observations (this archive, with page/eq. anchors)**:
1. **The reach-avoid winning guarantee for the car-model defender is never proven in this paper.** It rests on [13]'s point-capture ES claim plus a one-sentence subset argument for r > 0 (p. 1657) and Remark 1's trajectory-equivalence assertion (p. 1660). The paper's own theorems concern reference generation and tracking only; there is no barrier, no winning-set characterization, and no capture-before-goal proof for the car-vs-simple-motion game.
2. **The experiments run outside the proven sufficient-condition regime.** With the printed parameters (v_A = 0.18, v_D = 0.25 m/s, r = 0.35 m ⇒ α ≈ 1.389), Theorem 1 gives ϑ = α(v_A+v_D)/(r(α−1)) ≈ 4.39 rad/s, far above u_MD = 0.5 rad/s, so Theorem 2's finite-time guarantee (which needs u_MD > ϑ, Remark 1) does not apply to the demonstrated scenario. The guarantee chain's condition fails by roughly a factor of 9; successful capture is an empirical fact, not a corollary of the theory. Conservatism sources: (11) maximizes over both controls with x_{D,A} frozen, the worst case requires both velocity vectors to align with ∇θ_D^r (a "cooperative" spin that an adversarial attacker cannot exploit alone while also moving toward the goal), and φ is evaluated at the minimal separation R = r.
3. **No transient analysis**: during [t₀, T] the defender's heading differs from θ_D^r, so the ES-based distance guarantee (which presumes play of (4) from the start) is suspended; whether the attacker can exploit this window is open.
4. **Conservative uniform bound**: the global ϑ replaces the state-dependent φ(x_{D,A}) everywhere; a state-dependent switching analysis (guaranteeing tracking only where φ(x_{D,A}) < u_MD, i.e., ‖x_{D,A}‖ > r·α(α+sin φ)/(u_MD(α²+1+2α sin φ)) roughly) could enlarge the proven region; not attempted.
5. **Chattering**: bang-bang law (9) with sgn(·) gives discontinuous steering at V = 0 (sliding mode); discretization/actuator filtering effects are not discussed, though they are visible as the sim-vs-experiment gaps of Fig. 8.
6. **Information assumptions**: perfect knowledge of both positions in a common frame (achieved in hardware via SLAM + ArUco re-localization, Fig. 5–6); no robustness analysis to localization error, latency, or an attacker with partial observability.
7. **Minor technical blemishes**: (17) labeled "Gronwall's lemma" though it is direct integration (p. 1660); θ vs θ_A typo in (2) (p. 1657); the singular branch of (12) at x_{D,A} = 0 is included although the game terminates at ‖x_{D,A}‖ = r.
8. **Scale-up limits**: single defender, single attacker, 2D, straight target line ("for convenience," p. 1657); the extension to curved boundaries, slower defenders (α ≤ 1), and multiplayer is explicitly left open (p. 1661).

## 8. Citation Value
**Paragraph role**: This is the bridge paper between the analytic simple-motion reach-avoid literature (Yan et al.'s ES-based/barrier strategies [9], [13], which the skill's library holds as P-series entries) and physically deployable nonholonomic defenders. Its reusable assets are: (i) the closed-form heading reference (6) obtained by differentiating the ES/Apollonius pursuit law, with the geometric picture of equal-heading rays in relative-position space; (ii) the gradient-based worst-case derivative-bounding technique (Lemmas 2–3, Theorem 1) that converts an unknown adversarial input into a single uniform disturbance bound ϑ = α(v_A+v_D)/(r(α−1)) — a template applicable to any reference defined on relative position; (iii) the finite-time Lyapunov argument (Theorem 2) showing bang-bang rate-dominance yields finite-time heading lock; and (iv) a documented experimental pipeline (RTAB-Map + ArUco re-localization + ROS) for validating DG strategies on differential-drive robots, including the explicit caveat that theory and experiment operate in different regimes. Cite it when motivating car-like/fixed-wing defenders in reach-avoid or target-defense games, when converting simple-motion game strategies into executable controllers for nonholonomic agents, or when discussing implementation gaps between differential-game theory and robot experiments — e.g., "Lai et al. convert the evasion-space defense strategy into a bang-bang finite-time heading-tracking controller for a bounded-turn defender, proving |θ̇_D^r| ≤ α(v_A+v_D)/(r(α−1)) and finite tracking time whenever u_MD > ϑ, and validate the scheme on TurtleBot2 robots with vision-based re-localization [P047]."
