## 2. Research Background
Reach-avoid pursuit–evasion games in the sense of Isaacs ask whether pursuers can stop evaders from reaching a goal region, and at what cost. Most of the classical machinery (isochrons, Voronoi partitions for equal speeds, Apollonius circles for unequal speeds, HJI equations) was developed on the 2-D plane, while 3-D extensions — needed for target guarding, airspace/plane protection, and resource-region defense — remain sparse and are mostly restricted to equal-speed players or one-to-one encounters. For multiplayer multi-evader (MPME) instances the joint state lives in R^{3M+3N}, so direct HJI solution is ruled out by the curse of dimensionality and by the non-uniqueness of equilibria in general MPME games; practical methods therefore decompose the problem and assign pursuers (individually or in cooperative teams) to evaders. Existing assignment approaches (enumeration of pursuer–evader pairs for <5 players, exhaustive listing of all coalitions, 0–1 integer programming, sequential matching) target the maximum capture number and need the feasibility of every candidate pair as prior knowledge, which wastes computation and ignores interception cost. This paper, published in a venue focused on cognitive/developmental multiagent systems, builds a complete pipeline: analytic 3-D subgame solutions with heterogeneous speeds, plus real-time combinatorial teaming, validated against state-of-the-art matching schemes.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
**Problem.** N pursuers U = {P1,…,PN} and M evaders V = {E1,…,EM} with single-integrator dynamics and constant but different speeds move in the half-space Ω = {s ∈ R³ : z ≥ 0}; the goal is the infinite plane Ω_G = {z = 0} (p. 2086). The MPME game is not terminated event-wise; it is evaluated after all evaders are "at rest" by the number of successful escapes and the interception positions (game of degree, p. 2086). It is decomposed into multipursuer–single-evader (MPSE) subgames whose value is the evader's terminal height z_E(t_f) (eqs. (4)–(7)), and the MPME layer optimizes the sum of subgame capture times by bipartite matching (eq. (34)).

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Theory**: 3-D Apollonius-sphere dominance regions via a barrier function B(s) (eq. (8)); exact value functions and target points for 1P1E (Theorem 1), 2P1E (Theorem 2), 3P1E (Theorem 3) heterogeneous-speed reach-avoid subgames against a plane goal; degeneration analysis showing a pursuit team never needs more than 3 pursuers in 3-D (Remark 4).
2. **Modeling**: decomposition of the MPME game over a plane goal into MPSE subgames with termination set T = T_p ∪ T_e (eqs. (4)–(5)) and terminal payoff Φ = z_E(t_f) (eq. (6)); closed-loop (state-feedback) information structure, contrasted with the open-loop Stackelberg setting of [14] (Remark 3); MPME objective as total capture time W (eq. (34)).
3. **Algorithm**: transformation of MPME task assignment into weighted bipartite matching with edges feasible iff z(I*(i,j)) ≥ 0 (eq. (35)); a binary integer program (eq. (36)); a hierarchical matching algorithm (Algorithm 1, p. 2093) building 1P1E → max-cardinality → 2P1E → 3P1E teams; a state-feedback rematching method (Algorithm 2, p. 2094) with real-time target switching; complexity O(NM + N²/2) vs. O(N³M/6 + 5NM/6) for the 0–1 IP of [26] (Remark 5).
4. **Experiment**: 1P1E/2P1E/3P1E trajectory comparisons of cooperative vs. individual optimal strategies (Figs. 8–12, Table I), and 10P8E games comparing first assignments (sequential matching [22]: cost 22.809; Algorithm 1: 20.08) and full closed-loop games (0–1 IP [16], [26]: 6 captures; Algorithm 2: 7 captures, cost 19.8735).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results

### 5.1 Model, information structure, cost, termination (p. 2086–2087)
- **Dynamics (eq. (1))**: single integrators, ẋ_Pi = u_xi, ẏ_Pi = u_yi, ż_Pi = u_zi; likewise for E_j with controls v_j; controls are 3-D headings (yaw α, β and pitch ψ, γ). Speed constraints (eq. (2)): √(u_xi²+u_yi²+u_zi²) = u_i, √(v_xj²+v_yj²+v_zj²) = v_j. Full state x = (x_{E1}ᵀ,…,x_{EM}ᵀ,x_{P1}ᵀ,…,x_{PN}ᵀ)ᵀ ∈ R^{3M+3N}.
- **Assumption 1 (eq. (3))**: all speeds constant; pursuers keep moving until game end; speed ratio p_ij := u_i/v_j > 1 for EVERY pair (P_i, E_j). Headings may change instantaneously. The authors argue (p. 2087) that maximum-speed motion is optimal even with acceleration dynamics, and that the initial acceleration phase has negligible effect on the terminal interception position — an intuitive claim, not proved.
- **Termination (eqs. (4)–(5))**: T := T_p ∪ T_e with T_p = {x : z_E ≠ 0, ‖x_Pi − x_E‖ = 0 for ∃ i} (capture; point capture) and T_e = {x : z_E = 0, ‖x_Pi − x_E‖ ≠ 0 for ∀ i} (escape). Ties at the plane go to pursuers.
- **Cost and value (eqs. (6)–(7))**: J(x,u_i,v,t) = Φ(x(t_f)) = z_E(t_f); V(x,t) := min_v max_{u_i} J. The evader minimizes terminal height; sign of z_E(t_f) decides the game of kind, its magnitude the game of degree. *Note*: the min–max order in (7) puts the evader outside, whereas the verification in Appendix C computes max_{u_i} min_v Ṽ — the order is immaterial only if a saddle point exists, which is what is being proved; the inconsistency is never discussed.
- **Lemma 1 (p. 2087)**: under optimal play the optimal headings (yaw/pitch) of P_i and E are constant and all trajectories are straight lines. Proof: "It can be similarly proved to Theorem 1 in [18] based on Pontryagin's maximum principle" — *proof gap: no derivation given; the entire geometric development leans on this external PMP argument.*
- **Game of kind assumption**: throughout Section III the initial configuration satisfies R_E ∩ Ω_G = ∅ (E cannot force reaching the plane), so the game of degree is well posed (p. 2088, 2089, 2090). No characterization of the winning set boundary in terms of initial states is derived beyond this checkable condition.

### 5.2 Definition 1–2: Apollonius sphere and dominance regions (p. 2088, eqs. (8)–(11))
- Barrier function (eq. (8)): B(s) = p‖s − x_E‖ − ‖s − x_P‖ with p := u/v > 1. B(s) = 0 gives the Apollonius sphere S_EP (eq. (9)):
  (x − (p²x_E − x_P)/(p²−1))² + (y − (p²y_E − y_P)/(p²−1))² + (z − (p²z_E − z_P)/(p²−1))² = p²d²/(p²−1)²,
  where d = ‖x_P − x_E‖; center c = (p²x_E − x_P, p²y_E − y_P, p²z_E − z_P)ᵀ/(p²−1), radius r = pd/(p²−1) (eq. (10)).
- Dominance regions (eq. (11)): R_E = {s : B(s) ≤ 0} (inside the sphere, E arrives no later), R_P = {s : B(s) > 0}. If R_E ∩ Ω_G ≠ ∅, E wins the game of kind.

### 5.3 Theorem 1 (1P1E, p. 2088, eqs. (12)–(16))
- **Statement**: for dynamics (1) with one pursuer and one evader, if R_E ∩ Ω_G = ∅ initially, the value is the z-coordinate of the lowest point I* of the Apollonius sphere, I* = c − (0,0,r)ᵀ (eq. (13)), so
  V(x) = z_{I*} = (p²z_E − z_P)/(p²−1) − (p/(p²−1)) d   (eq. (14)).
  The optimal controls are the unit headings toward I* expressed in yaw/pitch (eq. (15)): v* = v(cosψ cosα, cosψ sinα, sinψ)ᵀ, u* = u(cosγ cosβ, cosγ sinβ, sinγ)ᵀ with (eq. (16))
  sinψ = (z_E − z_P − pd)/√((p²+1)d² − 2(z_E − z_P)pd),
  sinγ = (p(z_E − z_P) − d)/√((p²+1)d² − 2(z_E − z_P)pd),
  sinα = sinβ = (y_E − y_P)/√((x_E − x_P)² + (y_E − y_P)²).
- **Assumptions**: p > 1; R_E ∩ Ω_G = ∅; d ≠ 0 (players not coincident).
- **Proof approach (Appendix A, p. 2097–2098, eqs. (39)–(46))**: (i) compute all six partials of V (eq. (39)); V is C¹ for d ≠ 0; (ii) write the HJI equation −V_t = V_x·f(x,v*,u*) + g with V_t = 0 and zero running cost (eq. (40)–(41)); (iii) write the straight-line displacement vectors I*−E and I*−P (eq. (42)) and the resulting unit-heading strategies (eq. (43)), which rearrange to (15)–(16); (iv) evaluate the evader part V_x·f |_{E-terms} = −(pv/d)·d_IE (eq. (44)) and the pursuer part = (u/(pd))·d_IP (eq. (45)), where d_IE, d_IP are E's and P's distances to I*; (v) decisive step (eq. (46)): V_x·f = (u·d_IP − p²v·d_IE)/(pd) = 0, because the isochrony of I* (both arrive simultaneously) gives d_IP/d_IE = u/v = p and u = pv. Hence V is the C¹ solution of the HJI equation with (15)–(16) optimal.
- **Nature**: analytic, closed-form, global under the game-of-kind assumption; verification-type argument (HJI satisfied along candidate trajectories) rather than a viscosity/subsolution–supersolution proof.
- **Physical meaning**: the pursuer drags the "guaranteed capture locus" (the sphere) so that E's best guaranteed terminal height is exactly the sphere's lowest point; capture occurs at I*.
- **Extensibility**: Remark 1 (p. 2088–2089) notes that for a faster evader the game must switch to radius capture and Apollonius circles become Cartesian ellipses — declared out of scope. I independently verified (16) by differentiating (13)–(14) and re-computing d_IE, d_IP; the formulas are internally consistent, as are (39), (44)–(46).

### 5.4 Theorem 2 (2P1E, p. 2089–2090, eqs. (17)–(25))
- **Setup**: P1, P2 with speeds u1, u2, ratios p1, p2 > 1, form team C = {P1,P2} (eq. (17)); spheres S_EP1, S_EP2 with centers (xi,yi,zi) and radii ri from (10) (eq. (18)); E's dominance region is the lens R_E = R_E1 ∩ R_E2 (eq. (19)); assume R_E ∩ Ω_G = ∅. Studied configuration: centers c1, c2 on opposite sides of E; if they are on the same side, the cross-section degenerates to a tangent point and the game degenerates to 1P1E (p. 2089).
- **Statement**: R_E is bounded; the target point is its lowest point I* = (x0, y0, z0)ᵀ. At z0 the horizontal plane cuts both spheres in two circles that are externally tangent at x0, giving the tangency equation (eq. (20)):
  √(r1² − (z1−z0)²) + √(r2² − (z2−z0)²) = √((x1−x2)² + (y1−y2)²).
  Solving (a quadratic D z0² − 2H z0 + F = 0, lower root) yields (eq. (21)):
  z0 = (H − √(H² − DF))/D,
  with C := (x1−x2)² + (y1−y2)², D := ‖c1−c2‖² = (z1−z2)² + C, H := −(r1²−r2²−z1²+z2²)(z1−z2)/2 + C(z1+z2)/2, F := (r1²−r2²−z1²+z2²)²/4 + C(z1²+z2²−r1²−r2²)/2 + C²/4 (eq. (22)). Then x0 solves C x0² − 2S x0 + T = 0 (eq. (23)–(24)):
  x0 = (S − √(S² − CT))/C, y0 = [R − 2(x2−x1)x0 − 2(z2−z1)z0]/(2(y2−y1)),
  where R := r1²−r2²−x1²+x2²−y1²+y2²−z1²+z2², S := R(x2−x1)/2 − (x2−x1)(z2−z1)z0 − y1(x2−x1)(y2−y1) + x1(y2−y1)², T := [R/2 − (z2−z1)z0 − y1(y2−y1)]² + (y2−y1)²[(z0−z1)² − r1² + x1²]. Value V(x) = z0 (eq. (21)). Optimal strategies (eq. (25)): everyone heads straight to I* at full speed, v* = v‖I*−x_E‖⁻¹(x0−x_E, y0−y_E, z0−z_E)ᵀ, u_i* = u_i‖I*−x_Pi‖⁻¹(x0−x_Pi, y0−y_Pi, z0−z_Pi)ᵀ, i = 1,2.
- **Proof (Appendix B, p. 2097–2098, eq. (47))**: subtracting the two sphere equations gives the plane relation (x2−x1)x0 + (y2−y1)y0 + (z2−z1)z0 = R/2 (eq. (47)); substitute z0 (21) into (47) to get y0 as a function of x0; substitute back into (18) and eliminate y0 to get the quadratic in x0, i.e. (23)–(24). I re-derived S and T symbolically from this elimination and they are exactly correct. "Following steps similar to Appendix A, V(x) can be proved to be the solution of the HJI equation. Alternatively … Lagrange multiplier method … provided in Appendix C." — *proof gap: no explicit HJI verification is carried out for 2P1E; and the tangency premise "If x0 is the unique saddle point under optimal play, then the two planar circles are tangent at x0" (p. 2089) is asserted, not proved (uniqueness of the saddle and the external-tangency configuration are assumed).*
- **Remark 2 (p. 2090) — degeneration test**: compare z0 with the two individual targets z_{I*_{EP1}}, z_{I*_{EP2}} from (13). If z0 > max{z_{I*_{EP1}}, z_{I*_{EP2}}}, cooperation is strictly beneficial (intercept simultaneously and higher). If z0 ≤ max{…}, a pursuer is redundant and the optimum degenerates to 1P1E. Also: V "is not necessarily continuous" in the extreme configuration c1 = c2.
- **Remark 3 (p. 2090)**: contrast with the guarding game of [14] (two homogeneous same-speed defenders modeled as one central player with two inputs; attacker knows defenders' strategies → open-loop Stackelberg). Here strategies (15), (25), (32) are state-feedback, "remain optimal instantaneously", and yield closed-loop Nash equilibrium — the paper's key structural selling point.

### 5.5 Theorem 3 (3P1E, p. 2090–2091, eqs. (26)–(32))
- **Setup**: three pursuers, C = {P1,P2,P3} (eq. (26)); spheres S_EPi (eq. (27)); if the three spheres have a common intersection ∩ᵢ S_EPi ≠ ∅, a saddle point exists; otherwise degeneration to 2P1E or 1P1E. R_E = ∩ᵢ R_Ei (eq. (28)); assume R_E ∩ Ω_G = ∅. The target I* is the common intersection point of the three isochronous surfaces with the lower z-coordinate (two candidates in general).
- **Statement**: value (eq. (29)):
  V(x) = (−F′ − √(F′² − E′G))/E′,
  with (eq. (30)): F′ := B1(B0−x1) + C1(C0−y1) − z1, E′ := B1² + C1² + 1, G := (B0−x1)² + (C0−y1)² + z1² − r1², where the line through the two candidate intersection points is parametrized as
  B0 := (A21·Y31 − A31·Y21)/D′, B1 := (Y21·Z31 − Y31·Z21)/D′, C0 := (A31·X21 − A21·X31)/D′, C1 := (X31·Z21 − X21·Z31)/D′,
  A_i := r_i² − x_i² − y_i² − z_i², A_i1 := (−A_i − A1)/2, X_ij := x_i − x_j, Y_ij := y_i − y_j, Z_ij := z_i − z_j, D′ := X21·Y31 − Y21·X31 (i,j ∈ {1,2,3}, pairwise differences w.r.t. players 2,3 vs. 1). Target (eq. (31)): x(I*) = B0 + B1·V(x), y(I*) = C0 + C1·V(x), z(I*) = V(x). Strategies (eq. (32)): v* = v‖I*−x_E‖₂⁻¹(I*−x_E)ᵀ-direction, u_i* = u_i‖I*−x_Pi‖₂⁻¹ toward I* (the ‖·‖₂ subscripts denote Euclidean norms — verified on the rendered page).
- **Proof (Appendix C, p. 2098–2099, eqs. (48)–(59))**: (i) reduce E's problem to the convex program min_{s∈R³} F(s) = z subject to B_i(s) ≤ 0 ∀ P_i ∈ C (eq. (48)) — feasible set = intersection of balls (convex; note B_i itself is a difference of convex functions, but only the set matters); (ii) Lagrangian L = F + Σ λ_i B_i (eq. (49)) and duality min_s max_λ L = max_λ min_s L = max_λ L(s*,λ) (eq. (50)) — *strong duality invoked without constraint-qualification discussion*; (iii) KKT conditions (eq. (51)) with ∇B_i(s) = p_i(s−x_E)/‖s−x_E‖ − (s−x_Pi)/‖s−x_Pi‖ (eq. (52)); split by components (eq. (53)); (iv) complementary slackness: constraints with B_i(I*) < 0 are weak — the corresponding pursuer can be dropped without changing the optimum — so an effective reduced team C′ with all constraints active remains (eq. (55)); (v) invariance ∂B_i(I*)/∂t = 0 along optimal play (eq. (56)–(57)) — I* stays on each effective isochronous surface; (vi) decisive computation (eq. (54) + (58)): Ṽ = [0,0,1]·İ* = −Σ_{P_i∈C′} λ_i ∇B_i(I*)ᵀİ* = max_{u_i} min_v Σ_{P_i∈C′} λ_i[ (I*−x_Pi)ᵀu_i/‖I*−x_Pi‖ − p_i (I*−x_E)ᵀv/‖I*−x_E‖ ], which under the optimal headings (32) collapses to (eq. (59)):
  max_{u_i} min_v Ṽ = Σ_{P_i∈C′} λ_i(−p_i v + u_i) = 0, since u_i = p_i v (isochrony at I*).
  Hence s* = I* is the convex program's solution and a Nash equilibrium for E and all P_i ∈ C; the argument "applies to various scenarios, regardless of whether there exist redundant pursuers" (p. 2098–2099), and (51) is identified as the KKT generalization of the Lagrange multiplier method.
- **Nature**: analytic; the dynamic game is certified through a static KKT argument plus Lemma 1's straight-line/isochrony structure, not through a full HJI solution (explicitly avoided "due to the dimensionality curse", p. 2098).
- **Remark 4 (p. 2091) — degeneration and team-size bound**: 3P1E is necessary only if z(I*) > max over the three pairs {z_{I*_{E(Pi,Pj)}}}; otherwise a pair suffices. "The maximum scale of C is 3": capturing one evader in 3-D needs at most three pursuers, because the saddle point has at most three coordinates and each pursuer contributes one constraint (isochronous surface); any larger team C reduces to C′ with |C′| ≤ 3 and unchanged I*. *Critical note: this is a variable-counting dimensional argument (stated on p. 2087 as "the number of variables and the number of conditions must be equal"), presented without a formal lemma; four spheres can generically have no common point but the "surplus one will be eliminated" statement is justified only implicitly via complementary slackness (55).*

### 5.6 MPME layer: assignment, algorithms (p. 2091–2094)
- **Pursuit team (Definition 3, eq. (33))**: P_C = {P_i : i ∈ C}, C ⊂ {1,…,N}, |C| ≤ 3. Matched pursuers keep hunting after a capture ("will not cease activity but immediately continue seeking the next opponent", p. 2091).
- **Objective (eq. (34))**: W := Σ_{E_j∈V} t_f(C,j), t_f(C,j) := ‖I*(C,j) − x_{E_j}‖/v_j — the evader's time to the team target, which equals the common arrival time by isochrony. *Note the criterion switch: subgame values are terminal heights z_E(t_f), while assignment weights are capture times.*
- **Bipartite graph and BLP (eqs. (35)–(36))**: G = (U ∪ V, E), edge e_ij exists iff z(I*(i,j)) ≥ 0 (interception above the plane), weight w(i,j) = t_f(i,j); min Σ c_ij w(i,j) s.t. one edge per node, c_ij ∈ {0,1} — a binary integer program solved as a minimum-weight matching.
- **Hierarchical matching (Algorithm 1, p. 2093)**: (i) minimum-weight matching M1 on G (1P1E pairs; KM/Hungarian tools); (ii) sets S, S′ (matched/free pursuers), T, T′ (eq. (37)); Definition 4 lists how pursuers become free (N > M; unable to capture alone; task finished; matching change); (iii) while T′ ≠ ∅ and S′ ≠ ∅: maximum-cardinality matching M′ of G1 = (S′ ∪ T′, E1) to raise the capture-number lower bound; (iv) maximum-weight matching M2 on G2 = (S ∪ S′, E2), edges e_ii′ between working P_i and free P_i′ with weight w(i,i′) = t_f(i,j) − t_f(C,j) > 0 (time saved by teaming, eq. (38)) → 2P1E teams; (v) repeat analogously for 3P1E teams (E3, M3); (vi) output M = M1 ∪ M′ ∪ M2 ∪ M3 and W_M. *Minor pseudocode defect: line 8 forms T1, T′_1 but the loop condition at line 4 tests T′ and S′, so T′ is stale inside the loop (p. 2093).*
- **State-feedback rematching (Algorithm 2, p. 2094)**: every step Δt, remove intercepted/escaped evaders (V ← V \ E_j, moved to V′), recompute M_b by Algorithm 1 on remaining players, and adopt it if the capture number increases (|M_b| > |M_a|) or the cost decreases (W_{Mb} < W_{Ma}); matched players head to their (new) target points, free pursuers "move vertically downwards" (a standby policy), finished evaders stay stationary.
- **Complexity (Remark 5, p. 2093–2094)**: Algorithm 1 = O(N·M + |S|·|S′| + |S1|·|S′1|) ≤ O(NM + N²/2); Algorithm 2 repeats it and is "asymptotically less than O(NM + N²/2)" as M shrinks; the 0–1 IP considering all teams of [26] costs O(N³M/6 + 5NM/6). MPME assignment is declared NP-hard (no citation or proof given). For [4], [22] "the ratio between the performance of approximate solution and that of the optimal is bounded by a known constant, which is at least 1/3" — quoted, not proved here. No global-optimality guarantee is claimed for Algorithm 1 itself ("mostly approximate the global optimal matching", p. 2094).

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
**Author-stated:**
- Single-integrator, constant-speed models; initial acceleration phase ignored as "relatively small" (p. 2087); faster evaders (radius capture, Cartesian ellipses) explicitly out of scope (p. 2089).
- Real-world challenges acknowledged: unstable environments, complex dynamics, limited perception/computation (p. 2097). Future work: heterogeneous dynamics and payoff functions (p. 2097).

**Critical observations (with page/eq.):**
1. **Strong homogeneity assumption on ratios**: Assumption 1 requires p_ij = u_i/v_j > 1 for every pair (p. 2087, eq. (3)); speed superiority is one-sided and no analysis covers a pursuer slower than some evader.
2. **Game of kind assumed, not solved**: every subgame/edge presumes R_E ∩ Ω_G = ∅ (p. 2088, 2089, 2090; eq. (35)); no barrier surface or initial-set characterization is derived for the 3-D heterogeneous case — the game-of-kind/game-of-degree split is imposed by assumption.
3. **Proof gaps**: Lemma 1 proved only "similarly to Theorem 1 in [18]" (p. 2087); Theorem 2's HJI verification deferred by reference to Appendices A/C, and its key tangency/uniqueness premise is asserted (p. 2089, eq. (20)); Appendix C invokes strong duality (eq. (50)) and KKT without a constraint qualification, and B_i is a difference-of-convex function (only the feasible ball-intersection is convex); the min–max order differs between (7) and (58) without comment.
4. **Criterion mismatch between layers**: subgame value = terminal height z_E(t_f) (eq. (6)) but assignment weight = capture time (eq. (34)); minimizing Σ t_f is not shown to optimize (or be consistent with) the sum of game values; the capture number is prioritized lexicographically inside Algorithm 1 (step 3) without a formal multi-objective justification.
5. **No optimality guarantee for the hierarchy**: Algorithm 1 is a greedy layered construction; global optimality of W is not proved (only "mostly approximate", p. 2094); NP-hardness of MPME assignment is asserted without proof or citation (p. 2093); the "at least 1/3" approximation bound for [4], [22] is quoted (p. 2094).
6. **Team-size-3 claim is informal**: the "at most three pursuers" bound rests on a variables-vs-constraints counting argument (p. 2087, Remark 4 p. 2091), not a theorem; complementary slackness (eq. (55)) is the implicit justification.
7. **Point capture and tie-breaking**: T_p requires ‖x_Pi − x_E‖ = 0 exactly (eq. (5)) — physically idealized; ties at the plane are silently awarded to pursuers (z_E = 0 with ‖·‖ ≠ 0 required for escape).
8. **Regularity of V**: continuity of V can fail when c1 = c2 (Remark 2, p. 2090); no viscosity treatment anywhere — the HJI argument is a classical C¹ verification along candidate trajectories only.
9. **Experimental rigor**: single-scenario demonstrations; no runtime measurements despite the complexity claims of Remark 5; 10P8E initial data unreported; baseline implementation details (online vs. one-shot 0–1 IP) unstated; no robustness/noise/miss-model tests.
10. **Pseudocode defect**: Algorithm 1 line 8 updates T1/T′_1 but the while-condition (line 4) tests the stale T′ (p. 2093) — harmless in intent, sloppy in print.

## 8. Citation Value
Role in a paper's introduction: this is the natural citation for (a) the 3-D, heterogeneous-speed extension of Apollonius-sphere dominance analysis from one-to-one to many-to-one (2P1E, 3P1E) subgames against a plane goal, with explicit closed-form value functions and target points; (b) the argument that MPME reach-avoid games should be decomposed into value-annotated subgames and re-composed by weighted bipartite matching with online state-feedback rematching, as an alternative to maximum-cardinality or 0–1-IP assignment; and (c) evidence (Table I, 10P8E runs) that closed-loop cooperative strategies beat individual-optimal and open-loop baselines. It sits naturally next to Yan et al. [Automatica 2022] (game of kind for heterogeneous 3-D MPME), Yan et al. [T-RO 2020] (analytical barriers + task assignment), Garcia et al. [RA-L 2020] (equal-speed 3-D multiplayer reach-avoid), and Li–Wang–Xie [ACC 2022] (3-D/2-D mixed-dimension PE), and it is a baseline to beat for any learning-based or guaranteed-optimal 3-D multiplayer interception scheme.
Ready-to-adapt citation sentence: "For 3-D heterogeneous reach-avoid games, Gao et al. [P033] derive closed-form Apollonius-sphere value functions and saddle-point strategies for up-to-three-pursuer cooperative subgames against a plane goal, and minimize the total capture time of the resulting multiplayer game through a hierarchical bipartite matching algorithm with real-time state-feedback rematching, empirically outperforming sequential-matching and 0–1 integer programming assignments."
