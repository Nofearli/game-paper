## 2. Research Background
Capture-the-flag (CTF) is a two-target reach-avoid differential game: an attacker must first reach a flag region $\mathcal{F}$ and then a return region $\mathcal{R}$ while a team of faster defenders tries to capture it (Blake et al. 2004; Huang et al. 2011, 2015). Prior CTF analyses either used Hamilton–Jacobi reachability — computationally intensive and restricted to low dimensions (Huang et al. 2011, 2015) — or obtained complete solutions only for degenerate target shapes: point flag and point return (Pachter et al. 2020), point flag and half-plane return (Zhou et al. 2021), or phases treated separately without a complete solution (Garcia et al. 2018). In parallel, single-target reach-avoid games with simple motion and convex targets were shown to have values equal to optima of convex optimization problems (Yan et al. 2022; Lee & Bakolas 2023; Fu & Liu 2023), making the "value = optimization" idea attractive. Applications motivating reach-avoid modeling include UAV path planning, air traffic control, and ship escort (Margellos & Lygeros 2011; Aggarwal & Kumar 2020; Erzberger 2005; Goerlandt et al. 2017). This paper extends the optimization-based approach to the genuinely two-phase, multi-defender CTF game with general convex (not just point/strip/half-plane) target regions.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
**Problem.** In $\mathbb{R}^2$, $n$ defenders with simple motion (2), all strictly faster than the attacker ($v_{Di} > v_A > 0$, speed ratios $\xi_i = v_A/v_{Di}$), each with capture radius $r_{Di} \ge 0$, face one attacker. $\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{R}$ are non-empty closed convex sets represented by signed distance functions $g_{\mathcal{F}}, g_{\mathcal{R}}$ (eq. (1)). The attacker's payoff is $J(\alpha,\beta;X_0) = \inf_{t\in[t_r,t_f)} g_{\mathcal{R}}(x_A(t))$, with $+\infty$ if the attacker never reaches $\mathcal{F}$ (eq. (4)); defenders maximize, so the defenders win the game of kind iff $J > 0$. The paper characterizes the value $V(X)$ over the set $\mathcal{X}_F$ (initial states from which the attacker can reach $\mathcal{F}$ in the single-target game, eq. (37)-analog $g^*_{\mathcal{F}}(X)\le 0$) and gives equilibrium strategies, by recursing on the two game phases: phase 2 (after $\mathcal{F}$ is reached) is a single-target reach-avoid game with value $V^{(2)}$; phase 1 is handled with $V^{(2)}$ as terminal payoff (eqs. (5), (6)).

**Gap, as stated by the authors.** [Authors' claim, p. 1–2]: (i) "We are the first to investigate the multi-defender capture-the-flag game with general convex target regions"; prior CTF work used special/simple target shapes; (ii) HJ reachability "cannot be used for solving problems with multiple defenders due to the curse of dimensionality" (citing Chen et al. 2019, 2018, 2014); (iii) the optimization link of Zhou et al. (2021) "is only valid for game problems with a point flag region and a half-plane return region"; (iv) Garcia et al. ## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Modeling**: first formulation and complete solution of the multi-defender CTF game with general convex flag and return regions in $\mathbb{R}^2$ (Sections 3, 5); two-phase recursion with terminal payoff $J_{\mathcal{F}}$ (6) chaining $V^{(2)}$ into phase 1.
2. **Theory**: (a) the phase-2 value equals the optimum $g^*_{\mathcal{R}}(X)$ of a *convex* problem over the attackers' Apollonius dominance region (Theorem 1); (b) the CTF value equals the optimum $W^*(X)$ of a *quasi-convex* problem (19) (Theorems 11, 12, 14), with a unique minimum whenever $W^*(X) > V^{(2)}(X)$; (c) a defender-redundancy result: some subset of at most two defenders suffices to guarantee the game value (Theorem 15), useful for assigning defenders to multiple attackers.
3. **Algorithm**: an iterative two-step convex-optimization algorithm (Algorithm 1, Section 8) solving the quasi-convex problem with a global-convergence proof (Theorem 16).
4. **Experiment**: two numerical examples with rhombic flag regions visualizing the convex sub-level sets of $W$, the KKT tangency condition, and the monotone value drift along non-equilibrium attacker trajectories.

## 5. Key Mathematical Results

### 5.0 Model, information structure, cost, termination (Section 3, pp. 2–3)
- Dynamics (2): $\dot{x}_A = v_A u_A$, $\dot{x}_{Di} = v_{Di} u_{Di}$, $u_A, u_{Di} \in S^1$ (simple motion); assumption $\forall i:\ v_{Di} > v_A > 0$; $\xi_i := v_A/v_{Di}$.
- Regions (1): $\mathcal{F} = \{x \mid g_{\mathcal{F}}(x) \le 0\}$, $\mathcal{R} = \{x \mid g_{\mathcal{R}}(x) \le 0\}$ with signed distance functions, convex and $C^1$ outside the set (Hiriart-Urruty & Lemaréchal 1996); $\check{g} := \inf_x g_{\mathcal{R}}(x)$ (may be $-\infty$).
- Capture/terminal set (3): $T_C = \{X \mid \min_i d(x_A, x_{Di}) - r_{Di} \le 0\}$; initial set $\mathcal{X}_0 = \mathbb{R}^{2(n+1)} \setminus T_C$; focus on $\mathcal{X}_F \subseteq \mathcal{X}_0$.
- Information: perfect state, feedback (nonanticipative) strategies, $\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{V}_i$ = piecewise-continuous maps $\mathbb{R}^{2(n+1)} \to S^1$; value existence for phase 2 asserted via Friedman (1970) conditions.
- Payoff (4): $J = +\infty$ if $t_r = +\infty$ (captured before flag), else $\inf_{t\in[t_r,t_f)} g_{\mathcal{R}}(x_A(t))$; two-phase recursion via $T_{\mathcal{F}}$ (5) and $J_{\mathcal{F}}$ (6) with terminal payoff $V^{(2)}(X(t_r))$.
- HJI (7)–(8): state-independent Hamiltonian $H = v_A u_A^T \lambda_A + \sum_i v_{Di} u_{Di}^T \lambda_{Di}$; co-state constant along regular arcs ⇒ **regular equilibrium trajectories are straight lines** — the fact the whole optimization approach rests on. Section 4.1 heuristically maps the transversality conditions (9), (11) to the defenders' max problem (10) and the attacker's min problem (12).

### 5.1 Theorem 1 (p. 4, eqs. (13)–(17)) — value of the second phase
**Statement.** The value of the second game phase exists and $V^{(2)}(X) = g^*_{\mathcal{R}}(X)$ for all $X \in \mathcal{X}_0$, where
$$g^*_{\mathcal{R}}(X) = \min_{x \in \bar{\mathcal{A}}(X)} g_{\mathcal{R}}(x) \quad (13),\qquad \mathcal{A}(X) = \{x \mid q_i(x, X_i) < 0,\ \forall i\in[n]\},$$
$$q_i(x, X_i) = d(x, x_A) - \xi_i (d(x, x_{Di}) - r_{Di}).$$
$\bar{\mathcal{A}}(X)$ is the closure of the attacker's (strictly convex, Apollonius-type) dominance region; also $\mathcal{X}_{um} = \{X \mid g^*_{\mathcal{R}}(X) > \check{g}\}$ (14).
**Assumptions.** Simple motion (2), $v_{Di} > v_A$, closed convex $\mathcal{R}$; for the nontrivial branch $X \in \mathcal{X}_{um}$; if $g_{\mathcal{R}}$'s infimum is attained and $g^*_{\mathcal{R}} = \check{g}$ the result is immediate.
**Proof approach.** (1) Attacker side: moving straight toward a minimizer of (13) gets arbitrarily close to it un-captured ⇒ $\inf_\alpha \sup_\beta J \le g^*_{\mathcal{R}}$. (2) Trivial branch $g^*_{\mathcal{R}} = \check{g}$: every play yields $J \ge g^*_{\mathcal{R}}$. (3) Main branch, $g^*_{\mathcal{R}} > 0$: $g_{\mathcal{R}}$ is differentiable at the minimum $x_m$; KKT conditions $\nabla g_{\mathcal{R}}(x_m) + \sum_i \eta_i \partial q_i/\partial x_m = 0$, $\eta_i \ge 0$, complementary slackness hold. (4) Define the family of active subsets $\mathcal{K}_r$ and take a minimum-cardinality $E \in \mathcal{K}_r$; linear independence of $\{\partial q_i/\partial x_m\}_{i\in E}$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$ forces $|E| \le 2$ and strict positivity of the multipliers, hence $q_i(x_m, X_i) = 0$, $i \in E$. (5) Convex sufficiency (Bertsekas 2014): $x_m$ also minimizes $g_{\mathcal{R}}$ over $\bar{\mathcal{A}}(X_E)$. (6) Implicit function theorem: the KKT system defines a $C^1$ map $x_r(X_E') = \arg\min_{\bar{\mathcal{A}}(X_E')} g_{\mathcal{R}}$; differentiating the identities $q_i(x_r, X_i) = 0$ yields the value gradients (15) $\partial V_r^{(2)}/\partial x_A = \sum_{i\in E} \bar\eta_i \langle x_A - x_r\rangle$ and (16) $\partial V_r^{(2)}/\partial x_{Di} = -\bar\eta_i \xi_i \langle x_{Di} - x_r\rangle$. (7) Substituting into the Hamiltonian gives the decisive identity (17): $\max_{u_{Di}} \min_{u_A} H(X_E, U_E, \nabla V_r^{(2)}) = 0$ — defenders in $E$ alone prevent decrease of the value. (8) If $g^*_{\mathcal{R}} \le 0$ and $g_{\mathcal{R}}$ is non-differentiable on $\{g_{\mathcal{R}} \ge g^*_{\mathcal{R}}\}$, thicken the target: $\mathcal{R}_\epsilon = \{x \mid g_{\mathcal{R}}(x) \le g^*_{\mathcal{R}} - \epsilon\}$, signed distance $g_\epsilon$ equal to $g_{\mathcal{R}} - g^*_{\mathcal{R}} + \epsilon$ on $\partial \mathcal{R}_\epsilon$; replicate the argument to get $\sup_\beta \inf_\alpha J \ge g^*_{\mathcal{R}} - \epsilon$, then let $\epsilon \to 0$.
**Decisive relation:** KKT + (15)–(16) ⇒ (17). **Scope:** global value over $\mathcal{X}_0$; analytic characterization, constructive strategies. **Physical meaning:** the phase-2 value is the signed distance from $\mathcal{R}$ of the best point the attacker can still reach inside its dominance region. **Extensibility:** the $|E| \le \dim$ argument is dimension-generic (used again in Thm. 14/15).
**Consequences (pp. 5):** Definition 2 (**Type-I Effective Set**, I-ES): $E$ with $g^*_{\mathcal{R}}(X_E) = g^*_{\mathcal{R}}(X)$ and strict inequality for every non-empty proper subset (printed "E ⊂ N" — typo for $[n]$). Equilibrium strategies: choose an *active* I-ES; attacker and active defenders move straight toward $x_m(X)$: $\alpha^* = \langle x_m - x_A\rangle$, $\beta_i^* = \langle x_m - x_{Di}\rangle$; unused defenders arbitrary; straight-line equilibrium trajectories; strategies continuous. Also $g^*_{\mathcal{F}}(X) = \min_{x\in\bar{\mathcal{A}}(X)} g_{\mathcal{F}}(x)$ and $\mathcal{X}_F = \{X \in \mathcal{X}_0 \mid g^*_{\mathcal{F}}(X) \le 0\}$.

### 5.2 Two-layer optimization (Section 5, pp. 5–6, eqs. (18), (19), (22)–(23))
Defender $i$ can reach any $z_i \in C_i(y, X) = \{x \mid d(x, x_{Di}) \le \xi_i^{-1} d(y, x_A)\}$ by the time the attacker hits terminal point $y$. The worst-case (over defenders' terminal positions $z \in \mathcal{C}(y,X) = \prod_i C_i$) reachable set of the attacker in phase 2 is $\mathcal{A}_I(y, X) = \bigcap_{z \in \mathcal{C}(y,X)} \bar{\mathcal{A}}(y, z)$, and the worst-case payoff is the **inner problem**
$$W(y, X) = \min_{x \in \mathcal{A}_I(y, X)} g_{\mathcal{R}}(x) \quad (18),$$
whose minimizer is $x_m(y, X)$ when unique. The **outer problem** selecting the attacker's terminal position is
$$\min_y W(y, X) \quad \text{s.t. } y \in \bar{\mathcal{A}}(X) \cap \mathcal{F} \quad (19).$$
Candidate strategies: $\alpha(X) = \langle y_m - x_A\rangle$, $\beta_i(X) = \langle x_m(y_m, X) - x_{Di}\rangle$. The dual problem $\max_{z \in \mathcal{C}(y,X)} V^{(2)}(y, z)$ (22) has value $\tilde{W}$, with candidate maximizers $z_{i,m}(y,X) = x_{Di} + \xi_i^{-1} d(y, x_A)\langle x_m - x_{Di}\rangle$ (23).

### 5.3 Lemma 3 (p. 6, eqs. (20)–(21)) — half-space-like representation of $\mathcal{A}_I$
**Statement.** $\mathcal{A}_I(y, X) = \{x \mid h_{I,i}(x, y, X) \le 0,\ \forall i \in [n]\}$ (20) with $h_{I,i}(x,y,X) = d(x,y) + d(y,x_A) - \xi_i(d(x,x_{Di}) - r_{Di})$; if $y \in \mathcal{A}(X)$ and $x \in \partial \mathcal{A}_I$ with $h_{I,i}(x) = 0$, then $\partial h_{I,i}/\partial x = \langle x - y\rangle - \xi_i \langle x - x_{Di}\rangle$ (21) is an outward normal of $\partial \mathcal{A}_I$.
**Proof.** Two inclusions: ($\subseteq$-direction) for $x$ with $h_{I,i} \le 0$ and any $z \in \mathcal{C}$, the triangle inequality $d(x, z_i) \ge d(x, x_{Di}) - d(z_i, x_{Di})$ plus $\xi_i d(z_i, x_{Di}) \le d(y, x_A)$ gives $d(x,y) \le \xi_i(d(x,z_i) - r_{Di})$, so $x \in \bar{\mathcal{A}}(y,z)$ for all $z$; ($\supseteq$-direction) for $x$ violating $h_{I,i} \le 0$, place $z_i$ on the segment $[x, x_{Di}] \cap \partial C_i$ so that $\xi_i(d(x,z_i) + d(z_i,x_{Di}) - r_{Di}) = \xi_i(d(x,x_{Di}) - r_{Di}) < d(x,y) + d(y,x_A)$, whence $x \notin \bar{\mathcal{A}}(y,z)$. Normality: $y \in \mathcal{A}(X)$ implies $d(x,y) > 0$ and $d(x,x_{Di}) > 0$ on $\partial\mathcal{A}_I$, so $h_{I,i}$ is differentiable there. **Tools:** triangle inequality, set algebra. **Role:** since $h_{I,i}$ are convex in $x$, $\mathcal{A}_I$ is an intersection of convex level sets — by Yan et al. (2022) it is strictly convex, so for $(y,X)$ in $\mathcal{W} = \{(y,X) \mid y \in \bar{\mathcal{A}}(X),\ d(x_A,y) > 0,\ W(y,X) > \check{g}\}$ the problem (18) has a unique minimizer $x_m(y,X)$ on $\partial \mathcal{A}_I$; $W$ and $x_m$ are continuous on $\mathcal{W}$.

### 5.4 Corollary 4 (p. 6) — monotonicity toward the attacker (stated without proof)
$W((1-\eta)y + \eta x_A, X) \le W(y, X),\ \forall \eta \in (0,1)$: sliding the attacker's terminal point toward $x_A$ never worsens the worst-case payoff. **proof gap: no proof is printed** (it is used only qualitatively, e.g., in Scenario-one of Section 7.1.1).

### 5.5 Lemma 5 (p. 7, eqs. (24)–(25)) — min–max interchange
**Statement.** For $(y, X) \in \mathcal{W}$, $W(y, X) = \tilde{W}(y, X) = \max_{z\in\mathcal{C}(y,X)} V^{(2)}(y,z)$, and $z_m(y,X)$ of (23) is a maximizer of (22).
**Proof.** (1) $W \ge \tilde{W}$ since $\mathcal{A}_I \subseteq \bar{\mathcal{A}}(y,z)$ for every $z$. (2) KKT for the inner problem (18) at $x_m$: there exist non-positive $\eta_i$ with $\sum_i \eta_i \partial h_{I,i}/\partial x_m \in \partial g_{\mathcal{R}}(x_m)$ and $\eta_i h_{I,i}(x_m) = 0$ (24). (3) Active set $S = \{i \mid \eta_i < 0\}$; for $i \in S$, $h_{I,i}(x_m) = 0$ implies the decisive relation (25) $d(z_{i,m}, x_m) = d(x_{Di}, x_m) - d(x_{Di}, z_{i,m}) = \xi_i^{-1} d(x_m, y) + r_{Di}$, so $x_m \in \partial \mathcal{A}(y, z_{i,m})$ and $\partial h_{I,i}/\partial x_m$ is normal to $\partial \mathcal{A}(y, z_{i,m})$ by (21). (4) Hence (24) certifies $x_m$ as the *unique* minimum of the convex problem $\min_{x \in \cap_{i\in S} \bar{\mathcal{A}}(y, z_{i,m})} g_{\mathcal{R}}(x)$. (5) $\min_{\bar{\mathcal{A}}(y,z_m)} g_{\mathcal{R}} \ge g_{\mathcal{R}}(x_m)$ while $x_m \in \mathcal{A}_I \subseteq \bar{\mathcal{A}}(y, z_m)$ gives equality. **Tools:** KKT for convex programs (Hiriart-Urruty & Lemaréchal 1996). **Meaning:** the defenders' worst case is realized by playing toward $x_m$; inner and outer views coincide.

### 5.6 Definition 6, Lemma 7, Theorem 8, Corollary 9 (p. 7) — uniqueness of the defenders' best response
**Definition 6 (Type-II Effective Set, II-ES):** $E$ with $W(y, X_E) = W(y, X)$ and strict inequality for every non-empty proper subset; $|E| \le 2$ because KKT (24) with linear independence in $\mathbb{R}^2$ would otherwise hold on a proper subset, contradicting minimality (convex sufficiency).
**Lemma 7.** If $\{i\}$ is a II-ES for $(y,X) \in \mathcal{W}$, then $z_{i,m}$ is the unique maximizer of $\max_{z_i \in C_i} V^{(2)}(y, z_i)$. Proof: by (25), any $z \neq z_{i,m}$ in $C_i$ satisfies $\xi_i(d(z, x_m) - r_{Di}) > d(x_m, y)$, i.e., $x_m \in \mathcal{A}(y,z)$ (attacker can pass beyond $x_m$), so $V^{(2)}(y,z) < g_{\mathcal{R}}(x_m) = W(y, X_i)$.
**Theorem 8.** If $E = \{i,j\}$ is a II-ES for $(y,X) \in \mathcal{W}$, then $(z_{i,m}, z_{j,m})$ is the unique maximizer of $\max_{z_E \in C_i \times C_j} V^{(2)}(y, z_E)$. Proof (woven into the preceding text, no separate QED): $x_m \in \partial\mathcal{A}(y,z_{i,m}) \cap \partial\mathcal{A}(y,z_{j,m})$; if defender $j$ deviates to $z_j \neq z_{j,m}$ then $x_m \in \operatorname{int}\mathcal{A}(y,z_j)$, hence $x_m$ is not the minimum of $\min_{x\in\bar{\mathcal{A}}(y,z_{i,m},z_j)} g_{\mathcal{R}}$, so $V^{(2)}(y, z_{i,m}, z_j) < V^{(2)}(y, z_{i,m}, z_{j,m})$. **proof gap (minor):** the step "it is either not a minimum point of the optimization problem $\min_{\bar{\mathcal{A}}(y,z_{i,m},z_j)} g_{\mathcal{R}}$" is compressed — the strict-drop argument when $x_m$ is interior to one constraint set deserves an explicit uniqueness lemma (p. 7).
**Corollary 9.** Parametrizing $z_k = x_{Dk} + \xi_k^{-1} d(y,x_A) e^{\theta_k}$ on $\partial C_k$, the problem $\max_{\theta_i, \theta_j} V^{(2)}(y, z_i, z_j)$ has a unique maximizer $(\theta_{i,m}, \theta_{j,m})$ with $e^{\theta_{k,m}} = \langle z_{i,m} - x_{Di}\rangle$ for $k \in E$ (as printed; index slip $k$ vs $i$ in the final formula).

### 5.7 Lemma 10 (p. 8, eqs. (26)–(28), Fig. 4) — geometry behind quasi-convexity
**Setup (Section 6.2).** For $X \in \mathcal{X}_F$ define sub-level sets $S_c(X) = \{y \in \bar{\mathcal{A}}(X) \mid W(y,X) \le c\}$ (26). Convexity is proved by contradiction: suppose $y_1, y_2$ with $W(y_1) = W(y_2) = c$ but $W > c$ on the open segment $L = [y_1, y_2]$; set $\mathcal{A}_L = \bigcup_{y \in L} \mathcal{A}_I(y, X)$ (27), for which the exact identity $\min_{y \in L} W(y, X) = \min_{x \in \mathcal{A}_L} g_{\mathcal{R}}(x)$ (28) holds (both inclusions argued from $x \in \mathcal{A}_I(y) \Rightarrow W(y) \le g_{\mathcal{R}}(x)$ and conversely a minimizer $x \in \mathcal{A}_L$ lies in some $\mathcal{A}_I(y)$).
**Construction.** Let $x_1, x_3 \in \partial\mathcal{A}(X)$ lie on rays $x_A y_1$, $x_A y_2$; let $x_s$ be the reflection of $x_A$ across $L$ and $x_2, x_4 \in \partial\mathcal{A}(X_s)$ the analogous points; define four closed sets $O_1 \subseteq \mathcal{A}_I(y_1, X)$ (wedge $x_1y_1, y_1x_2$ + boundary arc), $O_2$ (region of $\bar{\mathcal{A}}(X)$ cut off by $x_3y_2, y_2y_1, y_1x_1$), $O_3 \subseteq \mathcal{A}_I(y_2, X)$, $O_4$ (mirror of $O_2$ w.r.t. $X_s$). Degenerate cases: $x_A \in L$ ⇒ $\mathcal{A}_L = \bar{\mathcal{A}}(X)$, $O_1 = O_3 = \emptyset$ (Fig. 5); $x_A$ on the extension of $L$ ⇒ $\mathcal{A}_L = \mathcal{A}_I(y_1, X)$ for the nearer $y_1$.
**Statement.** $\mathcal{A}_L = \bigcup_{k=1}^4 O_k$ and $\mathcal{A}_L$ is strictly convex.
**Proof.** $\bigcup O_k \subseteq \mathcal{A}_L$ from reachability through $L$; by symmetry $\mathcal{A}_L \subseteq \bar{\mathcal{A}}(X) \cap \bar{\mathcal{A}}(X_s)$; the two residual connected components $D_1, D_2$ of $(\bar{\mathcal{A}}(X) \cap \bar{\mathcal{A}}(X_s)) \setminus \bigcup_k O_k$ are unreachable: reaching $D_2$ through $L$ in shortest time requires hitting $y_2$ first, so $D_2 \cap \mathcal{A}_L \subseteq \mathcal{A}_I(y_2, X)$, but $D_2 \cap \mathcal{A}_I(y_2, X) = D_2 \cap O_3 = \emptyset$; likewise $D_1$. $\partial\mathcal{A}_L$ consists of four arcs of strictly convex boundaries, and any supporting line of $\mathcal{A}(X)$ or $\mathcal{A}(X_s)$ at the arc endpoints supports $\mathcal{A}_L$ ⇒ strict convexity. **proof gap (moderate):** the shortest-time claim "to reach a point in $D_2$ after passing through $L$ ... the attacker should first reach the position $y_2$" and the endpoint supporting-line argument are asserted with reference to Fig. 4 rather than proven analytically (p. 8).

### 5.8 Theorem 11 (p. 9) — quasi-convexity of $W$ (central structural result)
**Statement.** $W(\cdot, X)$ is quasi-convex on $\bar{\mathcal{A}}(X)$. Moreover, for distinct $y_1, y_2$ with $W(y_1, X) = W(y_2, X)$: (1) if $x_A = (1-\eta_a) y_1 + \eta_a y_2$ with $\eta_a \ge 1$ or $\eta_a \le 0$ (attacker collinear with the segment, outside it), then $W((1-\eta)y_1 + \eta y_2, X) = W(y_1, X) = V^{(2)}(X)$ for all $\eta \in (0,1)$; (2) otherwise $W((1-\eta)y_1 + \eta y_2, X) < W(y_1, X)$ for all $\eta \in (0,1)$.
**Proof approach.** (1) For $c > \check{g}$, take the contradiction setup of 5.7; by (28) and Lemma 10 the minimizer $x^*$ of $\min_{\mathcal{A}_L} g_{\mathcal{R}}$ is unique on $\partial\mathcal{A}_L$; associate $y^* \in L$: $y^* = y_1$ if $x^* \in \partial O_1$, the crossing $L \cap x_A x^*$ if $x^* \in \partial O_2$, $y_2$ if $x^* \in \partial O_3$, $L \cap x_s x^*$ if $x^* \in \partial O_4$. (2) *Non-collinear* case: $y^*$ is the unique minimizer on $L$ of $d(x_A, y) + d(x^*, y)$ (ellipse/reflection principle — the broken path through $L$ is shortest at the straight crossing), so $x^* \in \partial\mathcal{A}_I(y^*, X)$ and $x^* \notin \mathcal{A}_I(y, X)$ for $y \neq y^*$; hence $W(y, X) > g_{\mathcal{R}}(x^*) = W(y^*, X)$ — contradicting multiple minimizers of $\min_L W$. (3) *Internal collinear* ($x_A$ in the relative interior of $L$): $x_s = x_A$, $x_1 = x_2$, $x_3 = x_4$, $\mathcal{A}_L = O_2 \cup O_4 = \bar{\mathcal{A}}(X)$; $y^* = x_A$ is the unique minimizer. (4) *External collinear*: $\mathcal{A}_L = \mathcal{A}_I(y_1, X)$; $x^* = x_1$ forces $W \equiv c$ on all of $L$, contradicting the strict-over-interior hypothesis, and the KKT argument of Lemma 5 gives $W(y_1, X) = \min_{\bar{\mathcal{A}}(X)} g_{\mathcal{R}} = V^{(2)}(X)$. (5) $c = \check{g}$: $S_{\check{g}}(X)$ convex by a limiting argument $\big((1-\eta)y_1 + \eta y_2 \in S_{\check{g}+\epsilon}(X)\ \forall \epsilon > 0\big)$. Consequently $S_c(X)$ is convex for every $c$, i.e., $W$ is quasi-convex; the strict/flat dichotomy follows by re-running the three cases. **Decisive inequality chain:** (28) + strict convexity of $\mathcal{A}_L$ (Lemma 10) + the path-length minimizer uniqueness. **Consequences:** (19) is a *quasi-convex* program; every local minimum is global; $W$ is flat along a segment only at level $V^{(2)}(X)$.

### 5.9 Theorem 12 (p. 9) — uniqueness of the attacker's optimal terminal position
**Statement.** Assume $\bar{\mathcal{A}}(X) \cap \mathcal{F} \neq \emptyset$. The problem (19) has a unique minimum point if its optimal value is larger than $V^{(2)}(X)$.
**Proof.** If $y_1 \neq y_2$ both minimize with value $c = W(y_1, X)$, Theorem 11 forces the external-collinear case (otherwise the interior of $y_1y_2$ would be strictly cheaper), which implies $V^{(2)}(X) = c$ — contradicting $c > V^{(2)}(X)$. **Meaning:** whenever the flag matters (the attacker cannot guarantee the phase-2 value directly), its optimal flag-crossing point is unique and the equilibrium is well-defined.

### 5.10 Lemma 13 (p. 9, eqs. (29)–(31)) — Scenario one: $y_m \in \mathcal{A}(X)$
**Setting (Section 7).** $W^*(X) = \min_{y \in \bar{\mathcal{A}}(X) \cap \mathcal{F}} W(y, X)$; the target identity is $V(X) = W^*(X)$. If $W^*(X) = V^{(2)}(X)$, attacker and defenders can each guarantee $V^{(2)}(X)$ (defenders by the phase-2 equilibrium), so $V = V^{(2)} = W^*$. Assume henceforth $W^*(X) > V^{(2)}(X)$ (unique $y_m$) and first $W^* > 0$. If $y_m \in \mathcal{A}(X)$ then necessarily $y_m \in \mathcal{A}(X) \cap \partial\mathcal{F}$ (interior points of $\mathcal{F}$ on segment $x_A y_m$ would be cheaper by Corollary 4). Let $E$ be a II-ES for $(y_m, X)$, $p(y, X_E, \theta_E) = V^{(2)}(y, z_E)$.
**Statement.** $W^*(X_E) = W^*(X)$, and the KKT condition of (19) is $\langle x_A - y_m\rangle + \langle x_m - y_m\rangle \in K_{\mathcal{F}}(y_m)$ (29), where $K_{\mathcal{F}}(y) = \{\eta v \mid v \in \partial g_{\mathcal{F}}(y),\ \eta \in [0, +\infty)\}$ (as printed with "$k$" — typo for $\eta$).
**Proof.** (1) By Lemma 7/Corollary 9 the maximizing angles are unique, so Danskin's theorem (Danskin 2012) gives $\partial W/\partial y = \sum_{k \in E} \eta_k (\langle y - x_A\rangle + \langle y - x_m\rangle)$. (2) If $W^*(X_E) < W(y_m, X_E)$: quasi-convexity of $W$ + convexity of $\mathcal{F}$ yield $y \in \mathcal{F}$ with $\langle y - y_m\rangle^T \partial W/\partial y < 0$, hence some $y' \in \mathcal{F}$ with $d(y' - x_A) + d(y' - x_m) < d(y_m - x_A) + d(y_m - x_m)$, putting $x_m$ in the interior of $\mathcal{A}_I(y', X)$, so $W(y', X) < g_{\mathcal{R}}(x_m) = W^*(X)$ — contradiction. (3) Danskin again (minimizing $y_m$ over the state-independent set $\mathcal{Y}_F = \mathcal{Y} \cap \partial\mathcal{F}$) for the state gradients, combined with (15)–(16) and $e^{\theta_{i,m}} = \langle z_{i,m} - x_{Di}\rangle$: (30) $\partial W^*/\partial x_A = \left(\sum_{k\in E} \eta_k\right) \langle x_A - y_m\rangle$ and (31) $\partial W^*/\partial x_{Di} = \eta_i \xi_i \langle x_m - x_{Di}\rangle$, $i \in E$, with $\eta_i > 0$ from $\nabla g_{\mathcal{R}}(x_m) + \sum_i \eta_i \partial q_i/\partial x_m = 0$. (4) Consequently $\max_{u_{Di}} \min_{u_A} H(X_E, U_E, \nabla W^*) = 0$: the effective defenders can prevent decrease of $W^*$. **Tools:** Danskin's theorem (twice), implicit function theorem, KKT. **Decisive identities:** (30)–(31).

### 5.11 Scenario two (pp. 10–11, eqs. (32)–(37)) — $y_m \in \partial\mathcal{A}(X)$
Two degenerate sub-cases are dispatched first: a neighborhood with $\mathcal{Y} \cap \bar{\mathcal{A}} \cap \mathcal{F} = \mathcal{Y} \cap \bar{\mathcal{A}}$ gives $W^* = g_{\mathcal{R}}(y_m) = V^{(2)}(X)$; one with $\mathcal{Y} \cap \bar{\mathcal{A}} \cap \mathcal{F} = \mathcal{Y} \cap \mathcal{F}$ reduces to (30)–(31) with $x_m = y_m$. General case: $\partial\mathcal{A}(X)$ is piecewise smooth, each smooth arc generated by one defender; let $i$ be the defender whose arc contains $y_m$ ($q_i(y_m, X_i) = 0$); then $y_m(X_i) = y_m(X)$ and $W^*(X_i) = W^*(X)$. Because $d(y_m, z_{i,m}) = r_{Di}$ and $x_m = y_m$, the KKT condition is taken in the limit: (32) $\nabla g_{\mathcal{R}}(y_m) + \eta_i (e^\phi - \xi_i \langle y_m - x_{Di}\rangle) = 0$; since $g_{\mathcal{R}}$ is a distance function ($\|\nabla g_{\mathcal{R}}\| = 1$ outside $\mathcal{R}$), the multiplier is forced to (33) $\eta_i = \sqrt{1 + \xi_i^2 - 2\xi_i \cos(\theta_i - \phi)}^{-1}$ (printed as a radical with exponent $-1$; verified from the PDF rendering), which has a unique solution $\phi_m$; the $y$-gradient has the limit (34) $\partial W/\partial y = \eta_i(\langle y_m - x_A\rangle - e^{\phi_m}) = \nabla g_{\mathcal{R}}(y_m) + \eta_i \partial q_i/\partial y$. The outer KKT condition reads (35) $\partial W/\partial y + \eta_a \partial q_i/\partial y + \eta_f \nabla g_{\mathcal{F}}(y_m) = 0$, $\eta_a, \eta_f \ge 0$; $\eta_f = 0$ would imply $g_{\mathcal{R}}(y_m) = V^{(2)}(X_i) \le V^{(2)}(X)$, contradicting $W^*(X) > V^{(2)}(X)$, so $\eta_f > 0$. If $\eta_a > 0$, both constraints $g_{\mathcal{F}} = 0$ and $q_i = 0$ are active; the implicit function theorem makes $y_m(X_i)$ differentiable with $\partial y_m^T/\partial X_i\, \nabla g_{\mathcal{F}}(y_m) = 0$ and $\partial y_m^T/\partial X_i\, \partial q_i/\partial y + \partial q_i/\partial X_i = 0$; chaining with (15), (16), (35) yields (36) $\partial W^*/\partial x_A = (\eta_i + \eta_a)\langle x_A - y_m\rangle$ and (37) $\partial W^*/\partial x_{Di} = (\eta_i + \eta_a) \xi_i \langle y_m - x_{Di}\rangle$. If $\eta_a = 0$, (29) and (35) coincide and (30)–(31) apply verbatim. In all cases $\max \min H = 0$ ⇒ defenders prevent decrease. Non-smooth $\partial\mathcal{A}(X)$ at $y_m$: claimed to be a zero-measure sub-manifold (**proof gap: asserted, no proof, p. 11**); by continuity $W^*(X) = \max_{i \in S} W^*(X_i)$ with $S = \{i \mid q_i(y_m, X_i) = 0\}$, so the same defender strategies protect $W^*$. Non-differentiable $g_{\mathcal{F}}$ at $y_m$: enlarge $\mathcal{F}$ locally without changing $W^*(X)$ (Fig. 6) and reuse the analysis.

### 5.12 Theorem 14 (p. 11) — main result: value of the CTF game
**Statement.** The value of the capture-the-flag game with terminal set (3) and terminal payoff (4) exists and $V(X) = W^*(X)$ for all $X \in \mathcal{X}_F$.
**Proof.** Case $W^* = V^{(2)}$: done above. Case $W^* > V^{(2)}$ and $W^* > 0$: the defenders' strategy keeps $W^*$ non-decreasing while the attacker guarantees $W^*(X)$ by heading to $y_m$ and then playing the phase-2 equilibrium; hence $\sup_\beta \inf_\alpha J_{\mathcal{F}} \ge W^* \ge \inf_\alpha \sup_\beta J_{\mathcal{F}}$, so the value exists and equals $W^*$. Case $W^* \le 0$: $g_{\mathcal{R}}$ may be non-differentiable at $x_m$; the KKT condition then involves $\partial g_{\mathcal{R}}$ (subdifferential); replicating the $\epsilon$-thickening technique of Theorem 1's proof gives $\sup_\beta \inf_\alpha J_{\mathcal{F}} \ge W^* - \epsilon$ for all small $\epsilon$. **Scope:** global, over $\mathcal{X}_F$; analytic value, quasi-convex program (19) as the computational object. **Meaning:** the two-phase game with $n$ defenders and arbitrary convex regions collapses to one quasi-convex optimization problem plus one convex problem per evaluation.

### 5.13 Theorem 15 (p. 11) — two-defender sufficiency (assignment theorem)
**Statement.** For $n \ge 2$ and $X \in \mathcal{X}_F$, there exists a subset $E \subset [n]$ with $|E| \le 2$ such that $V(X_E) = V(X)$.
**Proof.** If $y_m \in \mathcal{A}(X)$: a II-ES for $(y_m, X)$ satisfies $W^*(X_E) = W^*(X)$ (Lemma 13) and $|E| \le 2$; if $y_m \in \partial\mathcal{A}(X)$: a single defender $i$ with $q_i(y_m, X_i) = 0$ has $W^*(X_i) = W^*(X)$. **Context:** stated (printed word "generation", evidently "generalization") as the CTF counterpart of the dimension-bound effective-set results of Deng et al. (2023), Yan et al. (2022, 2020) for single-target games. **Physical meaning:** for one attacker, at most two defenders matter; all others are redundant — the key enabler for matching-based multi-attacker/multi-defender assignment (as in Wang et al. 2023; Yan et al. 2022).

### 5.14 Equilibrium strategies (Section 7.2, eq. (38))
Attacker: $\alpha^*(X) = \langle y_m(X) - x_A\rangle$ (feedback form of the open-loop plan "go to $y_m$, then play phase-2 equilibrium"), unique when $W^* > V^{(2)}$. Defenders (maximin controls of the Hamiltonian, from (30), (31), (36), (37)): $\beta_i^*(X) = \langle x_m(y_m, X) - x_{Di}\rangle$ (38), where $x_m$ solves the convex problem (18) at $y = y_m$. Under (38) all equilibrium trajectories are piecewise straight (one turn at the flag).

### 5.15 Theorem 16 (pp. 11–12) — convergence of Algorithm 1
**Algorithm 1 (p. 11).** Initialize $x_0 \in \partial\mathcal{F} \cap \mathcal{A}(X)$; at iteration $k$: Step 1 — find $y_k \in \partial\mathcal{F}$ minimizing $d(x_A, y) + d(y, x_{k-1})$ over $y \in \mathcal{F}$ (a convex problem; solvable analytically for polygonal $\mathcal{F}$); Step 2 — find $x_k$ with $g_{\mathcal{R}}(x_k) = \min_{x \in \mathcal{A}_I(y_k, X)} g_{\mathcal{R}}(x)$ (convex problem (18)); stop when $g_{\mathcal{R}}(x_k) = \check{g}$, or $d(x_k, \partial\mathcal{A}(X)) \le \epsilon_{th,1}$, or $d(\langle x_A - y_k\rangle + \langle x_k - y_k\rangle, K_{\mathcal{F}}(y_k)) \le \epsilon_{th,2}$ (the last is the KKT test (29)).
**Statement.** With both subproblems solved exactly, $\lim_{k\to\infty} W(y_k, X) = W^*(X)$; and if $W^*(X) > V^{(2)}(X)$, then $y_k \to y_m(X)$.
**Proof.** (1) Each termination condition implies $W(y_k) = W^*$: $g_{\mathcal{R}}(x_k) = \check{g}$ (global lower bound); $x_k \in \partial\mathcal{A}(X)$ — then KKT (24) coincides with the optimality condition of (13), so $W(y_k) = V^{(2)}(X)$; and $\langle x_A - y_k\rangle + \langle x_k - y_k\rangle \in K_{\mathcal{F}}(y_k)$ is exactly (29). (2) Monotone descent: Step 1 gives $d(x_A, y_{k+1}) + d(y_{k+1}, x_k) \le d(x_A, y_k) + d(y_k, x_k)$, so $x_k \in \mathcal{A}_I(y_{k+1}, X)$ and $W(y_{k+1}) \le g_{\mathcal{R}}(x_k) = W(y_k)$. (3) Strict descent unless optimal: if $W^* < W(y_k)$, none of (1) holds, so (29) fails and the inequality in (2) is strict, placing $x_k$ in the interior of $\mathcal{A}_I(y_{k+1}, X)$: $W(y_{k+1}) < W(y_k)$ (printed "$W(y_k, x_k)$" — typo for $W(y_k, X)$). (4) Convergence of the decreasing sequence to $W^*$; uniqueness of $y_m$ (Theorem 12) upgrades value convergence to iterate convergence. **Tools:** KKT equivalence, monotone convergence. **Local/global:** global convergence of the value; the iterate limit is the unique global minimizer.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
**Author-stated (p. 12):** extension to reach-avoid games with more than two target regions; defenders slower than the attacker; non-convex target regions — the latter two "may require ... singular surfaces."
**Critical observations (with loci):**
1. **Simple motion only; 2-D only.** Dynamics (2) and the strict-convexity lemmas (Yan et al. 2022) are planar; the dimension bound $|E| \le 2$ (Theorems 8, 15) generalizes to $|E| \le \dim$ but is not stated for $\mathbb{R}^n$ (contrast Lee & Bakolas 2023, titled for $\mathbb{R}^n$).
2. **All defenders must be strictly faster** ($v_{Di} > v_A$, p. 3); slower or equal-speed defenders are excluded, so the classical slower-defender barrier literature is not covered.
3. **Flat regions of $W$.** Theorem 11(1): when $x_A$ is externally collinear with two equal-value points, $W \equiv V^{(2)}$ on a whole segment — the quasi-convex problem is not strictly quasi-convex at its bottom level; $y_m$ and hence the attacker's strategy are non-unique exactly when the flag is "irrelevant" ($W^* = V^{(2)}$). Algorithm 1's iterate convergence ($y_k \to y_m$) is guaranteed only for $W^* > V^{(2)}$ (Theorem 16).
4. **Unproven/soft steps:** Corollary 4 stated without proof (p. 6); the zero-measure claim for non-smooth $\partial\mathcal{A}$ at $y_m$ asserted without proof (p. 11); Lemma 10's component-exclusion and endpoint supporting-line arguments lean on Fig. 4 (p. 8); Theorem 8's uniqueness step is compressed (p. 7); value existence for phase 1 is obtained constructively rather than via a general existence theorem (only phase 2 invokes Friedman 1970, p. 3).
5. **Typographical issues noted:** Definition 2 prints $E \subset N$ for $[n]$ (p. 5); $K_{\mathcal{F}}(y) = \{\eta v \mid v \in \partial g_{\mathcal{F}}(y),\ k \in [0,+\infty)\}$ uses $k$ for $\eta$ (p. 10, eq. (29) context); "$W(y_k, x_k)$" for $W(y_k, X)$ in Theorem 16's proof (p. 12); "generation" for "generalization" before Theorem 15 (p. 11); "$g^*(X) = 1.55$" for $W^*(X)$ (p. 12); eq. (33) is a reciprocal radical (exponent $-1$ flattened in text extraction).
6. **No runtime/complexity analysis.** Equilibrium strategy (38) requires solving a convex program per evaluation; the paper reports neither computation times, iteration counts, nor tolerance sensitivity, so online applicability is unquantified. Subproblem exactness is assumed in Theorem 16 (inexact solvers unanalyzed).
7. **No capture-radius-0 or single-defender edge discussion in examples** — Example 2 uses one defender, but no comparison of one- vs two-defender values (the redundancy theorem is not numerically exercised).
8. **Information assumptions:** perfect state information, piecewise-continuous feedback; no delayed/noisy observation variants (contrast Fang et al. 2024/2025 asymmetric-observation reach-avoid work).

## 8. Citation Value
**Role.** This is the terminal point of the optimization-based CTF line (Garcia et al. 2018 → Pachter et al. 2020 → Zhou et al. 2021 → here) and the first complete solution of a multi-defender CTF game with general convex target regions; its two reusable assets are (i) the reduction "two-phase game value = quasi-convex program (19) with inner convex program (18)", including the Danskin-based state-gradient machinery ((30), (31), (36), (37)) that certifies equilibrium via the Isaacs condition, and (ii) the two-defender sufficiency theorem (Theorem 15), which justifies greedy/matching assignment of at most two defenders per attacker in large-scale scenarios. It also transfers the effective-set/dimension-bound technology of Yan et al. (2020, 2022) and Deng et al. (2023) from single-target to two-target games.
**Ready-to-adapt citation sentences.**
- "Deng, Shi and Zhong [P021] proved that the value of a multi-defender capture-the-flag game with convex flag and return regions equals the optimal value of a quasi-convex optimization problem, solvable by a globally convergent iterative algorithm, and that a subset of at most two defenders suffices to secure the game value."
- "The two-defender sufficiency result for capture-the-flag games [P021, Thm. 15] extends the dimension-bound effective-set property of single-target reach-avoid games to two-target settings and underpins defender-to-attacker assignment in multi-intruder scenarios."
