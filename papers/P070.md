## 2. Research Background

Pursuit–evasion games (PEGs) model scenarios from missile guidance to collision avoidance and multirobot cooperation. The single-pursuer single-evader case traces to Isaacs [19] and is solvable via Hamilton–Jacobi–Isaacs (HJI) equations with the pure pursuit / pure evasion pair as Nash strategies; multiplayer PEGs, however, make the HJI equations and the terminal manifold intractable, motivating structured suboptimal schemes. One such scheme is **relay pursuit** (Bakolas & Tsiotras [3], Automatica 2012, via dynamically changing Voronoi diagrams): only one pursuer is active at each instant while the others stay static — prudent for guarding applications (guards stay near their posts against decoys) and for fuel economy. Follow-ups include sequential pursuit under external disturbances via Zermelo–Voronoi diagrams [27], distributed relay control for general linear dynamics via AREs [23], and evader-side feedback strategies against relay pursuers [7]. The authors observe that in all existing relay schemes the active pursuer only shortens *its own* distance to the evader, so the capture potential of the $N-1$ **static** pursuers is never exploited — leaving capture time on the table. This paper builds a relay scheme in which the active pursuer may deliberately *drive the evader toward a static pursuer*, switching both the active pursuer and the pursuit strategy according to a state-dependent partition of the plane.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap

**Problem [authors' formulation, pp. 1959–1960]**: a planar PEG with $N$ pursuers $P_i$, $i\in I:=\{1,\dots,N\}$ and one evader $E$, all with single-integrator dynamics (1): $\dot x_{p_i}=v_p u_{p_i}$, $\dot x_e=v_e u_e$, $u_{p_i},u_e\in\mathcal U:=\{u\in\mathbb R^2:\|u\|\le 1\}$. Under **Assumption 1** the evader plays pure evasion w.r.t. the current active pursuer, $u_e=u_P(x_e,x_{p_\sigma})$ (Definition 3). Relay structure per **Definition 4**, eq. (4): $u_{p_i}=\mathsf U$ if $i=\sigma(t)$, else $0$, with assignment $\sigma:[0,\infty)\to I$. **Problem 1**: find $\mathsf U$ and $\sigma$ such that (i) capture is achieved — eq. (2) literally reads $\sum_{i=1}^N\|x_{p_i}(t_f)-x_e(t_f)\|=0$ — and (ii) $J=t_f$ is minimized. Notably **no assumption $v_p>v_e$ is made** (Remark 1, p. 1960), so slower pursuer cases are on the table.

**Gap [authors' claim, p. 1958–1959]**: "in the existing researches of relay pursuit scheme, the pursuer selected to be active only tries to shorten the distance between the evader and the active pursuer itself. The choice of achieving the capture by shortening the distances between the evader and the other static pursuers has been neglected... the capture time can be further decreased."

**Verified from the cited corpus**: [verified] the relay-pursuit references the paper positions against indeed fit the description: [3] (Bakolas & Tsiotras, relay pursuit via dynamic Voronoi diagrams) assigns the closest-Voronoi-cell pursuer doing classic pure pursuit; [23] (Du et al., Automatica 2017) is cooperative relaying with ARE-designed gains for stability, again chasing the evader directly; [27] (Sun & Tsiotras, Automatica 2017) is sequential pursuit of *multiple targets*, not steering one evader toward static co-pursuers. [verified] The "quickest descent control" tool is credited to Selvakumar & Bakolas [7] (IEEE TCYB 2021, feedback strategies for a reach–avoid game), where switching between control laws in different state-space regions was used on the *evader* side; P070 transplants the idea to the pursuer side as the "round pursuit" law. [authors' claim — plausible, unproven globally] the claim that a shorter capture time also means lower fuel consumption for constant-input pursuers is asserted (p. 1958) but not modeled (fuel is never formulated). The three stated technical challenges (p. 1959): (1) design a pursuit law that shortens the evader–*static* pursuer distance fastest; (2) choose active pursuer + strategy to decrease capture time; (3) extend from two pursuers to many.

## 4. Core Contributions

1. **Theory — round pursuit strategy (Definition 5, eqs. (7)–(8); Proposition 1)**: a quickest-descent control law $u_R=\lambda/\|\lambda\|$ with $\lambda=r_1\cos\theta/\|r_1\|+r_2/\|r_2\|$ that minimizes the second derivative of $\|r_2(t)\|$ (evader–static-pursuer distance) at every instant; geometrically, the active pursuer $P_1$ moves *perpendicular* to its line of sight ("round" $E$) until $P_1,E,P_2$ are co-linear ($\theta=0$), then chases head-on so that the **static** $P_2$ makes the capture (Remark 3, p. 1961).
2. **Theory — capture guarantee (Proposition 2, eq. (16))**: a closed-form sufficient condition on the initial geometry, $|\theta_0|<\frac{v_p}{v_e}\big(\frac{c_1+c_2}{c_2}\big)\ln\!\big(\frac{c_1+c_2}{c_1}\big)-\frac{v_p}{v_e}$, under which the round pursuit strategy forces $\theta\to 0$ and hence capture — valid even for $v_p\le v_e$.
3. **Theory — Apollonius-like circle (Lemma 2, eq. (42))**: a new circular locus $\|r_1\|\cos\theta/\|r_2\|=k_2$ (circle through $P_2$ with center on $P_1P_2$), analogous to the classical Apollonius circle (Lemma 1 [19]), used to delimit the dominance region of the round strategy.
4. **Theory — region-based switching and time superiority (Propositions 3–7, Algorithm 1)**: the plane is partitioned by a Voronoi bisector $l$, two circular dominance regions $O_1$ (pure pursuit better) and $O_2$ (round pursuit better), and a lens $A=O_a\cap O_b$ (switching beneficial); **Proposition 7** proves the region-based relay capture time is no worse than the classic relay scheme with hysteresis: $\phi_r\le\phi_p$ (eq. (76)).
5. **Modeling/algorithm — multipursuer extension (Propositions 8–10, Algorithm 2)**: the $N$-pursuer game is decomposed into $N-1$ two-pursuer games against the active $P_i$; dominance cells $V^i$, $O^i$, $B^{ij}$ are built from intersections of half-planes, Apollonius-like disks and pairwise tie-curves $s^k_{ij}$, yielding a complete assignment + strategy rule (Algorithm 2 reusing Algorithm 1).
6. **Experiment**: two numerical case studies ($v_p=1.1>v_e=1$ and $v_p=0.9\le v_e=1$) with $N=2$ and $N=4$, plus classic-relay-pursuit contrasts (Figs. 5–6), showing capture-time improvements (20.8 s vs 23.94 s at $N=2$; 8.3 s vs 23.94 s at $N=4$; and capture achieved at $N=4$ with $v_p<v_e$ where the classic scheme fails).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results (exhaustive)

### 5.0 Model, information structure, termination (pp. 1959–1960)

- **Dynamics (1)**: $\dot x_{p_i}(t)=v_p u_{p_i}(t)$, $\dot x_e(t)=v_e u_e(t)$, all controls in $\mathcal U=\{u\in\mathbb R^2:\|u\|\le1\}$; $v_p,v_e$ are maximum speeds. Pure kinematics (single integrators).
- **Information structure**: pursuers know all positions ($u_P$, $u_R$ depend on $x_e,x_{p_\sigma},x_{p_\tau}$); the evader is assumed to know the *active* pursuer's position (Remark 1, citing [3]) and plays $u_e=u_P(x_e,x_{p_\sigma})$ (Assumption 1). Static pursuers' positions are *not* used by the evader — the key behavioral asymmetry the scheme exploits. No communication/graph constraints, no delays.
- **Capture (Definition 1, eq. (2))**: $\sum_{i=1}^N\|x_{p_i}(t_f)-x_e(t_f)\|=0$. *Inconsistency to flag*: as written this demands **every** pursuer coincide with $E$; but the analysis on p. 1960 terminates when "E is captured by P1 **or** P2, i.e., $\|r_1(t_f)\|=0$ or $\|r_2(t_f)\|=0$". The intended meaning is clearly any-pursuer capture; eq. (2) is a mis-formalization (see §7).
- **Cost**: $J(u_{p},u_e)=t_f$ (p. 1959); pursuers minimize, evader maximizes (delay/avoid capture).
- **Strategies (Definitions 2–3, eq. (3))**: pure pursuit $u_P(x_e,x_{p_1})=\frac{x_e-x_{p_1}}{\|x_e-x_{p_1}\|}$; pure evasion $u_e=u_P(x_e,x_{p_1})$ (directly away from the active pursuer). For $N=1$ these are the Nash strategies of the HJI zero-sum game (cited to [5]); for $N>1$ HJI is intractable, hence relay pursuit (Definition 4, eq. (4)).

### 5.1 Round pursuit strategy — Proposition 1 (pp. 1960–1961)

**Setting**: $N=2$ (Assumption 2: $P_1$ closer to $E$ and exposed first, hence active; $P_2$ static). Dynamics (5): $\dot x_{p_1}=v_p u_{p_1}$, $\dot x_{p_2}=0$, $\dot x_e=v_e u_P(x_e,x_{p_1})$. Notation (Fig. 1): $r_1=x_e-x_{p_1}$, $r_2=x_e-x_{p_2}$, $\theta$ = angle between $r_1$ and $-r_2$. **Design Objective 1**: design $u_{p_1}$ so that $\|r_2(t_f)\|=0$ (capture *by the static pursuer*) in minimum time. **Remark 2** concedes the exact fixed-terminal-state, free-terminal-time optimal control problem is "analytically intractable" and numerically expensive; instead a **quickest descent problem** is posed.

- **Problem 2 (eq. (6))**: find $u^*_{p_1}(t)$ minimizing $J_t(u_{p_1}):=\frac{d^2}{dt^2}\|r_2(t)\|$ at each $t\in(0,t_f)$ (myopic/greedy criterion).
- **Definition 5, eqs. (7)–(8)**: the **round pursuit strategy** $u_R(x_e,x_{p_1},x_{p_2})=\lambda/\|\lambda\|$ if $\|\lambda\|\ne0$, else $r_1/\|r_1\|$, with
$$\lambda(x_e,x_{p_1},x_{p_2})=\frac{r_1\cos\theta}{\|r_1\|}+\frac{r_2}{\|r_2\|}.$$
- **Proposition 1**: $u^*_{p_1}=u_R(x_e,x_{p_1},x_{p_2})$ solves Problem 2. *Assumptions*: dynamics (5), Assumption 1 (pure evasion), $\|u_{p_1}\|\le1$.
- **Proof approach (verified by re-derivation)**: (i) since $P_2$ is static and $E$ flees directly away from $P_1$, $\dot r_2=v_e r_1/\|r_1\|$ (9); (ii) hence $\frac{d}{dt}\|r_2\|=\frac{v_e r_1^Tr_2}{\|r_1\|\|r_2\|}=-v_e\cos\theta$ (10), using $\cos\theta=-\frac{r_1^Tr_2}{\|r_1\|\|r_2\|}$ (17); (iii) differentiating again, $\frac{d^2}{dt^2}\|r_2\|=\frac{b_1+b_2\lambda^Tu_{p_1}}{\|r_1\|\|r_2\|}$ (11) with $b_1=v_e^2\sin^2\theta\|r_1\|$, $b_2=-v_pv_e\|r_2\|$ (12) — *decisive*: $J_t$ is **linear in $u_{p_1}$** with negative coefficient, so the minimizer maximizes $\lambda^Tu_{p_1}$ over the unit disk, giving $u_{p_1}=\lambda/\|\lambda\|$ (13); (iv) degenerate case $\theta=0$ ($\|\lambda\|=0$): $J_t\equiv 0$ regardless of $u_{p_1}$, so the third derivative is minimized instead — eq. (14): $\dot J_t=\frac{d^3}{dt^3}\|r_2\|=\frac{1}{v_e}\big(\frac{b_2}{\|r_1\|\|r_2\|}\big)^2(\chi^Tu_{p_1})^2\ge0$, where $\chi$ is the unit vector perpendicular to $r_1$ ($=\lambda/\|\lambda\|$ when $\theta\ne0$); the minimum iff $\chi^Tu_{p_1}=0$, i.e. $u_{p_1}=\pm r_1/\|r_1\|$, and the sign toward $E$ (15) maintains the co-linear state. I independently confirmed (14) by a small-$\theta$ expansion: $\frac{d^2}{dt^2}\|r_2\|=v_e\sin\theta\,\dot\theta$, $\dot\theta\approx\frac{b_2}{v_e\|r_1\|\|r_2\|}\chi^Tu_{p_1}$ as $\theta\to0$. ✔
- **Two structural identities drive everything** (my verification, consistent with Remark 3): $\lambda^T r_1=0$ (since $r_1^Tr_2=-\|r_1\|\|r_2\|\cos\theta$), so $u_R\perp r_1$ whenever $\|\lambda\|\ne0$; and $\|\lambda\|=|\sin\theta|$. Physical meaning: the active pursuer *orbits* the evader at radius increasing at full evader speed, rotating the evader's escape ray until it points at the static pursuer ($\theta=0$, co-linear state), after which $\|r_2\|$ decreases at the full rate $v_e$ until $P_2$ captures. Local-in-time (greedy) optimality; analytic, closed form; no HJI solved.
- **Extensibility**: the law needs only relative positions of three agents; reused verbatim in the $N$-pursuer case as $u_R(x_e,x_{p_\sigma},x_{p_\tau})$.

### 5.2 Capture guarantee — Proposition 2 (pp. 1961–1962)

- **Proposition 2**: with $P_1$ adopting $u_R$ (7), capture is achieved provided the initial condition satisfies the "easy-to-check" inequality (16):
$$|\theta_0|<\frac{v_p}{v_e}\Big(\frac{c_1+c_2}{c_2}\Big)\ln\Big(\frac{c_1+c_2}{c_1}\Big)-\frac{v_p}{v_e},$$
where $c_1=\|r_1(t_0)\|$, $c_2=\|r_2(t_0)\|$, $\theta_0=\theta(t_0)$. *Assumptions*: dynamics (5), $u_R$ adopted, $\theta_0\ne0$ handled symmetrically for either sign.
- **Proof approach**: (i) general rate from (18): $\dot\theta=\frac{b_1+b_2\lambda^Tu_{p_1}}{v_e\sin\theta\|r_1\|\|r_2\|}$, so under $u_R$, $\dot\theta=\frac{v_e}{\|r_2\|}\sin\theta-\frac{v_p}{\|r_1\|}$ (19) [verified by direct angular-rate computation ✔]; (ii) under $u_R$, $\dot r_1=v_e r_1/\|r_1\|-v_p\lambda/\|\lambda\|$ (20) and, because $\lambda\perp r_1$, $\frac{d}{dt}\|r_1\|=v_e$ (21), hence $\|r_1(t)\|=v_et+c_1$ (22); (iii) from (10), $\|r_2(t)\|\ge -v_et+c_2$ (23) (lower bound since $\cos\theta\le1$); (iv) for $\theta_0>0$, $\sin\theta\le\theta$ gives the differential inequality (24): $\dot\theta(t)\le\frac{v_e}{-v_et+c_2}\theta(t)-\frac{v_p}{v_et+c_1}$; (v) the comparison ODE $\dot{\bar\theta}=\frac{v_e}{-v_et+c_2}\bar\theta-\frac{v_p}{v_et+c_1}$, $\bar\theta(t_0)=\theta_0$ (25)–(26) yields $\theta(t)\le\bar\theta(t)$ (27) by the scalar comparison principle ([17] Hartman, ODE book); (vi) explicit solution (28): $\bar\theta(t)=\frac{v_pt-C\ln(v_et+c_1)+C\ln c_1+c_2\theta_0}{-v_et+c_2}$ with $C=\frac{v_p}{v_e}(c_1+c_2)$ (29); (vii) naming the numerator $y$, $\dot y=v_p-\frac{v_p(c_1+c_2)}{v_et+c_1}$ (30), $\ddot y=\frac{v_pv_e(c_1+c_2)}{(v_et+c_1)^2}\ge0$ (31), so $y$ attains its minimum at $t^*=c_2/v_e$: $y(t^*)=\frac{v_p}{v_e}c_2+\theta_0c_2+C\ln\frac{c_1}{c_1+c_2}$ (32); (viii) condition (16) $\iff y(t^*)<0$; by continuity there is $\hat t=t^*-\varepsilon$ with $y(\hat t)<0$ (33), and since $-v_e\hat t+c_2=v_e\varepsilon$, $\theta(\hat t)<\bar\theta(\hat t)=\frac{y(\hat t)}{v_e\varepsilon}<0$ (34); (ix) with $\theta(t_0)=\theta_0>0$ (35) and $\theta$ continuous, the intermediate value theorem gives $\check t\in[t_0,\hat t]$ with $\theta(\check t)=0$ — the co-linear state; symmetric for $\theta_0<0$.
- **Character**: global-in-time sufficient condition, purely analytic; *only sufficiency* is claimed ("as long as"). Physical meaning: the pursuer's angular steering authority is bounded by $v_p/\|r_1\|$ per unit time, while the evader's ray sweeps toward/away from $P_2$ at rate $v_e\sin\theta/\|r_2\|$; the window ends at $t^*=c_2/v_e$ (earliest possible arrival at $P_2$'s range), so the initial angle must fit inside the log-accurated budget. Note (16) is *easier* to satisfy when $c_2/c_1$ is larger (static pursuer relatively far in comparison) and always feasible with $v_p>v_e$; for $v_p\le v_e$ the right-hand side is still positive for admissible $c_1,c_2$ (it equals $\frac{v_p}{v_e}[\frac{1+s}{s}\ln(1+s)-1]>0$, $s=c_2/c_1$), which is what enables the slower-pursuer results (Remark 7).
- **Remark 4**: contrast with pure pursuit, whose effectiveness requires $v_p>v_e$; round pursuit's condition (16) involves geometry instead of a hard speed advantage.

### 5.3 Dominance regions — Lemmas 1–2, Propositions 3–4 (pp. 1962–1963)

Capture-time surrogates (Design Objective 2): for $v_p>v_e$, pure pursuit capture time $\phi_1=\|r_1\|/(v_p-v_e)$ (36). $\phi_2$ (round strategy) is not computable in closed form because $\|r_2\|$ decreases at a rate varying in $[v_e\cos\theta_0,\,v_e]$; hence two extremes $\phi_3=\|r_2\|/v_e$ and $\phi_4=\|r_2\|/(v_e\cos\theta)$ (37) bracket it: $\phi_3<\phi_2<\phi_4$ (38). Comparisons $\phi_1$ vs $\phi_3,\phi_4$ indirectly resolve $\phi_1$ vs $\phi_2$.

- **Lemma 1 [19] (Isaacs), eqs. (39)–(40)**: if $\frac{\|r_1\|}{\|r_2\|}=k_1$, $k_1\in[0,1]$, $E$ lies on an **Apollonius circle** with center $x_{C_1}=\frac{1}{1-k_1^2}x_{p_1}-\frac{k_1^2}{1-k_1^2}x_{p_2}$ and radius $r_{C_1}=\big\|\frac{k_1}{1-k_1^2}(x_{p_1}-x_{p_2})\big\|$. (Standard; cited, not re-proved.)
- **Lemma 2, eqs. (41)–(42) (new; "Apollonius-like circle")**: if $\frac{\|r_1\|\cos\theta}{\|r_2\|}=k_2$, $k_2\in[0,1]$, $E$ lies on a circle with center $x_{C_2}=\frac{1}{2k_2+2}x_{p_1}+\frac{2k_2+1}{2k_2+2}x_{p_2}$ and radius $r_{C_2}=\big\|\frac{1}{2k_2+2}(x_{p_1}-x_{p_2})\big\|$. *Proof (geometric, Fig. 2)*: construct $M$ with $P_1M\parallel EP_2$, $EM\perp P_1M$; let $N=EM\cap P_1P_2$; similarity gives $\angle P_1P_2E=\angle MP_1P_2$ (43), $\angle NEP_2=\angle NMP_1=\pi/2$ (44), hence $\frac{\|x_n-x_{p_1}\|}{\|x_n-x_{p_2}\|}=\frac{\|x_m-x_{p_1}\|}{\|x_e-x_{p_2}\|}=\frac{\|x_e-x_{p_1}\|\cos\theta}{\|x_e-x_{p_2}\|}=k_2$ (45), so $N$ is a **fixed** point on $P_1P_2$: $x_n=\frac{1}{k_2+1}x_{p_1}+\frac{k_2}{k_2+1}x_{p_2}$ (46); $E$ then lies on the circle centered $(x_n+x_{p_2})/2$ with radius $\|x_n-x_{p_2}\|/2$ — i.e., the Apollonius-like circle always **passes through $P_2$** with its center on segment $P_1P_2$ (my check: at $k_2=0$ it degenerates to the circle with diameter $P_1P_2$). ✔
- **Proposition 3 (eqs. (47)–(48))**: let $O_1$ be the Apollonius circle with $k=\frac{v_p-v_e}{v_e}$ (the locus $\phi_1=\phi_3$). If $E$ falls inside $O_1$, then $\phi_1<\phi_3<\phi_2$ (48) — **pure pursuit is faster**. (Assumes $v_p>v_e$ for (36); implicitly $k\le1$, i.e. $v_p\le2v_e$, for $k$ to obey Lemma 1's range.)
- **Proposition 4 (eqs. (49)–(50))**: let $O_2$ be the Apollonius-like circle with $k=\frac{v_p-v_e}{v_e}$ (the locus $\phi_1=\phi_4$). If $E$ falls inside $O_2$, then $\phi_2<\phi_4<\phi_1$ (50) — **round pursuit is faster**. (Same implicit $v_p\le2v_e$ restriction; "inside" = the side where $\|r_1\|\cos\theta/\|r_2\|>k$.)
- **Remark 5**: adopt $u_P$ if $x_e\in O_1$, $u_R$ if $x_e\in O_2$, and pure pursuit elsewhere (leftover region) — Design Objective 2 achieved. Note the decision is *conservative by construction*: the $\phi_3/\phi_4$ bracketing means the true boundary of $\phi_1$ vs $\phi_2$ lies inside $O_2\setminus O_1$, where pure pursuit is used by default.

### 5.4 Switching analysis — Lemmas 3–4, Propositions 5–6, Algorithm 1 (pp. 1963–1964)

Design Objective 3: decide whether/when switching the active pursuer helps. Surrogate for "capture by the other pursuer": $\phi_5(x_e,x_{p_2})=\|r_2\|/(v_p-v_e)$ (51).

- **Lemma 3**: with $l$ the (perpendicular) bisector of $P_1P_2$ and $V_1,V_2$ the open half-planes containing $P_1,P_2$ (a 1-D Voronoi partition), $\phi_1<\phi_5$ in $V_1$ and $\phi_1>\phi_5$ in $V_2$ (immediate from $\|r_1\|\lessgtr\|r_2\|$).
- **Lemma 4 (eqs. (52)–(55))**: let $I_a,I_b$ be the intersections of $l$ with $O_2$; let $O_a$ ($O_b$) be the circle through $P_1,P_2,I_a$ ($I_b$); $A:=O_a\cap O_b$. On $\partial A$, $\frac{\|r_1\|}{v_p-v_e}=\frac{\|r_2\|}{v_e\cos\theta}$ forces $\cos\theta=\frac{v_p-v_e}{v_e}$ (52)–(53); inside $A$, $\cos\theta>\frac{v_p-v_e}{v_e}$ (54), hence $\phi_4=\frac{\|r_2\|}{v_e\cos\theta}<\frac{\|r_2\|}{v_p-v_e}=\phi_5$ (55); outside $A$ the reverse. So $A$ is exactly the region where finishing with the *other* pursuer is faster than finishing by pure pursuit of $P_1$. (Requires $\frac{v_p-v_e}{v_e}\le1$, i.e. $v_p\le2v_e$, for the arccos/geometry to exist.)
- **Fig. 3**: with $O_1,O_2,O_a,O_b,l$ overlaid, the plane splits into three disjoint sets: $V_1\setminus O_2$ (blue), $A\cap O_2$ (green), $V_2\setminus A$ (red).
- **Proposition 5 (invariance of the pure-pursuit cell; eqs. (56)–(64))**: if $x_e(t_0)\in V_1\setminus O_2$ and $P_1$ plays $u_P$, then $E$ remains in $V_1\setminus O_2$, i.e. $\phi_1(t)<\phi_4(t)$ and $\phi_1(t)<\phi_5(t)$ on $[t_0,t_f]$. *Proof*: at $t_0$, $\phi_1(t_0)<\phi_4(t_0)$ (56) and $\phi_1(t_0)<\phi_5(t_0)$ (57) (Lemmas 3–4); under $u_P$ the rates are (58): $\dot\phi_1=-1$, $\dot\phi_4=-1+\tan^2\theta$, $\dot\phi_5=-\frac{v_e\cos\theta}{v_p-v_e}$ [all three verified by direct differentiation ✔ — note $\dot\phi_4$ uses $\dot\theta=\frac{v_e\sin\theta}{\|r_2\|}$ under pure pursuit]; first-order Taylor expansion (59) then propagates $\phi_1(t_0+\Delta t)<\phi_4(t_0+\Delta t)$ (60) stepwise to all $t$ (61), and similarly $\phi_1(t)<\phi_5(t)$ (64), with the sub-case $E\in A\setminus O_2$ argued to exit through $\partial A$ into $V_1\setminus A$ without ever entering $O_2$. *Tool*: stepwise first-order comparison (no formal comparison lemma invoked — see §7 note on rigor).
- **Proposition 6 (eqs. (65)–(75))**: if $x_e(t_0)\in V_1\cap O_2$ and $P_1$ plays $u_R$, then $E$ crosses $\partial A$ into $V_2\setminus A$, and thereafter $\phi_1(t)>\phi_5(t)$, $\phi_4(t)\ge\phi_5(t)$ on $[t_1,t_f]$. *Proof*: initially $\phi_4(t_0)<\phi_1(t_0)<\phi_5(t_0)$ (65) and $\theta(t_0)<\arccos\frac{v_p-v_e}{v_e}$ (66); under $u_R$, $\dot\theta=\frac{v_e}{\|r_2\|}\sin\theta-\frac{v_p}{\|r_1\|}$ (67); since $\|r_2\|\downarrow$ and $\|r_1\|\uparrow$, $\dot\theta$ increases, so either $\theta\to0$ (co-linear capture by $P_2$, eqs. (74)–(75): $\phi_4(t_1)=\phi_5(t_1)=0$, $t_1=t_f$, $\phi_1(t_1)>0$) or there is $t_1\in(t_0,t_f)$ with $\theta(t_1)=\arccos\frac{v_p-v_e}{v_e}$ (68) — i.e. $E$ hits $\partial A$. Meanwhile (69): $\dot\phi_1=\frac{v_e}{v_p-v_e}>0$ and $\dot\phi_4=-1+\big(\sin\theta-\frac{v_p\|r_2\|}{v_e\|r_1\|}\big)\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos^2\theta}$ [verified ✔] keep $\phi_4<\phi_1$ (70)–(71) on $[t_0,t_l]$ ($t_l$: crossing of $l$), after which (72)–(73) hold on $[t_1,t_f]$.
- **Remark 6**: $V_1\setminus O_2$ ⟹ never switch; $V_1\cap O_2$ ⟹ switch $P_1\to P_2$ after $E$ crosses $\partial A$ — Design Objective 3 achieved.
- **Algorithm 1 (region-based relay pursuit, two-pursuer case, p. 1964)** — inputs $x_e,x_{p_1},x_{p_2},\sigma,\tau$; at $t=0$: $\sigma=1,\tau=2$ if $x_e\in V_1$ else $\sigma=2,\tau=1$; then: if $x_e\in V_1\setminus O_2(x_{p_\sigma},x_{p_\tau})$ → $U=u_P(x_e,x_{p_\sigma})$; if $x_e\in A\cap O_2(x_{p_\sigma},x_{p_\tau})$ → $U=u_R(x_e,x_{p_\sigma},x_{p_\tau})$; if $x_e\in V_2\setminus A(x_{p_\sigma},x_{p_\tau})$ → swap $\sigma\leftrightarrow\tau$ and $U=u_P(x_e,x_{p_\sigma})$. Output $(\sigma,\tau,U)$.

### 5.5 Time superiority over classic relay pursuit — Proposition 7 (pp. 1964–1965)

- **Proposition 7 (eq. (76))**: with $\phi_r,\phi_p$ the capture times of the region-based scheme and of the **classic relay pursuit scheme** (pure pursuit by the active pursuer, switch with hysteresis), $\phi_r\le\phi_p$. *Assumptions*: $v_p>v_e$, Assumption 1, Assumption 2.
- **Proof approach**: (i) if $x_e(t_0)\in V_1\setminus O_2$, Prop. 5 gives no switch ever, $\phi_r=\frac{\|r_1(t_0)\|}{v_p-v_e}=\phi_p$ (77); (ii) if $x_e(t_0)\in V_1\cap O_2$, region-based: $\phi_r=t_1+\frac{\|r_2(t_1)\|}{v_p-v_e}\big|_{u=u_R}$ (78), $t_1$ = crossing time of $\partial A$; classic: switches when $\frac{\|r_1\|}{v_p-v_e}-\frac{\|r_2\|}{v_p-v_e}\ge\mu$ ($\mu>0$ hysteresis constant), an area $H(x_{p_1},x_{p_2})$ with hyperbola boundary $\partial H$, $\phi_p=t_h+\frac{\|r_2(t_h)\|}{v_p-v_e}\big|_{u=u_P}$ (79)–(80); (iii) intermediate quantity $\tilde\phi_p=t_a+\frac{\|r_2(t_a)\|}{v_p-v_e}\big|_{u=u_P}$ (81), $t_a$ = crossing time of $\partial A$ under pure pursuit; three cases: $t_h>t_a$ ⟹ $\phi_p=t_a+\frac{\|r_2(t_a)\|}{\bar v_a}$ (82) with $\bar v_a<v_p-v_e$ (83, via Lemma 4: past $\partial A$, $\theta>\arccos\frac{v_p-v_e}{v_e}$ so $\|r_2\|$ decays slower than $v_p-v_e$) ⟹ $\tilde\phi_p<\phi_p$ (84); $t_h=t_a\Rightarrow\phi_p=\tilde\phi_p$ (85); $t_h<t_a$ ⟹ $\tilde\phi_p=t_h+\frac{\|r_2(t_h)\|}{\bar v_h}$ (86) with $\bar v_h>v_p-v_e$ (87, inside $H$ before $\partial A$: $\theta<\arccos\frac{v_p-v_e}{v_e}$ so decay is faster) ⟹ $\tilde\phi_p<\phi_p$ (88); overall $\tilde\phi_p\le\phi_p$ (89); (iv) comparison of $\phi_r$ with $\tilde\phi_p$: $\theta(t_a)|_{u=u_P}=\arccos\frac{v_p-v_e}{v_e}$ (90); "For Proposition 1, $u_R$ is the minimizer of $J_t$" ⟹ $\|r_2(t_2)\||_{u=u_R}<\|r_2(t_2)\||_{u=u_P}$ (91); "For (18), $u_R$ is the minimizer of $\dot\theta$ as well" ⟹ $\theta(t_a)|_{u=u_R}<\theta(t_a)|_{u=u_P}$ (92), "It means $t_a<t_1$"; then $\phi_r=t_a+\frac{\|r_2(t_a)\|}{\bar v_r}\big|_{u=u_R}$ (93), $\bar v_r>v_p-v_e$ (94), hence $\phi_r<\tilde\phi_p$ (95); with (89) and (95), done.
- **Character**: analytic comparison; inequality is non-strict ($\le$) overall, strict ($<$) in the switching case. Physical meaning: switching at the geometrically correct boundary $\partial A$ — and steering with $u_R$ (which decays $\|r_2\|$ faster and keeps $\theta$ smaller) — never costs more time than the classic hyperbolic hysteresis rule.
- **Proof gaps / sloppiness (verified against rendered p. 1965)**: (a) eqs. (91)–(92) infer *pointwise dominance along two different closed-loop trajectories* from instantaneous (greedy) minimality of $J_t$ and $\dot\theta$; no comparison principle or monotonicity argument bridges the two trajectories, and the evader's own path differs under $u_R$ vs $u_P$ (so even the state regions $O_2,A$ are sampled along different paths). This is the weakest step of the paper. (b) $t_2$ in (91) is never defined. (c) The justification of (94) reads "For (95), we have $\bar v_r>v_p-v_e$" — a circular self-reference (evidently a typo; the intended argument is presumably that under $u_R$, $\theta<\arccos\frac{v_p-v_e}{v_e}$ until $t_1$, so $-\frac{d}{dt}\|r_2\|=v_e\cos\theta>v_p-v_e$ on $[t_a,t_1]$). (d) (93) reuses $t_a$ — a time defined *under $u_P$* — inside the $u=u_R$ capture time, conflating $t_a$ and $t_1$ (harmless if (93) is read as an identity defining the average decay speed $\bar v_r=\|r_2(t_a)\|/(t_f-t_a)$, but the notation is not clean).
- **Remark 7 (the $v_p\le v_e$ case)**: pure pursuit never captures ($\phi_1,\phi_5$ "can be regarded as $\infty$") as long as $E$'s initial position is not exactly on the two-pursuer connecting line; therefore the round pursuit strategy should **always** be adopted and the active pursuer **never** switched; capture holds iff (16).

### 5.6 Multipursuer extension — Propositions 8–10, Algorithm 2 (pp. 1965–1967)

- **Assumption 3**: $P_i$ is closest to $E$ initially (hence active first). The $N$-pursuer game is viewed as an aggregation of $N-1$ two-pursuer games $(E,P_i,P_j)$, $j\in I\setminus i$; notation $O_2^{ij},A^{ij},V_1^{ij},\phi_1^i,\phi_4^{ij},\theta^{ij},J_t^{ij}$.
- **Proposition 8 (eqs. (96)–(98))**: define $V^i=\bigcap_{j\in I\setminus i}V_1^{ij}$ (96), $O^i=\bigcup_{j\in I\setminus i}O_2^{ij}$ (97), $B^{ij}=\bigcap_{k\in I\setminus\{i,j\}}B^k_{ij}$ (98), where for $O_2^{ij}\cap O_2^{ik}\ne\emptyset$ the tie-curve $s^k_{ij}$ divides $O_2^{ij}\cup O_2^{ik}$ into $B^k_{ij}$ (containing $P_j$) and $B^k_{ij}^{\,c}$-side $B^j_{ik}$ (containing $P_k$), with every point of $s^k_{ij}$ satisfying $\frac{r_i^Tr_j}{r_j^Tr_j}=\frac{r_i^Tr_k}{r_k^Tr_k}$; if $O_2^{ij}\cap O_2^{ik}=\emptyset$, $B^k_{ij}=O_2^{ij}$. Then, with $\Phi^i:=\{\phi_1^i\}\cup\{\phi_4^{ij}:j\in I\setminus i\}$: $\phi_1^i=\arg\min\Phi^i$ on $V^i\setminus O^i$, and $\phi_4^{ij}=\arg\min\Phi^i$ on $V^i\cap B^{ij}$. *Proof*: on $V^i\setminus O^i$, each pair gives $\phi_1^i<\phi_4^{ij}$ (99) by Prop. 4; on $B^k_{ij}$, $\frac{r_i^Tr_j}{r_j^Tr_j}<\frac{r_i^Tr_k}{r_k^Tr_k}$ (100), which (using $\|r_i\|\cos\theta^{ij}=-\frac{r_i^Tr_j}{\|r_j\|}$) is equivalent to $\phi_4^{ij}<\phi_4^{ik}<\phi_1^i$ (101); if the disks are disjoint, $\phi_4^{ij}<\phi_1^i<\phi_4^{ik}$ (102) directly. [I verified the equivalence (100) $\iff$ ($\phi_4^{ij}$ vs $\phi_4^{ik}$) by algebra ✔ — the tie-curve criterion is exactly equal projected-distance ratios.] So the round strategy should target the static pursuer $P_j$ whose Apollonius-like disk contains $E$ with the best ratio — Design Objective 4.
- **Proposition 9 (eqs. (103)–(104))**: invariance — if $x_e(t_0)\in V^i\setminus O^i$ and $U=u_P(x_e,x_{p_i})$, then $\phi_1^i<\phi_4^{ij}$ (103) and $\phi_1^i<\phi_1^j$ (104) for all $j$, $t\in[t_0,t_f]$: $E$ stays in $V^i\setminus O^i$ and no switch helps. (Reduces to Prop. 5 pairwise.)
- **Proposition 10 (eqs. (105)–(108))**: if $x_e(t_0)\in V^i\cap B^{ij}$ and $U=u_R(x_e,x_{p_i},x_{p_j})$, then $E$ remains in $B^{ij}$ until crossing $\partial A^{ij}$ into $V^j\setminus A^{ij}$. *Proof*: $\phi_4^{ij}<\phi_1^i$ (105) (Prop. 6) and $\phi_4^{ij}<\phi_4^{ik}$ at $t_0$ (106); because $u_R$ minimizes $J_t^{ij}$ and $\dot\theta^{ij}$, it also minimizes $\dot\phi_4^{ij}$ (107), so $\phi_4^{ij}(t)<\phi_4^{ik}(t)$ persists (108) — again a greedy-trajectory-dominance step (same rigor caveat as Prop. 7).
- **Algorithm 2 (multipursuer case, p. 1967)**: at $t=0$ pick $\sigma=i$ with $x_e\in V^i$; pick $\tau=j$ with $x_e\in B^{ij}$; then run Algorithm 1 on $(\sigma,\tau)$.
- **Remark 10**: for $v_p\le v_e$: always $u_R$, never switch; the capture condition (16) is much easier to satisfy now because it suffices that $\sigma$'s geometry with **any one** of the $N-1$ static pursuers satisfies (16).

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues

**Author-stated**:
1. The evader is hard-wired to pure evasion w.r.t. the active pursuer (Assumption 1, Remark 1); the scheme's guarantees are relative to this behavior, and Remark 1's justification ("pure evasion is the optimal strategy to evade from the current active pursuer") ignores that evading the group optimally may differ.
2. For $v_p\le v_e$ the region-based switching machinery is inapplicable (Remark 7/10): only round pursuit, never switch, capture conditional on (16).
3. The round-strategy capture time $\phi_2$ has no closed form; strategy comparison proceeds through the bracket $\phi_3<\phi_2<\phi_4$ (38), so the true pure-pursuit/round-pursuit boundary is not characterized — the left-over region defaults to pure pursuit (Remark 5) and is not claimed optimal.
4. The optimal (non-greedy) steering problem is "analytically intractable" (Remark 2); $u_R$ is quickest-descent, i.e. pointwise, not globally time-optimal.

**Critical observations (with page/eq.)**:
5. **Termination condition mismatch**: Definition 1, eq. (2), requires $\sum_{i=1}^N\|x_{p_i}(t_f)-x_e(t_f)\|=0$ (all pursuers on $E$), but the actual termination is $\|r_1(t_f)\|=0$ **or** $\|r_2(t_f)\|=0$ (p. 1960) and Problem 1 restates (2). The formula should be a minimum/any-pursuer condition.
6. **Hidden speed-ratio restriction**: the applied constants $k=\frac{v_p-v_e}{v_e}$ must lie in $[0,1]$ (Lemmas 1–2) and $\arccos\frac{v_p-v_e}{v_e}$ must exist (Lemma 4, (68), (90)), both forcing $v_p\le2v_e$; the $v_p>2v_e$ regime (where switching may provably never help since $\cos\theta>\frac{v_p-v_e}{v_e}>1$ is impossible) is never discussed.
7. **Proof gaps in Proposition 7** (p. 1965): (91)–(92) establish pointwise state dominance between *two different closed-loop trajectories* from instantaneous minimality of $J_t$ and $\dot\theta$ — a greedy argument with no comparison principle; $t_2$ in (91) is undefined; (94) is justified by the circular sentence "For (95), we have $\bar v_r>v_p-v_e$"; (93) reuses $t_a$ (defined under $u_P$) inside the $u=u_R$ expression. The conclusion $\phi_r\le\phi_p$ is very plausible (and reproduced numerically), but the written proof is not airtight. The same greedy-dominance pattern underlies (107) in Proposition 10.
8. **Moving boundaries**: $O_2$, $A$, $V_i$ are state-dependent ($P_1$ moves; $\|r_1\|$ grows at full $v_e$ under $u_R$), yet crossing-time comparisons (78)–(86) treat $\partial A$, $\partial H$ as if fixed; invariance propositions (5, 9, 10) cover staying *inside* cells but not the interlaced geometry of two evolving boundary families.
9. **No switching-regularity analysis**: nothing prevents chattering near $\partial A$/$l$ (Algorithm 1 swaps $\sigma,\tau$ instantaneously); no dwell time, no Zeno-type argument, no state-estimation noise study.
10. **Empirical breadth**: single-instance, noise-free, deterministic simulations; no Monte Carlo over initial conditions, no comparison against other multiplayer PE schemes (e.g., simultaneous cooperative pursuit [5], [20]), no experimental platform; fuel-consumption motivation (p. 1958) never quantified.
11. **Terminology**: "round pursuit" rotation requires $\theta\ne0$; at $\sin\theta=0$ the law switches to head-on — the transition is well-defined but the resulting sliding between the two branches at the co-linear manifold is not analyzed.

## 8. Citation Value

**Paragraph role**: P070 is the reference for *strategy-augmented relay pursuit* — the point in the literature where relay pursuit stops being "closest pursuer chases with pure pursuit" and becomes "active pursuer chooses between direct chase and steering the evader toward a static teammate, with switching regions carved by Voronoi lines, Apollonius circles, and a new Apollonius-like circle." It is the natural citation for (i) Quickest-descent/greedy steering laws as analytically tractable substitutes for intractable fixed-terminal optimal control in PEGs (imported from [7] to the pursuer side), (ii) the Apollonius-like circle $\frac{\|r_1\|\cos\theta}{\|r_2\|}=k$ as a dominance-region tool (complementing Isaacs' Apollonius circle), (iii) capture guarantees for pursuer groups with $v_p\le v_e$ (slower-pursuer capture via geometric condition (16)), and (iv) proofs of capture-time dominance over hysteresis-based classic relay pursuit. Its assumptions (pure evasion, single integrators, $v_p\le2v_e$ implicit) bound the transferability of its guarantees.

**Suggested citation sentences**:
- "Classical relay pursuit assigns the active pursuer by proximity and lets it chase directly [3], [23], [27]; Pan and Yuan [P070] instead let the active pursuer play a quickest-descent 'round pursuit' law that steers the evader toward a static pursuer, and prove that the resulting region-based switching rule never increases capture time relative to the classic scheme."
- "The Apollonius-like circle of [P070] — the locus $\|r_1\|\cos\theta/\|r_2\|=k$ through the static pursuer — provides a dominance-region characterization for pursuit laws whose objective is a *relative* distance, and is reusable wherever a pursuer must herd an evader toward a teammate or a target."
