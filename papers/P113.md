## 2. Research Background

Multi-pursuer pursuit–evasion of a single evader in a bounded planar region can in principle be formulated as a multi-agent differential game whose value (time-to-capture) solves a Hamilton–Jacobi–Isaacs (HJI) PDE, but grid-based HJI solutions suffer the curse of dimensionality — the grid grows exponentially in the joint configuration space of all agents — and characteristics-based solutions require backward integration from terminal configurations (p. 64–65). Geometric alternatives (pure-distance pursuit, coordination arguments) are computationally cheap but are usually restricted to unobstructed, homogeneous-speed settings (p. 65). Discrete graph-search results (three pursuers necessary/sufficient on planar graphs; visibility-based and polygonal-domain pursuit) provide formal guarantees but their implementable continuous-space versions do not exist and require searching over large discrete action sets (p. 65). The paper proposes a middle path: a decentralized, real-time strategy in which pursuers share only agent positions and each minimizes the area of the evader's (generalized) Voronoi cell — with equal speeds in a convex domain this cell is exactly the set of points the evader reaches no later than any pursuer, so shrinking its area to zero is synonymous with capture.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap

**Problem** [authors' statement, p. 65–66]: N pursuers and one evader with kinematic (single-integrator velocity) dynamics in an open, simply connected region Ω ⊂ ℝ²; all speeds bounded (Eq. (2)); capture when at least one pursuer comes within distance r_c > 0 of the evader. Find an admissible cooperative pursuit strategy guaranteeing capture, computable in real time and decentralized (each pursuer computes its own input from the shared agent positions).

**Gap** [authors' claim, p. 64–65]: (i) HJI/differential-game solutions are optimal but computationally infeasible beyond few agents; (ii) geometric/graph-based strategies with capture guarantees (Aigner & Fromme 1984; Bhadauria et al. 2012; Ames et al. 2014) "currently implementable versions … do not yet exist" and require discrete action search — [verified as stated; these are claims about the cited literature's usability, not re-proven here]; (iii) prior geometric pursuits (Isaacs 1967; Alexander et al. 2006; Kopparty & Ravishankar 2005) are limited to simple environments and homogeneous speeds. The paper positions itself as complementary to the guarantee-providing line (Ames et al. 2014; Bhadauria et al. 2012) by delivering *computable* strategies for the special case of convex environments and equal speeds (p. 65). Elements of the convex/equal-speed result first appeared in the same group's CDC 2011 paper (Huang, Zhang, Ding, Stipanović, Tomlin 2011); this journal version claims three deltas (p. 65, Sec. 1.1): an equivalent, implementable characterization of the control (Theorem 2), a simpler capture proof via an energy function (Theorem 3), and the generalization to non-convex domains/unequal speeds with a modified-FMM numerical strategy plus extensive simulation benchmarking.

## 4. Core Contributions

1. **Strategy (modeling/algorithm)**: A cooperative pursuit law defined by the Voronoi partition of Ω generated by all agent positions; each pursuer moves in the direction that most steeply decreases the area A of the evader's Voronoi cell S_e (Eq. (6)); non-neighbors of the evader fall back to pure pursuit (Sec. 3.1). Decentralized: inputs decouple given the shared partition (information = agent positions only).
2. **Geometric characterization (theory)**: Theorem 2 — under the area-gradient strategy each pursuer heads exactly for the midpoint of its line of control B_i (the shared Voronoi edge), giving a closed-form, easily implementable input from local geometry (L_i, l_i, ‖ξ_i‖).
3. **Capture guarantee (theory)**: Theorem 3 — an energy function E = kA + ‖x_e − x_p‖² decreases at a linear rate Ė ≤ −c(β) under the strategy, proving finite-time capture for N ≥ 1 pursuers in convex domains with equal (unit) speeds; the proof technique (energy function) is claimed novel relative to guardable-path/shrinking-domain arguments of prior work (p. 67).
4. **Generalization (modeling/algorithm)**: Extension to non-convex domains and unequal (pursuer ≥ evader) speeds via the *safe-reachable set* S_e (Eqs. (11)–(13)) — the evader's reachability-dominance region — computed numerically by a modified fast marching method (Zhou et al. 2012); pursuers minimize the area of S_e by finite-difference gradients on a grid (Sec. 4.2). No formal capture proof in this general case (author-stated).
5. **Empirical study (experiment)**: Simulation benchmarking against pure-distance pursuit and a numerical HJI strategy (1 v 1, 40×40 grid, 500 random initial conditions), and against pure-distance pursuit for 1–3 pursuers in three domains (convex / simple non-convex / complex non-convex, 500 ICs each), with capture-time statistics for 1–5 pursuers (Tables 1–2) and real-time computation cost measurements (<1 ms analytic; ≈100 ms/pursuer FMM).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results

### 5.1 Problem formulation (Sec. 2, p. 65)

- **Dynamics (Eq. (1))**: ẋ_e = d, x_e(0) = x_e^0; ẋ_p^i = u_i, x_p^i(0) = x_{p}^{i,0}, i = 1,…,N — kinematic single integrators in ℝ².
- **Input constraints (Eq. (2))**: D = {d | ‖d‖ ≤ v_{e,max}}, U_i = {u_i | ‖u_i‖ ≤ v_{i,max}} (Euclidean norm).
- **State constraint (Eq. (3))**: x_e(t), x_p^i(t) ∈ Ω ∀t ≥ 0; inputs satisfying (2)–(3) are *admissible*.
- **Closed loop (Eq. (4))**: ẋ_p^i = μ_i(x_e, x_p^1, …, x_p^N); evader uses an admissible strategy γ; admissible strategy sets U, D.
- **Termination/winning condition**: capture when at least one pursuer is within distance r_c > 0 of the evader (p. 65). Pursuers win if capture occurs in finite time against all admissible evader inputs.
- **WLOG normalization** (Sec. 3 opening): by re-scaling, v_{e,max} = v_{i,max} = 1 for the convex-domain analysis.

### 5.2 Voronoi machinery and the pursuit strategy (Sec. 3.1, p. 66)

- **Voronoi partition** V(Ω) = {S_e, V_1, …, V_N} generated by {x_e, x_p^1, …, x_p^N}:
  S_e = {y ∈ Ω | ‖y − x_e‖ ≤ ‖y − x_p^i‖ ∀i ≤ N},  V_i = {y ∈ Ω | ‖y − x_p^i‖ ≤ min{‖y − x_e‖, ‖y − x_p^j‖}, ∀j ≠ i}.
- **Line of control**: N_e = set of pursuer indices that are Voronoi neighbors of the evader (cardinality n_e); B_i := edge shared by S_e and V_i (i ∈ N_e); L_i := length of B_i (Fig. 1). For equal-speed kinematic players each B_i is a segment of the perpendicular bisector of [x_p^i, x_e] — i.e., the degenerate (equal-speed) case of the Apollonius circle/ barrier familiar from reach-avoid games; the paper itself never uses the word "Apollonius" (verified: 0 occurrences), so this identification is interpretive, but it is exactly why the partition is computable in closed form here.
- **Area dependence**: A := area of S_e depends only on the neighboring pursuers' positions and is smooth in them; ȧ = ∂A/∂x_e·ẋ_e + Σ_{i=1}^N ∂A/∂x_p^i·ẋ_p^i (Eq. (5)); ∂A/∂x_p^i = 0 for i ∉ N_e.
- **Strategy (Eq. (6))**: for i ∈ N_e, u_i^* = μ_i^*(x_e, x_p^1, …, x_p^N) ≜ −(∂A/∂x_p^i)/‖∂A/∂x_p^i‖ (uses v_max = 1); for i ∉ N_e, u_i = (x_e − x_p^i)/‖x_e − x_p^i‖ (pure pursuit). Joint objective: minimize dA/dt; decouples per pursuer because A's dependence on each neighbor enters additively in (5).
- **Directional derivatives (Eqs. (7)–(8))**: with unit vectors η_h^i, η_v^i at pursuer i (Fig. 1: η_h^i points along ξ_i = x_e − x_p^i, i.e., perpendicular to B_i toward the evader side; η_v^i is parallel to B_i), D_h^i A and D_v^i A are defined as limits of [A_i^+(ε·η^i)|_{(x_e,x_p^1,…,x_p^N)} − A]/ε where A_i^+ is A evaluated with pursuer i displaced by ε·η^i (Eq. (7)); then ∂A/∂x_p^i = D_h^i A·η_h^i + D_v^i A·η_v^i (Eq. (8)).

### 5.3 Lemma 1 (p. 66, unnumbered equation block)

**Lemma 1** (proof deferred to Huang et al., CDC 2011). For any i ∈ N_e:
D_h^i A = −L_i/2,  D_v^i A = (l_i² − (L_i − l_i)²)/(2‖ξ_i‖),
where ‖ξ_i‖ = ‖x_e − x_p^i‖ and l_i is the (signed) offset along B_i between the foot of the perpendicular from x_p^i onto B_i and an endpoint of B_i (l_i is not formally defined in this paper; the geometry is from Huang et al. 2011 — inferred here and consistent with Eqs. (9)–(10)).

*Verification of the mechanism* (my reconstruction, consistent with all printed formulas): since B_i is the perpendicular bisector, moving pursuer i by ε along η_h^i (toward the evader) translates B_i away by ε/2, shaving a strip of area (ε/2)L_i → D_h^i A = −L_i/2; moving by ε along η_v^i (along B_i) rotates B_i about its interior with slope ε/‖ξ_i‖, changing area by −∫ over B_i of (ε s/‖ξ_i‖) ds = L_i(2l_i − L_i)/(2‖ξ_i‖) = (l_i² − (L_i − l_i)²)/(2‖ξ_i‖) using l_i² − (L_i − l_i)² = L_i(2l_i − L_i). Physical meaning: the gradient magnitude in the normal direction is half the boundary length; the tangential sensitivity encodes how off-center the pursuer's projection is.

### 5.4 Theorem 2 (p. 66, with proof via Eqs. (9)–(10) on p. 67)

**Theorem 2**. *Under the proposed pursuit strategy given in Eq. (6), pursuer i should always head for the midpoint of the line of control B_i.*

*Proof* (3 steps; purely geometric):
1. The input direction in the local frame is (D_h^i A, D_v^i A) (Eq. (6) = negative area gradient, Eq. (8)); with α the angle between the input and η_h^i,
   tan(α) = D_v^i A / D_h^i A = [(l_i² − (L_i − l_i)²)/(2‖ξ_i‖)] / (−L_i/2) = −(2l_i − L_i)/‖ξ_i‖ (Eq. (9)) — the decisive identity uses l_i² − (L_i − l_i)² = L_i(2l_i − L_i).
2. With β the angle between η_h^i and the vector from pursuer i to the midpoint of B_i, tan(β) = (l_i − (L_i/2))/(‖ξ_i‖/2) = −(2l_i − L_i)/‖ξ_i‖ (Eq. (10)); the denominator ‖ξ_i‖/2 is exactly the pursuer-to-B_i distance (perpendicular bisector), confirming ‖ξ_i‖ = pursuer–evader distance.
3. α = β, hence the steepest area-descent direction coincides with the direction to the midpoint of B_i. ∎

*Minor erratum (verified against the 300-dpi rendering, p. 67, Eq. (10))*: the middle expression (l_i − (L_i/2))/(‖ξ_i‖/2) equals +(2l_i − L_i)/‖ξ_i‖, not −(2l_i − L_i)/‖ξ_i‖ as printed; the numerator should be (L_i/2 − l_i) (offset of the midpoint from the foot of the perpendicular) for sign consistency. The conclusion (α = β, head to midpoint) is unaffected, as is Eq. (9), whose algebra checks exactly.
*Physical meaning*: each neighbor pushes its line of control straight toward the evader as fast as possible while simultaneously steering to the point where its tangential leverage about the cell boundary is neutralized — "push its line of control to the maximal extent along the most effective direction" (p. 67). Global result, analytic, closed-form; exact (not numerical) for the equal-speed convex case.

### 5.5 Theorem 3 — guaranteed capture via an energy function (Sec. 3.2, p. 67)

Setup: for the single-pursuer case (N = 1, subscript dropped) define the "energy" E = kA + z with k > 0 (chosen below) and z(t) = ‖x_e(t) − x_p(t)‖₂². Clearly E = 0 iff A = 0 and z = 0, both of which imply capture; E ≥ 0 always.

**Theorem 3**. *Under the pursuit strategy μ* given in Eq. (6), if the capture condition has not been met before time t > 0, then for some positive constants k, β, c(β): E(t) ≤ E(0) − c(β)t,* where E(0) is the initial energy.

*Proof* (5 steps; the case dichotomy is imported from Lemmas 4 and 5 of Huang et al. (2011)):
1. By Lemmas 4–5 of Huang et al. (2011), at any time one of the following holds (as printed): (1) Ȧ ≥ −β and ż ≤ −c(β); or (2) ż > c(β) and Ȧ < −β.
2. Always Ȧ ≤ 0 (asserted; a property of the strategy/geometric setting).
3. Case 1: Ė = kȦ + ż ≤ 0 + (−c(β)) = −c(β).
4. Speed/geometry bound: ż = 2δδ̇ with δ = ‖x_e − x_p‖, δ ≤ l_max (maximal distance within the domain; l_max not formally defined in this paper) and δ̇ ≤ 2 (equal unit speeds), hence ż ≤ 4l_max.
5. Choose k = (4l_max + c(β))/β. Case 2: Ė ≤ −kβ + 4l_max = −c(β). Hence Ė ≤ −c(β) always → E hits 0 in finite time → capture. ∎

*Immediate corollary* (my inference, not printed): the bound E(t) ≤ E(0) − c(β)t gives the explicit capture-time guarantee t_cap ≤ E(0)/c(β) = (kA(0) + ‖x_e(0) − x_p(0)‖²)/c(β).
*Proof gap (as printed)*: condition 2 reads "ż > c(β)", which together with condition 1 ("ż ≤ −c(β)" ∧ "Ȧ ≥ −β") leaves the situation (Ȧ ≥ −β, −c(β) < ż ≤ c(β)) uncovered, where Ė ≤ k·0 + c(β) need not be negative. For the dichotomy to be exhaustive the second condition must be "ż > −c(β)" (a likely typo — with that reading the split is exactly {ż ≤ −c(β)} vs {ż > −c(β)}, and the proof is complete because the case-2 estimate uses only Ȧ < −β and the universal bound ż ≤ 4l_max). Also note the dichotomy itself and the standing claim Ȧ ≤ 0 are not re-proved here; they are imported from Huang et al. (2011), so the "simpler proof" is simpler but not fully self-contained.
*Extension to N > 1* (p. 67, one sentence): pick any pursuer i that is a Voronoi neighbor of the evader and apply the N = 1 argument; the reduction is asserted, not written out (the multi-neighbor cell boundary and the corresponding Ȧ ≤ 0 claim are not re-derived — see Section 7).
*Local/global, analytic/numerical*: global (all admissible evader inputs, whole convex domain), analytic; the constants β, c(β) come from the geometry (Huang et al. 2011) and l_max from the domain diameter.
*Novelty claim* (p. 67): unlike guardable-path/shrinking-safe-domain arguments (Ames et al. 2014; Bhadauria et al. 2012), the proof shrinks a tailored energy at a linear rate; the authors also note one pursuer already suffices for capture here (known by specializing prior strategies to convex domains — their contribution is the proof technique for *this* strategy).

### 5.6 Qualitative properties (Sec. 3.3, p. 67)

(i) Closed-form analytic input → fast online implementation; (ii) capture guaranteed even with a single pursuer (vs heuristic pursuits); (iii) cooperation: each pursuer affects S_e only through its shared boundary (L_1, L_2 in Fig. 1) and pushes it maximally along the most effective direction; (iv) environment awareness: "the boundaries of the game domain provide additional line of controls as if there were more pursuers" — obstacles/boundaries enter the input design through the Voronoi partition automatically.

### 5.7 Extension: safe-reachable-set pursuit (Sec. 4, p. 67–69)

- **Safe-reachable set (Sec. 4.1)**: given x_e^0, x_p^{i,0}, y ∈ Ω is safe-reachable if ∃γ ∈ D, t ≥ 0 with x_e(t) = y and x_e(s) ∉ C(s) ∀s ∈ [0,t] and all μ_i ∈ U, where C(s) is the set of points any pursuer can reach (counting the endowed capture radius) within time s; S_e = {y ∈ Ω | y is safe-reachable}.
- **Time functions (Eqs. (11)–(12))**: φ(y) = min{t | x_e(t) = y, x_e(s) ∈ S_e ∀s ∈ [0,t]} (minimum time-to-reach constrained to S_e); ψ^c(y) = min{t | y ∈ C(t), x_p(s) ∈ Ω ∀s ∈ [0,t]} (minimum time-to-capture of an evader *stationary* at y).
- **Characterization (Eq. (13))**: single pursuer: S_e = {y | φ(y) < ψ^c(y)}; multiple pursuers: S_e = {y | φ(y) < ψ_i^c(y) ∀i} (evader must arrive before *all* pursuers). For equal speeds S_e coincides with the evader's cell in the generalized Voronoi decomposition (Cheung 2005); in general the coupled definitions (S_e needed to compute φ, φ needed to compute S_e) are resolved simultaneously by the modified FMM.
- **Information structure**: S_e is defined in the open-loop sense (a point in it is reachable by heading straight there, ignoring pursuer actions); the feedback nature comes from the receding-horizon implementation — S_e and the minimization are recomputed at each instant from current state measurements (p. 68). S_e guides pursuers only; it does not constrain the evader.
- **Generalized strategy (Sec. 4.2, two unnumbered equations, p. 68)**: pursuer i shares an *active boundary* with the evader if some portion of ∂S_e is determined purely by i, i.e., ψ_i^c(y) < ψ_j^c(y) along that portion for all other pursuers j; N_e := set of such pursuers; ∂A/∂x_p^i = 0 for i ∉ N_e (A := area of S_e, derivative structure as in (5)). Then
  u_i^* = μ_i^* ≜ −v_{i,max}·(∂A/∂x_p^i)/‖∂A/∂x_p^i‖ for i ∈ N_e; pure-distance pursuit for i ∉ N_e. On singular surfaces (multiple inputs giving the same area derivative, possible in non-convex domains) the pursuer may instead choose the input maximizing distance decrease.
- **Numerical computation (modified FMM, p. 68)**: (a) standard FMM computes ψ_i^c(y_{j,k}) for every grid node y_{j,k}; (b) modified FMM (Zhou et al. 2012) grows S_e from the current x_e, adding nodes with φ(y_{j,k}) < ψ_i^c(y_{j,k}) ∀i; (c) A ≈ N_s h² with N_s the count of safe nodes and h the grid spacing; (d) ∂A/∂x_p^i approximated by perturbing each pursuer by h horizontally and vertically and recomputing A; the strategy form is unchanged.
- **No capture theorem in the general case** [author-stated, p. 69]: "currently no proof has been found to guarantee capture in the non-convex case, although the area minimization strategy works well empirically." In convex domains with faster pursuers the guarantee "holds trivially" (e.g., match the evader's speed and run the equal-speed algorithm).
- **Pathology for slower pursuers (Fig. 3, p. 69)**: if a pursuer is slower than the evader, S_e can *grow* as the pursuer approaches — area minimization then likely fails; hence the requirement v_{i,max} ≥ v_{e,max}.
- **Non-convex asymmetry (Fig. 4, p. 69)**: the area game is no longer symmetric: an evader turning around an obstacle moves distance ε toward boundary point x_1 and simultaneously decreases its distance to x_2 by ε, while the pursuer maintaining x_1 on ∂S_e moves less than ε toward x_2 — so x_2 enters S_e regardless of the pursuer input; the evader can grow its safe-reachable set.
- **Dynamics-agnostic feature (p. 69)**: S_e is defined without explicit dependence on agent dynamics; FMM formulations extend to Dubins-car dynamics (Takei & Tsai 2013) and spatially varying speed profiles (Zhou et al. 2012), so the algorithm applies whenever S_e is efficiently computable.

### 5.8 Summary of the analytic skeleton

Formal items: Lemma 1 (directional derivatives), Theorem 2 (midpoint characterization), Theorem 3 (energy-based finite-time capture). Note there is **no Theorem 1** in the paper (numbering jumps from Lemma 1 to Theorem 2 — verified; presumably inherited from the CDC 2011 antecedent). Equations (1)–(4) model; (5)–(8) gradient machinery; (9)–(10) midpoint proof; (11)–(13) safe-reachable characterization; two unnumbered strategy equations in Sec. 4.2. All formal guarantees are for convex Ω with equal unit speeds and kinematic dynamics; everything beyond is heuristic/numerical.

Findings as printed: convex case clearly superior with a somewhat bimodal distribution; simple non-convex still a large majority in favor; complex non-convex shows decline because obstacles create corridors of width comparable to the (unreported) capture radius where pure pursuit still "traps" the evader — in the limit of a single long narrow corridor the two strategies should coincide; with 1 pursuer in the complex domain only 34% of trials were faster, with a long tail of losses attributed partly to numerical errors in the area differentiation and to evader escapes from confined regions; clear superiority with 2–3 pursuers demonstrates the benefit of cooperation. Capture time decreases near-linearly with few pursuers and sublinearly as redundant (non-active-boundary, direct-pursuit) pursuers accumulate; variance shrinks with more players since some pursuer is then likely to start in an advantageous position (p. 71).

No hardware/human-subject experiments are reported in this paper; the conclusions (Sec. 6) reference the companion line of work on reachable-set visualization for human agents (Chen, Zhou & Tomlin 2014a,b; Huang et al. 2011; Takei et al. 2012), noting GPS noise and time delay degraded optimal headings in practice while the reachable-set visualization still guided human agents correctly.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues

**Author-stated**:
1. Formal capture guarantee requires convex domain + kinematic dynamics + equal speeds; for the safe-reachable-set generalization "currently no proof has been found to guarantee capture in the non-convex case" (p. 69) — cooperation is validated only empirically there.
2. The general algorithm "is only effective in cases where the pursuers are at least as fast as the evader" — otherwise S_e may shrink as pursuers retreat (Fig. 3, p. 69).
3. Singular surfaces in non-convex domains (multiple inputs with identical area derivative) require an arbitrary tie-break (max distance decrease) (p. 68).
4. HJI comparison limited to a single pursuer by computational complexity; numerical differentiation of grid value functions causes sub-optimal actions (29%/8.5% inversion statistics, p. 69).
5. Complex non-convex environments erode the advantage (corridors of width ≈ capture radius), down to 34% wins at 1 pursuer with a long loss tail "in part due to numerical errors in the area differentiation" (p. 70).
6. Practical GPS noise and time delay can make optimal headings unreliable (Sec. 6, p. 71).

**Critical observations (with page/eq.)**:
7. *Sign slip in Eq. (10)* (p. 67): as printed, (l_i − (L_i/2))/(‖ξ_i‖/2) = +(2l_i − L_i)/‖ξ_i‖, contradicting the printed right-hand side −(2l_i − L_i)/‖ξ_i‖; the numerator should be (L_i/2 − l_i). Conclusion of Theorem 2 unaffected (Eq. (9) verified exactly).
8. *Non-exhaustive case split in Theorem 3* (p. 67): condition 2 "ż > c(β)" leaves (Ȧ ≥ −β, −c(β) < ż ≤ c(β)) uncovered; it should read "ż > −c(β)" for the dichotomy attributed to Lemmas 4–5 of Huang et al. (2011) to be exhaustive. With that reading the proof closes; as printed there is a gap.
9. *Imported lemmas*: Lemma 1, the Ȧ ≤ 0 property, and the capture dichotomy all lean on Huang et al. (2011); the claimed "simpler proof" is an energy wrapper around prior results, not a self-contained derivation. The multi-pursuer extension of Theorem 3 is a single sentence (p. 67) — the interaction of several lines of control inside one energy argument is not re-derived.
10. *Undefined symbols*: l_i (offset along B_i) and l_max (maximal in-domain distance) are used in Lemma 1 and Theorem 3 without formal definition in this paper (deferred to Huang et al. 2011); the capture-time bound t_cap ≤ E(0)/c(β) is immediate from Theorem 3 but never stated, and constants β, c(β) are not computed for any concrete domain.
11. *Theorem numbering*: no Theorem 1 exists (Lemma 1 → Theorem 2 → Theorem 3), suggesting numbering inherited from the conference antecedent.
12. *Evaluation scope*: the evader in multi-pursuer trials uses a fixed heuristic (farthest geodesic point of S_e), not an adversarially optimal strategy, so Table 1/2 percentages measure performance against one behavioral model; r_c, domain sizes, and FMM grid resolution are UNREPORTED, limiting reproducibility; only kinematic equal-speed players are simulated despite the dynamics-agnostic claims (Dubins-car results are deferred to references).
13. *Conceptual*: the Voronoi boundary for equal speeds is the degenerate Apollonius barrier (perpendicular bisector); the paper never makes this (or any barrier/Isaacs-solution) connection explicit — the relation of the "line of control" to the classical Apollonius-circle analysis of reach-avoid games is left implicit.

## 8. Citation Value

This is the journal archetype of the "Voronoi/area-minimization cooperative pursuit" line: it converts a high-dimensional multi-agent differential game into N independent low-dimensional gradient descent problems coupled only through a shared geometric partition, and it is one of the few cooperative pursuit papers with an explicit finite-time capture proof for a *decentralized, implementable* law (energy function E = kA + ‖x_e − x_p‖² decreasing at rate c(β)). Cite it (a) as the canonical reference for Voronoi-partition-based cooperative pursuit and the "line of control" concept in convex domains with equal speeds; (b) for the safe-reachable-set generalization connecting pursuit to fast-marching/Eikonal reachability in non-convex domains with unequal speeds and richer dynamics; (c) as the standard empirical baseline (against pure pursuit and grid-based HJI) in multi-pursuer capture studies; or (d) as the journal version of Huang et al. (CDC 2011) when tracing provenance of the midpoint/area-gradient strategy. Typical citation sentence: "Geometric decentralized laws that provably capture a single evader in finite time exist for convex domains with equal speeds — e.g., the Voronoi area-minimization strategy of Zhou et al. (2016), where each pursuer drives toward the midpoint of its line of control so that the evader's cell area decreases at a linear rate — but no such guarantee is known for the safe-reachable-set generalization to non-convex domains."
