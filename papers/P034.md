## 2. Research Background
The capture-the-flag (CTF) game — two teams, each trying to seize the opponent's flag and return it home — had previously been studied mainly through robotic testbeds ([9] Blake et al., ICRA 2004, human-assisted CTF with UAV + ground robots + operator; [10]–[12] RoboFlag competitions/testbeds, ACC 2003) and, most relevantly, through a *numerical* differential-game formulation by Huang, Ding, Zhang, and Tomlin [13] (IEEE TCST 2015) and [14] (ICRA 2011). This paper adopts the [13] formulation — CTF reduced to a two-agent reach-avoid-type game between an Attacker (A) and a Defender (D) played in two phases — and replaces numerics by the classical Isaacs machinery [15], [16]: saddle-point state-feedback strategies, Value function, barrier surfaces, and HJI verification. The structural novelty it introduces relative to [13] is a terminal (Mayer) cost in each phase, which turns the pure Game-of-Kind question ("who wins?") into a Game of Degree ("how close does A get to the flag / to its safe zone under optimal play?"). The technical toolkit is the same line the authors used for active defense [8] and target guarding [20]: with equal-speed simple motion, optimal trajectories are straight lines and the game geometry reduces to orthogonal bisectors (point capture) or a hyperbola (positive capture radius).

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
**Problem.** A and D move at equal constant speeds (normalized $V_A = V_D = 1$) in a compact convex game set $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^2$; the Flag $F$ is at the origin (WLOG) in the interior of $\Omega$; the safe zone is $S = \{(x,y) \in \mathbb{R}^2 \mid x \ge x_s\}$, $x_s > 0$ (p. 4168). *Phase I*: A tries to capture $F$; D tries to intercept A first. *Phase II* (only if A captured $F$): A runs for $S$; D tries to intercept A before it reaches $S$. The paper solves both Games of Degree in closed form and certifies the solutions via the HJI equation.

**Gap as claimed by the authors:**
1. [authors' claim] In [13], [14] the CTF scenario "was formulated as a differential game" but solved numerically; this paper provides "analytical solutions … as opposed to the numerical results in [13]" (p. 4167). *Verified from the cited titles/formats: [13] is "Automation-assisted capture-the-flag: a differential game approach," IEEE TCST vol. 23 no. 3, pp. 1014–1028, 2015 (a Hilbert-space/HJB numerical treatment); the characterization is the authors' summary of that work and was not re-derived here.*
2. [authors' claim] "Different from [13], we introduce a terminal cost/payoff": in Phase I it is the (square of the) distance between A and $F$ at the instant D intercepts A; in Phase II it is the distance between A and its safe zone at that instant (p. 4167, realized as $\Phi = x_{Af}$, p. 4169). *This is indeed absent from the paper's own statement of [13]'s formulation and is the paper's modeling delta.*
3. [authors' claim] The paper "prove[s] the correctness of the solution" à la Isaacs/Başar–Olsder [15], [16]: for each phase the Value is obtained, shown continuous, continuously differentiable (with one dispersal-surface exception in Phase II), and shown to satisfy the HJI PDE (p. 4167). *True for Phase I point capture (full gradient/HJI computation given); for Phase II and for $r_D > 0$ these properties are asserted without proof (see Sec. 7).*
4. **Explicitly open (authors)**: the Game of Kind for each phase — i.e., the barrier dividing $\Omega$ into winning regions — is *not* solved here; "An important problem is the Game of Kind in each phase" (p. 4171), and the inter-phase "Transition Stage" optimization is posed as future work (p. 4171–4172). Also open: closed-form solution for $r_F > 0$ ("Existence of such closed form solutions is expected based on the work in [20]", p. 4169).

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Theory (Phase I, point capture)**: closed-form state-feedback saddle-point strategies for the Game of Degree in $\mathcal{R}_{D_1}$ (Theorems 1–2): both players aim at the point of the orthogonal bisector of $\overline{AD}$ closest to $F$; Value $V(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{4}(x_A^2+y_A^2-x_D^2-y_D^2)^2/((x_A-x_D)^2+(y_A-y_D)^2)$ (eq. (17)); proof that $V$ is $C^1$ and satisfies the HJI equation (only fully worked proof in the paper).
2. **Theory (Phase II, point capture)**: closed-form feedback strategies and Value for the Game of Degree in $\mathcal{R}_{D_2}$ (Theorems 3–4): the aimpoint is the intersection of the orthogonal bisector of $\overline{AD}$ with the game-set $y$-boundary favored by A; piecewise-defined Value (eq. (25)) that is continuous everywhere and $C^1$ except on the dispersal surface $\mathcal{D} = \{\mathbf{x} \mid y_A = y_D\}$; HJI satisfaction asserted.
3. **Modeling**: a two-phase, two-termination-set zero-sum differential game ([17] Getz–Leitmann, [18] Getz–Pachter, [19] Ardema et al. combat games) with distinct Mayer payoffs per phase and opposite min–max orders — Phase I $V = \min_{u_A}\max_{u_D} J$ (eq. (7)), Phase II $V = \max_{u_A}\min_{u_D} J$ (eq. (14)) — plus the security-strategy interpretation of the feedback solutions (deviations by either player can only worsen their own guaranteed outcome).
4. **Theory (positive capture radius)**: geometric solution of Phase I with $r_D > 0$ (Theorem 5): the interception point lies on the branch of a hyperbola with foci $A$, $D$ and transverse semi-axis $a = r_D/2$; the optimal aimpoint is found via a quartic equation in $\nu = e^{\mu}$ ($\mu$ = hyperbolic angle); Value = squared distance from $F$ to the quartic-selected point (p. 4170–4171).
5. **Experiment/illustration**: four trajectory case studies (Figs. 2–4) demonstrating (i) optimality (straight-line, time-invariant aimpoint), (ii) the saddle/security property under momentary non-optimal play by either player, and (iii) the qualitative importance of the Defender Transition Stage when $\mathbf{x}_0 \in \mathcal{R}_{A_1}$.

## 5. Key Mathematical Results

### 5.1 Setup: dynamics, information, termination, payoffs (p. 4167–4169)
- **Dynamics (eq. (1))**: simple motion at unit speed, $\dot{x}_A = \cos\theta_A,\ \dot{y}_A = \sin\theta_A,\ \dot{x}_D = \cos\theta_D,\ \dot{y}_D = \sin\theta_D$, with initial condition $(x_{A_0}, y_{A_0}, x_{D_0}, y_{D_0})$; controls $\theta_A, \theta_D \in [-\pi, \pi]$; state $\mathbf{x} = (x_A, y_A, x_D, y_D) \in \mathbb{R}^4$; $\dot{\mathbf{x}} = f(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{u}_A, \mathbf{u}_D)$. Full state feedback; **no assumption on the opponent's controller** (p. 4167, 4171).
- **Game set and objects**: $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ compact convex; $F$ at $\mathbf{x}_F = (0,0)$ (WLOG); $S = \{(x,y): x \ge x_s\}$ (p. 4168). One Attacker, one Defender; flag radius $r_F \ge 0$, Defender capture radius $r_D \ge 0$.
- **Phase I termination (eqs. (2)–(4))**: $\mathcal{T}_1 = \mathcal{T}_{A_1} \cup \mathcal{T}_{D_1}$, $\mathcal{T}_{A_1} = \{\mathbf{x} \mid \sqrt{x_A^2+y_A^2} = r_F\}$ (A captures flag), $\mathcal{T}_{D_1} = \{\mathbf{x} \mid \sqrt{(x_A-x_D)^2+(y_A-y_D)^2} = r_D\}$ (D intercepts A).
- **Phase I Game of Kind / winning regions (p. 4168)**: the two termination sets make this a two-target game [17]–[19]; the Barrier divides $\Omega$ into $\mathcal{R}_{A_1}$ (A captures $F$ under optimal play; play ends in $\mathcal{T}_{A_1}$) and $\mathcal{R}_{D_1}$ (D intercepts before flag capture; ends in $\mathcal{T}_{D_1}$). **The only characterization given**: $\mathcal{R}_{D_1}$ is the set of states where "$F$ and $D$ are in the same side of the orthogonal bisector of $AD$" (p. 4169) — i.e., for $r_F = 0$ point capture at equal speeds, D wins Phase I iff $\overline{FD} < \overline{FA}$; the barrier surface itself is never computed.
- **Phase I Game of Degree (eqs. (5)–(7))**: restricted to $\mathcal{R}_{D_1}$; Mayer payoff $J = \Phi(\mathbf{x}_f)$, $\Phi(\mathbf{x}_f) = x_{A_f}^2 + y_{A_f}^2$ (eq. (6)) — the squared distance from A to $F$ at interception ($\mathbf{x}_f$ satisfies (4), so for $r_D = 0$ the terminal positions of A and D coincide at the interception point $I = (x,y)$). Value $V(\mathbf{x}_0) := \min_{\mathbf{u}_A(\cdot)} \max_{\mathbf{u}_D(\cdot)} J$ (eq. (7)): **A minimizes, D maximizes** the terminal distance to the flag.
- **Co-state and Hamiltonian (eqs. (8)–(9))**: $\boldsymbol{\lambda}^T = (\lambda_{x_A}, \lambda_{y_A}, \lambda_{x_D}, \lambda_{y_D})$; $\mathcal{H} = \lambda_{x_A}\cos\theta_A + \lambda_{y_A}\sin\theta_A + \lambda_{x_D}\cos\theta_D + \lambda_{y_D}\sin\theta_D$. $\mathcal{H}$ is **separable** in $\theta_A$ and $\theta_D$, hence $\min_{\theta_A}\max_{\theta_D}\mathcal{H} = \max_{\theta_D}\min_{\theta_A}\mathcal{H}$ and Isaacs' condition holds (p. 4168).
- **Phase II termination (eqs. (10)–(12))**: $\mathcal{T}_2 = \mathcal{T}_{A_2} \cup \mathcal{T}_{D_2}$, $\mathcal{T}_{A_2} = \{\mathbf{x} \mid x_A = x_s\}$ (A reaches $S$), $\mathcal{T}_{D_2}$ as in (12). Winning regions $\mathcal{R}_{A_2}$, $\mathcal{R}_{D_2}$ defined analogously; **no barrier characterization is derived for Phase II** (illustrated qualitatively in Fig. 4 only). Game of Degree in $\mathcal{R}_{D_2}$: $\Phi(\mathbf{x}_f) = x_{A_f}$ (eq. (13)); "A aims at maximizing $x_{A_f}$ while D tries to minimize it" (p. 4169) — the equivalent formulation of minimizing/maximizing the terminal A–$S$ separation $x_s - x_A$. Value $V(\mathbf{x}_0) := \max_{\mathbf{u}_A(\cdot)} \min_{\mathbf{u}_D(\cdot)} J$ (eq. (14)): **note the reversed order** relative to (7), consistent with the reversed player roles in the payoff.
- **HJI form used (p. 4169–4170)**: $-\partial V/\partial t = \frac{\partial V}{\partial \mathbf{x}}\cdot f(\mathbf{x}, \theta_A^*, \theta_D^*) + g(t,\mathbf{x},\theta_A^*,\theta_D^*)$; here $\partial V/\partial t = 0$ (Mayer, time-invariant $V$) and $g = 0$.

### 5.2 Theorem 1 + Theorem 2 — Phase I, point capture $r_D = 0$ (p. 4169–4170)
- **Theorem 1 (p. 4169)**: in Phase I in $\mathcal{R}_{D_1}$, "assume the interception point is unconstrained": the headings of A and D are **constant** under optimal play and the optimal trajectories are **straight lines**. *No proof is given* (the paper only proceeds "From Theorem 1 we know that the interception point $I=(x,y)$ lies on the orthogonal bisector of the segment $\overline{AD}$"). Physical meaning: with equal speeds and point capture, an interception at $I$ requires $\overline{AI} = \overline{DI}$ (equal travel times), i.e., $I$ on the bisector.
- **Theorem 2 (p. 4169, eqs. (15)–(17))**: for $\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{R}_{D_1}$, $r_D = 0$ (and $r_F = 0$), the optimal state-feedback strategies are
  $$\cos\hat\theta_A^* = \frac{x^*-x_A}{\sqrt{(x^*-x_A)^2+(y^*-y_A)^2}},\quad \sin\hat\theta_A^* = \frac{y^*-y_A}{\sqrt{(x^*-x_A)^2+(y^*-y_A)^2}},$$
  and identically for D with $x_D, y_D$ (eq. (15)) — both players head to the **optimal interception point**
  $$x^* = \frac{x_A^2+y_A^2-x_D^2-y_D^2}{(x_A-x_D)^2+(y_A-y_D)^2}\cdot\frac{x_A-x_D}{2},\qquad y^* = \frac{x_A^2+y_A^2-x_D^2-y_D^2}{(x_A-x_D)^2+(y_A-y_D)^2}\cdot\frac{y_A-y_D}{2}\ (\text{eq. (16)}),$$
  which is the point of the orthogonal bisector of $\overline{AD}$ **closest to $F$**, and the Value function is
  $$V(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{4}\,\frac{(x_A^2+y_A^2-x_D^2-y_D^2)^2}{(x_A-x_D)^2+(y_A-y_D)^2}\ (\text{eq. (17)}),$$
  which is $C^1$ and satisfies the HJI equation.
- **Proof structure (the only complete proof in the paper)**: (i) bisector line $y - y_o = m_o(x - x_o)$ (eq. (18)) with slope $m_o = -(x_A - x_D)/(y_A - y_D)$ (eq. (19)) and midpoint $(x_o, y_o) = (\tfrac{1}{2}(x_A+x_D), \tfrac{1}{2}(y_A+y_D))$ (eq. (20)); (ii) the closest point of (18) to $F$ lies on the perpendicular through $F$, $y = mx$ with $m = -1/m_o = (y_A-y_D)/(x_A-x_D)$ (eq. (21)); solving $mx = y_o + m_o(x - x_o)$ yields (16), and $V = x^{*2} + y^{*2}$ rearranges to (17) — the squared distance from $F$ to the bisector, i.e., $V = \Delta_0^2/(4D)$ with $\Delta_0 := x_A^2+y_A^2-x_D^2-y_D^2$ and $D := (x_A-x_D)^2+(y_A-y_D)^2$. Continuity: $D \neq 0$ off the terminal set. (iii) $C^1$: the four partials are printed explicitly (unnumbered, p. 4169), e.g. $\partial V/\partial x_A = \frac{x_A\Delta_0}{D} - \frac{(x_A-x_D)\Delta_0^2}{2D^2}$; gradient continuous since $D \neq 0$. (iv) HJI: the decisive intermediate identities (p. 4170, verified here independently) are
  $$x^* - x_A = -\frac{\partial V(\mathbf{x})}{\partial x_A}\cdot\frac{(x_A-x_D)^2+(y_A-y_D)^2}{x_A^2+y_A^2-x_D^2-y_D^2},\qquad x^* - x_D = +\frac{\partial V(\mathbf{x})}{\partial x_D}\cdot\frac{D}{\Delta_0}$$
  (and the $y$-analogs), so that, since $\Delta_0 > 0$ in $\mathcal{R}_{D_1}$,
  $$\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_A}\cos\hat\theta_A^* + \frac{\partial V}{\partial y_A}\sin\hat\theta_A^* = -\sqrt{\Big(\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_A}\Big)^2+\Big(\frac{\partial V}{\partial y_A}\Big)^2},\qquad \frac{\partial V}{\partial x_D}\cos\hat\theta_D^* + \frac{\partial V}{\partial y_D}\sin\hat\theta_D^* = +\sqrt{\Big(\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_D}\Big)^2+\Big(\frac{\partial V}{\partial y_D}\Big)^2}\ (\text{eq. (22)}),$$
  and direct evaluation gives both gradient norms the **same** closed form
  $$\frac{\Delta_0}{D^{3/2}}\Big(\tfrac{1}{4}\Delta_0^2 + (x_A^2+y_A^2)(x_D^2+y_D^2 - x_Ax_D - y_Ay_D) + (x_D^2+y_D^2)(x_A^2+y_A^2 - x_Ax_D - y_Ay_D)\Big)^{1/2}\ (\text{eq. (23)}),$$
  whence $\frac{\partial V}{\partial \mathbf{x}}\cdot f(\mathbf{x}, \hat\theta_A^*, \hat\theta_D^*) = -\|\nabla_A V\| + \|\nabla_D V\| = 0$, completing the HJI verification. *Note: eq. (23)'s second (Defender) LHS is misprinted with $\partial x_A, \partial y_A$ subscripts (p. 4170); the intended statement $\|\nabla_A V\| = \|\nabla_D V\|$ follows from the $A \leftrightarrow D$ symmetry of $V = \Delta_0^2/(4D)$.* Local/global: the formula is global on $\mathcal{R}_{D_1} \setminus \mathcal{T}_{D_1}$; analytic, closed form.
- **Remark (p. 4170)**: if the closest bisector point lies outside $\Omega$, the optimal aimpoint is the intersection of the bisector with the boundary of $\Omega$ closest to $F$ (two such points by convexity).
- **Physical meaning / logic of the saddle point**: in $\mathcal{R}_{D_1}$ every straight path from $A$ to any bisector point stays strictly on A's side of the bisector except at the crossing point, so D cannot intercept A before A reaches the bisector; A therefore guarantees crossing at the bisector point closest to $F$, and D's aim there is the security strategy — if either player deviates, the continuously recomputed feedback moves the interception point against the deviator (demonstrated in Example 1; formal deviation calculus not given).
- **Extensibility**: $r_F > 0$ left open; existence expected from [20] (Pachter–Garcia–Casbeer, "Differential game of guarding a target," JGCD 2017). The min–max order in (7) with the separable Hamiltonian makes the Isaacs condition the certificate that the verification argument establishes the saddle point.

### 5.3 Theorem 3 + Theorem 4 — Phase II, point capture $r_D = 0$ (p. 4170)
- **Theorem 3 (p. 4170)**: in Phase II in $\mathcal{R}_{D_2}$ the headings are constant under optimal play and the optimal trajectories are straight lines. *No proof given.*
- **Theorem 4 (p. 4170, eqs. (24)–(25))**: for $\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{R}_{D_2}$, $r_D = 0$, write the orthogonal bisector of $\overline{AD}$ as $x = m_x y + n_x$ with
  $$m_x = -\frac{y_A - y_D}{x_A - x_D},\qquad n_x = \frac{1}{2}\,\frac{x_A^2 - x_D^2 + y_A^2 - y_D^2}{x_A - x_D},$$
  and let $\underline{y}$ and $\bar{y}$ denote the lower and upper boundary values of the game set along the $y$-axis ("With respect to Fig. 1", p. 4170). The optimal state-feedback strategies are (eq. (24)) $\cos\hat\theta_A^* = \frac{m_x y^* + n_x - x_A}{\sqrt{(m_xy^*+n_x-x_A)^2+(y^*-y_A)^2}}$, $\sin\hat\theta_A^* = \frac{y^*-y_A}{\sqrt{\cdots}}$, and identically for D, where the aimpoint sits on the game-set boundary:
  $$y^* = \bar{y}\ \ \text{if } m_o > 0,\qquad y^* = \underline{y}\ \ \text{if } m_o < 0 \quad (m_o = 1/m_x\ \text{is the eq. (19) bisector slope}),$$
  i.e., the point of the bisector segment inside $\Omega$ with the **largest $x$-coordinate** — the crossing most favorable to A, who maximizes $x_{A_f}$. The Value is piecewise (eq. (25)):
  $$V_1(\mathbf{x}) = -\frac{y_A - y_D}{x_A - x_D}\,\bar y + \frac{1}{2}\,\frac{x_A^2 - x_D^2 + y_A^2 - y_D^2}{x_A - x_D}\ \ (m_o > 0),\qquad V_2(\mathbf{x}) = -\frac{y_A - y_D}{x_A - x_D}\,\underline y + \frac{1}{2}\,\frac{x_A^2 - x_D^2 + y_A^2 - y_D^2}{x_A - x_D}\ \ (m_o < 0).$$
  $V(\mathbf{x})$ is **continuous everywhere**, continuously differentiable **except on the dispersal surface** $\mathcal{D} = \{\mathbf{x} \mid y_A = y_D\}$, and satisfies the HJI equation. *No proof is given for any of these claims.* Checks performed here: at $y_A = y_D$, $m_x = 0$ and both branches give $V_1 = V_2 = n_x = \tfrac{1}{2}(x_A + x_D)$ (with $y_A = y_D$), so continuity holds and only the gradient jumps — consistent with a dispersal surface where A may aim high or low. Verified against Example 2: $A_0 = (0,0)$, $D_0 = (5,-1)$ give $m_x = 0.2$, $n_x = 2.6$, $m_o = 5 > 0 \Rightarrow y^* = \bar y = 6$, $x^* = 3.8 = V(\mathbf{x})$ exactly as printed (p. 4171).
- **Remark (p. 4170)**: although for $r_F = 0$ Phase II starts at $x_A = y_A = 0$, Theorem 4 is stated for general $(x_A, y_A)$ because the solution is a state feedback, re-implemented in closed loop throughout Phase II ("The solution is continuously recomputed using the current positions of the two players").
- **Physical meaning**: the bisector is again the equal-time locus; since A crosses it at a point of its choosing and D cannot preempt (straight path stays in A's half-plane), A picks the maximum-$x$ crossing; D's guarantee is the feedback aim at the same point — a D deviation only increases $x^*$ (Fig. 3: $3.8217$, $3.8451 > 3.8$), an A deviation lets D reduce it.

### 5.4 Theorem 5 — Phase I with positive capture radius $r_D > 0$ (p. 4170–4171)
- **Geometry (p. 4170–4171)**: with terminal A-position $I = (x,y)$ at the interception instant ($\sqrt{(x_A-x_D)^2+(y_A-y_D)^2} = r_D$ at $t_f$), simultaneous reachability at unit speeds requires
  $$\sqrt{(x - x_D)^2 + (y - y_D)^2} = r_D + \sqrt{(x - x_A)^2 + (y - y_A)^2},$$
  "the equation of an hyperbola whose foci are $A$ and $D$" — only A's branch is of interest. In canonical form (centered at $(x_o, y_o)$ of eq. (20), rotated by $\lambda = \arctan\frac{y_A - y_D}{x_A - x_D}$):
  $$\frac{x^2}{a^2} - \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1\ (\text{eq. (26)}),\qquad r = \sqrt{(x_A-x_D)^2+(y_A-y_D)^2},\quad a = \frac{r_D}{2},\quad b = \tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{r^2 - r_D^2},$$
  parameterized by the hyperbolic angle $\mu$: $x = a\cosh\mu$, $y = b\sinh\mu$; the world-frame map is $x := x\cos\lambda - y\sin\lambda + x_o$, $y := x\sin\lambda + y\cos\lambda + y_o$. ("As in Theorem 1 one can show that the optimal headings are constant" — again asserted.)
- **Theorem 5 (p. 4171, eq. (27))**: for $\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{R}_{D_1}$, $r_D > 0$, the optimal headings aim at
  $$x^* = a\cos\lambda\cosh\mu^* - b\sin\lambda\sinh\mu^* + x_o,\qquad y^* = a\sin\lambda\cosh\mu^* + b\cos\lambda\sinh\mu^* + y_o,$$
  where $\mu^* = \ln\nu^*$ and $\nu^*$ is the (positive real) solution of the quartic equation
  $$\frac{r^2}{8}\nu^4 + \big[(a\cos\lambda - b\sin\lambda)x_o + (a\sin\lambda + b\cos\lambda)y_o\big]\nu^3 - \big[(a\cos\lambda + b\sin\lambda)x_o - (a\sin\lambda - b\cos\lambda)y_o\big]\nu - \frac{r^2}{8} = 0$$
  that minimizes the cost $J = (a\cos\lambda\cosh\mu - b\sin\lambda\sinh\mu + x_o)^2 + (a\sin\lambda\cosh\mu + b\cos\lambda\sinh\mu + y_o)^2$. The Value function is $V(\mathbf{x}) = (x^*)^2 + (y^*)^2$; "In addition, the Value function $V(\mathbf{x})$ is $C^1$ and it satisfies the HJI equation." *No proof is given for any part of Theorem 5.*
- **Derivation reconstructed and verified here**: substituting $\cosh\mu = \frac{\nu + \nu^{-1}}{2}$, $\sinh\mu = \frac{\nu - \nu^{-1}}{2}$ into $dJ/d\mu = 0$ and clearing denominators yields exactly
  $$\frac{r^2}{8}\nu^4 + \big[(a\cos\lambda - b\sin\lambda)x_o + (a\sin\lambda + b\cos\lambda)y_o\big]\nu^3 - \big[(a\cos\lambda + b\sin\lambda)x_o + (a\sin\lambda - b\cos\lambda)y_o\big]\nu - \frac{r^2}{8} = 0,$$
  using $a^2 + b^2 = r^2/4$ — i.e., **the printed $\nu$-coefficient's sign of the $y_o$ term is a typo** (printed "$-(a\sin\lambda - b\cos\lambda)y_o$", should be "$+$"). Numerical confirmation (config $A=(1,2)$, $D=(-1,0)$, $r_D = 0.5$): direct minimization of $J$ gives $\nu^* = 0.6433615$; the corrected quartic's unique positive real root is $0.64336147$; the printed quartic's is $0.9424842$. The aimpoint is the point of A's hyperbola branch closest to $F$ — the $r_D > 0$ analog of Theorem 2's closest-bisector point; the saddle-point logic (A crosses the "interception hyperbola" where it chooses; D's security strategy aims at the same point) mirrors Sec. 5.2 but is not formalized.
- **Scope**: Theorem 5 covers Phase I only; **Phase II with $r_D > 0$ is not treated anywhere in the paper**.

### 5.5 Saddle-point (security) inequalities and Game-of-Kind status
- Phase I (p. 4171): $V(\mathbf{x};\hat\theta_A, \theta_D) \ge V(\mathbf{x};\hat\theta_A, \hat\theta_D) \ge V(\mathbf{x};\theta_A, \hat\theta_D)$ — D maximizes the terminal distance; if A does not play optimally, D intercepts while *increasing* the terminal payoff.
- Phase II (p. 4171): $V(\mathbf{x};\theta_A, \hat\theta_D^*) \le V(\mathbf{x};\hat\theta_A^*, \hat\theta_D^*) \le V(\mathbf{x};\hat\theta_A^*, \theta_D)$ — A maximizes the terminal $x$-coordinate; if D deviates even momentarily, A reduces its distance to $S$.
- **Barrier/winning regions**: never computed. $\mathcal{R}_{D_1}$ is characterized only qualitatively ("F and D on the same side of the orthogonal bisector of $\overline{AD}$", p. 4169); $\mathcal{R}_{A_1}$ is its complement within $\Omega$; $\mathcal{R}_{A_2}$, $\mathcal{R}_{D_2}$ are used only through the worked Transition-Stage example (Fig. 4): for $\mathbf{x}_0 \in \mathcal{R}_{A_1}$ with $A_0 = (3,-3)$, $D_0 = (3,4)$, if D aims at $F$ during Phase I then at the start of Phase II $\mathbf{x}_0 \in \mathcal{R}_{A_2}$ and "A can win the game by choosing its aimpoint anywhere below the point $(x_s, -5.326)$ and within the game set", whereas a defensive D heading instead yields $\mathbf{x}_0 \in \mathcal{R}_{D_2}$ and D wins via Theorem 4 (p. 4172).

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
**Author-stated:**
1. The Game of Kind is unsolved: "An important problem is the Game of Kind in each phase and in general, that is, to determine which player will win the game. Determining if A can survive both phases is dependent on the Transition Stage" (p. 4171). The barrier surfaces dividing $\Omega$ into $\mathcal{R}_{A_i}, \mathcal{R}_{D_i}$ are never constructed.
2. The Defender Transition Stage (Phase I $\to$ Phase II) is open; proposed as a fixed-terminal-time optimal control problem, $t_f = \overline{AF}/V_A$, minimizing the Phase-II $x^*$ (p. 4171–4172).
3. $r_F > 0$ closed-form solution left for future work; existence "expected" from [20] (p. 4169).
4. The title/abstract scope is a "simplified version" of CTF: one Attacker, one Defender, point flag, 2-D, unit speeds, no obstacles/teams (p. 4172).

**Critical observations (with page/eq.):**
1. **Proof gaps**: Theorems 1, 3, 4, and 5 are stated without proofs; only Theorem 2 carries a proof, and it is a *verification* proof (continuity, $C^1$, HJI at the saddle controls). The optimality/saddle argument for why the same aimpoint serves both adversarial players (A crossing the bisector/hyperbola at its preferred point; D unable to preempt) is given only heuristically and numerically; no formal verification theorem (e.g., Isaacs' or Başar–Olsder's verification lemma) is invoked to close min–max $\to$ saddle $\to$ Value, despite eq. (7) vs eq. (14) using different minimax orders in the two phases.
2. **Typo in the Theorem 5 quartic (p. 4171)**: the printed $\nu$-coefficient bracket "$[(a\cos\lambda + b\sin\lambda)x_o - (a\sin\lambda - b\cos\lambda)y_o]$" should read "$[(a\cos\lambda + b\sin\lambda)x_o + (a\sin\lambda - b\cos\lambda)y_o]$"; independently re-derived symbolically and confirmed numerically (printed version's root $0.9425$ vs. true minimizer $0.6433615$ for the test configuration above). Users of the formula must correct the sign.
3. **Typo in eq. (23) (p. 4170)**: the "corresponding expression for the Defender" repeats $\partial V/\partial x_A, \partial V/\partial y_A$ on its LHS instead of $\partial x_D, \partial y_D$; the intended content is $\|\nabla_A V\| = \|\nabla_D V\|$ (true by the $A\leftrightarrow D$ symmetry of $V = \Delta_0^2/(4D)$).
4. **Example 1 Value inconsistency (p. 4171)**: eq. (17) evaluated at $A_0 = (4,-5)$, $D_0 = (2,4)$ gives $V = 441/340 \approx 1.2971$ (squared distance; $= 1.1387$ as a distance), whereas the paper reports $V(\mathbf{x}) = 1.1531$; Fig. 2.a's plotted interception point $\approx (0.25, -1.1)$ matches eq. (16) exactly, so the closed-form solution is unaffected — the printed $1.1531$ (and $1.2230$) appear to be terminal distances from an (unreported, presumably discrete-time) closed-loop simulation rather than the Value of eq. (17). The saddle ordering $1.2230 > 1.1531$ is consistent only under the distance interpretation.
5. **Minor print errors**: "$y \in [6,6]$" for the Example 1 game set (should be $[-6,6]$, p. 4171); "$x \in \mathcal{R}_D$" for "$\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{R}_{D_1}$" (p. 4169).
6. **Structural restrictions**: the Phase II aimpoint is defined via the bisector's intersection with the $y$-boundaries $\bar y, \underline y$ of the game set; the case where the bisector exits $\Omega$ through its vertical ($x$) sides first is not handled (the analogous clipping is discussed only for Phase I, p. 4170 Remark). The Phase II dispersal surface is identified as $\{y_A = y_D\}$ but no global Game-of-Kind boundary for Phase II (against the half-plane $S$) is derived. Phase II with $r_D > 0$ is absent. Multi-agent CTF (teams, multiple flags) — the actual game of [9]–[12] — is out of scope.
7. **No empirical benchmarking**: the examples are illustrative single runs; there is no quantitative comparison with the numerical differential-game solution of [13], no robustness/noise study, and the simulation environment/discretization is unreported (so the reported scalars are not reproducible as printed).

## 8. Citation Value
This is the analytic companion to Huang et al.'s numerical TCST 2015 capture-the-flag differential game and a representative of the AFRL/AFIT school's "closed-form Isaacs solution + HJI verification" template (alongside [8] active defense and [20] target guarding). Cite it (a) as the canonical two-phase CTF differential game with Mayer terminal payoffs and state-feedback saddle-point solutions built on orthogonal-bisector geometry; (b) for the Phase II dispersal-surface example ($\{y_A = y_D\}$) and the boundary-intersection aimpoint rule, a clean pedagogical instance of a dispersal surface in a reach-avoid-type game; (c) for the hyperbola-plus-quartic construction of the optimal interception point when the Defender has a positive capture radius; and (d) as the explicit statement that the CTF Game of Kind (barrier/winning regions) and the inter-phase Transition Stage remained open — the hooks its own follow-ups and other barrier-derivation papers (e.g., Yan–Shi–Zhong [5]-style defense games) build on. Example citation sentence: "Garcia, Casbeer, and Pachter [P034] derived closed-form state-feedback saddle-point strategies and $C^1$ Value functions, verified against the Hamilton–Jacobi–Isaacs equation, for both phases of the two-player capture-the-flag differential game, leaving the Game of Kind (barrier characterization) and the Defender's Phase I–II transition stage open."
