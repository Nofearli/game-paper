## 2. Research Background

Differential game theory (Isaacs [1]) provides the natural framework for pursuit-evasion and reach-avoid conflicts between adversarial teams, but the Hamilton–Jacobi–Isaacs (HJI) equation is usually avoided because of its computational intractability: in reach-avoid games solved by viscosity/numerical HJI methods [10]–[13], computation time degrades severely as state dimension grows. Two-termination-set differential games (Getz–Leitmann [14], Getz–Pachter [15], Ardema–Heymann–Rajan [16]) partition the state space by a Barrier surface into winning regions, within each of which a different Game of Degree is played. Garcia, Casbeer and Pachter (AFRL/AFIT) have built a research line on *analytically solvable, verifiable* reach-avoid games (capture-the-flag [19], active defense [22]); this letter extends that program to 3D with two cooperative pursuers guarding a stationary point target against one evader. Motivation is autonomous aerial-robot conflict scenarios [25] that require fast, on-line, state-feedback guidance laws with robustness guarantees against adversarial actions, with applications beyond military missions (law enforcement, wildlife herding [26]–[28]).

## 3. Research Problem and Gap

**Problem.** Two pursuers $P_1, P_2$ cooperate to capture an evader $E$ before it reaches a stationary point target (the origin) in Euclidean 3D space; all three players have the same (normalized) unit speed. Solve both the Game of Kind (who wins) and the Game of Degree (value + saddle-point strategies), with verification.

**Gap, [authors' claim] (p. 1, col. 2):** "Similar work addressing reach-avoid games has been reported in [29]–[32] where several scenarios with large number of agents, different speed players, and players with positive capture radii have been considered. In particular, the recent paper [33] considered a similar problem in the 3D space with three pursuers and one evader; the main difference is that the target set is a plane in the 3D space. The main result in [33] is to solve the Game of Kind by determining the Barrier surface. In this paper we solve the Game of Kind and, more importantly, we synthesize and verify the saddle-point strategies, thus, providing a complete solution to the Game of Degree as well."

**[Verified from the text]**: Ref. [33] is Yan, Shi, Zhong, "Construction of the barrier for reach-avoid differential games in three-dimensional space with four equal-speed players," CDC 2019 — indeed only a Barrier (Game of Kind) construction. The letter's differentiator is HJI *verification* (p. 1: "Verification is often overlooked due to challenges in solving the HJI equation; however, verification is of great importance in order to guarantee that the unique saddle-point solution of the game has been in fact synthesized" — verification = obtaining a $C^1$ Value and proving it solves the HJI equation). The paper also introduces a genuinely cooperative element absent from "pairwise outcome" approaches [9]: the two pursuers may force *simultaneous* capture, which yields a strictly better payoff (capture point farther from the target) than either pursuer's individual solution — but only when the state lies in the simultaneous-capture subregion $\mathcal{R}_{ps}$; otherwise forcing simultaneous capture is counterproductive and the individual (one-on-one) solution is the team-optimal play (p. 4258, top). This degeneration logic (2v1 → 1v1 on part of $\mathcal{R}_p$) is the paper's practical contribution beyond geometry.

## 4. Core Contributions

1. **(Theory, Game of Kind)** Explicit Barrier function for the 2P1E target-guarding game in $\mathbb{R}^9$: $B(\mathbf{x}) = \max\{B_1(\mathbf{x}), B_2(\mathbf{x})\}$, $B_i = R_E^2 - R_i^2$ (eq. (21)), partitioning $\mathbb{R}^9$ into pursuer-team winning region $\mathcal{R}_p$ and evader winning region $\mathcal{R}_e$ (eq. (5)).
2. **(Theory, Game of Degree, 1v1 building block)** Theorem 2: closed-form $C^1$ Value $V = \frac{1}{4}(R_E^2-R_1^2)^2/\Gamma^2$ (eq. (13)) with verified HJI solution and straight-line saddle-point strategies (eqs. (14)–(15)) toward the foot of the perpendicular from the origin to the Apollonius bisector plane.
3. **(Theory, Game of Degree, 2v1 simultaneous capture)** Theorem 3: closed-form $C^1$ Value (eq. (22)) whose optimal aimpoint is the point of the *bisector-plane intersection line* closest to the origin (eqs. (23)–(26)); HJI verification via an explicit gradient computation ((29)–(35)). Corollary 1 handles the degenerate case $c=0$ (eqs. (36)–(39)) with full HJI verification; cases $a=0$, $b=0$, or two zero components asserted "similar" (p. 4263).
4. **(Modeling/decision logic)** Capture-type determination (Section V): a pair of signed tests $\Upsilon_1, \Upsilon_2$ (p. 4263) that decide, in closed loop, whether the team outcome is simultaneous capture (Theorem 3), single capture by $P_1$ or $P_2$ (Theorem 2 applied to the active pursuer), or best-of-two individual captures; explicitly distinguished from the "active pursuer" concept of [33] (p. 4263: "[33]'s active pursuer only has to do with construction of the boundary of the dominance region of the evader").
5. **(Experiment/robustness)** Four simulation examples (Figs. 3–6) quantifying the saddle-point guarantee: closed-loop implementation against a non-optimal evader improves the pursuers' payoff ($R_{Ef}^2 = 14.453 > V = 10.374$); non-cooperative pursuers degrade it ($R_{Ef}^2 = 9.8345 < V$); pure-pursuit pursuers lose the game outright (evader reaches the target).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results (MOST IMPORTANT)

### 5.0 Dynamics, information structure, cost, termination

- **Dynamics (eq. (1), p. 4258)**: single integrators in $\mathbb{R}^3$, $\dot{x}_E = u_x, \dot{y}_E = u_y, \dot{z}_E = u_z$; $\dot{x}_1 = v_x, \dot{y}_1 = v_y, \dot{z}_1 = v_z$; $\dot{x}_2 = w_x, \dot{y}_2 = w_y, \dot{z}_2 = w_z$. Complete state $\mathbf{x} := (\mathbf{x}_E, \mathbf{x}_1, \mathbf{x}_2) \in \mathbb{R}^9$; control set $\mathcal{U} := \{u \in \mathbb{R}^3 \mid \|u\|_2 = 1\}$ with controls $\mathbf{u}$ (E), $\mathbf{v}$ (P1), $\mathbf{w}$ (P2); speeds normalized equal ("Without loss of generality, the speeds have been normalized", p. 4258).
- **Information structure**: full-state feedback; "the players do not know the control implemented by the opponent. Each player only implements the state-feedback optimal strategies" (p. 4264). Non-anticipative (open-loop min–max values are written over feedback strategy classes; eqs. (8), (10)).
- **Termination (eqs. (2)–(4), p. 4258)**: two-termination-set game $\mathcal{T} := \mathcal{T}_p \cup \mathcal{T}_e$ with $\mathcal{T}_p := \{\mathbf{x} \mid \|\mathbf{x}_1 - \mathbf{x}_E\|_2 = 0\} \cup \{\mathbf{x} \mid \|\mathbf{x}_2 - \mathbf{x}_E\|_2 = 0\}$ (capture; pursuer team wins) and $\mathcal{T}_e := \{\mathbf{x} \mid \|\mathbf{x}_E\|_2 = 0\}$ (evader reaches the origin; evader wins). Terminal time $t_f$ = first instant $\mathbf{x}(t) \in \mathcal{T}$.
- **Cost / Game of Degree (eqs. (7)–(10), p. 4258)**:
  - If $\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{R}_p$: $J = \Phi_p(\mathbf{x}(t_f))$, $\Phi_p := x_{Ef}^2 + y_{Ef}^2 + z_{Ef}^2$ (eq. (7)); $V(\mathbf{x}_0) := \min_{\mathbf{u}(\cdot)} \max_{\mathbf{v}(\cdot),\mathbf{w}(\cdot)} J$ (eq. (8)). E minimizes its terminal distance to the target; pursuers maximize it — a practically meaningful payoff that penalizes sub-optimal pursuer play.
  - If $\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{R}_e$: $J = \Phi_e(\mathbf{x}(t_f))$, $\Phi_e := \min_i \{ (x_{Ef}-x_{if})^2 + (y_{Ef}-y_{if})^2 + (z_{Ef}-z_{if})^2 \}$, $i=1,2$ (eq. (9)); $V(\mathbf{x}_0) := \min_{\mathbf{v}(\cdot),\mathbf{w}(\cdot)} \max_{\mathbf{u}(\cdot)} J$ (eq. (10)). Pursuers, unable to capture, minimize terminal separation from E.
  - Printed erratum: eq. (8) literally prints $J(\mathbf{u}(\cdot), \mathbf{u}(\cdot), \mathbf{w}(\cdot); \mathbf{x}_0)$; the second argument should be $\mathbf{v}(\cdot)$ (verified on page image, p. 4258).
- **Barrier/winning regions (eqs. (5)–(6))**: $\mathcal{R}_p := \{\mathbf{x} \mid B(\mathbf{x}) > 0\}$, $\mathcal{R}_e := \{\mathbf{x} \mid B(\mathbf{x}) < 0\}$, $\mathcal{B} := \{\mathbf{x} \mid B(\mathbf{x}) = 0\}$.

### 5.1 Theorem 1 (p. 4258, no numbered equation)

**Statement.** "Consider the differential game (1), (7), (9). The regular optimal headings of E, $P_1$, and $P_2$ are constant under optimal play and their trajectories are straight lines."

**Assumptions.** Dynamics (1) (unit-speed single integrators), terminal-only cost (7) or (9); "regular" optimal play (the Hamiltonian minimization/maximization returns the co-state-aligned heading).

**Proof approach (Isaacs/Pontryagin, 3 steps).**
1. The Hamiltonian $H = \sum_j \lambda_{jE} u_j + \sum_j \lambda_{j1} v_j + \sum_j \lambda_{j2} w_j$, $j = x,y,z$, with co-state $\lambda^T = (\lambda_{xE},\lambda_{yE},\lambda_{zE},\lambda_{x1},\lambda_{y1},\lambda_{z1},\lambda_{x2},\lambda_{y2},\lambda_{z2}) \in \mathbb{R}^9$, is *linear* in all three controls; the pointwise min $\mathbf{u}$ / max $\mathbf{v}, \mathbf{w}$ aligns each control with its co-state block.
2. Since the dynamics are state-independent and the cost is terminal-only, the co-state dynamics are $\dot{\lambda}_{xE} = \dot{\lambda}_{yE} = \dots = \dot{\lambda}_{z2} = 0$ (all nine co-states constant).
3. Constant co-states ⇒ constant optimal headings ⇒ straight-line optimal trajectories. $\blacksquare$

**Decisive equation**: $\dot{\lambda} \equiv 0$ (p. 4259, top). **Physical meaning**: equal-speed, costless-running games in 3D inherit the 2D structure — optimal play is a "race" along straight lines to a fixed interception point. **Extensibility**: justifies the geometric aimpoint constructions of Theorems 2–3 and of the capture-type logic of Section V.

### 5.2 Barrier functions and the Game of Kind (Section III eq. (12); Section IV eqs. (20)–(21))

- 1v1: $R_E = \sqrt{x_E^2+y_E^2+z_E^2}$, $R_1 = \sqrt{x_1^2+y_1^2+z_1^2}$ (eq. (11)); $B : \mathbb{R}^6 \to \mathbb{R}$, $B(\mathbf{x}) = R_E^2 - R_1^2$ (eq. (12)).
- 2v1: additionally $R_2 = \sqrt{x_2^2+y_2^2+z_2^2}$ (eq. (20)); $B_1(\mathbf{x}) = R_E^2 - R_1^2$, $B_2(\mathbf{x}) = R_E^2 - R_2^2$, and
$$B(\mathbf{x}) = \max\{B_1(\mathbf{x}), B_2(\mathbf{x})\} \quad (21).$$
- **Rationale given (p. 4259)**: with equal speeds, each player's 3D dominance region is the half-space bounded by the plane orthogonal to segment $PE$ (the perpendicular bisector plane); $P$ wins iff the origin lies on $P$'s side ($R_1 < R_E \iff B > 0$); the limiting case is the origin *on* the plane. The 2v1 Barrier takes the max because the team wins if *either* pursuer individually guards the target.
- **Proof gap (critical observation)**: the Barrier (12), (21) is *asserted* from dominance-plane geometry and the two-target literature [14]–[16]; no formal Game-of-Kind proof (e.g., construction of a guaranteed evading trajectory for $B<0$ and a guaranteed guarding trajectory for $B>0$) is carried out, unlike [33] where Barrier construction is the main result. This is consistent with [14]–[16] and with [22], but a careful reader should treat eq. (21) as a (well-motivated) conjecture verified only implicitly by the Game-of-Degree analysis on $\mathcal{R}_p$.

### 5.3 Theorem 2 (p. 4259, eqs. (13)–(19)) — 1 pursuer vs. 1 evader on $\mathcal{R}_p$

**Statement.** For the 1P1E differential game (state $\mathbf{x} = (\mathbf{x}_E, \mathbf{x}_1) \in \mathbb{R}^6$) with $\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{R}_p$, the Value function is $C^1$ and solves the HJI PDE; it is
$$V(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{4}\frac{(R_E^2 - R_1^2)^2}{\Gamma^2} \quad (13), \qquad \Gamma = \sqrt{(x_E-x_1)^2+(y_E-y_1)^2+(z_E-z_1)^2},$$
with optimal state-feedback strategies
$$\mathbf{u}^* = \tfrac{1}{\Gamma_E}[x^*-x_E,\, y^*-y_E,\, z^*-z_E], \quad \mathbf{v}^* = \tfrac{1}{\Gamma_1}[x^*-x_1,\, y^*-y_1,\, z^*-z_1] \quad (14),$$
where the optimal interception point is
$$I^* = (x^*,y^*,z^*) = \frac{R_E^2 - R_1^2}{2\Gamma^2}[x_E - x_1,\, y_E - y_1,\, z_E - z_1] \quad (15),$$
$\Gamma_E = \|(x^*,y^*,z^*)-\mathbf{x}_E\|$, $\Gamma_1 = \|(x^*,y^*,z^*)-\mathbf{x}_1\|$.

**Assumptions.** $\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{R}_p$ (i.e., $R_E^2 > R_1^2$: the target is closer to $P$ than to $E$); $\Gamma \neq 0$ (game not over; p. 4259: "where $\Gamma \neq 0$, otherwise, the evader has been captured"); point capture; equal unit speeds.

**Proof approach (geometric construction + HJI verification, 6 steps).**
1. Write the bisector (dominance) plane OP: $(x_E-x_1)x + (y_E-y_1)y + (z_E-z_1)z = \frac{1}{2}(R_E^2 - R_1^2)$ (eq. (16)); normal $\vec{r} = (x_E-x_1)\hat{i} + (y_E-y_1)\hat{j} + (z_E-z_1)\hat{k}$. E, minimizing terminal distance to the origin, aims at the plane point closest to the origin.
2. That point $I^*$ lies on the line through the origin orthogonal to OP: $\frac{x}{x_E-x_1} = \frac{y}{y_E-y_1} = \frac{z}{z_E-z_1}$ (eq. (17)).
3. Substitute (17) into (16): $(x_E-x_1)x + \frac{(y_E-y_1)^2}{x_E-x_1}x + \frac{(z_E-z_1)^2}{x_E-x_1}x = \frac{1}{2}(R_E^2-R_1^2)$; solve to get $x^* = \frac{R_E^2-R_1^2}{2}\frac{x_E-x_1}{\Gamma^2}$, and by symmetry $y^*, z^*$ — eq. (15).
4. Compute $V(\mathbf{x}) = (x^*)^2+(y^*)^2+(z^*)^2 = \frac{1}{4}(R_E^2-R_1^2)^2/\Gamma^2$ (eq. (13)); differentiate to get the gradient (18) — all six components, e.g. $\partial V/\partial x_E = \frac{R_E^2-R_1^2}{\Gamma^2}x_E - 2\frac{x_E-x_1}{\Gamma^2}V$, $\partial V/\partial x_1 = 2\frac{x_E-x_1}{\Gamma^2}V - \frac{R_E^2-R_1^2}{\Gamma^2}x_1$. Printed erratum: the third line of (18) ($\partial V/\partial z_E$) prints "$y_E$" in its first term where "$z_E$" belongs (verified on the page image, p. 4259).
5. Rewrite the gradient compactly (eq. (19)): $\partial V/\partial \mathbf{x}_E = -\frac{R_E^2-R_1^2}{\Gamma^2}[x^*-x_E,\, y^*-y_E,\, z^*-z_E]$ and $\partial V/\partial \mathbf{x}_1 = \frac{R_E^2-R_1^2}{\Gamma^2}[x^*-x_1,\, y^*-y_1,\, z^*-z_1]$.
6. HJI check ($\partial V/\partial t = 0$, $g = 0$, stationary game): $\frac{\partial V}{\partial \mathbf{x}} \cdot \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{u}^*, \mathbf{v}^*) = \frac{R_E^2-R_1^2}{\Gamma^2}\left(\frac{\|(x^*,y^*,z^*)-\mathbf{x}_1\|^2}{\Gamma_1} - \frac{\|(x^*,y^*,z^*)-\mathbf{x}_E\|^2}{\Gamma_E}\right)$. **Decisive equation**: since $I^*$ lies on the bisector plane it is *equidistant* to both players, $\Gamma_E = \Gamma_1$, so $\frac{\partial V}{\partial \mathbf{x}}\cdot \mathbf{f} = \frac{R_E^2-R_1^2}{\Gamma^2}(\Gamma_1 - \Gamma_E) = 0$ (p. 4260, top). $\blacksquare$

**Local/global, analytic/numerical**: analytic, valid globally on $\mathcal{R}_p \subset \mathbb{R}^6$ (away from $\Gamma = 0$). **Physical meaning**: $V$ is the squared distance from the target at which E will be captured; as $\mathbf{x} \to \mathcal{B}$ ($R_E^2 \to R_1^2$), $V \to 0$ — on the Barrier the capture point collapses onto the target, matching the Game-of-Kind boundary. Note $I^*$ is a fixed point in space (time-invariant under optimal play), and $V$ is exactly the payoff the examples use. **Extensibility**: this theorem is the building block for the capture-type tests of Section V (the individual capture points $I_1', I_2'$ come from (15) applied to each pursuer) and for the single-capture regions of the 2v1 game.

### 5.4 Theorem 3 (p. 4260, eqs. (22)–(28), proof p. 4260–4262) — 2 pursuers vs. 1 evader, simultaneous capture

**Statement.** For the differential game (1)–(2), (7) with two pursuers and one evader, assume $\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{R}_{ps} \subset \mathcal{R}_p$ (the subregion where simultaneous capture by both $P_1$ and $P_2$ occurs under optimal play). Then the Value function is $C^1$ and solves the HJI PDE; it is
$$V(\mathbf{x}) = x_L^2 + y_L^2 - \frac{(a x_L + b y_L)^2}{a^2 + b^2 + c^2} \quad (22),$$
where
$$x_L = \frac{1}{2}\frac{(y_E-y_1)(R_E^2-R_2^2) - (y_E-y_2)(R_E^2-R_1^2)}{(x_E-x_2)(y_E-y_1) - (x_E-x_1)(y_E-y_2)}, \quad y_L = \frac{1}{2}\frac{(x_E-x_2)(R_E^2-R_1^2) - (x_E-x_1)(R_E^2-R_2^2)}{(x_E-x_2)(y_E-y_1) - (x_E-x_1)(y_E-y_2)} \quad (23),$$
and
$$a = (y_E-y_1)(z_E-z_2) - (y_E-y_2)(z_E-z_1),\quad b = (x_E-x_2)(z_E-z_1) - (x_E-x_1)(z_E-z_2),\quad c = (x_E-x_1)(y_E-y_2) - (x_E-x_2)(y_E-y_1) \quad (24).$$
The optimal state-feedback strategies are (14) for E and P1 together with
$$\mathbf{w}^* = \tfrac{1}{\Gamma_2}[x^*-x_2,\, y^*-y_2,\, z^*-z_2], \quad \Gamma_2 = \|(x^*,y^*,z^*)-\mathbf{x}_2\| \quad (25),$$
and the optimal interception point is
$$x^* = aT + x_L,\quad y^* = bT + y_L,\quad z^* = cT, \qquad T = -\frac{a x_L + b y_L}{a^2+b^2+c^2} \quad (26).$$

**Assumptions.** $\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{R}_{ps}$ (so the outcome is simultaneous capture); generic case $c \neq 0$ (the denominator of (23) equals $-c$; the degenerate case is Corollary 1); point capture; equal speeds; $\Gamma \neq 0$ for each player pair.

**Proof approach (3D analytic geometry + brute-force HJI verification, 6 steps).**
1. Under simultaneous capture the capture point must lie on *both* bisector planes: OP1 (eq. (16)) and OP2: $(x_E-x_2)x + (y_E-y_2)y + (z_E-z_2)z = \frac{1}{2}(R_E^2-R_2^2)$ (eq. (27)).
2. The intersection of two planes is a line with direction $\vec{r}_l = a\hat{i} + b\hat{j} + c\hat{k}$ obtained as the cross product of the two plane normals — eq. (24); a point $(x_L, y_L, 0)$ on the line is obtained by setting $z_L = 0$ and solving the two plane equations — eq. (23).
3. Parameterize the line: $\frac{x-x_L}{a} = \frac{y-y_L}{b} = \frac{z}{c} = T_L$ (eq. (28)). E aims at the point $I^*$ of this line closest to the origin: intersect (28) with the orthogonal line through the origin; **decisive equation** $T = -\frac{a x_L + b y_L}{a^2+b^2+c^2}$ (the orthogonal-projection parameter), giving (26). Then $V = \|\mathbf{I}^*\|^2$, which expands exactly to (22) (verified: $\|\mathbf{I}^*\|^2 = x_L^2+y_L^2 + 2T(ax_L+by_L) + T^2(a^2+b^2+c^2)$ with $T$ as above).
4. Compute the gradient: first the partials of the line point, e.g. $\frac{\partial x_L}{\partial x_E} = \frac{y_1-y_2}{c}(x_E - x_L)$, $\frac{\partial x_L}{\partial y_E} = \frac{\frac{1}{2}(R_2^2-R_1^2) + (y_1-y_2)y_E - (x_2-x_1)x_L}{c}$, $\frac{\partial x_L}{\partial z_E} = \frac{y_1-y_2}{c}z_E$ (eq. (29)); $\frac{\partial y_L}{\partial x_E} = \frac{\frac{1}{2}(R_1^2-R_2^2) + (x_2-x_1)x_E - (y_1-y_2)y_L}{c}$, $\frac{\partial y_L}{\partial y_E} = \frac{x_2-x_1}{c}(y_E-y_L)$, $\frac{\partial y_L}{\partial z_E} = \frac{x_2-x_1}{c}z_E$ (eq. (30)); then chain-rule into $\partial V/\partial x_E, \partial V/\partial y_E, \partial V/\partial z_E$ (eq. (31)).
5. **Key algebraic reduction** ((32)→(35), p. 4261): after adding/subtracting $\frac{2(y_1-y_2)}{c}(x^*)^2$ and $\frac{2(x_2-x_1)}{c}x^*y^*$ and using the *intermediate identity*
$$\tfrac{1}{2}(R_1^2-R_2^2) - (z_1-z_2)z^* + (x_2-x_1)x^* = (y_1-y_2)(y_L + bT) \quad (34),$$
together with $x^* = aT + x_L$, $z^* = cT$, the gradient collapses to the compact form
$$\frac{\partial V}{\partial \mathbf{x}_E} = -\frac{2}{c}\left[(y_1-y_2)x^* + (x_2-x_1)y^*\right][x^*-x_E,\, y^*-y_E,\, z^*-z_E],\quad \frac{\partial V}{\partial \mathbf{x}_1} = -\frac{2}{c}\left[(y_2-y_E)x^* + (x_E-x_2)y^*\right][x^*-x_1,\, y^*-y_1,\, z^*-z_1],\quad \frac{\partial V}{\partial \mathbf{x}_2} = -\frac{2}{c}\left[(y_E-y_1)x^* + (x_1-x_E)y^*\right][x^*-x_2,\, y^*-y_2,\, z^*-z_2]$$
(p. 4261, bottom; "similar expressions" claim covers the remaining components).
6. HJI evaluation ($\partial V/\partial t = 0$, $g=0$): $\frac{\partial V}{\partial \mathbf{x}}\cdot \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u}^*,\mathbf{v}^*,\mathbf{w}^*) = -\frac{2}{c}\sum_{\xi \in \{E,1,2\}}[\cdot_\xi]\frac{\|\mathbf{I}^*-\mathbf{x}_\xi\|^2}{\Gamma_\xi}$; since $I^*$ is equidistant to *all three* players ($\Gamma_E = \Gamma_1 = \Gamma_2$, each equal to $\Gamma_E$),
$$\frac{\partial V}{\partial \mathbf{x}}\cdot \mathbf{f} = -\frac{2\Gamma_E}{c}\Big[(y_1-y_2)x^*+(x_2-x_1)y^* + (y_2-y_E)x^*+(x_E-x_2)y^* + (y_E-y_1)x^*+(x_1-x_E)y^*\Big] = 0$$
(p. 4262): the $x^*$-coefficients sum to $(y_1-y_2)+(y_2-y_E)+(y_E-y_1) = 0$ and the $y^*$-coefficients sum to $(x_2-x_1)+(x_E-x_2)+(x_1-x_E) = 0$ — a telescoping cancellation. $\blacksquare$

**Local/global, analytic/numerical**: analytic; valid on $\mathcal{R}_{ps}$ (generic case $c \neq 0$). **Physical meaning**: cooperation moves the capture point off each pursuer's individual Apollonius aimpoint onto the intersection line of the two bisector planes, strictly increasing the terminal target-to-evader distance when $\mathcal{R}_{ps}$ is entered — this is the quantified "benefit of two pursuers" (p. 4260). The Value (22) is the squared distance of the origin to that intersection line's closest point. **Extensibility**: template for N-pursuer games (target guarding by $N \ge 2$ equal-speed defenders) — the intersection of $N$ hyperplanes and projection of the origin onto it; also the basis of the follow-on papers in this archive (e.g., Garcia et al. 2021 multiple-pursuer multiple-evader games).

### 5.5 Corollary 1 (p. 4262, eqs. (36)–(39)) — degenerate case c = 0

**Statement.** Same game as Theorem 3, $\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{R}_{ps}$, and $c = 0$ (WLOG). The Value function is $C^1$ and solves the HJI PDE; it is
$$V(\mathbf{x}) = (z^*)^2 + \frac{a^2 y_L^2}{a^2+b^2} \quad (36),$$
where
$$y_L = \frac{1}{2}\frac{(z_E-z_1)(R_E^2-R_2^2) - (z_E-z_2)(R_E^2-R_1^2)}{(y_E-y_2)(z_E-z_1) - (y_E-y_1)(z_E-z_2)}, \quad z^* = \frac{1}{2}\frac{(y_E-y_2)(R_E^2-R_1^2) - (y_E-y_1)(R_E^2-R_2^2)}{(y_E-y_2)(z_E-z_1) - (y_E-y_1)(z_E-z_2)} \quad (37),$$
$a, b$ as in (24); strategies by (25) with aimpoint $x^* = aT,\; y^* = bT + y_L$ (38), $T = -\frac{b y_L}{a^2+b^2}$.

**Proof approach (4 steps).**
1. When $c = 0$ the line (28) cannot be parameterized with $z/c$; the intersection line is parallel to the $x$–$y$ plane, so its $z$-coordinate is constant, denoted $z^*$. WLOG set $x_L = 0$ and solve plane equations (16), (27) for $z^*$ and $y_L$ — eq. (37).
2. Line equation: $\frac{x}{a} = \frac{y-y_L}{b} = T_L$, $z = z^*$ (eq. (39)); project the origin orthogonally: $T = -\frac{b y_L}{a^2+b^2}$, giving (38) and $V = (x^*)^2+(y^*)^2+(z^*)^2 = (z^*)^2 + \frac{a^2y_L^2}{a^2+b^2}$ (36) (verified by expansion).
3. Gradient computed explicitly (three long expressions for $\partial V/\partial x_E, \partial V/\partial y_E, \partial V/\partial z_E$, p. 4262, right column); "only the nonzero terms $a$ and $b$ appear in the denominator of $V(\mathbf{x})$ and $\frac{\partial V}{\partial \mathbf{x}}$; the Value function is $C^1$" — no singularity despite the appearance of $c$ in Theorem 3's formulas.
4. HJI check: the common terms cancel and the remainder reduces through the chain (p. 4262–4263) $\frac{\partial V}{\partial \mathbf{x}}\cdot\mathbf{f} = \frac{4ax^*}{b^2\Gamma_E}\left[(a^2+b^2)T + b y_L\right] \overset{(38)}{=} \frac{4ax^*}{b^2\Gamma_E}\left[-b y_L + b y_L\right] = 0$. **Decisive equation**: $(a^2+b^2)T + b y_L = 0$ by the definition of $T$. $\blacksquare$

**Extensibility**: the paper states (p. 4263) that "similar analysis and expressions can be obtained in the cases where $a = 0$ or $b = 0$ or in the particular case where any two of $a,b,c$ are equal to zero" — asserted, not written out. **Critical observation**: since the denominator of (23) equals $-c$ and (37)'s denominators are the analogous $c=0$-case combinations, exactly one of the two representations is always well-defined whenever the two plane normals are not parallel; the truly singular configuration (parallel bisector planes, i.e., collinear E, P1, P2 with proportional normals) is not discussed.

### 5.6 Capture-type determination (Section V, p. 4263 — conditions, not a numbered theorem)

Let $I_1' = (x_1', y_1', z_1')$ and $I_2' = (x_2', y_2', z_2')$ be the individual optimal capture points from Theorem 2 applied to (E, P1) and (E, P2). Define the signed plane-side functionals
$$\Upsilon_1 = (x_E-x_1)x_2' + (y_E-y_1)y_2' + (z_E-z_1)z_2' - \frac{R_E^2-R_1^2}{2}, \qquad \Upsilon_2 = (x_E-x_2)x_1' + (y_E-y_2)y_1' + (z_E-z_2)z_1' - \frac{R_E^2-R_2^2}{2}.$$
$\Upsilon_1 > 0$ ⟺ $I_2'$ lies on the E-side of plane OP1 ($\Upsilon_1 < 0$: P1-side); analogously for $\Upsilon_2$. Decision rule:
- $\Upsilon_1 < 0$ **and** $\Upsilon_2 < 0$ ⇒ simultaneous capture optimal; capture point by (26) (Theorem 3 applies; $\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{R}_{ps}$).
- $\Upsilon_1 < 0 < \Upsilon_2$ ⇒ $P_1$ single-handedly captures; $P_2$ inactive; $I^* = I_1'$.
- $\Upsilon_1 > 0 > \Upsilon_2$ ⇒ $P_2$ single-handedly captures; $P_1$ inactive; $I^* = I_2'$.
- $\Upsilon_1 > 0$ **and** $\Upsilon_2 > 0$ ⇒ both $I_1'$ and $I_2'$ are reachable by E; the active pursuer is the one whose capture point gives E the smaller cost (closest to the target).
The tests are re-evaluated every time step in closed loop (Example 1). The paper explicitly contrasts this with [33]: "determining the type of capture is a different problem than the distinction of active pursuers in [33]" (p. 4263).

### 5.7 Game of Degree on $\mathcal{R}_e$ (p. 4258 — no theorem)

For $\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{R}_e$ the paper gives only the qualitative result: "Since the target is stationary, the players' optimal strategies are to aim directly at the target" (p. 4258, after eq. (10)). No Value function is derived and no HJI verification is performed for the evader-winning region. **Proof gap (critical observation)**: the $\mathcal{R}_e$ Game of Degree (value $\min_{\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}}\max_{\mathbf{u}} \Phi_e$, eq. (10)) is stated but unsolved; the min–max ordering there also deserves scrutiny since the maximizing evader nominally has the informational advantage.

- Eq. (18), third line, first term prints $y_E$ instead of $z_E$ (p. 4259).

## 6. Simulation and Experiments

**Scenario.** Four 3D trajectory simulations, all with the *same* initial condition as Example 1; closed-loop (state-feedback) implementation where at each instant every player re-reads the state, recomputes the capture type via $\Upsilon_1, \Upsilon_2$, updates the capture point, and applies the current saddle-point heading. Players do not know the opponent's applied control.

**Figure-to-claim mapping:**
- **Fig. 3 (Ex. 1)**: validates Theorem 3 + (26) — straight-line optimal trajectories, time-invariant capture point $I^*$, simultaneous capture, realized payoff equals $V = 10.374$ (sanity check: $\|I^*\|^2 = 10.37$, consistent to rounding).
- **Fig. 4 (Ex. 2)**: validates robustness of the saddle-point strategies — evader defects by heading straight to the target (guidance unknown to pursuers); pursuers, still playing (25)–(26) in closed loop, curve their trajectories and capture it *farther* from the target than $V$: $R_{Ef}^2 = 14.453 > V$ — "their payoff improves" (p. 4263).
- **Fig. 5 (Ex. 3)**: validates the cooperative-contribution claim — pursuers play individual (non-cooperative) optimal solutions while E plays optimally; E is captured *closer* to the target than under the team solution: $R_{Ef}^2 = 9.8345 < V$ — quantifying the value of cooperation (and matching p. 4258's remark that forcing simultaneous capture when inappropriate is counterproductive).
- **Fig. 6 (Ex. 4)**: validates necessity of the game-theoretic solution — pursuers implement Pure Pursuit (always pointing at the evader) and thereby "allow the Evader to actually win the game since the Evader is able to reach the point target" (p. 4264). PP is the only tested baseline that flips the Game-of-Kind outcome.
- **Metrics**: terminal squared distance to target $R_{Ef}^2$ vs. saddle value $V$ (Examples 2–3); win/loss (Example 4). No runtime/complexity measurements.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues

**Author-stated / structural:**
1. Point target, point capture, *equal speeds only* (p. 4258); no capture radius, no speed superiority, no turning constraints — explicitly left to related work [29]–[32].
2. Stationary target at the origin (WLOG by translation, but no moving-target extension).
3. The $\mathcal{R}_e$ Game of Degree is not solved — only the qualitative "aim directly at the target" remark (p. 4258); no Value/HJI verification on the evader's winning region.
4. Degenerate direction cases other than $c=0$ ($a=0$, $b=0$, two zero components) are asserted "similar" but not written out (p. 4263); the fully singular configuration (parallel bisector planes / collinear geometry) is not discussed.
5. The Game-of-Kind Barrier $B = \max\{B_1, B_2\}$ (eq. (21)) is asserted from dominance geometry rather than proven (no formal two-target capturability analysis); the authors themselves position Barrier *construction* as [33]'s contribution.
6. Only the simultaneous-capture region $\mathcal{R}_{ps}$ gets a dedicated Value (Theorem 3); on $\mathcal{R}_p \setminus \mathcal{R}_{ps}$ the team solution is inherited from Theorem 2 with one pursuer inactive — correct but not stated as a formal theorem with its own HJI verification for the 9D state.
7. Future work (p. 4264): "analyze reach-avoid games with higher number of players."

**Critical observations (with page/eq.):**
8. The verification paradigm assumes the saddle point exists and is unique and that $V$ is $C^1$ *on each region separately*; continuity of $V$ across the Barrier $\mathcal{B}$ and across the internal interfaces of $\mathcal{R}_p$ (boundaries of $\mathcal{R}_{ps}$ where simultaneous capture switches to single capture) is neither shown nor discussed — at such switching surfaces the closed-loop aimpoint jumps ($I^*$ from (26) vs. $I_i'$ from (15)), so global $C^1$-ness of the composed feedback value fails, and the "unique saddle-point" claim (p. 1) should be read per-region.
9. The min–max in (8) has the evader as the *inner-outcome-constrained* minimizer while in (10) the roles swap; the paper does not address Isaacs' verification conditions (e.g., no discussion of possible non-uniqueness of the costate or of corners on $\mathcal{B}$), relying instead on the explicit $C^1$ construction — acceptable here because $V$ is written in closed form, but the argument "$V$ is $C^1$ and solves HJI ⇒ saddle point" is used without stating the required controllability/terminal-surface transversality hypotheses.
10. Examples are a single initial condition (one geometry); no Monte Carlo study, no sensitivity to the $\Upsilon$-tests near decision boundaries, no real-robot or hardware component; simulation platform and integrator UNREPORTED (p. 4263–4264).
11. Minor errata: eq. (8) argument typo; eq. (18) third-line $y_E/z_E$ typo; duplicated organization paragraph (p. 4258); "verifying the the proposed solution" (p. 4264, doubled word).

## 8. Citation Value

**Paragraph role.** This is the 3D, equal-speed, two-defender cornerstone of the AFRL "verifiable differential games" line: it is the paper that (i) lifts Garcia–Casbeer–Pachter's 2D target-guarding/active-defense results [19], [22] to $\mathbb{R}^9$ with a complete Game-of-Kind + verified Game-of-Degree solution, (ii) supplies the exact 2P1E barrier $\max\{R_E^2-R_1^2, R_E^2-R_2^2\}$ and the simultaneous-capture Value (22) used later for teaming and task allocation, and (iii) provides the canonical example of HJI *verification by explicit closed-form construction* as an alternative to numerical HJI (Margellos–Lygeros [10], Zhou et al. [11]) and to pairwise-outcome/matching methods (Chen–Zhou–Tomlin [9], Yan et al. [29]–[33]). Within this archive it is the direct 3D counterpart of P033-type capture-the-flag work and the natural baseline against which 3D multiplayer reach-avoid papers with heterogeneous speeds, convex targets, or imperfect information position themselves. **Suggested citation sentence:** "For equal-speed players in 3D, Garcia, Casbeer and Pachter obtained the closed-form Barrier and a $C^1$ Value function verified via the HJI equation for the two-pursuer–one-evader target-guarding game, showing that cooperative simultaneous capture strictly improves the defenders' payoff whenever the state lies in the simultaneous-capture region [P035]." A second use: "Unlike purely geometric Barrier constructions [33], the saddle-point strategies in [P035] are synthesized *and verified*, and their closed-loop implementation provably exploits any non-optimal adversary behavior."
