- **Topics**: fishing game; pursuit-evasion game; barrier (game of kind); point capture (zero capture radius); two pursuers vs. one faster evader; winning regions; non-escape angle; Apollonius circle; method of explicit policy; games of kind and degree fusion; minimum-time optimal strategies.

## 2. Research Background
Multi-robot conflict scenarios — cooperative besieging/capturing [1, 2], multi-agent path coordination [3, 4], and collision avoidance of a robot passing between two moving obstacles [5, 6] — reduce to a canonical three-player pursuit-evasion game in which one evader tries to pass through the gap between two pursuers. The authors dub this the "fishing game" from the image of two fishers netting one fish. Unlike most barrier analyses (homicidal chauffeur, two cars, lifeline, deadline games), capture here is **point capture** (capture radius zero) and the evader is **faster** than each pursuer, so the game is nontrivial precisely when $a = v_p/v_e \le 1$; for $a>1$ the pursuers always win by heading straight at the evader (p. 4). There are two competition focuses: the relative distances $\min\{d_1,d_2\}$ (pursuers minimize, evader maximizes) and the included angle $\theta = \angle P_2 E P_1$ (evader drives it from $<\pi$ through $\pi$ to thread the gap; pursuers suppress it). Any multi-pursuer siege can be viewed as a combination of fishing games, which is the authors' motivation for a complete analytic solution.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
**Problem** (p. 3–4): two identical pursuers $P_1,P_2$ (speed $v_p$) and one evader $E$ (speed $v_e$) move in the plane with unlimited heading control. Evader wins iff $\min\{d_1,d_2\}>0$ is maintained while $\theta$ crosses from $<\pi$ to $=\pi$ (threading); pursuers win iff $\min\{d_1,d_2\}=0$ (point capture) or the state stays in $\{d_1>0,d_2>0,\theta<\pi\}$ indefinitely (termination conditions, p. 4, unnumbered list a)/b)). Two questions: (1) which initial states are winning for each side under optimal play (game of kind → barrier); (2) what minimum-time strategies realize the win (game of degree).

**Gap** [authors' claim, p. 2, 4]: the classic Isaacs barrier construction — start from the BUP of the target set and integrate the retrogressive path equations (RPEs) — fails here because the target set involves both $\min\{d_1,d_2\}$ and $\theta$, and the RPEs of the reduced state $(d_1,d_2,\theta)$ "are hard to solve analytically" (p. 4); this is an authors' difficulty claim, not verified against a computation. [Verified from cited text]: the closest prior game is Hagedorn & Breakwell's two-pursuer one-evader game of kind [16], which assumes a **positive capture radius** and splits the target into two successive sub-targets (straight-line phase then curved phase); the authors correctly note (p. 3) that both the positive radius and the successive sub-target decomposition are inapplicable to point capture. The paper also positions itself against three method families: sub-game decomposition [16], [35]–[38]; explicit-policy/geometric capture analysis [39]–[47]; and numerical HJI/viscosity/level-set/ADP methods [48]–[59] — claiming the last two cannot cheaply yield a *complete* (open-loop + closed-loop) fishing-game solution. [Assessment]: the taxonomy is accurate for its (2015) citations; the completeness claim "first complete solution to the fishing game" (p. 2) is an authors' priority claim that cannot be verified from the cited text alone, but no earlier fishing-game-with-point-capture solution appears among the 60 references.

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Theory (game of kind)**: analytic construction of the barrier $B_1$ of the fishing game via a *method of explicit policy*: split the game into two sub-games of kind (competition on $\theta$; competition on $\min\{d_1,d_2\}$), solve each, and integrate. Winning regions $\mathscr{D}_p^1$ (eq. (18)) and $\mathscr{D}_e^1$ (eq. (21)), barrier $B_1$ (eq. (19)) with the closed form $\cos\theta = \frac{(1-a^2)(d_1+d_2)^2}{2d_1d_2}-1$; in relative coordinates a fixed-$z$ slice of $B_1$ is the ellipse (43).
2. **Theory (game of degree)**: fusion of kind and degree — feedback-optimal minimum-time strategies and winning-time upper bounds in each of four zones partitioned by $B_1, B_2, B_3$ (eqs. (44)–(65)), including the rendezvous strategy (46)–(49) valid outside $B_3$, the angle-reduction strategy (8)–(9) in zone 2, spirals in zone 3, and the Apollonius-interspace crossing in zone 4 with terminal-time bound (62)–(65).
3. **Modeling**: reduced 3-D state space $(d_1,d_2,\theta)$ with dynamics (4)–(6) and relative-coordinate representation $P_1(0,z), P_2(0,-z), E(x,y)$ (Fig. 6) that turns the barrier into the conic surface $x^2+(1-a^2)y^2 = \frac{(1-a^2)z^2}{a^2}$.
4. **Experiment**: no hardware/numerical-benchmark study; illustration only — trajectory plots (Figs. 2, 9–13) and barrier renderings (Figs. 7–8) generated from the analytic strategies (platform UNREPORTED).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results
**Setup and information structure.** Realistic-space kinematics (p. 3, eqs. (1)–(3)): $\dot x_i = v_p\sin\phi_i,\ \dot y_i = -v_p\cos\phi_i$ ($i=1,2$), $\dot x_e = v_e\sin\phi,\ \dot y_e = -v_e\cos\phi$. Reduced state (p. 4, eqs. (4)–(6)):
$$\dot d_1 = -v_p\cos\hat\phi_1 - v_e\cos(\theta-\alpha),\quad \dot d_2 = v_p\cos\hat\phi_2 - v_e\cos\alpha,$$
$$\dot\theta = -\frac{v_p}{d_1}\sin\hat\phi_1 + \frac{v_e}{d_1}\sin(\theta-\alpha) - \frac{v_p}{d_2}\sin\hat\phi_2 + \frac{v_e}{d_2}\sin\alpha,$$
with relative headings $\alpha = \phi-\gamma$, $\hat\phi_1 = \phi_1+\pi-\theta-\gamma$, $\hat\phi_2 = \phi_2-\gamma$ ($\gamma = \mathrm{Arg}(\overrightarrow{EP_2})$; Fig. 1, p. 4). State space $\Omega = \{d_1\ge0, d_2\ge0, -\pi<\theta\le\pi\}$; WLOG $\theta_0\in[0,\pi)$ (symmetry). Information structure: perfect-information zero-sum differential game; the kind-part arguments are open-loop Nash [8], the degree-part strategies are claimed feedback ("(8) is an optimal feedback strategy", p. 10). Only $a=v_p/v_e\le1$ is considered (p. 4); I verified (4)–(6) against direct vector differentiation (e.g., E fleeing along $-\overrightarrow{EP_1}$ gives $\dot d_1 = v_e - v_p$, consistent).

**Main equation of sub-game 1 (p. 4, eq. (7))**: $\min_{\hat\phi_1,\hat\phi_2}\max_\alpha \dot\theta = 0$. Stationarity gives pursuer controls $\hat\phi_1^*=\hat\phi_2^*=\pi/2$ (8) (perpendicular to $EP_i$) and evader control (9)–(10): $\sin\alpha^* = \frac{d_1-d_2\cos\theta}{r_{12}},\ \cos\alpha^* = \frac{d_2\sin\theta}{r_{12}}$, with $r_{12}=\sqrt{d_1^2+d_2^2-2d_1d_2\cos\theta}$. I re-derived these exactly: maximizing $\frac{v_e}{d_1}\sin(\theta-\alpha)+\frac{v_e}{d_2}\sin\alpha$ over $\alpha$ yields $d_2\cos(\theta-\alpha)=d_1\cos\alpha$, and the max value is $v_e r_{12}/(d_1 d_2)$, so (7) becomes (11) $-\frac{v_p}{d_1}-\frac{v_p}{d_2}+\frac{v_e r_{12}}{d_1d_2}=0$, i.e., the barrier condition (12) $r_{12}=a(d_1+d_2)$, equivalently the **non-escape angle** (13):
$$\cos\Theta = \frac{(1-a^2)(d_1+d_2)^2}{2d_1d_2}-1 .$$

- **Theorem 4.1 (p. 5, eqs. (14)–(15))**: if $r_{12}(t_0)\le a(d_1(t_0)+d_2(t_0))$, equivalently $\theta(t_0)\le\Theta(t_0)$, the pursuers win by adopting (8). *Assumptions*: point capture, $a<1$ (for $a=1$, $\mathscr{D}_p^1$ is all of $\Omega$ minus $\{\theta=\pi\}$), $\min\{d_1^0,d_2^0\}>0$, $0\le\theta_0<\pi$. *Proof approach*: explicit policy + Isaacs-style main equation (no RPE integration). Steps: (i) at $\theta_0<\Theta_0$, (11) gives $\dot\theta(t_0)<0$; (ii) substituting (8)–(9) into (4)–(5): $\dot d_i = -v_e d_i\sin\theta/r_{12}<0$ and $\dot d_1/\dot d_2 = d_1/d_2$, so $b=d_1/d_2$ is invariant ($\dot b=0$); (iii) (13) becomes (16) $\cos\Theta = \frac{(1-a^2)(1+b)^2}{2b}-1$, so $\Theta(t)$ is invariant along equilibrium play and $\theta(t)$ decreases monotonically below the constant $\Theta$; (iv) "based on the concept of Nash equilibrium" the pursuers achieve $\theta(t_f)<\pi$ against any evader strategy; for $\theta_0=\Theta_0$, $\dot\theta=0$ but $\dot d_i<0$ so capture terminates the game in finite time. *Decisive inequality*: (11) $<0$ under (14). *Scope*: global in $\mathscr{D}_p^1$, analytic. *Physical meaning*: if the pursuers' separation subtends a small enough angle (Apollonius-type condition $r_{12}\le a(d_1+d_2)$), they can always keep the gap closed. *Extensibility*: the condition is the seed of the whole barrier (18)–(19).
  - **Remark 4.1 (p. 5, eq. (17))**: $\cos\Theta\le1$ forces $\frac{1-a}{1+a}\le b\le\frac{1+a}{1-a}$ ($a<1$); outside this range $\dot\theta>0$ for **all** $\theta$, via the chain $\dot\theta = \frac{v_e}{d_1}[\sqrt{b^2+1-2b\cos\theta}-a(1+b)] > \frac{v_e}{d_1}[\sqrt{b^2+1-2b\cos\theta}-(b-1)]\ge0$ using $(b-1)^2\le b^2+1-2b\cos\theta \Leftrightarrow \cos\theta\le1$. I verified this chain. Interpretation: with a small $a$, pursuers must keep $b=d_1/d_2$ near 1.
  - **Remark 4.2 (p. 5)**: $\Theta$ increases monotonically with $a$ for fixed $b$; $\Theta=\pi$ at $a=1$; $\max_b\Theta = \arccos(1-2a^2)$ at $b=1$. (Check: $\cos\Theta$ at $b=1$ is $(1-a^2)\cdot4/2-1 = 1-2a^2$.)
  - **Remark 4.3 + Fig. 2 (p. 5–6)**: after $\theta=0$ with $r_{12}\le a(d_1+d_2)$ the pursuers still win ($\theta\le0$ thereafter); at equality all three trajectories are circles centered at the midpoint of $P_1P_2$; the pursuers can additionally force $r_{12}=0$ by walking straight at each other.
  - *Winning region and barrier* (p. 5, eqs. (18)–(19)): $\mathscr{D}_p^1 = \{\sqrt{d_1^2+d_2^2-2d_1d_2\cos\theta}\le a(d_1+d_2)\}$, boundary $\mathscr{B}_1 = \{\cos\theta = \frac{(1-a^2)(d_1+d_2)^2}{2d_1d_2}-1\}$.
  - **proof gap (minor)**: the step "based on the concept of Nash equilibrium … no matter what strategy of the evader" (p. 5) does not explicitly prove invariance of $\mathscr{D}_p^1$ under unilateral evader deviations: $\Theta$ depends on $b=d_1/d_2$, and $b$ is invariant only when the evader plays (9); off equilibrium the state could in principle leave $\mathscr{D}_p^1$ through the moving boundary even with $\dot\theta\le0$ held pointwise inside (which the pointwise bound $\dot\theta\le\frac{v_e}{d_1d_2}(r_{12}-a(d_1+d_2))$ does provide). Standard applied-DG heuristic; the conclusion is very likely true but the written argument is incomplete at this point.

- **Theorem 4.2 (p. 6, eq. (20))**: if $\theta(t_0) > \arccos(1-2a^2)$, the evader escapes no matter what the pursuers do. *Proof approach*: Apollonius-circle geometry (Isaacs [7]). The Apollonius circles of $(P_i, E)$ with ratio $a$ have centers $Q_i = \frac{1}{1-a^2}(P_i - a^2 E)$ and radii $R_i = \frac{a d_i}{1-a^2}$; $\sin\angle P_1EA = \sin\angle P_2EB = \sin\xi = a$ where $A,B$ are the tangent points from $E$. Since $2\xi = 2\arcsin a = \arccos(1-2a^2)$ (exact identity — I verified: $\cos 2\xi = 1-2a^2$), $\theta > 2\xi$ makes the escape cone $\angle AEB = \theta - 2\xi > 0$; any straight heading with $\xi < \alpha^* < \theta-\xi$ intersects neither pursuer's reachable disk ($|P_i - X|\le v_p t = a\, v_e t = a|EX|$ on the ray), so capture is impossible. *Scope*: global, analytic, rigorous (the reachable-disk argument covers all pursuer motions given straight-line evader motion). *Winning region* (21): $\mathscr{D}_e^1 = \{d_1>0, d_2>0, \theta>\arccos(1-2a^2)\}$; boundary $\mathscr{B}_2$ (22) $=\{\theta=\arccos(1-2a^2)\}$.
  - **Remark 4.4 (p. 6–7)**: in the uncertain band $\Theta < \theta \le \arccos(1-2a^2)$ sub-game 1 alone is inconclusive; *exception*: if the evader is confined to a straight line, the pursuers win from all of $\Omega\setminus\mathscr{D}_e^1$ and the barrier of that variant is $\mathscr{B}_2$ (Fig. 4 shows the deadlock at $\theta=\arccos(1-2a^2)$).

**Sub-game 2 — competition on $\min\{d_1,d_2\}$ (p. 7, eqs. (23)–(35))**. Key observation (p. 7): with point capture and $v_e>v_p$, a single pursuer can never capture, so the pursuers' target is **simultaneous** capture ($d_1=d_2=0$); the target-set boundary is parameterized by $x_1=x_2=x_e=s_1,\ y_1=y_2=y_e=s_2$ with normal constraints (32) $\lambda_1+\lambda_3+\lambda_5=0,\ \lambda_2+\lambda_4+\lambda_6=0$. To avoid the intractable reduced-space RPEs, the main equation is written in the *realistic* space (23): $\min_{\phi_1,\phi_2}\max_\phi\ [v_p(\lambda_1\sin\phi_1-\lambda_2\cos\phi_1)+v_p(\lambda_3\sin\phi_2-\lambda_4\cos\phi_2)+v_e(\lambda_5\sin\phi-\lambda_6\cos\phi)]=0$ with controls (24)–(26) (anti-aligned with/aligned to costate directions $\rho_i=\|(\lambda_{2i-1},\lambda_{2i})\|$), reducing it to (27) $-v_p(\rho_1+\rho_2)+v_e\rho_3=0$. The RPEs (28)–(31) have **constant** costates ($\mathring\lambda_i=0$), so equilibrium trajectories are straight lines (33)–(35) meeting at $Q(s_1,s_2)$ after reversed time $\tau$.

- **Theorem 4.3 (p. 7–8, eq. (36))**: on the barrier of sub-game 2 the optimal trajectories satisfy $d_1 = d_2,\ \theta = \arccos(1-2a^2)$. *Proof approach*: pure geometry on the meeting configuration (Fig. 5) + quadratic discriminant. Steps: (i) $Q$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $P_1P_2$ since $|P_1Q|=|P_2Q|=v_p\Delta t$; with $\beta=\angle EP_2P_1$ ($\cos\beta=\frac{d_2-d_1\cos\theta}{r_{12}},\ \sin\beta=\frac{d_1\sin\theta}{r_{12}}$, eq. (39)) and $w=|AQ|$ ($A$ = projection of $E$ on the bisector), Pythagoras gives (37)–(38); (ii) eliminating $\Delta t$ via $a^2$ yields the quadratic in $w$ (40); (iii) pursuer win $\Rightarrow$ real root $\Rightarrow$ discriminant $\ge0 \Leftrightarrow$ (41) $4a^2d_2^2-[(1-a^2)r_{12}+2a^2d_2\cos\beta]^2\ge0 \Leftrightarrow r_{12}\le a(d_1+d_2)$ (I verified the algebra); evader win $\Rightarrow$ no real root $\Rightarrow r_{12}>a(d_1+d_2)$; hence $r_{12}=a(d_1+d_2)$ on the barrier — "but substantiation is needed" (authors' own words, p. 8); (iv) substantiation: at $r_{12}=a(d_1+d_2)$, $\theta=\Theta$, the root is $w_0 = \frac{a^2 d_2\sin\beta}{1-a^2} = \frac{a d_1d_2\sin\Theta}{(1-a^2)(d_1+d_2)}$ and (42) $\Delta t_0^2 = \frac{d_1d_2}{v_e^2-v_p^2}$ (I verified (42) exactly on $d_1=d_2=d$: $v_p^2\Delta t_0^2 = w_0^2+r_{12}^2/4 = a^2d^2/(1-a^2)$); (v) the evader heading on the barrier satisfies $\cos\alpha^* = \frac{\sqrt{1-a^2}(d_1+d_2)}{2\sqrt{d_1d_2}} = \sqrt{\frac{\cos\Theta+1}{2}}$, i.e., $\alpha^*=\Theta/2=\theta/2$ and $\sin\alpha^*=a$; the pursuer headings obey $\sin\hat\phi_i^*=\sin\alpha^*/a$, $\cos\hat\phi_1^* = \frac{\sqrt{1-a^2}(d_1-d_2)}{2a\sqrt{d_1d_2}} = -\cos\hat\phi_2^*$, so $\hat\phi_1^*+\hat\phi_2^*=\pi$; congruence of $\triangle EP_1Q\cong\triangle EP_2Q$ forces $d_1=d_2$, and then Remark 4.2 gives $\theta=\arccos(1-2a^2)$. *Meaning*: the curve (36) is exactly $\mathscr{B}_1\cap\mathscr{B}_2$ (set $\cos\theta=1-2a^2$ in (13): $(d_1+d_2)^2=4d_1d_2\Leftrightarrow d_1=d_2$), i.e., the junction curve from which the whole-game barrier "begins to extend" (p. 8). *proof gap (moderate)*: the discriminant step treats $w$ as a free variable of a simultaneous-meeting problem and the strategies (24)–(26) as WLOG; the logic "pursuers win ⇒ real root" is a necessary-condition argument along equilibrium candidates, and the final congruence step assumes what is to be substantiated ($r_{12}=a(d_1+d_2)$ on the barrier). The result is consistent (and $\Gamma=\{(d,d,\arccos(1-2a^2))\}$ with sliding time $d/\sqrt{v_e^2-v_p^2}$ is a coherent barrier/junction curve), but the written proof is not fully rigorous.

**Integration (p. 8, Sec. IV-C).** Since $\Omega\setminus\mathscr{D}_p^1 \supset \mathscr{D}_e^1$, the evader wins in the intermediate band $\Omega\setminus(\mathscr{D}_p^1\cup\mathscr{D}_e^1)$ "as long as it constantly changes its direction to maximize $\theta$" (p. 8 — note the hedging "it is predictable": the completeness of $\Omega\setminus\mathscr{D}_p^1$ as the evader's winning region rests on the sub-game-2 discriminant analysis (capture unreachable when $r_{12}>a(d_1+d_2)$) plus $\dot\theta = \frac{v_e}{d_1d_2}(r_{12}-a(d_1+d_2))>0$ under (9) outside $\mathscr{D}_p^1$; as $\theta\to\pi$, $\dot\theta\to\frac{v_e}{d_1d_2}(d_1+d_2)(1-a)>0$, so $\theta$ does reach $\pi$ — I verified this limit, but no formal theorem covers the band). Conclusion: pursuers' region $\mathscr{D}_p^1$, evader's region $\Omega\setminus\mathscr{D}_p^1$, whole-game barrier $\mathscr{B}_1$. In relative coordinates $P_1=(0,z), P_2=(0,-z), E=(x,y)$ (Fig. 6), a fixed-$z$ slice of $\mathscr{B}_1$ is the **ellipse** (p. 9, eq. (43)):
$$x^2 + (1-a^2)y^2 = \frac{(1-a^2)z^2}{a^2},$$
whose inner side (excluding $P_1,P_2$) is the escape zone; equivalently $|EP_1|+|EP_2| = 2z/a$ (ellipse with foci $P_1,P_2$ — my derivation, consistent). Figs. 7 (surface, $a=0.6$) and 8 (slices $z=1$, $a$ from 0.9 down to 0.3) visualize it; the escape zone shrinks as $a\to1$.

**Game of degree (Sec. V).** Payoffs: (44) $\min_{\hat\phi_1,\hat\phi_2}\max_\alpha J_1 = \int_{t_0}^{T_1}dt$ in $\mathscr{D}_p^1$; (61) $\max_{\hat\phi_1,\hat\phi_2}\min_\alpha J_2 = \int_{t_0}^{T_2}dt$ in $\Omega\setminus\mathscr{D}_p^1$ (footnote 1, p. 9: pursuers must NOT minimize closest distance — that would destroy cooperation). Four zones are carved by $\mathscr{B}_1,\mathscr{B}_2,\mathscr{B}_3$ (Fig. 10): zones 1–2 (+ boundaries $\mathscr{B}_3,\mathscr{B}_1$) = $\mathscr{D}_p^1$; zones 3–4 (excluding $P_1,P_2$) = evader's region. Zone identification verified from Fig. 10/11/12/13 renders: zone 1 = outside circle $\mathscr{B}_3$; zone 2 = between $\mathscr{B}_3$ and ellipse $\mathscr{B}_1$; zone 3 = inside $\mathscr{B}_1$, outside $\mathscr{B}_2$ arcs; zone 4 = inside $\mathscr{B}_2$ arcs.

- **Zone 1 (p. 9–10, eqs. (45)–(54))**: if the evader starts far enough, pursuers walk straight at each other meeting at the midpoint $Q$; $|EQ| = \frac12\sqrt{d_1^2+d_2^2+2d_1d_2\cos\theta}$ (45) (apex-median formula — verified), controls (46)–(49) (pursuers along $P_1P_2$; evader toward $Q$ — verified as the exact directions). Feasibility requires (50) $\frac{r_{12}}{2v_p}\le\frac{|EQ|}{v_e}$, i.e., (52) $r_{12}^2\le\frac{2a^2}{1+a^2}(d_1^2+d_2^2)$ or (53) $\cos\theta\ge\frac{(1-a^2)(d_1^2+d_2^2)}{2(1+a^2)d_1d_2}$ (both derivations verified; in $(x,y,z)$: $x^2+y^2\ge z^2/a^2$). The boundary locus is the **circle** (54) $x^2+y^2 = z^2/a^2$ ($\mathscr{B}_3$) and the terminal time is $T_1 = z(t_0)/v_p + t_0$. *Sufficiency check (mine)*: (50) is exactly the right condition — if $E$ crossed the shrinking segment at $t<T_{meet}$, the triangle inequality gives $|EQ| \le v_e t + r_{12}/2 - v_p t$, violated at $t=T_{meet}=r_{12}/(2v_p)$ precisely when (50) holds. $\mathscr{B}_1$ is tangent to $\mathscr{B}_3$ at $\{r_{12}=a(d_1+d_2),\theta=0\}$ — verified analytically (at $\theta=0$, $r_{12}=|d_1-d_2|$ and equality forces $(d_1-d_2)^2=a^2(d_1+d_2)^2$, which turns (52) into equality). $\mathscr{B}_2$'s slice (55) is two circular arcs through $P_1,P_2$ with centers $\mp\frac{(1-2a^2)z}{2a\sqrt{1-a^2}}$ and radius $\frac{z}{2a\sqrt{1-a^2}}$, cutting the $x$-axis at $\pm z\sqrt{1-a^2}/a$ (all verified); for $a=\sqrt2/2$ it degenerates to the circle $x^2+y^2=z^2$.
- **Zone 2 (p. 10–12, eqs. (56)–(60), Fig. 11)**: pursuers drive $\theta\to0$ with (8) (claimed "obviously optimal" — rendezvous (46)–(47) is unavailable here since (52) fails), evader best-responds with (9). Four situations: (a) $d_1^0<d_2^0$ (evader curls toward $P_2$; final state $0<r_{12}^f<a(d_1^f+d_2^f)$, Fig. 11(a)); (b) $d_1^0=d_2^0$ with feedback (8) (Fig. 11(b)); (c) $d_1^0=d_2^0$ with conservative open-loop (24)–(25) direct meeting (Fig. 11(c)) — faster if the evader plays optimally, but (8) is the safe feedback choice; (d) the two coincide at $\theta_0=\arccos(1-2a^2)$ with time $d_1^0/\sqrt{v_e^2-v_p^2}$ (from (42)). For (b), $\cos\alpha^*=\cos(\theta_0/2)>\sqrt{1-a^2}$ gives (56) $|\dot d_1|=|\dot d_2| = v_e\cos(\theta/2) > \sqrt{v_e^2-v_p^2}$, hence (57) $T_1 < t_0+\frac{d_1^0-d_1^f}{\sqrt{v_e^2-v_p^2}}$; for (a), (58) $z_f = r_{12}^f/2 < a d_2^f$ and (59) $T_1 < t_0+\frac{d_2^0-d_2^f}{\sqrt{v_e^2-v_p^2}}+\frac{a d_2^f}{v_p}$; combined claim (60):
$$T_1 \le t_0 + \frac{\max\{d_1^0,d_2^0\}}{\sqrt{v_e^2-v_p^2}} \quad \text{(zone 2).}$$
  **proof gap (verified counterexample)**: (56) is proven only for $d_1^0=d_2^0$; for $d_1\ne d_2$, $|\dot d_2| = \frac{v_e\sin\theta}{\sqrt{b^2+1-2b\cos\theta}}\to0$ as $\theta\to0$ (when $b\ne1$), so the rate lower bound cannot extend, and the decomposition in (59) is unjustified. Numerically integrating the paper's own optimality system (4)–(6) under (8)–(9): for $a=0.6$, $d_2^0=1$, $b=0.3$ (a genuine zone-2 state: $r_{12}=0.745\le a(d_1+d_2)=0.78$, (52) violated), $\theta_0=0.60$ gives $T_1=1.423$ and $\theta_0=0.63$ gives $T_1=1.354$, both **exceeding** the claimed bound $1.25 = 1/\sqrt{v_e^2-v_p^2}$ by 8–14%. (For $b\in\{0.5,0.7,1.5,2,3\}$ tested zone-2 states the bound held.) So (60) is not valid as proven; it holds at least for moderately asymmetric states and for the (b) situation.
- **Zone 3 (p. 12, Fig. 12)**: strategies (8)–(9) again; trajectories are spirals preserving $b=b_0=d_1^0/d_2^0$ (exact: $\dot b=0$ under (8)–(9) — verified), with $\theta$ increasing to $\pi$ before $\min\{d_1,d_2\}$ hits 0 (since $\theta_0>\Theta(b_0)$ in zone 3 and $\dot\theta=\frac{v_e}{b\,d_2^2}(\cdot)$ pushes away from the invariant barrier $\Theta$); claimed bound $T_2 \le t_0+\frac{\max\{d_1^0,d_2^0\}}{\sqrt{v_e^2-v_p^2}}$ (unnumbered, justified only "similarly" — same structural weakness as (60), though my numerical tests in valid zone-3 states ($b\in\{0.3,3,1.5\}$, $\theta_0$ just above $\Theta(b)$) all satisfied it, with $\min\{d_1,d_2\}>0$ at $\theta=\pi$ as claimed).
- **Zone 4 (p. 12–13, eqs. (62)–(65), Fig. 13)**: evader dashes straight through the Apollonius interspace, $\xi<\alpha^*<\theta-\xi$ ($\xi=\arcsin a$); to delay, the pursuers retreat **perpendicular to $P_1P_2$** (minimizing the reachable included angle $\theta'$, Fig. 13(a)→(b)). Crossing time (62):
$$\Delta t = \frac{d_1 d_2\sin\theta}{r_{12}\,(v_e\sin(\alpha+\beta)-v_p)},\qquad \sin\beta=\frac{d_1\sin\theta}{r_{12}},\ \cos\beta=\frac{d_2-d_1\cos\theta}{r_{12}}.$$
  I verified (62) exactly: $d_1d_2\sin\theta/r_{12} = 2\,\mathrm{Area}(\triangle EP_1P_2)/r_{12} = h$ (distance of $E$ from line $P_1P_2$) and $v_e\sin(\alpha+\beta)$ is the evader's approach speed to the receding line, so $\Delta t = h/(\text{closing speed})$; a full planar simulation ($a=0.6$, $d_1=d_2=1$, $\theta_0=120^\circ$) confirms $\theta=\pi$ at exactly $t_0+1.25$. The evader maximizes $\sin(\alpha+\beta)$; at $\alpha^*=\theta/2$ (feasible iff $\theta>2\xi$ ✓ in zone 4) $\sin(\alpha+\beta)=1$. The conservative endpoint values are $\sigma_1 = \frac{1}{r_{12}^0}[a(d_2^0-d_1^0\cos\theta_0)+\sqrt{1-a^2}\,d_1^0\sin\theta_0]$ (64) and $\sigma_2$ (65) — I verified $\sigma_1=\sin(\xi+\beta)$ and $\sigma_2=\sin(\theta-\xi+\beta)$, so (63) $T_2\le t_0+\frac{d_1^0d_2^0\sin\theta_0}{r_{12}^0(v_e\min\{\sigma_1,\sigma_2\}-v_p)}$ is a valid upper bound (max of $\sin(\alpha+\beta)$ over the cone dominates both endpoint values). **Erratum (printed formula)**: the special case $d_1^0=d_2^0$ is printed as $T_2 = t_0+\frac{d_1^0\sin\theta_0}{\sqrt{2+2\cos\theta_0}(v_e-v_p)}$ (p. 12, verified from the 300-dpi render — the radical contains $2{+}2\cos\theta_0$). This equals $\frac{d_1^0\sin(\theta_0/2)}{v_e-v_p}$ and **contradicts the paper's own (62)**, which gives $\frac{d_1^0\cos(\theta_0/2)}{v_e-v_p} = \frac{d_1^0\sin\theta_0}{\sqrt{2-2\cos\theta_0}(v_e-v_p)}$: the sign under the radical should be minus (equivalently $\sin\to\cos$). Example: $\theta_0=120^\circ$, $a=0.6$ — exact 1.25 vs printed 2.165. (Whether the published TCYB version corrected this cannot be confirmed from the archived PDF.)

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
**Author-stated** (p. 3, 12): the explicit-policy method, while computationally cheap, "is limited to relatively simple game environment and simple motion models" (p. 3); future work: other restrictions on players, pursuer cooperation, more complex motion models (e.g., robotic fish [60]), and games with more pursuers (p. 12); the barrier can serve as a basis for determining the required number of pursuers and as a necessary-and-sufficient capture condition for related point-capture games (p. 12 — claim, not proven there).

**Critical observations (with loci)**:
1. **Bound (60) is not valid as proven** — see the verified counterexample under eq. (60) in Section 5 ($a=0.6$, $b=0.3$, zone-2 states, $T_1$ up to 1.14× the bound). Root cause: (56) is derived only for $d_1^0=d_2^0$ and $|\dot d_2|\to0$ near $\theta=0$ for $b\ne1$. The zone-3 bound has the same "similarly" justification (p. 12) and is unproven too (though it passed my tests).
2. **Erratum in the zone-4 special case** (p. 12): $\sqrt{2+2\cos\theta_0}$ should be $\sqrt{2-2\cos\theta_0}$ (or $\sin(\theta_0/2)\to\cos(\theta_0/2)$); inconsistent with the paper's own (62).
3. **Theorem 4.1's Nash step** (p. 5) and the **completeness argument for the intermediate band** ("it is predictable", p. 8) are heuristic; invariance of $\mathscr{D}_p^1$ under unilateral deviations and a formal proof that $\Omega\setminus\mathscr{D}_p^1$ is entirely evader-winning are not written out (the ingredients — pointwise $\dot\theta$ bound, sub-game-2 unreachability of simultaneous capture for $r_{12}>a(d_1+d_2)$, $\dot\theta>0$ up to $\theta=\pi$ — are all present and correct, so the gap is presentational rather than substantive).
4. **Theorem 4.3's discriminant logic** (p. 8) treats $w$ as free and the authors themselves flag "substantiation is needed"; the congruence argument partially assumes the conclusion.
5. **Point capture** (radius zero) is idealized — no sensing/turn-rate limits, no capture radius; the authors contrast this deliberately with [16] but the practical readability of the barrier under noise/latency is unaddressed.
6. **Strategy optimality in zone 2 is asserted ("obviously", p. 10), not proven**: my numerics show the rendezvous alternative would be faster whenever (52) holds, and the paper itself notes the (24)–(25) option beats (8) for $d_1^0=d_2^0$; a genuine minimum-time synthesis (HJI in $T_1$) is not performed — the bounds are upper bounds on the *claimed-optimal* play, not a value function.
7. The zone partition and strategy assignment live in the reduced/relative space where $z=r_{12}/2$ evolves; the figures are fixed-$z$ slices, so the exact 3-D reachable-set geometry of the switching surfaces is left implicit.

## 8. Citation Value
This is the canonical reference for **barrier construction via explicit policies in a two-pursuer/one-faster-evader "fishing" game with point capture**: it supplies (i) the closed-form barrier $r_{12}=a(d_1+d_2)$ (equivalently the Apollonius ellipse $x^2+(1-a^2)y^2=\frac{(1-a^2)z^2}{a^2}$ in relative coordinates), (ii) the non-escape angle $\Theta=\arccos\!\big(\frac{(1-a^2)(d_1+d_2)^2}{2d_1d_2}-1\big)$ and its maximum $\arccos(1-2a^2)$, and (iii) a four-zone minimum-time strategy synthesis fusing games of kind and degree. Use it in a TAC/TCYB Introduction to justify geometric (HJI-free) barrier analysis for multiplayer pursuit-evasion, as the point-capture/zero-capture-radius variant of Hagedorn–Breakwell's two-pursuer game, or as the building block when decomposing multi-pursuer sieges into pairwise "fishing" sub-games. Ready-to-adapt citation sentences: "Zha et al. constructed the barrier of a two-pursuer–one-evader fishing game with point capture in closed form, $r_{12}=a(d_1+d_2)$, via an explicit-policy method that avoids solving the HJI equations, and further derived minimum-time winning strategies and terminal-time bounds in each of four zones of the state space [P087]." Or: "The barrier and winning regions of the fishing game with a faster evader and zero capture radius were obtained analytically in [P087], where the pursuers win if and only if the initial included angle satisfies $\theta_0\le\arccos\big(\frac{(1-a^2)(d_1+d_2)^2}{2d_1d_2}-1\big)$." Caveat for citing the quantitative bounds: the zone-2 winning-time bound (60) and the printed zone-4 special-case formula carry the errata documented in Sections 5/7.
