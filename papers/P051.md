## 2. Research Background

Offline imitation learning (IL) datasets for multi-agent games are crowdsourced from many demonstrators and therefore contain trajectories of widely varying quality, from expert to amateur; standard IL algorithms (e.g., behavior cloning) treat all samples as homogeneous and can absorb undesirable, sub-optimal behaviors (p. 1). Reinforcement learning avoids this only at the cost of massive online interaction, which is sample-inefficient, costly and risky (game playing, autonomous driving, robotic manipulation motivating examples, p. 1). A natural remedy is to characterize each trajectory by a *strategy* — the demonstrators' latent behavioral signature — and filter out sub-optimal data before running any IL algorithm. However, in multi-agent games the demonstrator identity ("player ID") is typically unavailable in offline data, the effective strategy of a player varies with the opponent met within each episode, and zero-sum games break the usual "closer-to-uniform-random-is-worse" heuristic (a uniformly random strategy can be a Nash equilibrium, e.g., rock–paper–scissors) (pp. 1–3). The paper proposes STRIL, a plug-in preprocessing framework combining unsupervised per-trajectory strategy representations, two performance indicators, and indicator-based dataset filtering, validated on Two-player Pong, Limit Texas Hold'em and Connect Four.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap

**Problem** [authors' formulation, p. 3]: given a set Γ of trajectories τ = ((o₀,a₀),…,(o_T,a_T)) from a multi-player competitive zero-sum game, generated by diverse players without identity labels, with trajectory rewards available only for a subset Γ′ ⊂ Γ, extract per-trajectory strategy representations, distinguish player skill levels, and learn an expert policy via IL on a filtered dataset. Standing assumption: *the strategy of a player is consistent within a single trajectory* (p. 3).

**Gap** [authors' claims, pp. 1–2, with verification status]:
1. *Player-identification requirement*: [authors' claim] existing policy-representation learning needs agent IDs — "Grover et al. (2018) … use the information of agent identification during training … However, this information may not be provided in the offline datasets" (p. 2). Verified as the authors' characterization of Grover et al. (ICML 2018); consistent with that paper's use of an agent-distinguishing loss, not re-verified against the source.
2. *Strong assumptions in expertise estimation*: [authors' claim] the closest work, ILEED (Beliaev et al. 2022), "assume[s] that the demonstrator has a vector indicating the expertise of latent skills … and considers the policy worse when it is closer to uniformly random distribution. However, this assumption cannot be satisfied even in simple games like RPS, where a uniformly random strategy constitutes a Nash equilibrium" (p. 2). The RPS counterexample is the authors' own, sound argument against monotone-entropy expertise priors in zero-sum games; the description of ILEED's assumption matches ILEED's published abstract-level framing (not re-verified in detail).
3. *Environment dynamics / rankings / demonstrator IDs*: [authors' claim] prior sub-optimal-data IL methods (Brown et al. 2020; Chen et al. 2021; Zhang et al. 2021; Kim et al. 2022; Xu et al. 2022) "require environment dynamics, the rankings over the demonstrations, or the identification of demonstrators" (p. 2) — STRIL requires none of these, only ≤5% reward labels for one of its two indicators. Verified as authors' characterization; consistent with the cited literature's known requirements (T-REX/D-REX need rankings, DemoDICE needs imperfect+optimal split, etc.).
4. *Cooperative-setting restriction*: [authors' claim] Franzmeyer et al. (2024, ICLR), which also filters offline multi-agent data, "assumes a cooperative setting and requires reward information" (p. 2); STRIL targets competitive zero-sum games and needs no rewards for RI.
5. *Leakage of opponent information*: [authors' claim] plan-VAE approaches such as Play-LMP (Lynch et al. 2020) with an encoder outputting latent plans are "unsuitable for multi-player games, potentially leaking opponent information from the observations" (p. 2) — motivates conditioning the P-VRNN on the ego observation only. This is the authors' argument, not an experimental demonstration.

**Net gap**: no existing method learns per-trajectory strategy representations for *competitive* multi-agent games *without* player IDs, dynamics models, rankings, or (for one indicator) any reward signal, while provably tolerating the entropy-Nash pathology of zero-sum play.

## 4. Core Contributions

1. **Modeling (representation learning)**: the P-VRNN — a Variational Recurrent Neural Network (Chung et al. 2015) whose conditioning variable is *partially trainable*: the strategy representation **l** of each trajectory is a free, per-trajectory trainable variable (randomly initialized), held constant along the trajectory and jointly optimized with the four networks φ_pri, φ_enc, φ_dec, φ_rec to minimize the negative variational lower bound (Eqs. (1)–(9), p. 3–4). No encoder produces **l**; the representation *is* the learned per-trajectory latent code, and player IDs are never used.
2. **Theory (indicators, partially formal)**: (a) the **Randomness Indicator** RI(τ) = Σₜ H(p_θ(a_t|z_≤t,a_<t,o_≤t,l)) — cumulative one-step action-distribution entropy, from the identity that the expected reconstruction loss equals the decoder entropy (Eq. (10), p. 4); reward-free and fully unsupervised. (b) the **Exploited Level** EL(τ) — the conditional expected loss of the trajectory's mixed strategy against the demonstrator population (Eqs. (12)–(13), p. 4), with a neighborhood estimator EL_δ (Eq. (14), p. 5) and two unnumbered analytic claims: the limit lim_{δ→0⁺} EL_δ(τ) = EL(τ) and the bound EL_δ(τ) < ε₁ + αδM (p. 5).
3. **Algorithm (STRIL pipeline)**: three-step plug-in — (i) joint P-VRNN + representation training; (ii) indicator estimation (RI from the trained model; EL via a two-layer MLP regressor **L** trained on the ≤5% reward-labeled subset); (iii) filtered IL: keep trajectories below the p-th percentile of the chosen indicator, Γ̃_p = {τ ∈ Γ | I(τ) < I_p}, and run any off-the-shelf IL algorithm with masked loss (Eq. (15), p. 5).
4. **Experiment**: on Two-player Pong, Limit Texas Hold'em (RLCard) and Connect Four (PettingZoo), strategy representations cluster by demonstrator and rank by expertise without IDs; filtering by RI/EL improves BC, IQ-Learn and ILEED in 8 of 9 game×algorithm cells of Table 1 (WS metric, Eq. (16)), approaching the oracle "Best" (data from the single dominant demonstrator) in several cases (pp. 5–7).

### 5.0 Game model, information structure, reward, termination (Preliminaries, p. 2; Problem Formulation, p. 3)

- **Model**: Markov game (Littman 1994) = POMDP adapted to n agents: state space S; agent i has action space Aⁱ and observation space Oⁱ (a 'null' action fills Aⁱ when i does not act). Per step t: (1) i observes o_t^i ∈ Oⁱ and samples a_t^i ~ πⁱ(oⁱ,·), πⁱ: Oⁱ × Aⁱ → [0,1]; (2) receives reward r_t^i: S × Aⁱ → ℝ; (3) state transitions by T: S × A¹ × ⋯ × Aⁿ → S (p. 2). No dynamics equations are given for the three test games — the environments are black-box simulators.
- **Return**: trajectory τ = ((o₀ⁱ,a₀ⁱ),…,(o_Tⁱ,a_Tⁱ)); r̂ⁱ(τ) = Σ_{t=0}^{T} r_tⁱ; expected payoff rⁱ(π₋ᵢ,πⁱ) = 𝔼_{τ∼(π₋ᵢ,πⁱ)}[r̂ⁱ(τ)]. *Argument order convention: first slot = opponents, second slot = focal player* (p. 2). Termination: trajectories run to the games' natural end (length T); no artificial truncation or winning-condition equation is given.
- **Best responses and exploitability** (p. 2): BR(π₋ᵢ) = argmax_{π′ⁱ} rⁱ(π₋ᵢ,π′ⁱ); BR(πⁱ) = argmax_{π′₋ᵢ} Σ_{j∈P, j≠i} rʲ(π′₋ᵢ,πⁱ), which equals argmin_{π′₋ᵢ} rⁱ(π′₋ᵢ,πⁱ) in zero-sum games. Strategy-profile exploitability E(π) = Σ_{i∈P}(rⁱ(π₋ᵢ,BR(π₋ᵢ)) − rⁱ(π₋ᵢ,πⁱ)); in zero-sum *symmetric* cases the per-player version is E(πⁱ) = −rⁱ(BR(πⁱ),πⁱ) = Σ_{j≠i} rʲ(BR(πⁱ),πⁱ) (the equality Σ_j rʲ ≡ 0 in zero-sum). πⁱ is ε-Nash iff E(πⁱ) ≤ ε (p. 3, top).
- **Information structure of the model**: the P-VRNN conditions on the *ego* observation o_t only (never on opponent observations), explicitly "to disentangle the strategy of the opponent player from the strategy representation" (p. 3) — this is the anti-leakage design decision vis-à-vis plan-VAEs.

### 5.1 Generative model of P-VRNN (p. 3, Eqs. (1)–(4))

- **Eq. (1)**: p(a_≤T, z_≤T | o_≤T, l) = Π_{t=1}^{T} p(a_t | z_≤t, a_<t, o_≤t, l) · p(z_t | a_<t, z_<t, o_≤t, l) — "generation" × "prior" factors. The latent z_t captures the stochastic decision state; **l** is the trajectory-level strategy representation.
- **Eq. (2)** (prior): z_t | h_{t−1}, o_t, l ∼ 𝒩(μ_pri,t, diag(σ²_pri,t)), [μ_pri,t, σ_pri,t] = φ_pri(h_{t−1}, o_t, l). Gaussian with diagonal covariance (VAE convention, p. 3).
- **Eq. (3)** (generation/decoder): a_t | h_{t−1}, z_t, o_t, l ∼ Cat(μ_dec,t), μ_dec,t = φ_dec(h_{t−1}, z_t, o_t, l) — categorical over the discrete action set.
- **Eq. (4)** (recurrence): h_t = φ_rec(h_{t−1}, a_t, z_t, o_t, l) — h_t compresses all past actions/latents/observations.
- *Architecture note*: Figure 2 (p. 3) decomposes P-VRNN into four modules (Prior φ_pri, Generation φ_dec, Recurrence φ_rec, Inference φ_enc); each takes **l** as an input. Internal sizes, layer types (GRU vs LSTM), and latent dimension of z_t are **UNREPORTED**. The "partially-trainable" in the name refers to **l** being a trainable variable (drawn in Fig. 2 as combined diamond+circle), unlike all conditioning in the original VRNN.

### 5.2 Inference model and training objective (pp. 3–4, Eqs. (5)–(9))

- **Eq. (5)**: q(z_≤T | a_≤T, o_≤T, l) = Π_{t=1}^{T} q(z_t | a_≤t, z_<t, o_≤t, l) — factorized approximate posterior.
- **Eq. (6)** (encoder): z_t | h_{t−1}, a_t, o_t, l ∼ 𝒩(μ_enc,t, diag((σ_enc,t)²)), [μ_enc,t, σ_enc,t] = φ_enc(h_{t−1}, a_t, o_t, l).
- **Eq. (7)**: 𝓛 = 𝔼_{q_φ(z_≤T|a_≤T,o_≤T,l)} [ Σ_{t=1}^{T} (𝓛_Recon,t + 𝓛_Reg,t) ] — negative variational lower bound, following Chung et al. (2015).
- **Eq. (8)**: 𝓛_Recon,t = −log p_θ(a_t | z_≤t, a_<t, o_≤t, l).
- **Eq. (9)**: 𝓛_Reg,t = KL(q_φ(z_t|a_≤t,z_<t,o_≤t,l) ‖ p_θ(z_t|a_<t,z_<t,o_≤t,l)).
- **Representation-learning procedure (the core mechanism)**: at training start, **l** is randomly initialized per trajectory τ; during training the φ-networks are optimized over all trajectories while each **l** is optimized individually, so "**l** should be adjusted to more effectively capture and express the unique strategies of each trajectory"; the process is unsupervised, without player identification (p. 4). Note this is *amortization-free* representation learning: one trainable vector per dataset trajectory (new trajectories would require re-training or an added encoder, which is not addressed — extensibility gap).

### 5.2-bis Result 1 (entropy identity, p. 4, Eq. (10)) — decisive intermediate step for RI

- **Statement**: for a well-trained P-VRNN whose decoder matches the demonstrator's true action distribution, the expected one-step reconstruction loss equals the decoder's entropy: 𝔼_{p_θ(a_t|z_≤t,a_<t,o_≤t,l)}[𝓛_Recon,t] = Σ_{i=1}^{n_t} −p_θ(a_{t,i}|·) log p_θ(a_{t,i}|·) = H(p_θ(a_t | z_≤t, a_<t, o_≤t, l)) (Eq. (10)).
- **Assumptions**: (i) the model is well trained so that "the predicted action distribution closely matches the true probability distribution of the corresponding strategy of the trajectory" (p. 4) — an *assumption*, not a proved guarantee (no identifiability or distribution-matching result is given for P-VRNN training); (ii) discrete action set {a_{t,1},…,a_{t,n_t}}.
- **Proof approach**: direct — plug the definition 𝓛_Recon,t = −log p_θ(a_t|·) into the expectation taken under p_θ itself; the negative-log expectation of a categorical under itself is its entropy. Trivial analytically; the content is the *interpretation*: the expected reconstruction loss measures the intrinsic randomness of the strategy, not the model error.
- **Meaning**: a deterministic (skilled) player yields low decoder entropy; a "random" player yields high entropy. The paper explicitly adopts "[t]he hypothesis of Beliaev et al. (2022), a strategy with more randomness is considered worse" (p. 4) — inheriting the ILEED heuristic that the authors themselves criticized in its expertise-vector form; here it is used only as a filtering signal, and the RPS pathology (random = Nash) is acknowledged in Related Work as the reason RI alone can mislead in equilibrium-randomizing games (p. 2 vs p. 4 tension; see §7).

### 5.2-ter Result 2 (Randomness Indicator, p. 4, Eq. (11))

- **Statement**: RI(τ) = Σ_{t=1}^{T} H(p_θ(a_t | z_≤t, a_<t, o_≤t, l)) — cumulative decoder entropy along the trajectory under its own representation **l**.
- **Properties**: reward-free, fully unsupervised (p. 4); global (whole-trajectory), analytic given the trained model; requires sampling z_≤t (from the posterior) to evaluate H. Lower RI ⇒ "more stable/deterministic" ⇒ kept by filtering (low-percentile retention, Eq. (15)).
- **Physical meaning**: proxy for behavioral consistency; in the experiments low-RI trajectories coincide with dominant demonstrators in Pong and Hold'em (Fig. 3b,f,j).

### 5.2-quater Result 3 (Exploited Level, pp. 4–5, Eqs. (12)–(13))

- **Setup/assumptions**: a measure dπ is defined on strategy space Π; strategies of the agents generating Γ and its labeled subset Γ′ are both sampled according to dπ. A trajectory should strictly map to a distribution over strategies τ(π) with ∫_{Π} τ(π) dπ = 1, but the mixture ∫_Π π τ(π) dπ is viewed as a single mixed strategy, so the notation π(τ) is used (p. 4).
- **Eq. (12)**: EL(τ) = 𝔼_π[ −r(π, π(τ)) | r(π, π(τ)) ≤ 0 ], where per the preliminaries convention r(π, π(τ)) is the expected trajectory reward *of the player using strategy π(τ)* against opponents playing π ~ dπ (the demonstrator population); "by default" wording on p. 4.
- **Eq. (13)**: EL(τ) = ∫_{π∈Π} (−r(π, π(τ)))₊ dπ / ∫_{π∈Π} 𝟙_{r(π,π(τ))≤0} dπ, with (x)₊ = max{x,0}, 𝟙_c the indicator. The numerator over all of Π equals the integral restricted to {r ≤ 0} because (−r)₊ vanishes on {r > 0}, so (13) is exactly the conditional expectation (12).
- **Meaning**: EL(τ) is the average amount by which τ's strategy *loses* to the demonstrator population, over the population members that beat it — "the extent to which the demonstrators exploit the strategy of τ" (pp. 4–5). High EL ⇒ exploitable ⇒ sub-optimal; low EL near Nash strategies (a strategy that cannot be exploited has few negative-reward opponents, driving EL down). Filtering keeps *low*-EL trajectories.
- **Status**: definitional; no closed form (r against the population is unavailable), hence the estimator below. Local (per-trajectory), analytic-only.

### 5.2-quinquies Result 4 (neighborhood estimator EL_δ, p. 5, Eq. (14)) + Claim A (limit) + Claim B (bound)

- **Eq. (14)**: EL_δ(τ) = Σ_{d(f(τ),f(τ′))<δ} (−r(π̂, π(τ′)))₊ / Σ_{d(f(τ),f(τ′))<δ} 𝟙_{r(π̂,π(τ′))≤0}, where d is a metric over the representation space and f maps trajectories to learned representations (f(τ) = **l**, the P-VRNN trainable code).
- **Proof gap (notation)**: **π̂ in Eq. (14) is never defined** (p. 5). For the quotient to approximate EL(τ) of Eq. (12), π̂ must range over the demonstrator population paired with each labeled τ′ (i.e., r(π̂, π(τ′)) = r̂(τ′), the recorded reward of the labeled trajectory), and the sums must run over the reward-labeled subset Γ′ — neither point is stated. If π̂ were a fixed reference strategy the limit claim below would be false, so the paired-population reading is the only consistent one.
- **Claim A (limit)**: "It can be proved that lim_{δ→0⁺} EL_δ(τ) = EL(τ)" (p. 5). *Assumptions*: strategies sampled from dπ; similar representations ⇒ similar strategies (Claim B's assumption); implicitly, the labeled-set density around f(τ) is positive. *Proof approach*: none given — "proof gap: no proof, no appendix (the PDF has no supplementary material); the claim requires an argument that the empirical conditional mean over a shrinking neighborhood converges (law of large numbers over the dπ-sampled population plus continuity of the reward in the strategy), which cannot hold pointwise for a single fixed labeled sample without further regularity."
- **Lipschitz bridge (asserted, p. 5)**: "Due to the Lipschitz continuity of the P-VRNN with respect to the representation, the trajectories with similar strategy representations have similar strategies." *Proof gap*: no Lipschitz-constant analysis is given; nothing in the training objective (Eq. (7)) enforces a bounded global Lipschitz constant w.r.t. **l** (indeed **l** is free per trajectory, so nearby codes need not correspond to nearby strategies — the assumption is empirical, supported only by the clustering visualizations of Fig. 3).
- **Claim B (bound)**: "It can be proved that EL_δ(τ) < ε₁ + αδM" (p. 5) under: (i) π(τ) is ε₁-Nash; (ii) any pure strategy can exploit another strategy by at most M; (iii) similar representations induce similar strategies — if d(f(τ₁), f(τ₂)) < δ then ∫_Π |τ₁(π) − τ₂(π)| dπ < αδ, α a constant. *Proof approach (reconstructed, not in paper)*: for ε₁-Nash π(τ), every individual loss term satisfies (−r(π,π(τ)))₊ ≤ E(π(τ)) ≤ ε₁ (no population member beats the BR's payoff); replacing π(τ) by a neighbor π(τ′) with L¹-strategy distance < αδ perturbs the reward by at most M·αδ via assumption (ii) applied to the strategy difference; averaging over the neighborhood gives EL_δ(τ) < ε₁ + αδM. *Proof gap*: strictly inequality "<" vs "≤" unexplained; M is an abstract unmeasured constant (and unbounded payoff differences between mixed strategies at L¹-distance αδ would need a Lipschitz condition on the payoff functional, only heuristically covered by (ii)); the conditional (loss-only) averaging means the bound mixes the exploitation bound ε₁ with a worst-case perturbation term — plausible but never formally derived. Also note the bound is *local in δ* and vacuous as a filtering criterion unless M is small relative to ε₁.
- **Extensibility**: EL_δ is a generic "kernel regression of the loss over a latent ball" recipe; nothing in it is P-VRNN-specific beyond the metric d on codes — it could wrap any trajectory encoder.

### 5.2-sexies Result 5 (EL regressor, p. 5, unnumbered objective)

- **Statement**: train an operator **L** (a two-layer MLP) to estimate EL from the representation: minimize Σ_{r̂ⁱ≥0} ‖L(lⁱ) − r̂ⁱ‖₂ over the labeled trajectories τⁱ, "so that the prediction from L(l) becomes close to the mean of satisfying reward r̂ ≥ 0 nearby. After training L, we can directly obtain the EL of a single trajectory τ … By applying EL estimator L on the representation f(τ) of trajectory τ, we can get the desired result L(f(τ))" (p. 5).
- **Proof gap (sign inconsistency)**: as literally printed, with r̂ⁱ = "the trajectory reward of the i-th trajectory" (τⁱ's own return, per the preliminaries' r̂ⁱ(τ) definition), the regression target is the *winning* margin of nearby trajectories, which estimates dominance, not EL (the mean *loss*). Consistency with Eqs. (12)–(14) requires r̂ⁱ to denote the payoff earned *against* π(τⁱ) (the exploiter's side, = −(τⁱ's own reward) in a zero-sum game); Figure 1's Step-2 panel (p. 2) indeed plots the EL estimation scatter with y-axis labeled **−rⁱ** against lⁱ, supporting the negated-reward reading. The text never states this sign flip — a genuine notation gap a re-implementer must resolve (the authors' released intent, and the only reading under which "L(f(τ))" equals EL(τ), is r̂ⁱ := −r̂ⁱ(τⁱ) restricted to exploited τⁱ).
- Note also the conditioning mismatch: EL is a *conditional* mean (only losing opponents), while the MLP regression is an unconditional local mean over the r̂ ≥ 0 subset chosen by neighborhood — the relation between the two averagings is not formalized.

### 5.3 Result 6 (filtered IL objective, p. 5, Eq. (15)) and metric (Eq. (16))

- **Eq. (15)**: for indicator I and percentile p (I_p defined by P_τ[I(τ) < I_p] = p), the filtered dataset is Γ̃_p = {τ ∈ Γ | I(τ) < I_p} and the IL loss becomes 𝓛_p(π) = 𝔼_τ[ 𝟙_{I(τ)<I_p} · 𝓛^IL(π, τ) ]; e.g., vanilla BC has 𝓛^IL(π,τ) = Σ_{t=0}^{|τ|} log π(a_t|o_t). p → 0 discards more data; p = 1 recovers the base IL algorithm exactly — the plug-in property.
- **Eq. (16)**: evaluation metric WS(I, π_i) = min_{j∈I} r_i(π_j, π_i) — the Worst Score of the imitated policy over the demonstrator set I (worst-case reward against the population's members), capturing "the worst-case scenario against opponent strategies in the dataset" (p. 5). Rewards for evaluation: (N_win − N_lose)/N_game for Pong and Connect Four; average chip differential for Limit Texas Hold'em; N_game = 2000 (p. 5).
- **Status**: Eq. (15) is a trivial masking identity; the analytic content of STRIL's filtering claim (that the masked risk concentrates on the dominant policy's data) is *empirical*, supported by Table 1/Fig. 4, not theoretical.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues

**Author-stated** (p. 7, Conclusion): future work to use P-VRNN as a customized behavior-prediction model; to explore the geometry of the strategy-representation space; to integrate the indicators into IL beyond hard filtering. In-text caveats: RI is unstable across percentiles and can *hurt* ILEED on Connect Four (pp. 6–7); EL needs reward labels (5%) while RI needs none (pp. 4–5).

**Critical observations** (with page/eq):
1. **No proofs for the two analytic claims** (p. 5): lim_{δ→0⁺} EL_δ = EL and EL_δ < ε₁ + αδM are asserted ("it can be proved") with no derivation and no appendix; the Lipschitz continuity of the P-VRNN w.r.t. the representation is assumed, not established, and nothing in the objective (Eq. (7)) enforces it (proof gap, §5.2-quinquies).
2. **Undefined symbol and sign ambiguity**: π̂ in Eq. (14) is never defined (p. 5); the EL-regressor target Σ_{r̂ⁱ≥0}‖L(lⁱ) − r̂ⁱ‖₂ is inconsistent with EL's sign convention unless r̂ⁱ is read as the *negated* reward (Fig. 1's −rⁱ axis supports this, but the text says "trajectory reward of the i-th trajectory") (p. 5; proof gap, §5.2-sexies).
3. **The ε₁-Nash hypothesis is unverifiable in-domain**: the bound's premise (π(τ) is ε₁-Nash) presupposes knowledge of exploitability — exactly the quantity IL cannot compute; the bound therefore validates the estimator only circularly (p. 5).
4. **RI inherits the entropy heuristic the paper itself refuted**: Related Work criticizes ILEED because uniform-random play can be a Nash (RPS) (p. 2), yet RI equates high entropy with bad play (Eq. (11), p. 4); the method is safe only where dominant strategies are deterministic — the authors' own Connect Four/ILEED failure and the RI instability across p are symptoms (pp. 6–7).
5. **Per-trajectory trainable codes do not amortize**: since **l** is a free variable optimized per training trajectory (p. 4), encoding a *new* trajectory requires a new optimization; no incremental encoder or out-of-sample procedure is given (extensibility gap).
6. **Scale/realism**: only two-player zero-sum games with small discrete action sets; trajectory counts, P-VRNN sizes, training budgets are UNREPORTED (§6 table); WS is computed against *dataset* demonstrators only — never against a best response or an external agent pool, so "dominance within the dataset" is the only notion of strength ever measured (Eq. (16), p. 5).
7. **Consistency details**: intro claims "less than 5%" reward labels (p. 1) vs exactly 5% in experiments (p. 5); the assumption "strategy of a player is consistent within a single trajectory" (p. 3) is untestable without IDs and is load-bearing for both the representation semantics and Claim A; Fig. 3's qualitative clustering has no quantitative cluster-separation metric.

## 8. Citation Value

**Paragraph role**: this is the *learning-based, data-curation* pole of the pursuit-evasion/reach-avoid literature — the counterpoint to analytic solution concepts. In a differential-game paper it serves three roles: (i) in the Introduction/Related Work as evidence that when opponent models or demonstrator identities are unavailable, latent strategy representations plus exploitability-flavored indicators (EL is a sampled, population-based surrogate of exploitability, cf. the E(π) definition on p. 2) can rank and filter behaviors in competitive games — the data-driven analog of computing a best response; (ii) as a methodological source for hybrid analytic+learned pipelines, where geometric solution concepts (Apollonius/barrier constructions) supply the reward labels or the dominant-strategy anchor that STRIL's EL estimator needs, and STRIL supplies the mechanism for pruning heterogeneous demonstration data before policy distillation; (iii) as an honest example of zero-sum-game-specific pathologies (entropy ≠ incompetence under equilibrium randomization) that any learned opponent-modeling module in a game-theoretic pipeline must confront.

**Ready-to-adapt citation sentence**: "Lei et al. (AAAI-25) learn per-trajectory strategy representations with a partially-trainable-conditioned VRNN and derive reward-free (Randomness Indicator) and label-efficient (Exploited Level) quality indicators that filter sub-optimal demonstrations, substantially improving behavior cloning, IQ-Learn and ILEED in two-player zero-sum games such as Pong, Limit Texas Hold'em and Connect Four — a data-curation counterpart to analytic exploitability, but restricted to small discrete-action games and supported only empirically (no proofs of the stated estimator-consistency and ε₁ + αδM bounds)."
