## 2. Research Background
Pursuit-evasion (PE) has classically been treated as a differential game with known models and complete information (Isaacs; Garcia et al.; Lopez et al.), while incomplete-information settings have been increasingly handled by reinforcement learning (single-loop policy iteration for linear zero-sum games, Q-learning ADP). Separately, safety-critical control via control barrier functions (CBFs) enforces forward invariance of a safe set, both model-based (Ames et al.; Ding et al.; adaptive CBFs) and, more recently, model-free via safe RL (Marvi & Kiumarsi; Li et al.). The intersection — "safety-aware" PE games in which *both* the pursuer and the evader must keep their own trajectories inside prescribed safe sets (obstacle avoidance, safe distances, speed limits) while playing the game — is nascent: the authors cite Gaussian-process/finite-time-RL safety-aware PE games (Kokolakis & Vamvoudakis), safe tracking in mixed zero-sum games (Qin et al.), and formation-surrounding / attack-defense guidance with collision constraints (Xiong & Zhang; Kang et al.). This paper joins the two threads: a CBF-based safeguarding layer is superposed on learning-based game strategies obtained by off-policy RL, so that safety holds even during the learning iterations.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
**Problem (Problem 1, p. 5442)**: Pursuer ẋ_p = Ax_p + Bu_p (1), evader ẋ_e = Ax_e + Bu_e (2) — identical linear dynamics, x_p, x_e ∈ R^n, u_p, u_e ∈ R^m — each subject to a safe set C_j = {x_j ∈ R^n : h_j(x_j) ≥ 0} (3a) with boundary ∂C_j (3b) and interior Int(C_j) (3c), j ∈ {p, e}. With position difference x = x_p − x_e,
- ẋ = Ax + Bu_p − Bu_e  (4).
Strategies are constrained to the additive form
- u_j = u_j^s(x_j) + u_j^n(x), j ∈ {p, e}  (5),
where u_j^s is a safeguarding control and u_j^n the nominal game strategy. **Problem 1**: with system matrix A unknown and any x_j(0) ∈ Int(C_j), design u_j of the form (5) such that Int(C_j) is forward invariant, and provide a sufficient condition for asymptotic stability of (4) (i.e., lim_{t→∞} x(t) = 0 = capture). Assumptions: (A, B) controllable (Assumption 1); safe sets bounded (Assumption 2, following [33]); a persistent-excitation (PE) condition (Assumption 3, (23)).

**Gap — [authors' claim]**: existing PE strategies [1], [6], [11], [19] do not handle safety of players' trajectories, and existing quadratic-programming safe-control/safe-RL methods [15], [20], [21] are "inapplicable since in the presence of safety (3) and unknown system dynamics of (1), the existence of a feasible solution may not be always guaranteed" (Remark 1, p. 5442). **[Verified from cited text]**: the cited PE works (e.g., Garcia et al. multi-pursuer games, Lopez et al. graph-based strategies) indeed contain no state-constraint machinery, so the first half is accurate. The second half (infeasibility of CBF-QP under unknown dynamics) is asserted, not proven anywhere in the paper — it is a plausibility argument (a QP needs the model in its constraints; feasibility of CBF-QPs is in general not guaranteed), which the authors then circumvent *by construction*: instead of filtering a game strategy through a QP, they add an analytic repelling term (6) and bound the nominal strategy itself via a nonquadratic cost. **Additional gap the authors claim**: existing RL approaches [15], [16] use quadratic costs and cannot deliver bounded strategies compatible with Lemma 1, and their convergence analyses do not cover the multiple-network off-policy scheme with a nonquadratic integrand (Remark 5, p. 5444–5445). This is fair: [15] (single-loop policy iteration, Automatica 2024) and [16] (Al-Tamimi et al. Q-learning, Automatica 2007) both treat quadratic zero-sum games. What the paper does *not* claim, and does not have, is classical differential-game structure (barriers, Apollonius/dominance regions, finite-time capture): the game is reduced to a saddle-point optimal-control problem on the error state (4) with capture = asymptotic stability.

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Theory (safety layer)**: A safeguarding control u_j^s(x_j) = −c_j B^T∇B_j(x_j) (6) built on a reciprocal-form CBF B_j = b_j² (7) satisfying (8), with a feasibility condition (9) (a relaxation of the third condition of [34, Assumption 1], Cohen & Belta); Lemma 1 proves forward invariance of Int(C_j) whenever the nominal strategy u_j^n(x) is bounded — no QP, no model of A needed, B only.
2. **Theory (bounded game strategies + capture)**: A new nonquadratic game cost (13) with tanh^{-1}-integral effort terms (Abu-Khalaf–Lewis-type saturating cost) whose saddle-point solution (14)–(16) is *bounded by construction* (|u_j^{n*}| < λ_j), making it seamlessly compatible with Lemma 1; Theorem 1 gives a sufficient condition (29) under which the closed loop (4) remains asymptotically stable despite the safeguarding perturbation, i.e., capture is preserved.
3. **Algorithm (model-free learning)**: Off-policy RL (extension of Jiang & Jiang's off-policy ADP architecture [31] to the safe-game setting): critic least-squares update (24) from integrated data (21)–(22), actor update (25), all with A unknown; Algorithm 1 (p. 5444) runs the whole procedure and safety is maintained at every iteration because the safeguarding term is active during data collection (20). Lemma 2 proves (N_v, N_j → ∞) convergence of the learned value and policies to V* and u_j^{n*} on a compact Ω ∋ 0.
4. **Experiment (simulation)**: Double-integrator PE with four circular obstacles validating avoidance, capture, weight convergence, a baseline comparison against the IRL approach of [41] (which enters unsafe zones), a gain-tradeoff study for c_j, and a constructed failure case where (29) fails and capture is lost.

## 5. Key Mathematical Results

### 5.1 Dynamics, information structure, cost, termination
- **Dynamics**: (1), (2), identical (A, B); A unknown to both players, B known (B enters (6), (16), (22), (25) — see §7). Error dynamics (4).
- **Information structure**: nominal strategies are functions of the relative state x only, u_j^n(x); safeguarding controls are functions of each player's own absolute state x_j; so each player needs its own position and the relative position — decentralized in the safety channel, centralized-in-x in the game channel.
- **Cost (13, p. 5442)**: V(x) = ∫_0^∞ [x^T(τ)Qx(τ) + J(u_p, u_e)] dτ, with
  J(u_p, u_e) = 2∫_0^{u_p} (λ_p tanh^{-1}(v/λ_p))^T R_p dv − 2∫_0^{u_e} (λ_e tanh^{-1}(v/λ_e))^T R_e dv,
  Q = Q^T > 0, R_j = diag(r_{j1}, …, r_{jm}) > 0, λ_j > 0. Writing q(u) = 2∫_0^u (λ tanh^{-1}(v/λ))^T R dv (35) (odd, convex, nonnegative), J = q(u_p) − q(u_e): the pursuer's effort is penalized, the evader's effort enters as a reward offset. Because tanh^{-1}(v/λ) → ±∞ as v → ±λ, the stationarity conditions deliver controls bounded by ±λ — the entire point of the nonquadratic design.
- **Game value and HJI**: V*(x) = min_{u_p} max_{u_e} V(x) (14); Hamiltonian H(x, ∇V, u_p, u_e) = x^TQx + J + ∇V^T(Ax + Bu_p − Bu_e) (15); HJI: H(x, ∇V*, u_p^{n*}, u_e^{n*}) = 0 with V*(0) = 0 (17).
- **Saddle-point strategies (16, p. 5443)**: u_j^{n*}(x) = −λ_j tanh( (1/(2λ_j)) R_j^{-1} B^T∇V*(x) ), j ∈ {p, e} — obtained from ∂H/∂u_j = 0. Note both players' optimal controls share the same formula; the evader's opposing influence enters the dynamics through the −Bu_e term of (4) (its stationarity equation has the −∇V^TB sign flipped, which produces the same tanh expression).
- **Termination/winning**: capture = asymptotic stability of (4) (lim_{t→∞} x(t) = 0); no finite-time capture notion, no barrier surface in the Isaacs sense.

### 5.2 CBF construction and Lemma 1 (forward invariance)
**CBF construction (pp. 5442–5443)**: For j ∈ {p, e},
- u_j^s(x_j) = −c_j B^T∇B_j(x_j)  (6), c_j > 0;
- B_j(x_j) = (b_j(x_j))²  (7), with b_j : Int(C_j) → R_{≥0} continuously differentiable satisfying
  - 1/α_{j1}(h_j(x_j)) ≤ b_j(x_j) ≤ 1/α_{j2}(h_j(x_j))  (8a),
  - inf_{u_j∈R^m} {ḃ_j(x_j)} ≤ α_{j3}(h_j(x_j))  (8b),
  for class-K functions α_{j1}, α_{j2}, α_{j3} (reciprocal/CBF-style conditions in the sense of [34]);
- feasibility: ‖∇b_j^T(x_j)B‖ ≠ 0 for x_j ∈ N(∂C_j), a neighborhood of ∂C_j (9) — explicitly "a relaxation of the third condition of [34, Assumption 1]".
The example uses b_j = 1/h_j, so B_j → ∞ as x_j → ∂C_j, giving the "repelling force" of (6).

**Lemma 1 (p. 5442)**: Consider Int(C_j) of (3). Under Assumptions 1 and 2, if u_j^n(x) is bounded for all x ∈ R^n, then for any x_j(0) ∈ Int(C_j), under (5) with u_j^s from (6), x_j(t) ∈ Int(C_j), j ∈ {p, e}, for all t ≥ 0.
- **Assumptions used**: A1 (controllability — invoked in the statement but never used in the proof), A2 (bounded safe set — used implicitly to speak of compact boundary neighborhoods), boundedness of u_j^n on all of R^n.
- **Proof approach** (for j = p; e identical): (i) From (7)–(8a), B_p has inf_{Int(C_p)} B_p ≥ 0 and lim_{x_p→∂C_p} B_p = ∞ (10), hence lim_{x_p→∂C_p}‖∇B_p^T‖ = ∞. (ii) From (9), assert existence of b > 0 with b ≤ ‖∇B_p^T(x_p)B‖/‖∇B_p^T(x_p)‖. (iii) Compute the derivative under (5)–(6): Ḃ_p = ∇B_p^T(Ax_p + Bu_p^n) − c_p‖∇B_p^T B‖² ≤ ‖∇B_p^T‖²( [(‖Ax_p‖ + ‖Bu_p^n‖)/‖∇B_p^T‖] − c_p b² ) — the decisive inequality chain, using ‖∇B_p^T B‖² ≥ b²‖∇B_p^T‖². (iv) Since u_p^n bounded and ‖∇B_p^T‖ → ∞ at the boundary, the bracket → −c_p b², so lim_{x_p→∂C_p} Ḃ_p = −∞ < 0 (11). (v) Contradiction/extension argument: if B_p(x_p(t)) → ∞ at some finite t_1, then Ḃ_p must blow up to +∞ as t → t_1 (to reach ∞ in finite time), contradicting (11); hence B_p < ∞ ∀t ≥ 0 (12), so b_p < ∞, so 1/α_{p1}(h_p) < ∞, i.e., h_p > 0 ∀t — x_p(t) ∈ Int(C_p).
- **Analytic/local**: analytic proof; per-player (local in the sense each player's own safe set), global in the state space of Int(C_j).
- **Physical meaning**: the gradient-descent term (6) along −B^T∇B_j grows unboundedly strong near the boundary and provably overwhelms any bounded game strategy plus the drift term, so safety strictly dominates the game.
- **Extensibility**: any CBF of the reciprocal form (7)–(8) with (9); the authors state (p. 5445) the same lemma can supply the safety layer for heterogeneous scenarios and for plain safety-constrained optimal control.
- **Proof gaps**: (a) step (ii) claims a *uniform* b > 0 from the pointwise condition (9) (‖∇b^T B‖ ≠ 0); continuity plus nonvanishing on the non-compact set N(∂C_p) ∩ Int(C_p) does not by itself give a positive infimum — a mild rigor gap (p. 5442–5443, between (9) and (11)); in the example B = I_2 makes the ratio trivially 1. (b) Typo: the limit in (11) is written lim_{x_p→∂C} (missing subscript). (c) A1 is never invoked.

### 5.3 NN approximation and off-policy learning architecture
**RL architecture (pp. 5443–5444)** — state/action/reward precisely:
- *State (critic input)*: relative state x ∈ Ω (compact set containing the origin). Value function approximated on a single-layer linear-in-parameters network by the Weierstrass high-order approximation theorem [36], [37]: V*(x) = ω_v φ_v(x) + e_v(x) = Σ_{l=1}^{N_v} ω_v^l φ_v^l(x) + e_v(x) ∀x ∈ Ω (18), with linearly independent smooth basis φ_v^l : R^n → R and ideal weights ω_v ∈ R^{N_v}.
- *Actions (actors)*: two networks û_{j,i+1}(x) = Σ_{l=1}^{N_j} ω̂_{j,i}^l φ_j^l(x) (19b) approximating the pursuit and escape policies; critic Û_i(x) = Σ_l ω̂_{v,i}^l φ_v^l(x) (19a).
- *Reward/cost*: the running integrand x^TQx + J(u_p, u_e) of (13) — no discounted reward; infinite-horizon undiscounted integral game cost.
- *Data collection (20)*: on [t_0, t_K] apply ǔ_j = u_{j0}(x) + u_j^s(x_j) + e_j, j ∈ {p, e} — admissible initial control + safeguarding + exploration noise; record x(t).
- *Critic update (24)*: least squares ω̂_{v,i}^T = (Φ_iΦ_i^T)^{-1}Φ_iΨ_i, where (21)–(22) stack, over k = 0, …, K−1, φ_{ki}^l = φ_v^l(x(t_{k+1})) − φ_v^l(x(t_k)) − ∫_{t_k}^{t_{k+1}} (∇φ_v^l)^T B[(u_p − û_{p,i}) − (u_e − û_{e,i})] dt and Ψ_i^k = ∫_{t_k}^{t_{k+1}} (−x^TQx − J(û_{p,i}, û_{e,i})) dt — i.e., the integrated HJI residual decoupled from the applied (exploratory) input, the hallmark of off-policy ADP [31]. A is eliminated because only B-weighted integrals of the applied input occur.
- *Actor update (25)*: ω̂_{j,i+1}^T = −(φ_j^Tφ_j)^{-1} φ_j^T λ_j tanh( (1/(2λ_j)) R_j^{-1} B^T ∇φ_v(x) ω̂_{v,i}^T ) — projecting the tanh target policy (16) onto the actor basis; φ_j (as a stacked matrix) is used but never precisely defined (notational gap).
- **Assumption 3 (PE, (23))**: (1/K)Σ_{k=0}^{K−1} Φ_i^k (Φ_i^k)^T ≥ ζ I_{N_v} for K ≥ K_0, some ζ > 0 — "the persistent excitation condition"; Remark 3 states (23) is not impacted by the safeguarding controller and can be enforced by the noise e_j.
- **Algorithm 1 (p. 5444, 15 lines)**: choose sizes/bases/weights of Q, R_j, tolerances ε_1, ε_2; choose b_j satisfying (8), build (6)–(7); find admissible u_{j0}; collect x(t) on [0, t_K] under (20); repeat: (22) → (24) → (25) until ‖ω̂_{v,i} − ω̂_{v,i−1}‖ ≤ ε_1 and ‖ω̂_{j,i} − ω̂_{j,i−1}‖ ≤ ε_2; then set u_j^n(x) = û_{j,i*+1}(x) = Σ_l ω̂_{j,i*}^l φ_j^l(x) (26) and compose (5). (Index typo in (26): û_{j,i*+1} written with weights ω̂_{j,i*}.)
- Remark 4 (p. 5444) candidly states the composed design (5) "is no longer an optimal one" because of the added safeguarding term.

### 5.4 Lemma 2 (convergence of the learning scheme)
**Lemma 2 (p. 5444)**: Assume there exists an initial admissible control policy û_{j0}(x) = u_{j0}(x) for (19b). Under Assumption 3, given any ε_1 > 0, ε_2 > 0, there exist integers N_v* > 0, N_j* > 0 such that, with the converged quantities (27), |Û_{i*}(x) − V*(x)| < ε_1 (28a) and ‖û_{j,i*+1}(x) − u_j^{n*}(x)‖ < ε_2 (28b) for all x ∈ Ω, provided N_v > N_v*, N_j > N_j*.
- **Proof (appendix, pp. 5447–5449), three parts**:
  - *Part I — exact policy iteration converges*: define the exact iteration V_i via the HJI residual (32) and u_{j,i+1} = −λ_j tanh((1/(2λ_j))R_j^{-1}B^T∇V_i) (33). Show lim_{i→∞}V_i = V*, lim_{i→∞}u_{j,i} = u_j^{n*} (34a–b). Tools: decompose H into the optimal part plus q-differences (36)–(37); under admissibility [42, Definition 1] (Abu-Khalaf & Lewis) capture occurs and V(x) = ∫_0^∞ H dτ + V(x(0)) (38), giving V(x) = V*(x(0)) + ∫_0^∞ [q(u_p) − q(u_p^{n*}) + q(u_e^{n*}) − q(u_e) + ∇V*^TB(u_p − u_p^{n*} + u_e^{n*} − u_e)]dτ (39); define ρ (40) and, since tanh^{-1} is increasing, conclude via the integral mean value theorem ρ(u_p, u_e^{n*}) ≥ 0, ρ(u_p^{n*}, u_e) ≤ 0; then invoke **[39, Th. 4]** (Kartal et al.) for (34). Decisive inequality: monotonicity of the tanh^{-1} integrand makes the "excess cost" ρ sign-definite.
  - *Part II — NN approximation vanishes*: with exact quantities written as networks plus errors (43a–b) and the "ideal" approximants Ṽ_i, ũ_{j,i+1} defined by (45)–(46), subtract the integrated identities (44)/(50) to get ê_{k,i} − ẽ_{k,i} = (ŵ_{v,i} − ω̃_{v,i})Φ_i^k (51), hence Σ_k e_{k,i}e_{k,i}^T = (ŵ_{v,i} − ω̃_{v,i})(Σ_k Φ_i^k Φ_i^{kT})(ŵ_{v,i} − ω̃_{v,i})^T (52). Weierstrass gives e_{j,i}, e_{v,i}, ẽ_i → 0 as N_j, N_v → ∞ (53)–(55); the decisive bound under Assumption 3: ‖ŵ_{v,i} − ω̃_{v,i}‖² ≤ (1/(ζK))Σ_k‖e_{k,i}‖² ≤ (1/ζ)max_k‖e_{k,i}‖² → 0 (uses (23) to invert the Gram-like sum). So |Û_i − Ṽ_i| → 0 (56). For actors, the tanh Lipschitz/mean value argument yields ‖û_{j,i+1} − ũ_{j,i+1}‖ ≤ (1/2)(‖B‖/λ_min(R_j))‖∇φ_v(x)ω̂_{v,i}^T − ∇Ṽ_i(x)‖ (57) → 0 (58). Conclude by induction over i (59)–(61).
  - *Part III — triangle inequalities*: |V_{i*} − V*| ≤ ε_1/2, ‖u_{j,i*+1} − u_j^{n*}‖ ≤ ε_2/2 (62) by (34); ‖Û_{i*} − V_{i*}‖ ≤ ε_1/2, ‖û_{j,i*+1} − u_{j,i*+1}‖ ≤ ε_2/2 (63) by Part II; summing gives (28).
- **Analytic**: yes, deterministic analysis, uniform on the compact Ω; no probability (noise enters only through data, not the analysis).
- **Proof gaps**: (a) Part I outsources the core convergence to [39, Th. 4] without checking that theorem's hypotheses for the present nonquadratic zero-sum cost; moreover (40) is written with mismatched arguments — ρ(u_p, u_e^{n*}) but the integrand contains u_p^{n*} — evidently a typo for ρ(u_p, u_p^{n*}), and the "integral mean value theorem" is applied to a vector integrand without the componentwise discussion (p. 5448). (b) Part II's (57)–(58) require ‖∇φ_v ω̂_{v,i}^T − ∇Ṽ_i‖ → 0; since Ṽ_i = ω̃_{v,i}φ_v + ẽ_i (49), this needs the *gradient* of the approximation error ∇ẽ_i to vanish, which the Weierstrass theorem (function-only approximation, [36], [37]) does not provide — an implicit assumption strengthening (53) to C¹-approximation (p. 5448–5449). (c) The PE bound step silently assumes the quadratic-form lower bound transfers from (23) to the error vector; standard in [31] but not re-derived.

### 5.5 Theorem 1 (main result: safety + capture)
**Theorem 1 (p. 5444)**: Consider pursuer (1) and evader (2) subject to (3). Under Assumptions 1–3, for any x_j(0) ∈ Int(C_j), under the strategies (5) with u_j^s given by (6) and u_j^n given by (26): (i) Int(C_j) is forward invariant, and (ii) the pursuer can successfully capture the evader if
- x^TQx > −J(u_p^{n*}(x), u_e^{n*}(x)) + (∇V*(x))^T B( u_p^s(x_p) − u_e^s(x_e) + ε_e(x) − ε_p(x) )  (29),
  where ε_j(x) = u_j^{n*}(x) − û_{j,i*+1}(x), j ∈ {p, e}, are the policy-approximation errors.
- **All assumptions**: A1, A2, A3; initial admissible u_{j0} (via Lemma 2); N_v, N_j large enough (via (28b)); b_j satisfying (8)–(9).
- **Proof approach**: (i) range of tanh is (−1, 1) ⇒ ‖u_j^{n*}(x)‖ < λ_j (boundedness of the ideal game strategies — the payoff of the nonquadratic cost); (ii) (28b) ⇒ ‖u_j^n(x)‖ = ‖û_{j,i*+1}(x)‖ ≤ ε_2 + λ_j on Ω; (iii) Lemma 1 ⇒ forward invariance of Int(C_j); (iv) "As in [39]", V* is a positive definite smooth solution of (17); use V* as Lyapunov function along (4) with u_j = û_{j,i*+1} + u_j^s:
  V̇*(x) = (∇V*)^T(Ax + Bu_p^{n*} − Bu_e^{n*}) + (∇V*)^T B(u_p^s − u_e^s + ε_e − ε_p) = −x^TQx − J(u_p^{n*}, u_e^{n*}) + (∇V*)^T B(u_p^s − u_e^s + ε_e − ε_p);
  the decisive equation: the nominal game already gives V̇* = −x^TQx − J < 0 by (17) and positive definiteness, and (29) states exactly that the CBF-induced perturbation term does not outweigh this descent; (v) then V̇* < 0, and by **[40, Th. 4.1]** (Khalil, Nonlinear Systems), (4) is asymptotically stable ⇒ lim x = 0 ⇒ capture.
- **Local/global, analytic/numerical**: analytic sufficient condition, pointwise in x; global on the invariant safe set provided (29) holds along trajectories (the paper does not phrase the "for all x along the trajectory" quantifier explicitly — the condition involves the unknown ∇V* and u_j^{n*}, so it is an a posteriori/existential certificate rather than an a priori testable inequality).
- **Physical meaning**: the capture guarantee survives the safety layer if, whenever the safeguarding controls fire (pursuer's u_p^s pushes away from obstacles, evader's u_e^s likewise, the two entering with opposite signs through B), the induced Lyapunov-drift disturbance remains smaller than the nominal game's descent rate x^TQx + J. The failure scenario of Fig. 10 (a "cage" of obstacles) shows that when safety detours dominate, the evader escapes — the condition is not vacuous.
- **Extensibility**: Remark 5 (p. 5444–5445): the framework extends to heterogeneous dynamics and multiple safety constraints by replacing b_j, the cost (13), networks (19) and data (22); the authors also advertise applicability beyond games to safety-constrained optimal control.
- **Proof gaps / caveats**: (a) Lemma 1 requires u_j^n bounded on all of R^n, but (28b) delivers boundedness only on Ω; the implicit bridge — trajectories remain in a bounded set by A2, so Ω can be taken large enough — is never spelled out (p. 5444). (b) The claim "V*(x) is a positive definite smooth solution of (17)" is attributed to [39]; for a nonquadratic cost with tanh integrals this inherits Part I's reliance on [39]. (c) (29) contains unknown objects (∇V*, u_j^{n*}, and even ε_j which depends on the unknown ideal solution), so it cannot be checked before learning; the paper verifies it only by falsification (Fig. 10 scenario "condition (29) does not hold", p. 5446, without showing the computation).

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
**Author-stated**:
- The composed design (5) "is no longer an optimal one, since for the satisfaction of the safety (3), the safeguarding control (6) is additionally designed" (Remark 4, p. 5444).
- Future work: "multiagent cluster games under multiple safety constraints" (Section V, p. 5447).

**Critical observations** (with page/eq. anchors):
1. **"Model-free" is partial**: A is unknown, but B appears explicitly in the safeguarding law (6), the optimal strategy (16), the data regressors (22) and the actor update (25); the abstract's "our strategies do not require the knowledge of system dynamics" (p. 5440) overstates — the input matrix and the safe-set functions h_j must be known, and an admissible initial controller u_{j0} is required (Algorithm 1, step 5).
2. **Game-theoretic scope**: with identical (A, B) and error dynamics (4), the "game" is a saddle-point optimal-control problem on x; no Isaacs barrier, no finite-time capture, no Apollonius-type geometry; capture is only asymptotic (Theorem 1; Fig. 2). The evader is simultaneously made safe by its own CBF layer, which is reasonable, but the two CBFs are decoupled — pursuer–evader collision (capture itself) is not a safety object.
3. **The capture condition (29) is a posteriori**: it contains the unknown ∇V*, the unknown optimal strategies and the resulting approximation errors ε_j; it cannot be verified before or without solving the problem, and the paper never demonstrates an actual check of (29) — the "not captured" example merely asserts it fails (p. 5446). No constructive procedure or sufficient condition in terms of c_j, λ_j, Q, R_j alone is offered.
4. **Rigor gaps in Lemma 1**: uniform constant b from pointwise (9) unjustified (p. 5443); Assumption 1 (controllability) is stated but unused.
5. **Rigor gaps in Lemma 2**: convergence of Part I outsourced to [39, Th. 4] without hypothesis checking; mismatched arguments in (40) (p. 5448); (57)–(58) implicitly need C¹ (gradient) consistency of the Weierstrass approximation, which function-only approximation does not give (p. 5448–5449); φ_j in (25) never defined as a data matrix.
6. **Boundedness quantifier mismatch**: Lemma 1 assumes u_j^n bounded on all of R^n; Theorem 1's proof only establishes this on Ω (p. 5444), bridged informally by A2.
7. **Empirical breadth**: single scenario family, single run per configuration, no statistics, no hardware experiment, no computational-time reporting, no tuning sensitivity beyond the c_j study; the safety layer's behavior under state/input disturbances, measurement noise, or delay is untouched; the PE Assumption 3 is enforced by designed sinusoids rather than tested.

## 8. Citation Value
This paper is the reference point for the *architecture* "CBF safeguarding layer + off-policy ADP game solver" for two-player PE with state constraints under unknown drift: cite it (i) as evidence that safety layers and learning-based game strategies can be composed so that invariance holds throughout learning (Lemma 1 needs only boundedness of the game strategy, which tanh-based costs supply), (ii) for the nonquadratic tanh^{-1} game cost (13) that yields inherently bounded saddle-point strategies — a useful device whenever input saturation or bounded-strategy lemmas are needed, and (iii) for the perturbation-Lyapunov capture condition (29) quantifying how much safety-layer interference a pursuit guarantee can absorb. Typical citation sentence: "Jia et al. combine a CBF-based safeguarding controller with off-policy reinforcement learning to solve a safety-aware pursuit-evasion game with unknown dynamics, proving forward invariance of the players' safe sets and giving a sufficient Lyapunov condition for capture [P046]." It also serves as the contrast point for works pursuing genuine differential-game safety (HJI/barrier-based PE with constraints) or multi-agent, input-perturbed, and experimentally validated safe PE, all of which the authors leave open.
