## 2. Research Background
Multiplayer PE games in continuous state spaces are dominated by two solution philosophies: the HJI equation, which is intrinsically hard to solve and suffers the curse of dimensionality as the number of players grows, and geometric methods (Apollonius circles, Voronoi diagrams, dominance regions), which deliver analytic strategies for simple-motion players (p. 1). For N-on-M games the community has largely converged on a two-stage scheme: first allocate evaders to pursuers (or coalitions), then solve the resulting 1-on-1 (or N-on-1) games geometrically ([25]–[33], p. 2). The authors argue that three practical aspects are under-represented both separately and jointly: (i) true heterogeneity — here pursuers are velocity-controlled single integrators (agile robots such as quadrotors) while evaders are damped double integrators (vehicles that cannot change velocity abruptly), with per-player maximum speed, capture radius and sensing range; (ii) distributed implementation — most existing allocation is centralized (Hungarian method, linear-sum programming, MILP) regardless of the robots' limited sensing/communication; and (iii) the makespan objective — the pursuer team wants all captures done in minimum time $T_m=\max_i T_i$, which a linear-sum assignment cost does not capture. The paper builds a complete pipeline addressing all three at once.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
**Problem (Problem 1, p. 4, eq. (3))**: $N$ pursuers $P_i$ with dynamics $\dot x_{pi}=v_{xpi},\ \dot y_{pi}=v_{ypi}$, $\|v_{pi}\|\le v_{mpi}$ (1) and $M$ evaders $E_j$ with $\dot v_{xej}=-\mu v_{xej}+u_{ej}\cos\theta_{ej}$, $\dot v_{yej}=-\mu v_{yej}+u_{ej}\sin\theta_{ej}$, $u_{ej}\in[0,u_{mej}]$, $\theta_{ej}\in[0,2\pi)$ (2); capture when $\|p_i-e_j\|\le\epsilon_i$; pursuer $P_i$ observes players and receives information only within sensing range $r_i$ (directed sensing graph $\mathcal F_d=(\mathcal Y_{\mathcal P},\mathcal S_d)$, $\mathcal S_d=\{(P_i,P_k)\,|\,\|p_i-p_k\|\le r_i\}$; bipartite observation graph $\mathcal G_d=(\mathcal Y,\mathcal Z_d)$, $\mathcal Z_d=\{(P_i,E_j)\,|\,\|p_i-e_j\|\le r_i\}$, p. 4). **Assumption 1**: $v_{mpi}\le u_{mej}/\mu$ for all $i,j$ (pursuer not faster than the evader's maximum steady-state speed — the nontrivial regime; a faster pursuer would trivially always capture). **Assumption 2**: distinct initial positions, no initial capture. Each pursuer chases at most one evader and vice versa; allocation is fixed at game start (pursuers "insist" on their target), evaders do not cooperate and respond after pursuers declare allocation — "like a Stackelberg game" (p. 3). Design a distributed allocation plus optimal pursuit such that (1) the team captures the most evaders and (2) the makespan $T_m=\max_{i=1,\dots,N}T_i$ ($T_i=0$ if no target) is least.

**Gap — [authors' claim]** (pp. 2–3): (i) existing two-stage multiplayer PE/reach-avoid works [25]–[33] mostly assume homogeneous simple-motion players; heterogeneity in [28] means only different speeds and capture radii; [27] (Dubins pursuers vs. simple-motion evaders) is truly heterogeneous but its strategies "are induced by the game of two simple motion players"; (ii) allocation in [26]–[30], [33], [34] is centralized (maximum-cardinality matching / Hungarian / linear-sum programming); distributed attempts exist ([38] decentralized assignment with communication; [39] CBBA-based perimeter defense "but the intruders are assumed to have no intelligence thus there is no game") but are "not fully explored for multiplayer PE games"; (iii) makespan was optimized only via centralized mixed-integer linear programming ([25], [40]), while the common linear-sum cost "cannot adequately characterize" the pursuers' goal. **[Verified from cited text]**: the external claims cannot be checked against the cited papers from this PDF alone; internally, the claims are consistent with the paper's own reference list and the contribution statement, and the three-part gap is exactly what Theorems 1–5 deliver (isochron capture theory for the heterogeneous pair → analytic winning region → sensing threshold → distributed min-makespan allocation), so the gap statement is traceable. One nuance worth flagging: Assumption 1 itself rules out the "fast pursuer" case, so the theory covers only pursuers slower than the evaders' steady-state speed — heterogeneity in speed is one-sided by design.

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Problem/modeling**: First formulation (per authors) of a multiplayer PE game integrating player heterogeneity (different dynamics, speeds, accelerations, capture radii, sensing ranges), fully distributed decision-making with limited sensing/communication, and the minimum-makespan team objective; plus a four-subproblem decomposition (P1 capture condition/strategies, P2 winning region, P3 sensing-range threshold, P4 distributed allocation) forming an overall framework (Section III-A, pp. 4–5).
2. **Theory (P1, Theorem 1)**: A novel isochron-based method for the 1-on-1 game between a simple-motion pursuer and a damped double-integrator evader: capture condition (15), optimal pursuit strategy (17), optimal evasion strategy (18), and value $V_{ij}=\tau^\ast$, validated by substitution into the HJI equation (21)–(27) — avoiding solving the HJI PDE (pp. 5–7).
3. **Theory (P2/P3, Theorems 2–4)**: Analytic classification of the pursuer's winning-region boundary $\mathcal B_{ij}$ (Table I, five cases; envelope + extremal circles, p. 8); a feasible evasion strategy when $p_i\notin\mathcal W_{ij}$ with guaranteed survival until $\tau(\bar\epsilon_i)$ (Theorem 3, Lemma 3, p. 9); and a closed-form lower bound $\bar\gamma=\gamma(v_{Mp},\epsilon_M,u_M,v_{Me})$ (34) on the sensing range guaranteeing conflict-free information sufficiency for allocation ($\mathcal T_i^c\subseteq\mathcal T_i^d$, $\mathcal N_i^{(cd)}=\mathcal N_i^{cd}$, p. 10).
4. **Algorithm + guarantee (P4, Algorithm 1, Theorem 5)**: A fully distributed min-makespan allocation (distributed Hungarian method [37] + binary search over the makespan), with finite-time convergence to the optimum of "maximum cardinality matching (40) + minimum makespan among maximum matchings (41)" and hence a complete solution of Problem 1 (pp. 11–12); worst-case communication complexity $O(N^3\log\frac{\bar T-\underline T}{\Delta})$ rounds (Remark 5).
5. **Extension + experiments**: An event-triggered dynamic re-allocation variant (Section IV-D) and Matlab simulations: 1-on-1 strategy validation, 100-scenario allocation benchmark vs. `matchpairs` / distributed Hungarian / exhaustive ground truth, and an 8-vs-10 full-game simulation (Section IV).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results

### 5.0 Model, information structure, cost (pp. 3–4)
- **Pursuers (1)**: single integrators, $\dot p_i=v_{pi}$, $\|v_{pi}\|\le v_{mpi}$; **evaders (2)**: damped double integrators with damping factor $\mu>0$ and acceleration input $u_{ej}\in[0,u_{mej}]$ at heading $\theta_{ej}$; the maximum steady-state speed is $u_{mej}/\mu$ (reached by accelerating along a constant direction, p. 3). Heterogeneity: per-player $v_{mpi}$, $\epsilon_i\ge 0$ (capture radius; both point and nonzero capture), $r_i>0$ (sensing), $u_{mej}$.
- **Capture topologies (p. 5)**: $\mathcal G_c=(\mathcal Y,\mathcal Z_c)$, $\mathcal Z_c=\{(P_i,E_j)\,|\,P_i\text{ can capture }E_j\}$; possible targets $\mathcal T_i^c$; feasible targets $\mathcal T_i^{cd}=\mathcal T_i^d\cap\mathcal T_i^c$; conflict graph $\mathcal F_c=(\mathcal Y_{\mathcal P},\mathcal S_c)$, $\mathcal S_c=\{(P_i,P_k)\,|\,\mathcal T_i^c\cap\mathcal T_k^c\ne\emptyset\}$; sensing-restricted conflict subgraph $\mathcal F^{(cd)}$ with $\mathcal S^{(cd)}=\{(P_i,P_k)\,|\,\mathcal T_i^{cd}\cap\mathcal T_k^{cd}\ne\emptyset\}$; Remark 1 distinguishes $\mathcal N_i^{cd}$ (possible-target conflicts within sensing) from $\mathcal N_i^{(cd)}$ (feasible-target conflicts).
- **Payoff (4)–(5)**: $J_{ij}=\int_0^{t_f}1\,dt=t_f$; zero-sum, $\min_{u_{pi}}\max_{u_{ej}}J_{ij}$; Hamiltonian (6) $H_{ij}=\lambda_1v_{xpi}+\lambda_2v_{ypi}+\nu_1v_{xej}+\nu_2v_{yej}+\nu_3(-\mu v_{xej}+u_{ej}\cos\theta_{ej})+\nu_4(-\mu v_{yej}+u_{ej}\sin\theta_{ej})$.
- **Team objective (3) and allocation programs (40)–(41) (pp. 4, 11)**: $T_m=\max_iT_i$; with $z_{ij}=1$ iff $(P_i,E_j)\in\mathcal Z_c$ and cost $T_{ij}$ = capture time from Theorem 1 ($T_{\max}$ if capture impossible), solve $\max_A\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}$ s.t. $a_{ij}\in\{0,1\}$, $a_{ij}\le z_{ij}$, $\sum_i a_{ij}\le1$, $\sum_j a_{ij}\le1$ (40); then $\min_{\mathcal M\in\mathfrak M}\max_{i,j}a_{ij}T_{ij}$ over the maximum-cardinality matching set $\mathfrak M$ (41). The paper stresses (41) "makes more sense than $\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}T_{ij}$ since the pursuers chase their targets simultaneously" (p. 11) — i.e., a bottleneck assignment, not a linear-sum assignment.

### 5.1 Lemma 1 (p. 5) — optimality of extremal constant-direction strategies
**Statement**: In the game (5) between $P_i$ (1) and $E_j$ (2), the optimal strategy of $P_i$ is to move at maximum speed along a constant direction ($\|v_{pi}^\ast\|=v_{mpi}$, $v_{xpi}^\ast,v_{ypi}^\ast$ constant); the optimal strategy of $E_j$ is to accelerate along a constant direction at maximum acceleration ($u_{ej}^\ast=u_{mej}$, $\theta_{ej}^\ast$ constant). Admissible strategy sets (7)–(8): $\mathcal U_{pi}=\{v_{pi}|\,\|v_{pi}\|=v_{mpi}\}$, $\mathcal U_{ej}=\{u_{ej}|\,u_{ej}=u_{mej},\ \theta_{ej}\text{ constant}\}$.
**Proof approach**: "The result is followed by the fact that the optimal strategies should minimize or maximize the Hamiltonian (6). The detailed proof is omitted here." **Proof gap**: the Hamiltonian is linear in both controls, so extrema lie on the control-set boundary, but the constancy of the directions (no switching, no state-dependence) is asserted, not derived from a co-state/switching-function analysis; no PMP system is written down. Physical meaning: the damped evader's best delay tactic is a maximal, straight burn; the pursuer's best response is a straight maximal-speed run. Extensibility: this reduction is what makes the isochron method work — the entire strategy space collapses to a one-parameter family indexed by time $\tau$ and heading.

### 5.2 Definition 1 / Lemma 2 (p. 5, eqs. (9)–(13)) — isochrones
**Definition 1 (Isochrones)**: the set of positions reachable by $P_i$ (resp. $E_j$) under strategies in $\mathcal U_{pi}$ (resp. $\mathcal U_{ej}$) at a common time $\tau\in(0,+\infty)$; the family over $\tau$ is the isochrone cluster.
**Lemma 2**: $P_i$'s isochron of time $\tau$ is $\mathcal I_{pi}(\tau)=\{(x,y)^\top|\,\Gamma_{pi}(x,y,\tau)=0\}$, $\tau\ge0$ (9), with $\Gamma_{pi}(x,y,\tau)=(x-x_{pi})^2+(y-y_{pi})^2-v_{mpi}^2\tau^2$ (10) — a circle centered at $c_{pi}=p_i$ with radius $R_{pi}=v_{mpi}\tau$. $E_j$'s isochron (11)–(12):
$$\Gamma_{ej}(x,y,\tau)=\Big(x-x_{ej}+\tfrac{v_{xej}}{\mu}(e^{-\mu\tau}-1)\Big)^2+\Big(y-y_{ej}+\tfrac{v_{yej}}{\mu}(e^{-\mu\tau}-1)\Big)^2-\tfrac{u_{mej}^2}{\mu^2}\Big(\tau+\tfrac{e^{-\mu\tau}-1}{\mu}\Big)^2,$$
a circle (13) centered at $c_{ej}=\big(x_{ej}-\tfrac{v_{xej}}{\mu}(e^{-\mu\tau}-1),\ y_{ej}-\tfrac{v_{yej}}{\mu}(e^{-\mu\tau}-1)\big)^\top$ with radius $R_{ej}=\tfrac{u_{mej}}{\mu}\big(\tau+\tfrac{e^{-\mu\tau}-1}{\mu}\big)$.
**Proof approach**: integrate the closed-loop state equations under $\|v_{pi}\|=v_{mpi}$, $u_{ej}=u_{mej}$, constant $\theta_{ej}$ from (1)(2), then eliminate the state variables ("detailed proof is omitted here"). The evader's isochron center moves as the unforced damped trajectory $e_j+\frac{v_{ej}}{\mu}(1-e^{-\mu\tau})$, and the radius $(u_{mej}/\mu)(\tau+(e^{-\mu\tau}-1)/\mu)$ is the damped-rocket distance — strictly increasing and asymptotically linear with slope $u_{mej}/\mu$. Analytic, exact, global. **Extensibility**: the same integration trick applies to any dynamics whose reachable set at fixed $\tau$ over the reduced strategy set is a circle (or union of circles); this is the technical heart enabling everything downstream.

### 5.3 Definition 2 / eq. (14) (p. 6) — extended isochrones and the capture locus
**Definition 2**: extended isochron $\mathcal I_{pi}^{\epsilon}(\tau)=\{(x,y)^\top|\,\Gamma_{pi}^{\epsilon}(x,y,\tau)=0\}$ with $\Gamma_{pi}^{\epsilon}=(x-x_{pi})^2+(y-y_{pi})^2-(v_{mpi}\tau+\epsilon_i)^2$ — a circle of radius $R_{pi}+\epsilon_i$: capture with radius $\epsilon_i$ means the evader's isochron must intersect the $\epsilon_i$-inflated pursuer circle at a common time. Eliminating $\tau$ (with $\tau=(\sqrt{(x-x_{pi})^2+(y-y_{pi})^2}-\epsilon_i)/v_{mpi}$) yields the implicit capture locus $\Phi_{ij}(x,y)=0$ (14) used in Fig. 2/6 for the intersection geometry.

### 5.4 Theorem 1 (p. 6, eqs. (15)–(18)) — capture condition, optimal strategies, value
**Statement**: Given the pursuit-evasion problem (5) between $P_i$ (1) and $E_j$ (2), the capture is guaranteed if there exists a finite $\tau$ satisfying
$$Q_{ij}(\tau)=0,\qquad R_{pi}(\tau)+\epsilon_i>R_{ej}(\tau),\tag{15}$$
where $Q_{ij}(\tau)=\|c_{pi}(\tau)-c_{ej}(\tau)\|^2-(R_{pi}(\tau)+\epsilon_i-R_{ej}(\tau))^2$ (16) and $c_{pi},c_{ej},R_{pi},R_{ej}$ are from (13). Let $\tau^\ast$ be the minimum such $\tau$. Then the optimal pursuit strategy is $u_{pi}^\ast=(v_{xpi}^\ast,v_{ypi}^\ast)^\top$ with
$$v_{xpi}^\ast=\tfrac{x_{cej}(\tau^\ast)-x_{cpi}(\tau^\ast)}{R_{pi}(\tau^\ast)+\epsilon_i-R_{ej}(\tau^\ast)}\,v_{mpi},\quad v_{ypi}^\ast=\tfrac{y_{cej}(\tau^\ast)-y_{cpi}(\tau^\ast)}{R_{pi}(\tau^\ast)+\epsilon_i-R_{ej}(\tau^\ast)}\,v_{mpi}\tag{17}$$
(the pursuer runs at full speed along the line $c_{pi}(\tau^\ast)\to c_{ej}(\tau^\ast)$); the optimal evasion strategy is $u_{ej}^\ast=u_{mej}$ and
$$\cos\theta_{ej}^\ast=\tfrac{x_{cej}(\tau^\ast)-x_{cpi}(\tau^\ast)}{R_{pi}(\tau^\ast)+\epsilon_i-R_{ej}(\tau^\ast)},\quad \sin\theta_{ej}^\ast=\tfrac{y_{cej}(\tau^\ast)-y_{cpi}(\tau^\ast)}{R_{pi}(\tau^\ast)+\epsilon_i-R_{ej}(\tau^\ast)}\tag{18}$$
(the evader burns at full acceleration toward the tangency point, i.e., straight at the pursuer's center); the value is $V_{ij}=\tau^\ast$.
**Assumptions**: dynamics (1)(2), strategy sets (7)(8), $Q_{ij}(0)=\|p_i-e_j\|^2-\epsilon_i^2>0$ (no initial capture, Assumption 2), $\tau^\ast$ finite; $W\ne0$ for the gradient step (see below).
**Proof approach** (pp. 6–7): (1) Interpret (16) as the circle-containment predicate: $Q_{ij}(\tau^\ast)=0$ = internal tangency of $\mathcal I_{pi}^{\epsilon}(\tau^\ast)$ and $\mathcal I_{ej}(\tau^\ast)$; $R_{pi}+\epsilon_i>R_{ej}$ = the extended pursuer circle contains the evader circle; minimality of $\tau^\ast$ plus $Q_{ij}(0)>0$ means tangency happens "for the first time". (2) Geometric argument: closed parts of the intersection curve $\Phi_{ij}=0$ ending at tangency points contain all possible capture points; among them the tangency point is where the evader achieves maximum capture time, hence it is the game's capture point $f_{ej}$. (3) Compute $f_{ej}$ by collinearity of $c_{pi}(\tau^\ast),c_{ej}(\tau^\ast),f_{ej}$ with $\overline{c_{pi}f_{ej}}=R_{pi}(\tau^\ast)+\epsilon_i$, $\overline{c_{ej}f_{ej}}=R_{ej}(\tau^\ast)$ (19); similarly the pursuer's stopping point $f_{pi}$ on $\mathcal I_{pi}(\tau^\ast)$ at distance $\epsilon_i$ from $f_{ej}$ (20); (17) is "move straight to $f_{pi}$" and (18) is obtained by substituting $(x_{ej}(\tau^\ast),y_{ej}(\tau^\ast))^\top=f_{ej}$ into the closed-loop evader state equations $x_{ej}(\tau)=x_{ej}+\frac{u_{mej}}{\mu}\cos\theta_{ej}\tau+\frac{e^{-\mu\tau}-1}{\mu}(\frac{u_{mej}}{\mu}\cos\theta_{ej}-v_{xej})$ (p. 7). (4) **HJI validation**: propose $V_{ij}=\tau^\ast$ and verify the HJI equation $-\partial V_{ij}/\partial t=(\partial V_{ij}/\partial p_i)^\top\frac{dp_i}{dt}+(\partial V_{ij}/\partial\xi_j)^\top\frac{d\xi_j}{dt}+1$ (21); differentiate $Q_{ij}(V_{ij})=0$ w.r.t. $x_{pi}$ to get (22) with the decisive quantities $d_{cx}=x_{cpi}-x_{cej}$ (23), $d_{cy}=y_{cpi}-y_{cej}$ (24), $d_R=R_{pi}+\epsilon_i-R_{ej}$ (25), obtaining the gradients (26): $\partial V_{ij}/\partial x_{pi}=d_{cx}/W$, $\partial V_{ij}/\partial y_{pi}=d_{cy}/W$, $\partial V_{ij}/\partial x_{ej}=-d_{cx}/W$, $\partial V_{ij}/\partial y_{ej}=-d_{cy}/W$, $\partial V_{ij}/\partial v_{xej}=\frac{e^{-\mu V_{ij}}-1}{\mu}\frac{d_{cx}}{W}$, $\partial V_{ij}/\partial v_{yej}=\frac{e^{-\mu V_{ij}}-1}{\mu}\frac{d_{cy}}{W}$, where
$$W=d_R\big(v_{mpi}-\tfrac{u_{mej}}{\mu}(1-e^{-\mu V_{ij}})\big)+(d_{cx}v_{xej}+d_{cy}v_{yej})e^{-\mu V_{ij}}=-Q_{ij}'(\tau^\ast)>0,$$
positivity following from the sign change $Q_{ij}(\tau^{\ast-})>0$, $Q_{ij}(\tau^{\ast+})<0$. (5) Substituting (26) and the strategies (17)(18) into the right side of (21) gives $=0$ identically (27) — the decisive verification equation; $\partial V_{ij}/\partial t=0$, so the HJI equation is satisfied.
**Decisive intermediate**: $W=-Q_{ij}'(\tau^\ast)>0$ — the single sign-change of $Q_{ij}$ at $\tau^\ast$ is what makes the value $C^1$ off the barrier.
**Remarks 2–3 (p. 7)**: (R2) if $R_{pi}+\epsilon_i=R_{ej}$ the two circles coincide and any point on them is a capture point — $P_i$ selects its strategy following $E_j$; (R3) if $Q_{ij}'(\tau^\ast)=0$ (tangency "around" $\tau^\ast$), $P_i$ is on the barrier of its winning region, the value is discontinuous there ([5], Isaacs), yet capture "is still said to be guaranteed" with (17) — a definition-level patch, not a proof.
**Local/global; analytic/numerical**: global, closed-form; reduces the 1-on-1 game to a one-dimensional root search $Q_{ij}(\tau)=0$ over $\tau$ (monotone geometric objects), with the second condition (15b) selecting containment. **Physical meaning**: capture is guaranteed iff the pursuer's $\epsilon_i$-inflated reach circle swallows the evader's entire reach circle at some common time; before that time the evader always has escape directions; at tangency the only optimal evader move is straight at the tangency point. **Extensibility**: any evader dynamics with computable isochrone radii/centers plugs into the same tangency test. **Proof gaps**: (a) the geometric claim that the tangency point is the game-theoretic capture point is argued by picture-level reasoning ("the intersection's closed parts that end at the internally tangent points contain all the points where the pursuer can capture the evader", p. 6–7) rather than a lemma; (b) the HJI verification shows $V_{ij}=\tau^\ast$ is *a* solution of (21) under the two optimal strategies but no formal verification/Comparison theorem (viscosity or classical, uniqueness) is invoked — the optimality claim rests on the geometric construction plus the HJI check; (c) Lemma 1's proof is omitted, so the restriction to (7)(8) is itself unproven.

### 5.5 Theorem 2 (p. 8, Table I) — analytic winning-region boundary
**Statement**: For $E_j$ with canonical initial state $\xi_j=(0,0,v_{xej},0)^\top$, $v_{xej}\ge0$ (any case reduces to this by rigid transformation), the boundary $\mathcal B_{ij}$ of $\mathcal W_{ij}$ (Definition 3, p. 7: all starting positions of $P_i$ guaranteeing capture) is composed as in Table I:

| maximum speed | initial speed | boundary $\mathcal B_{ij}$ | example |
|---|---|---|---|
| $v_{mpi}=u_{mej}/\mu$ | $v_{xej}\le v_{mpi}$ | $\mathcal C_f$ | Fig. 5(a) |
| $v_{mpi}=u_{mej}/\mu$ | $v_{xej}>v_{mpi}$ | $\mathcal C_0\cup\mathcal E_1\cup\mathcal C_{f1}$ | Fig. 5(b) |
| $v_{mpi}<u_{mej}/\mu$ | $v_{xej}=0$ | $\mathcal C_m$ | Fig. 5(c) |
| $v_{mpi}<u_{mej}/\mu$ | $0<v_{xej}\le v_{mpi}$ | $\mathcal E_2$ | Fig. 5(d) |
| $v_{mpi}<u_{mej}/\mu$ | $v_{xej}>v_{mpi}$ | $\mathcal C_0\cup\mathcal E_3$ | Fig. 5(e) |

with (p. 8): $\mathcal C_0=\{(x,y)^\top|Q(x,y,0)=0,\ x\le-\frac{u_{mej}\epsilon_i}{v_{xej}}\}$ (minimum circle, $\tau=0$); $\mathcal C_m=\{(x,y)^\top|Q(x,y,\tau_m)=0\}$ (largest circle at the radius-maximizing time); $\mathcal C_f=\{(x,y)^\top|\bar Q(x,y)=0\}$ and $\mathcal C_{f1}=\{(x,y)^\top|\bar Q(x,y)=0,\ x\ge-\frac{u_{mej}}{\mu^2}(\epsilon_i+\frac{u_{mej}}{\mu^2})\}$ (limit circles as $\tau\to\infty$); envelopes $\mathcal E_1=\{x=f(\tau),y=g(\tau),\tau\ge0\}$, $\mathcal E_2$ with $\tau\in[\tau_1,\min(\tau_M,\tau_2)]$, $\mathcal E_3$ with $\tau\in[0,\min(\tau_M,\tau_2)]$, where
$$Q(x,y,\tau)=\big(x+\tfrac{v_{xej}}{\mu}(e^{-\mu\tau}-1)\big)^2+y^2-R_\tau^2,\quad \bar Q(x,y)=\lim_{\tau\to+\infty}Q=(x-\tfrac{v_{xej}}{\mu})^2+y^2-(\epsilon_i+\tfrac{u_{mej}}{\mu^2})^2,$$
$$f(\tau)=-\tfrac{v_{xej}}{\mu}(e^{-\mu\tau}-1)+R_{\tau x},\quad g(\tau)=\pm R_\tau\sqrt{K(\tau)},\quad R_\tau=R_{pi}(\tau)+\epsilon_i-R_{ej}(\tau),$$
$$R_{\tau x}=-\tfrac{e^{\mu\tau}}{v_{xej}}\big(v_{mpi}-\tfrac{u_{mej}}{\mu}(1-e^{-\mu\tau})\big)R_\tau,\quad K(\tau)=1-\tfrac{e^{2\mu\tau}}{v_{xej}^2}\big(v_{mpi}-\tfrac{u_{mej}}{\mu}(1-e^{-\mu\tau})\big)^2\ge0,$$
$$\tau_m=\arg\max R_\tau=-\tfrac1\mu\log\big(1-\tfrac{\mu v_{mpi}}{u_{mej}}\big),\quad \tau_1=\tfrac1\mu\log\tfrac{u_{mej}/\mu-v_{xej}}{u_{mej}/\mu-v_{mpi}},\quad \tau_2=\tfrac1\mu\log\tfrac{u_{mej}/\mu+v_{xej}}{u_{mej}/\mu-v_{mpi}},$$
and $\tau_M$ is the root of $R_\tau=0$ (exists only if $v_{mpi}<u_{mej}/\mu$, since $R_\tau$ then eventually decreases).
**Proof approach** (pp. 8): (1) $p_i\in\mathcal W_{ij}$ iff $\exists\,\tau$ with $Q_{ij}(\tau)=0$, $\tau<\tau_M$ (so $R_\tau>0$); fixing $\tau$, the set of admissible $p_i$ is the circle $Q(x,y,\tau)=0$ with center $(-\frac{v_{xej}}{\mu}(e^{-\mu\tau}-1),0)$ and radius $R_\tau$ — hence $\mathcal W_{ij}$ is a union of circles and its boundary consists of some circles plus the envelope. (2) The envelope solves $\{Q=0,\ \partial Q/\partial\tau=0\}$; since the elimination is hard, the parametric form (28) $x(\tau)=f(\tau)$, $y(\tau)=g(\tau)$ is derived instead. (3) Case analysis: (I) $v_{xej}=0$ — constant centers, biggest circle wins ($\mathcal C_f$ if $v_{mpi}=u_{mej}/\mu$ since $R_\tau$ grows forever; $\mathcal C_m$ if $v_{mpi}<u_{mej}/\mu$, using $\tau_m<\tau_M$). (II) $v_{xej}\ne0$ — envelope: (i) $v_{mpi}=u_{mej}/\mu$: if $v_{xej}\le v_{mpi}$ circles never overlap (center drift slower than radius growth) → $\mathcal C_f$; if $v_{xej}>v_{mpi}$, $K(\tau)\ge0$ for all $\tau>0$ and the boundary is minimum circle + envelope + limit circle, using the endpoint limits (29) $f(0)=-\frac{v_{mpi}\epsilon}{v_{xej}}$, $\lim_{\tau\to+\infty}f(\tau)=-\frac{u_{mej}}{\mu^2}(\epsilon_i+\frac{u_{mej}}{\mu^2})$ → $\mathcal B_{ij}=\mathcal C_0\cup\mathcal E_1\cup\mathcal C_{f1}$; (ii) $v_{mpi}<u_{mej}/\mu$: $K(\tau)\ge0$ restricts $\tau\in[\tau_1,\tau_2]$ and $R_\tau>0$ only on $(0,\tau_M)$; $v_{xej}\le v_{mpi}$ ⇒ $\tau_1\ge0$ and $\min(\tau_M,\tau_2)>\tau_m$ so no circle encircles all others → pure envelope $\mathcal E_2$; $v_{xej}>v_{mpi}$ ⇒ $\tau_1<0$, keep $\tau\ge0$ portion, $\mathcal C_0$ contributes → $\mathcal C_0\cup\mathcal E_3$.
**Local/global, analytic/numerical**: global analytic 2D geometry in the canonical frame; evaluating membership of a query point requires only a 1D search over $\tau$ (as in the heat maps of Fig. 4). **Physical meaning**: the winning region is an egg/teardrop-shaped set elongated along $\pm v_{ej}$; its shape and the type of its boundary are completely determined by two speed ratios. **Extensibility**: the envelope-of-circles technique applies to any pair with circle-form isochrones. **Proof gap (minor)**: the derivation of the parametric envelope (28) is not shown (only "we then acquire the parametric equation of the envelope"); the endpoint identity $f(0)=-\frac{v_{mpi}\epsilon}{v_{xej}}$ uses $\epsilon$ (not $\epsilon_i$) — a typo-level inconsistency.

### 5.6 Theorem 3 + Lemma 3 (p. 9, eqs. (30)–(31)) — feasible evasion outside the winning region
**Setting**: $P_i$ (actual capture radius $\epsilon_i^0$) at $p_i\notin\mathcal W_{ij}(\epsilon_i^0)$: capture not guaranteed, but the intersection $\mathcal I_{pi}^{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal I_{ej}$ is concave near $\mathcal W_{ij}$'s boundary, leaving the evader a narrow safe channel (Fig. 6). Idea: inflate the imagined capture radius to the critical value $\bar\epsilon_i$ at which $p_i$ lies on the boundary: $\bar\epsilon_i$ solves $f(\tau,\bar\epsilon_i)=x_{pi},\ g(\tau,\bar\epsilon_i)=y_{pi}$ (30) (for the $\mathcal E_2$-type boundary), giving capture time $\tau(\bar\epsilon_i)$; the evader then plays $u_{ej}^\ast(\bar\epsilon_i,\tau(\bar\epsilon_i))$ from (18).
**Theorem 3**: if $P_i$ with capture radius $\epsilon_i^0$ is initially at $p_i\notin\mathcal W_{ij}(\epsilon_i^0)$, then $E_j$ using strategy $u_{ej}^\ast(\bar\epsilon_i,\tau(\bar\epsilon_i))$ cannot be captured at least until $\tau(\bar\epsilon_i)$, where $\bar\epsilon_i$ is such that $p_i\in\mathcal B_{ij}(\bar\epsilon_i)$.
**Proof**: contradiction — if $P_i$ (radius $\epsilon_i^0<\bar\epsilon_i$) could capture at some $\tau_0<\tau(\bar\epsilon_i)$, then a hypothetical $P_i$ with radius $\bar\epsilon_i$ could also capture at $\tau_0$ using (17), contradicting that $\tau(\bar\epsilon_i)>\tau_0$ is the capture time under optimal strategies. One-paragraph, monotonicity-based, local in time.
**Lemma 3**: if $p_i\in\mathcal W_{ij}$, the capture time decreases when the capture radius increases. **Proof**: implicit differentiation of $Q(\tau^\ast)=0$ w.r.t. $\epsilon_i$ gives the decisive identity $\frac{\partial\tau^\ast}{\partial\epsilon_i}=-\frac{R_\tau}{W}<0$ (p. 9) (signs: $R_\tau>0$ inside $\mathcal W_{ij}$, $W>0$ per Theorem 1). Consequently the chosen $\bar\epsilon_i$ is worst-case optimal: $\bar\epsilon_i=\arg\max_{\epsilon_i}\tau(\epsilon_i)$ s.t. $p_i\in\mathcal W_{ij}(\epsilon_i)$ (31) — the evader maximizes guaranteed survival by assuming the largest capture radius that still keeps $p_i$ winning.
**Honest limitation (authors' own, p. 9)**: "we want to emphasize that this strategy is able to avoid the capture forever and we will verify it in simulations, though we are unable to provide the complete proof yet" — the theorem only guarantees survival on $[0,\tau(\bar\epsilon_i))$.

### 5.7 Theorem 4 (p. 10, eqs. (32)–(39)) — sensing-range threshold
**Statement**: Under Assumption 1, suppose $P_i$ knows four global parameters $v_{Mp}\ge\max_i v_{mpi}$, $\epsilon_M\ge\max_i\epsilon_i$ (32), $u_M\le\min_j u_{mej}$, $v_{Me}\ge\max_j\|v_{ej}\|$ (33). If $r_i\ge\bar\gamma=\gamma(v_{Mp},\epsilon_M,u_M,v_{Me})$ (34), where
$$\bar\gamma=\begin{cases}2(\epsilon_M+\frac{u_M}{\mu^2}) & v_{Mp}=\frac{u_M}{\mu},\ v_{Me}\le v_{Mp}\\ \frac{v_{Me}}{\mu}+2\epsilon_M+\frac{u_M}{\mu^2} & v_{Mp}=\frac{u_M}{\mu},\ v_{Me}>v_{Mp}\\ 2\big((v_{Mp}-\frac{u_M}{\mu})\bar\tau_m+\epsilon_M+\frac{v_{Mp}}{\mu}\big) & v_{Mp}<\frac{u_M}{\mu},\ v_{Me}=0\\ \bar f(\min(\bar\tau_M,\bar\tau_2))-\bar f(\bar\tau_1) & v_{Mp}<\frac{u_M}{\mu},\ v_{Me}\in(0,v_{Mp}]\\ \bar f(\min(\bar\tau_M,\bar\tau_2))+\epsilon_M & v_{Mp}<\frac{u_M}{\mu},\ v_{Me}>v_{Mp}\end{cases}$$
with $\bar\tau_m=-\frac1\mu\log(1-\frac{\mu v_{Mp}}{u_M})$ (35), $\bar\tau_1=\frac1\mu\log\frac{u_M/\mu-v_{Me}}{u_M/\mu-v_{Mp}}$, $\bar\tau_2=\frac1\mu\log\frac{u_M/\mu+v_{Me}}{u_M/\mu-v_{Mp}}$ (36), $\bar f(\tau)=-\frac{v_{Me}}{\mu}(e^{-\mu\tau}-1)+\bar R_{\tau x}$ (37), $\bar R_{\tau x}=-\frac{e^{\mu\tau}}{v_{Me}}(v_{Mp}-\frac{u_M}{\mu}(1-e^{-\mu\tau}))\bar R_\tau$ (38), $\bar R_\tau=v_{Mp}\tau+\epsilon_M-\frac{u_M}{\mu}(\tau+\frac{e^{-\mu\tau}-1}{\mu})$ (39), and $\bar\tau_M$ the root of $\bar R_\tau=0$ — then $P_i$ can observe all its possible target evaders and receive information from all pursuers sharing a possible target with it, i.e. $\mathcal T_i^c\subseteq\mathcal T_i^d$ and $\mathcal N_i^{(cd)}=\mathcal N_i^{cd}=\mathcal N_i^c\subseteq\mathcal N_i^d$.
**Proof approach** (p. 10): (1) Define the winning region's range as its major-axis length $\gamma_{ij}=b_r-b_l$ (axis collinear with $v_{ej}$); $p_i\in\mathcal W_{ij}$ implies $\|p_i-e_j\|\le\gamma_{ij}$, so $r_i\ge\gamma_{ij}$ suffices for observation. (2) Compute $b_l,b_r$ case-by-case from Theorem 2's boundary pieces (e.g. $v_{mpi}=u_{mej}/\mu$, $v_{xej}\le v_{mpi}$: $b_l=\frac{v_{xej}}{\mu}-\epsilon_i-\frac{u_{mej}}{\mu^2}$, $b_r=\frac{v_{xej}}{\mu}+\epsilon_i+\frac{u_{mej}}{\mu^2}$; envelope cases use $f(\bar\tau_1)$, $f(\min(\tau_M,\tau_2))$ endpoints, with explicit $f(\tau_M),f(\tau_2),f(\tau_1)$ formulas), yielding $\gamma_{ij}=\gamma(v_{mpi},\epsilon_i,u_{mej},v_{xej})$. (3) **Monotonicity** (decisive step): $\gamma_{ij}$ increases in $v_{xej},v_{mpi},\epsilon_i$ and decreases in $u_{mej}$; moreover $b_r$ is non-decreasing and $b_l$ non-increasing in $v_{mpi},\epsilon_i$, so larger pursuer capability enlarges $[b_l,b_r]$ and covers the old interval. Hence $r_i\ge\gamma(v_{Mp},\epsilon_M,u_M,v_{Me})\ge\max_{j\in\mathcal T_i^c}\gamma_{ij}$ gives $\mathcal T_i^c\subseteq\mathcal T_i^d$. (4) For a competing pursuer $P_k$ with $p_k\in\mathcal W_{kj}$, the inter-pursuer distance is bounded via the same interval-covering argument on $\gamma(\max(v_{mpi},v_{mpk}),\max(\epsilon_i,\epsilon_k),u_{mej},v_{mej})$; taking global worst cases gives $\mathcal N_i^c\subseteq\mathcal N_i^d$.
**Remark 4 (p. 10)**: intuition — faster/longer-reaching pursuers enlarge winning regions; a faster-moving evader (large $\|v_{ej}\|$) is less maneuverable (damping bleeds speed) and enlarges them; a more agile evader (large $u_{mej}$) shrinks them.
**Physical meaning**: a *design rule* linking per-pair game geometry to the communication graph: with $r_i\ge\bar\gamma$, every pursuer sees everyone it could conflict with, so the local greedy information is globally sufficient. **Extensibility/cost**: $\bar\gamma$ uses global worst-case bounds, so it is conservative when players are dispersed; the four parameters must be known a priori (e.g., by design specification). Note only $v_{Mp}\le u_M/\mu$ cases appear in (34) — consistent with Assumption 1.

### 5.8 Lemma 4 (p. 11) + Algorithm 1 (p. 12) — distributed min-makespan allocation
**Definition 4 (Pursuer's group)**: $\mathcal Y_i^P\cup\mathcal Y_i^E$ with $\mathcal Y_i^P=\{P_k|\text{path from }P_i\text{ to }P_k\text{ in }\mathcal F^c\}\cup\{P_i\}$ and $\mathcal Y_i^E=\{E_j|\exists P_k\in\mathcal Y_i^P,(P_k,E_j)\in\mathcal Z^c\}$ — the smallest communication/negotiation unit.
**Lemma 4**: with $\mathcal F_i^{(cd)}=(\mathcal Y_i^P,\mathcal S_i^{(cd)})\subseteq\mathcal F^{(cd)}$, $\mathcal S_i^{(cd)}=\{(P_k,P_l)|P_k,P_l\in\mathcal Y_i^P,\ P_k\in\mathcal N_l^{(cd)}\}$, if $r_i\ge\bar\gamma\ \forall i\in\mathcal Y_i^P$ then $\mathcal F_i^{(cd)}$ is strongly connected. **Proof**: $r_i\ge\bar\gamma$ ⇒ $\mathcal N_i^{(cd)}=\mathcal N_i^c$, so $\mathcal S_i^{(cd)}=\{(P_k,P_l)|P_k,P_l\in\mathcal Y_i^P,P_k\in\mathcal N_l^c\}$, i.e., $\mathcal F_i^{(cd)}$ is the induced subgraph of $\mathcal F^c$ on $\mathcal Y_i^P$, connected by definition of $\mathcal Y_i^P$. (Note: $\mathcal F^c$ is undirected, so "strongly connected" reduces to connected — the wording is inherited from [37].)
**Algorithm 1 (Distributed Minimum Makespan Task Allocation for $P_i$, p. 12)**: (i) compute capture times of all observed evaders $\mathcal T_i^d$ via (15), get $\mathcal T_i^{cd}$; (ii) exchange target information with $\mathcal N_i^d$, then within $\mathcal N_i^{cd}$ flood to obtain the group $\mathcal Y_i^P,\mathcal Y_i^E$; (iii) acquire group-wide max capture time $\bar T_i$, min $\underline T_i$, and minimum feasible-time gap $\delta_i$; (iv) primary allocation: build weighted bipartite graph $G_{orig}^i=((\mathcal Y_i^P,\mathcal Y_i^E),E_{orig}^i,\omega_{orig}^i)$ with $E_{orig}^i=\{(i,j)|j\in\mathcal Y_i^E\}$ (42) and $\omega_{orig}^i=T_{ij}$ if $j\in\mathcal T_i^c$, $T_{\max}$ otherwise (43); run the **Distributed Hungarian method** of [37] (lines 9–10); record group cardinality $\bar M_c$ and makespan $\bar T_M$; (v) **binary search** on the makespan: while $T_r-T_l>\delta_i$, set $mid=(T_l+T_r)/2$, replace every $T_{ij}>mid$ by $T_{\max}$ (evaders believed uncapturable), re-run Distributed Hungarian, get $M_c,T_M$; accept ($T_r\leftarrow mid$, update target) iff $T_M\le\bar T_M$ and $M_c\ge\bar M_c$, else $T_l\leftarrow mid$ (lines 14–34). Each pursuer runs the same algorithm on its own local graph view; consensus emerges because group members share the group and costs.

### 5.9 Theorem 5 (pp. 11–12) — convergence and optimality of the whole solution
**Statement**: For the PE game of $N$ pursuers (1) and $M$ evaders (2), suppose $r_i\ge\bar\gamma\ \forall i$. If the pursuers execute Algorithm 1 at the start of the game, after a finite time all pursuers converge to an assignment which is the optimal solution of (40)(41). Then, if the pursuers use strategies (17) to chase their target evaders (if any), Problem 1 is solved.
**Proof approach** (pp. 11–12), by reduction to quoted results on the Distributed Hungarian algorithm [37]: (1) **Game structure**: evaders respond after allocation is declared (Stackelberg), so both sides use (18)/(17) and each $T_{ij}$ is the well-defined game value from Theorem 1 (15) — this fixes the cost matrix. (2) **Information sufficiency**: $r_i\ge\bar\gamma$ ⇒ Theorem 4 gives $\mathcal T_i^{cd}=\mathcal T_i^c$, $\mathcal N_i^{(cd)}=\mathcal N_i^{cd}=\mathcal N_i^c$; Lemma 4 ⇒ $\mathcal F_i^{(cd)}$ strongly connected, so each $P_i$ knows its whole group. (3) **Cardinality optimality**: Lemma 5 (Theorem 2 in [37]): on a strongly connected communication graph, with every pursuer knowing $\mathcal Y_i^P,\mathcal Y_i^E$ and its costs $\{T_{kj}\}$, the Distributed Hungarian converges in finite time to a common assignment optimal for the group's maximum-cardinality (assignment) problem — solving (40). (4) **Consensus propagation**: Lemma 6 (Corollary 3 in [37]): once one pursuer finds a (perfect) matching, after $|\mathcal Y_i^P|-1$ communication rounds every group member holds the same solution — justifying the stop/synchronize steps (lines 11–12, 24–25). (5) **Makespan optimality**: the binary search terminates in finite time with $\delta_i>0$, and the acceptance test (line 26: $T_M\le\bar T_M$ and $M_c\ge\bar M_c$) guarantees maximum assigned evaders and minimum makespan — solving (41). (6) Capture follows from Theorem 1 since all players use (17)(18); capture times match the $T_{ij}$ used in the allocation. Global, finite-time, exact (no approximation gap beyond the $\delta_i$ tolerance).
**Remark 5 (complexity, p. 12)**: per pursuer, Distributed Hungarian converges in $O(|\mathcal Y_i^P|^3)$ communication rounds (Lemma 7, Corollary 4 in [37]) and binary search in $O(\log\frac{\bar T_i-\underline T_i}{\delta_i})$ iterations; worst case (one group of all pursuers) total $O\big(N^3\log\frac{\bar T-\underline T}{\Delta}\big)$ rounds, with $\bar T=\max_{i,j,T_{ij}<T_{\max}}T_{ij}$, $\underline T=\min_{i,j}T_{ij}$, $\Delta$ the minimum difference of feasible capture times.
**Proof gap / observation**: the exactness of the bottleneck solution depends on the binary search's ability to distinguish makespans, i.e., on knowing $\delta_i=\min$ gap of all feasible capture times group-wide (line 5 of Algorithm 1) — acquiring $\delta_i$ requires global cost information within the group (by communication, consistent with the model, but it is an extra synchronization not analyzed for message complexity); if $\delta_i$ is unknown or costs are continuous, the algorithm yields an additive $\delta_i$-suboptimal makespan. Also Theorem 5's "optimal solution of (40)(41)" is asserted per group; optimality of the union of per-group optimal matchings for the *global* problem holds because groups are disconnected components of the conflict/capture graph (no cross-group interaction exists), which the proof uses implicitly via Lemma 5's per-group statement. The dynamic event-triggered variant (Section IV-D) carries **no formal guarantee** — the claim "the performance of dynamic allocation is no worse than that of Algorithm 1" (p. 15) is informal.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
**Author-stated**: (i) the permanent-escape property of the Theorem 3 evasion strategy is unproven ("we are unable to provide the complete proof yet", p. 9; repeated in Conclusion: "Another interesting point is the validation of the proposed feasible evasion strategy"); (ii) future work: games with more cooperation, "such as several pursuers cooperating to capture an evader" (p. 15) — the current framework strictly assigns one pursuer per evader; (iii) evaders do not cooperate and move only after allocation is declared (Stackelberg commitment assumption, p. 3).
**Critical observations**: (1) Lemma 1 and Lemma 2 proofs are omitted (pp. 5) — the extremality/constancy of the optimal strategies and the circle form of the isochrones, which everything else builds on, are asserted; a rigorous PMP derivation is absent. (2) Theorem 1's optimality argument is a geometric construction plus an HJI-equation check (27) without an explicit verification theorem (no viscosity/classical uniqueness or comparison argument); the barrier case $Q_{ij}'(\tau^\ast)=0$ where $W=0$ and the gradients (26) blow up is handled only by Remark 3's redefinition of "guaranteed". (3) The exactness of Theorem 5 hinges on $\delta_i$ (minimum feasible-capture-time gap) being known to every group member — a global quantity requiring extra synchronization whose message complexity is not analyzed; with unknown $\delta_i$ the makespan is only $\delta_i$-suboptimal. (4) $\bar\gamma$ in (34) is a worst-case bound over global parameters $(v_{Mp},\epsilon_M,u_M,v_{Me})$; in dispersed heterogeneous teams it can force very large sensing/communication ranges (in the 8-vs-10 sim, $r_i=6.9$ for everyone regardless of individual capability). (5) The allocation benchmark uses random synthetic costs rather than game-derived $T_{ij}$, so the end-to-end optimality claim (Theorem 5 with Theorem 1 costs) is exercised only qualitatively in the single 8-vs-10 scenario; no Monte-Carlo on game-derived costs, no centralized-MILP ([25],[40]) baseline, no timing. (6) The dynamic/event-triggered variant has no convergence or no-worse formal proof. (7) $T_{ij}$ presumes the assigned evader plays the optimal evasion (18); if an evader deviates (e.g., non-optimally flees elsewhere), the realized capture times differ from the allocation costs (capture itself is still guaranteed whenever $p_i\in\mathcal W_{ij}$). (8) Pursuers are single integrators (velocity-controlled); the quadrotor motivation (p. 2) requires an inner-loop mapping not discussed; no experiments on hardware. (9) Minor typos: Definition 3 says "$\mathcal W_{ij}$ ... capture of $E_i$" (should be $E_j$, p. 7); $f(0)=-\frac{v_{mpi}\epsilon}{v_{xej}}$ uses $\epsilon$ instead of $\epsilon_i$ (29, p. 8); eqs. (19)(20) drop the $(\tau^\ast)$ on $x_{cej},x_{cpi}$ in the numerators.

## 8. Citation Value
**Paragraph role**: This paper is the reference point for *bottleneck-objective (makespan) distributed task allocation layered on pairwise heterogeneous pursuit games*. Its most reusable assets are: (a) the isochron-based capture condition (Theorem 1) — an Apollonius-style tangency test between a linearly growing circle (simple-motion pursuer, inflated by the capture radius) and the damped-evader isochrone circle whose center and radius are explicit exponentials in $\mu\tau$ — giving a closed-form value, pursuit and evasion strategies without solving the HJI equation; (b) the complete analytic taxonomy of winning-region boundaries (Theorem 2, Table I) via envelopes of one-parameter circle families; (c) the idea of converting a game-theoretic spatial quantity (winning-region major axis, Theorem 4) into a *communication/sensing-range design rule* guaranteeing conflict-free distributed assignment; and (d) the exact reduction of min-makespan assignment (max-cardinality first, then bottleneck) to a distributed Hungarian + binary search with finite-time optimality (Theorem 5). Cite it when surveying two-stage multiplayer PE/reach-avoid games with heterogeneous dynamics, distributed allocation in PE games, makespan vs. linear-sum objectives, isochron/reachability-curve geometric methods, or sensing-range co-design for multi-robot pursuit.
**Suggested citation sentences**: "For a simple-motion pursuer against a damped double-integrator evader, the capture condition and saddle-point strategies can be obtained by an isochron-tangency test with value equal to the first internal-tangency time, avoiding the HJI equation [P054]." / "Beyond maximum-cardinality assignment, Li et al. derive a fully distributed algorithm that attains the minimum-makespan matching in finite time, together with an analytic lower bound on the sensing range that renders the local information sufficient for conflict-free allocation [P054]."
