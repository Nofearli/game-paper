## 2. Research Background
The reconnaissance game is a multi-stage adversarial paradigm in which a scout team must (i) approach a target, (ii) observe it for a nonzero dwell time, and (iii) retreat to a safe area, all while evading encirclement by a counter-reconnaissance (defender) team; only completion of all three phases changes the score (p. 1, Sec. I; Fig. 1, p. 3). Classical analytic treatments — single-stage attack-defense, pursuit-evasion and reach-avoid games solved via Hamilton–Jacobi–Isaacs (HJI) equations, Apollonius-type constructions, or closed-form value functions (paper's Sec. II-A, citing TAC/Automatica/TAES work) — are argued to degrade for multi-stage tasks with heterogeneous agents, obstacles, and partial observability because of the curse of dimensionality and discontinuous stage transitions (p. 1–2). The reconnaissance game lineage itself is short: Plante & Levis (IEEE TAC 1972) and Lee & Bakolas (ACC 2023) treated two-player variants with idealized perception (instantaneous reconnaissance, complete information) (p. 2, refs. [15], [16]). On the learning side, MADDPG/MATD3/MAPPO-style multi-agent RL handles complexity but typically assumes full observability, while trajectory predictors such as Goal-LBP, multi-level spatial–temporal models and IA-LSTM target structured traffic rather than sparse-observation adversarial games (p. 3). The paper draws on computational neuroscience — the prefrontal cortex (PFC) encoding phase-dependent valuation signals [21], [22] — to motivate a brain-inspired hybrid predictive network (HPN) fused with reinforcement learning, validated in simulation and on a physical TurtleBot3 arena.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
**Problem (Sec. III-A, p. 3)**: a 2D multi-agent reconnaissance game with red scouts, blue defenders, a hexagonal-star target, a green safe area, and maze-like obstacles (Fig. 1). In each of the three stages scouts must avoid encirclement; "at least one scout must successfully perform reconnaissance on the target and return to the safe zone"; score changes only when scouts complete all stages. Agents have heterogeneous observation ranges and action sets. Information structure — **known**: (i) scouts' own state variables, (ii) positions of target, safe area, map boundaries, (iii) states of defenders detected within sensing range; **unknown**: (i) defender states/actions outside the observation range, (ii) defenders' long-term strategies and future real trajectories (p. 3). The formal skeleton is a partially observable Markov game (Sec. III-B): local observation sets $O_1^t,\dots,O_N^t$, action sets $A_1^t,\dots,A_N^t$, global state space $S$, transition $P: S\times A_1^t\times A_2^t\cdots\times A_N^t\to S$, stochastic policies $\pi_i: O_i^t\times A_i^t\to[0,1]$ with $\pi_i(a_i^t|s_i^t)=P[A_i^t=a_i^t\,|\,S_i^t=s_i^t]$, rewards $r_i: S\times A_i^t\to\mathbb R$, and Bellman optimality equations (1)–(2) (p. 3).

**Gap — [authors' claim]** (p. 1–3): (1) *Task-level*: "Existing methods struggle to construct a continuous and differentiable cost function [5] that can accurately model the entire reconnaissance process" — the three interrelated stages defeat single-cost formulations. (2) *Multiagent*: partial observability and information asymmetry "lead to strategic suboptimality" [6]. (3) *Environmental*: the HJI equation's curse of dimensionality in high-dimensional complex environments "makes it computationally intractable to derive closed-form optimal strategies" [7]. Sec. II-B adds three "predominant characteristics": precision-modeling requirements with exponential complexity under heterogeneous agents/discontinuous transitions [19]; environmental simplification (static/quasi-static, few obstacles) causing dimensional explosion of HJI [20]; and "Idealized Perception Assumptions — most literature operates under complete information paradigms with instantaneous reconnaissance (the reconnaissance stage time is assumed to be zero)" [15], [16]. Sec. II-C adds a learning-side gap: CNN local-image features [28] "cannot quantitatively determine whether the agent's next decision leans toward cooperation or competition"; hybrid predictors (Goal-LBP [29], multi-level spatial–temporal [30], IA-LSTM [31]) "mainly focus on structured traffic scenarios and lack explicit treatment of sparse observability or robustness analysis in multi-agent adversarial games"; and "existing literature has limited research on trajectory prediction's impact on agent decision-making" [32].
**[Verified from cited text]**: the characterizations of [15] (Plante & Levis, "On the reconnaissance game," IEEE TAC 1972 — two-agent reconnaissance, open-loop solutions) and [16] (Lee & Bakolas, ACC 2023, two-player reconnaissance game with half-planar target and retreat regions) match those papers' titles/venues as printed, and the internal attribution of contributions to refs. [10] (Dubins-car/homicidal-chauffeur reach-avoid, Automatica 2024) and [11] (partially observed multi-player stochastic differential games under directed graphs, Automatica 2024) is consistent with their printed titles. The quantitative claims behind gaps (1)–(3) are asserted via citations and are not independently demonstrated in the paper. One verifiable citation defect: ref. [17] (Huang, Ding, Zhang, Tomlin, "Automation-Assisted Capture-the-Flag," IEEE TCST 23(3):1014–1028) is printed with year **2022**; that volume/issue/pages correspond to **2015** (the paper is present in this library as Huang et al. 2015), so the printed year is wrong. Note also that the HJI motivation is never operationalized — the paper's own model is a discrete-action Markov game and no HJI computation is attempted anywhere.

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Modeling**: A multi-stage (approach–reconnaissance–retreat) reconnaissance game cast as a partially observable Markov game with unequal sensing ranges and discrete action sets, plus a **phase-specific reward system** "grounded in game-theoretic decomposition and cognitive science": total payoff $J = J_{approach}+J_{recon}+J_{retreat}$ (eq. (25), p. 6) over three sub-games $\{G_{approach},G_{recon},G_{retreat}\}$, implemented via Table I and eqs. (26)–(28), with PFC-inspired interpretation of each term (p. 6–7).
2. **Architecture — ImpLSTM** (Sec. IV-A, p. 4): an LSTM variant "inspired by the prefrontal cortex temporal dependency processing mechanisms" in which forget/input gates use an impulse (Gaussian-erf) activation $\phi$ with a surrogate gradient $\phi'(u)=\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{\pi}}e^{-\alpha^2(u-V_{th})^2}$ for backpropagation (eq. (3)); the authors assert a gradient-estimation error bound $O(e^{-\alpha^2})$ and local-minimum convergence under standard SGD assumptions, and differentiate ImpLSTM from spiking neural networks (differentiable hidden dynamics, no discrete spike-timing instability).
3. **Architecture — HPN** (Sec. IV-B/C, p. 4–5): a conditional-GAN-style **Hybrid Predictive Network** predicting opponent (defender) future position trajectories from partial history: ImpLSTM encoder–decoder generator with random noise $z$ (eqs. (7)–(10)) and an ImpLSTM+MLP discriminator (eqs. (11)–(13)); trained with a hybrid objective $L_{total}=L_{GAN}+\lambda L_2$ (eqs. (22)–(24)) claimed to avoid mode collapse and the "averaging" artifact of pure L2 prediction.
4. **Algorithm — RL-based HPN** (Sec. IV-D, p. 5–6; Algorithm 1): Actor–Critic training in which HPN predictions $\widehat Y$ shape relative-position features $\Delta$ that enter both the critic's target (eq. (15)) and the actor input (eq. (14)); experience entries $\mathcal O(X,\widehat Y,a^t,r^t,\Delta)$; parameter updates (16)–(20) with soft target updates; plus an informal **robustness/convergence analysis**: prediction-error-bounded value deviation $|\widehat Q-Q|\le L_Q\epsilon$ with safe margin $\epsilon<\delta/L_Q$, Robbins–Monro almost-sure convergence to a stationary point, and a contraction-mapping stability claim (p. 6). A per-iteration computational-complexity analysis (eqs. (29)–(35), p. 12–13) accompanies the algorithm.
5. **Experiments**: (a) ETH/UCY pedestrian-trajectory prediction vs. Kalman, LSTM and a surrogate-gradient SNN (Table III, Fig. 6, ADE/FDE); (b) 2v2 reconnaissance-game simulations vs. DDPG, MADDPG, MATD3, MAPPO with a full round-robin score matrix (Table IV, Fig. 7–8); (c) module ablations HPN-ImpLSTM (no GAN) and HPN-GAN (no ImpLSTM) (Fig. 9–10); (d) physical Gazebo/ROS TurtleBot3 experiments at 3/10/20 s reconnaissance durations (Fig. 11–13, success rates 0.8889/0.7626/0.7368).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results
**No formal theorem, lemma, proposition, or corollary environments exist in this paper.** All analytic content is inline prose plus one algorithm block and one complexity table. The main results are extracted below as R1–R9 with exact equations and stated proof gaps.

### 5.0 Game primitives
- **Dynamics/environment**: no continuous dynamics equations are given. The game is discrete-time with discrete action sets: red agents and blue agent 1 use $\{up, down, left, right, stop\}$; blue agent 2 additionally has diagonal moves (p. 9). Sensing: red agents and blue agent 1 have observation range 5 (Euclidean distance units in simulation), blue agent 2 has range 7 (p. 9); physical values 0.25 m / 0.25 m / 0.35 m with defender capture radius 0.15 m (p. 10).
- **Phases / termination / winning** (p. 3, p. 9): stage 1 approach, stage 2 reconnaissance ("The second stage lasts 10 time steps" in simulation; 3 s / 10 s / 20 s in the physical task), stage 3 retreat. Scouts win (+1) only if at least one scout completes reconnaissance of the target and returns to the safe zone without being captured; tie 0; loss −1; "The score changes only when scouts complete all stages." Capture is an absorbing negative event ($r_4=r_8=-45$).
- **Information structure**: partially observable Markov game with transition $P: S\times A_1^t\times\cdots\times A_N^t\to S$ (as printed — a deterministic map to $S$; a stochastic kernel notation would be standard). Bellman optimality (1)–(2): $V^*(s)=\max_{a\in A}\big(R_s^a+\gamma\sum_{s'\in S}P_{ss'}^a V_{\pi_i}(s')\big)$, $Q^*(s,a)=R_s^a+\gamma\sum_{s'\in S}P_{ss'}^a\max_{a'\in A}Q^*(s',a')$, $\gamma\in(0,1]$ (p. 3). Notation slip: (1) writes $V^*$ on the left but $V_{\pi_i}$ inside the expectation.

### R1 — ImpLSTM cell (p. 4, eq. (3))
**Statement**: with input $x_i^t$, hidden $h_i^{t-1}$, and gates
$f_i^t=\phi\big(W_f\cdot[h_i^{t-1},x_i^t]+b_f\big)$, $i_i^t=\phi\big(W_i\cdot[h_i^{t-1},x_i^t]+b_i\big)$, $\tilde C_i^t=\sigma\big(W_C\cdot[h_i^{t-1},x_i^t]+b_C\big)$, $C_i^t=\sigma\big(f_i^t\cdot C_i^{t-1}+i_i^t\cdot\tilde C_i^t\big)$, $o_i^t=\sigma\big(W_o\cdot[h_i^{t-1},x_i^t]+b_o\big)$, $h_i^t=o_i^t\cdot C_i^t$,
where the "impulse" activations are $\phi(u)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\alpha u}e^{-t^2}dt$ (i.e., $\tfrac12(1+\operatorname{erf}(\alpha u))$) and "$\sigma(u)$ is the step function" (p. 4).
**Rationale/meaning**: $\phi$ replaces the logistic gate nonlinearity to emulate spiking neurons' membrane-threshold firing while keeping the long/short-memory interpretation of $C_i^t$/$h_i^t$ ("$C_t$ represents the long-term memory... $h_t$ simulates the short-term memory").
**Proof/analysis given (prose only)**: none for the cell itself; training uses the surrogate $\phi'(u)=\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{\pi}}\cdot e^{-\alpha^2(u-V_{th})^2}$ "to solve the problem that the spikes signal cannot train the neural network according to the back propagation (BP) algorithm."
**Proof gaps**: (i) the printed $\phi$ (upper limit $\alpha u$) has exact derivative $\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt\pi}e^{-\alpha^2u^2}$, which matches the surrogate only if the upper limit is $\alpha(u-V_{th})$ — the printed pair is inconsistent; (ii) if $\sigma$ were truly a step function, $C_i^t$ and $h_i^t$ would be binary and the claimed "differentiable hidden dynamics" would fail — in practice $\sigma$ must be a smooth sigmoid, but the paper never says so; (iii) the extra outer $\sigma$ wrapping the cell-state update $C_i^t=\sigma(\cdot)$ is nonstandard (LSTM cell states carry no output nonlinearity) and is not justified.

### R2 — Surrogate-gradient error bound and convergence claim (p. 4, prose)
**Statement [authors' claim]**: "we adopt the exponential surrogate $\phi'(u)=\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{\pi}}e^{-\alpha^2(u-V_{th})^2}$, and show that the gradient estimation error is bounded by $O(e^{-\alpha^2})$, which guarantees convergence to a local minimum under standard stochastic gradient descent assumptions. This provides theoretical evidence that the surrogate gradient remains stable during training, avoiding gradient vanishing."
**Analysis**: this is the paper's only ImpLSTM "theory." No bound is derived, no assumptions (loss smoothness, gradient boundedness, step sizes) are listed, no lemma is stated, and the constant inside $O(\cdot)$ and the quantity the error is measured against (true derivative of what — the step function's distributional derivative?) are unspecified. Extensibility: the claim is generic surrogate-gradient folklore (cf. SNN literature) restated for the erf surrogate; it is not specific to the reconnaissance game.

### R3 — HPN prediction objective (p. 4, eqs. (4)–(6))
**Statement**: opponent $i$'s strategy is its position sequence; history $X_i^{1:t}=\{(x_k^i,y_k^i)\in\mathbb R^2\,|\,k=1,\dots,t\}$, $i\in\{1,\dots,n\}$ (4); ground truth over horizon $T$: $Y_i^{1:T}=\{(x_k^i,y_k^i)\,|\,k=t+1,\dots,t+T\}$ (5); prediction $\widehat Y_i^{1:T}$ (6) with objective $\widehat Y_i^{1:T}=f(X_i^{1:t};W')$ (unnumbered, p. 4). **Generator** (p. 5): embedding $E_i^t=f_{FC1}(X_i^t;W_E)$ (7); encoder $h_i^t=\mathrm{ImpLSTM}_{encoder}(E_i^t,h_i^{t-1};W_{encoder})$ (8); decoder with noise $z$: $h_{d,i}^t=\mathrm{ImpLSTM}_{decoder}(h_i^t,z;W_{decoder})$ (9); output $\widehat Y_t^i=(\hat x_t^i,\hat y_t^i)=f_{FC1}(h_{d,i}^t)$ (10). **Discriminator**: $P_t^i=f_{FC1}([T_t^i,\widehat Y_t^i];W_D)$ (11); $h_{dis,i}^t=\mathrm{ImpLSTM}(h_{dis,i}^{t-1},P_t^i;W_{dis})$ (12); $Y_{dis,i}=\mathrm{MLP}(h_{dis,i}^t,P_t^i;W_{MLP})$ (13). Local, data-driven; no analytic solution properties (identifiability, error rates) are derived. **Gap**: the discriminator input mixes real trajectory $T_t^i$ and prediction $\widehat Y_t^i$ in (11) without defining $T_t^i$'s relation to $X_i^{1:t}$.

### R4 — Actor–Critic training equations (p. 5–6, Algorithm 1, eqs. (14)–(20))
**Statement**: experience tuple $\mathcal O(X,\widehat Y,a^t,r^t,\Delta)$ where $\Delta_{ij}$ is the relative position between defender $j$ and scout $i$ and $\Delta$ collects all such features. Actor: $a_t^i=f_i(s_t^i,\Delta_{ij})$ (14). Critic target: $y^t=r_t^i+\gamma Q_i(\widehat Y_{t+1},a_{t+1},\Delta)$ (15). Critic loss: $L(\omega_i)=\frac1B\sum\big(y^t-Q_i(X_t,a_t,\Delta)\big)^2$ (16). Policy gradient: $\nabla_{\theta_i}J(\theta_i)=\frac1B\sum_t\big[\nabla_{\theta_i}f_i(X_t^i)\cdot\nabla_{a_i}Q_i(X_t,a_t,\Delta)\big]$ (17). Updates: $\theta_i\leftarrow\theta_i+\eta_A\nabla_{\theta_i}J(\theta_i)$ (18); $\omega_i\leftarrow\omega_i-\eta_C L(\omega_i)$ (19); soft target updates $\theta_i'=\tau\theta_i'+(1-\tau)\theta_i$, $\omega_i'=\tau\omega_i'+(1-\tau)\omega_i$ (20). Algorithm 1 additionally trains the GAN head online: discriminator loss $L_D=-\frac{1}{N^*}\sum[\log D(X_i,a_i;\theta_D)+\log(1-D(X_i,a_i';\theta_D))]$, $\theta_D\leftarrow\theta_D-\alpha_D\nabla_{\theta_D}L_D$; fake action $a_i'\sim G(X_i;\theta_G)$; generator loss $L_G=-\frac1N\sum[D(X_i,a_i';\theta_D)]+\,*\,\|\widehat Y_i-X_i\|_2^2$ (the L2 weight is printed as a literal asterisk "*"), $\theta_G\leftarrow\theta_G+\alpha_G\nabla_{\theta_G}L_G$; target values recomputed identically to (15).
**Meaning**: this is MADDPG-style deterministic actor–critic (centralized critic input includes all agents' states/actions plus relative-position features) with the GAN adversarial head co-trained in the same loop; $\widehat Y$ (predicted opponent trajectories) is injected into both the critic's next-state argument and $\Delta$.
**Proof gaps / inconsistencies**: (i) Algorithm 1 uses the *generator* as the policy sampler ($a_t\sim\pi(a_t|X_t;\theta_G)$ "using generator G") and has $D$ judge *actions* $(X_i,a_i)$ — while Sec. IV-B/C define $G$/$D$ on opponent *trajectories*; the two roles are never reconciled, so the implemented dataflow between HPN and the actors cannot be reconstructed exactly from the paper; (ii) $N^*$ (discriminator loss) vs $N$ (generator loss) and $N$'s reuse for both agent count and inner-loop step count; (iii) the L2 term compares the predicted *future* $\widehat Y_i$ with the observed *past* $X_i$ — dimensionally meaningful only if horizons match; (iv) $L(i)=\frac1N\sum(y_t^i-Q_i(X_t,a_t,\Delta))^2$ in Algorithm 1 line 19 vs $\frac1B$ in eq. (16).

### R5 — GAN + L2 hybrid objective (p. 6, eqs. (22)–(24))
**Statement**: $L_{total}=L_{GAN}+\lambda L_2$ (22), with
$L_{GAN}=\min_G\max_D\mathbb E_{P_{data}(x_{1:t}^i)}\big[x_{1:t}^i\log(\widetilde Y_{1:T}^i)+\mathbb E_{P_{Gen}(\widetilde Y_{1:T}^i)}[(1-x_{1:t}^i)\log(1-\widetilde Y_{1:T}^i)]\big]$ (23) and $L_2=\|\widetilde Y_{1:T}^i-X_{1:t}^i\|_2^2$ (24).
**Claimed role**: $L_{GAN}$ optimizes $G$ and $D$; $L_2$ is a "strategy diversity loss" preventing the averaging artifact of pure-L2 prediction; $\lambda$ is a hyperparameter. The prose (p. 6) argues ImpLSTM-as-generator-backbone stabilizes GAN training ("ImpLSTM provides stable temporal representation to prevent GAN mode collapse, while GAN enhances data efficiency and improves robustness under sparse sensing").
**Proof gap (decisive)**: eq. (23) as printed is not a valid GAN objective — trajectories $x_{1:t}^i$ and $\widetilde Y_{1:T}^i$ (elements of $\mathbb R^2$ sequences) appear where binary labels $x\in\{0,1\}$ and discriminator probabilities $\in[0,1]$ belong, and the second expectation is nested inside the first instead of being a separate data term. Eq. (24) subtracts a length-$T$ future from a length-$t$ history. The formulas cannot be taken literally; evidently a conditional-GAN binary cross-entropy on discriminator outputs plus an L2 regression regularizer was intended.

### R6 — Phase-specific reward system (p. 6–7, Table I, eqs. (25)–(28))
**Statement**: "Following Stackelberg decomposition principles, the total payoff is $J=J_{approach}+J_{recon}+J_{retreat}$" (25). Table I (p. 7) entries **as printed** (typos preserved): *Approach $R_{app}$*: $r_1=d_{target}$; $r_2=-350$ (agent encounters obstacles); $r_3=-5$ (agent moves every step). *Reconnaissance $R_a$*: $r_4=-45$ ("Captured by Defeneder"); $r_5=200$ (reconnaissance succeed); $r_6=45$ (scout in opponent territory) / $100/d_{boundary}$ (scout in own territory); $r_7=0$ if $d_{enemy}>5$, else $-150/d_{enemy}$. *Retreat* (header typo "Reteart" $R_d$): $r_8=-45$ (captured); $r_9=200$ (in safe zone); **a second entry also labeled $r_9$**: $45$ (scout in own territory) / $100/d_{boundary}$ (scout in opponent territory); $r_{10}=150/d_{enemy}$ if $d_{enemy}<5$, else $0$. Quantities: $d_{target}$ (target–scout distance), $d_{enemy}$ (defender–scout distance), $d_{boundary}$ (agent–own-territory-boundary distance). Compact implementation: $r_{app}=d_{target}-I_{obstacle}-I_{captured}$ (26); $r_{reconnaissance}=I_{success}+\frac{100}{d_{boundary}}-\frac{150}{d_{enemy}}$ (27); $r_{retreat}=I_{safe}+\frac{150}{d_{enemy}}-I_{captured}$ (28), "where $I_\cdot$ are event indicators in Table.I."
**Claimed foundations**: (a) game-theoretic — the terms "$d_{target}$, $d_{enemy}$, and $d_{boundary}$ are not ad hoc penalties but proxies for local equilibrium conditions in each stage" (p. 7); (b) cognitive — $r_1$ = PFC "uncertainty reduction preference" (curiosity in approach), $r_5$/$r_6$ = "success-oriented valuation", $r_9$/$r_{10}$ = "cooperative survival valuation", citing [21] (Barraclough et al., Nature Neuroscience 2004) and [22] (Lee et al., Front. Comput. Neurosci. 2022). Author-stated caveat: "reward values should be adjusted according to practical circumstances" (p. 7).
**Proof gaps / critical notes**: (i) no Stackelberg mathematics follows eq. (25) — the decomposition is additive reward shaping, not a bilevel game analysis; (ii) eq. (27) drops the territorial term $r_6$ and the indicator gating of $r_7$; eq. (28) drops the boundary-type term of the second $r_9$ — the "implementation" equations are inconsistent summaries of Table I; (iii) the retreat proximity term $+150/d_{enemy}$ *rewards* defender proximity (blows up as $d_{enemy}\to0$) while the text asserts "150/d_enemy deters defender encounters" (p. 7) — a probable sign error, printed as-is; (iv) the duplicate $r_9$ index leaves $r_6$-type behavior in retreat unindexed.

### R7 — Prediction-error robustness bound (p. 6, unnumbered display)
**Statement**: with $Y$ the ground-truth trajectory, $\widehat Y$ the HPN prediction, $\epsilon=\|\widehat Y-Y\|_2$, the decision value under prediction is approximated as $\widehat Q(s,a)=Q(s,a;Y)+\epsilon\cdot\frac{\partial Q}{\partial Y}$, "and the deviation is bounded as $|\widehat Q(s,a)-Q(s,a)|\le L_Q\cdot\epsilon$. Thus, the robustness of the RL policy can be theoretically guaranteed provided that prediction error remains within $\epsilon<\delta/L_Q$."
**Analysis**: a first-order Taylor/Lipschitz argument. **Proof gaps**: $L_Q$ (evidently a Lipschitz constant of $Q$ w.r.t. the trajectory argument) and $\delta$ (an admissible value-deviation margin) are never defined; no proof, no assumption on $Q$'s smoothness; the bound is heuristic. Its use later (p. 11–12) to explain the empirical success-rate decay with reconnaissance duration ("longer horizons enlarge $\epsilon$, tightening the robustness margin") is qualitative only — $\epsilon$ is never measured as a function of horizon.

### R8 — Convergence and contraction claim (p. 6, eq. (21))
**Statement**: "under the standard assumptions of stochastic approximation, the Actor-Critic updates $\theta_i\leftarrow\theta_i+\eta_A\nabla_{\theta_i}J(\theta_i)$, $\omega_i\leftarrow\omega_i-\eta_C\nabla_{\omega_i}L(\omega_i)$ (21), with appropriately chosen learning rates $\eta_A,\eta_C$ satisfying the Robbins–Monro conditions, guarantee almost sure convergence to a stationary point of the expected return $J(\theta)$. Since the prediction error of HPN is bounded ($\epsilon<\delta/L_Q$), the perturbed value function $\widehat Q(s,a)$ remains a contraction mapping, ensuring stability of training."
**Analysis**: an appeal to classical two-timescale stochastic-approximation results; no assumption list (Markov sampling from a replay buffer violates the standard i.i.d. hypotheses without extra machinery), no proof that $\widehat Q$ inherits contraction from the Bellman operator under trajectory perturbations. **Proof gap**: asserted, not proven; no theorem environment.

### R9 — Equilibrium-existence claim and complexity analysis (p. 9; p. 12–13, eqs. (29)–(35), Table V)
**Existence [authors' claim]** (p. 9): "the reconnaissance game formulated as a finite partially observable Markov game guarantees the existence of at least one mixed-strategy Nash equilibrium," with three pieces of "numerical evidence": (i) convergence of average rewards in Fig. 7; (ii) shrinking reward-variance bands across seeds; (iii) self-play score $-0.03\pm0.16$ (Table IV) "a typical characteristic of Nash equilibrium." **[Verification]**: for a finite game, existence of a mixed Nash equilibrium is Nash's theorem — the claim is plausible but is asserted without citation or proof, and no connection between the learned policy and the equilibrium is established; the "evidence" is purely empirical.
**Complexity**: with $N$ agents, $N_o$ opponents, input dim $d_x$, hidden width $d_h$, discriminator MLP width $d_m$, layer counts $L_G,L_D,L_\pi,L_Q$, batch $B$, discriminator steps per generator step $k_D$, horizons $T_{obs},T_{pred}$: generator $O\big(BL_G(T_{obs}+T_{pred})d_h^2\big)$ (29); discriminator $O\big(k_DB[L_DT_{pred}d_h^2+T_{pred}(d_hd_m+d_m^2)]\big)$ (30); actor $O\big(BNL_\pi(d_sd_h+d_h^2)\big)$ (31); critic $O\big(BL_Q[(Nd_s+N_d_a+NN_od_\Delta)d_h+d_h^2]\big)$ (32); pairwise relative computation $O(BNN_o)$ + EnvStep (33); total per iteration (34) $O\big(B[L_G(T_{obs}+T_{pred})d_h^2+k_D(L_DT_{pred}d_h^2+T_{pred}d_m^2)+NL_\pi d_h^2+L_Q(N^2d_\Delta d_h+d_h^2)]\big)+$EnvStep; simplified under $d_h$ dominant, $d_m\sim d_h$, moderate $N$: $O\big(B[k_DT_{pred}d_h^2+Nd_h^2+N^2d_\Delta d_h]\big)$ (35). The $N^2d_\Delta d_h$ term reflects the centralized critic consuming all agents' states/actions and $N\cdot N_o$ relative-position features — i.e., the approach does not remove the exponential-in-agents complexity of exact HJI solutions but replaces it with polynomial per-iteration learning cost. Analytic, big-O only; no constants, no wall-clock model (validated empirically by Table V).

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
**Author-stated**: (i) the accuracy–duration trade-off: "as the reconnaissance time increases... the success rate of scouts in completing tasks decreases... longer reconnaissance demands stable prediction over extended horizons, increasing accumulated error" (p. 11–12); (ii) three proposed mitigations — hierarchical prediction (intent layer + trajectory layer), uncertainty-aware RL (Bayesian HPN penalizing high-uncertainty actions), and dynamic/adaptive prediction horizons scaling with observability (p. 12); (iii) reward magnitudes "should be adjusted according to practical circumstances" (p. 7); (iv) future work: attention mechanisms, multi-team games, strategy migration across agent compositions (Sec. VI).
**Critical observations (with page/eq.)**:
1. **No formal analysis**: every "theoretical guarantee" — the $O(e^{-\alpha^2})$ surrogate-gradient bound (p. 4), the robustness margin $\epsilon<\delta/L_Q$ (p. 6), the contraction-mapping stability, the Robbins–Monro almost-sure convergence (p. 6, eq. (21)), and mixed-Nash existence (p. 9) — is an unproven prose paragraph; $\delta$, $L_Q$, $\alpha$, $V_{th}$ are never given values or definitions, and no assumptions are enumerated.
2. **Architecture opacity**: Algorithm 1 casts the GAN generator as the actors' policy sampler and the discriminator as an action judge, while Secs. IV-B/C define G/D on opponent trajectories; the precise wiring of $\widehat Y\to\Delta\to$ actor/critic is prose-only (p. 5, Sec. IV-F-d). The method is not reproducible from the paper alone.
3. **Malformed objective**: eq. (23) is not a valid GAN loss as printed (trajectories in place of labels/probabilities, nested expectations), and eq. (24)'s $L_2$ compares future to past; Algorithm 1's generator loss carries a literal "*" weight placeholder (p. 5).
4. **Reward-system inconsistencies**: duplicate index $r_9$ and "Reteart"/"Defeneder" typos in Table I (p. 7); the retreat term $+150/d_{enemy}$ (Table I $r_{10}$, eq. (28)) rewards defender proximity, contradicting the stated deterrence intent (p. 7); eqs. (27)–(28) silently drop Table I terms ($r_6$, boundary term, indicator gating).
5. **Motivation–method mismatch**: HJI/curse-of-dimensionality framing (p. 1–2) is never engaged — the game is discrete-action and no HJI, barrier, Apollonius, or value-iteration machinery appears; the contribution is a learning architecture, not a game-theoretic solution concept.
6. **Evaluation asymmetries**: HPN's predictor could be grafted onto any baseline, but only MADDPG is effectively "HPN minus HPN" (p. 9); no ablation of the reward system; "statistically significant" (p. 9) is claimed without tests or confidence intervals; seeds behind Table IV unreported; the ETH/UCY prediction benchmark involves pedestrians, not adversarial defenders, so the headline Table III numbers do not measure in-game prediction quality.
7. **Centralization in the physical loop**: the workstation "receives the positions of all robots" (p. 10), i.e., global position information exists off-board; the limited-perception claim holds only for the onboard sensing modality (LiDAR-based defender estimation), not for the decision pipeline.
8. **Copyediting**: success-rate typo 0.0889 vs. figure value 0.8889 (p. 11 vs. Fig. 12(b)); MATD3/MADDPG values swapped in text vs. Table IV (p. 9); "As shown in Fig.4..." cross-reference error (p. 9); ref. [17] year printed 2022 where 2015 is correct (TCST 23(3):1014–1028); ZARA2 Kalman ADE anomaly (0.55/0.64); text/table column mismatch around Table V (p. 13).

## 8. Citation Value
This paper is the library's representative of the **learning-based, opponent-modeling branch of multi-stage reconnaissance/attack-defense games**: it complements the analytic line (Plante–Levis 1972; Lee–Bakolas 2023; capture-the-flag via HJI/Apollonius) by showing how a three-phase reach–observe–retreat mission with unequal sensing ranges, obstacles, and nonzero dwell time can be handled by an Actor–Critic method whose state is augmented with GAN-predicted defender trajectories, and it is one of the few entries with a physical multi-robot (TurtleBot3/Gazebo) validation including a measured success-rate-vs-dwell-time trade-off. Its citation value is strongest for (a) motivating learning over HJI when stages, partial observability, and perception limits coexist; (b) predictor-in-the-loop RL design (predictions entering both critic targets and relative-position features) and its informal error-propagation bound; (c) brain-inspired/spiking-motivated components (ImpLSTM, PFC-grounded reward phasing) in game decision-making. It should be cited with the caveat that its guarantees are heuristic (no theorems; undefined constants; malformed GAN objective as printed) — e.g.: "Shen et al. formulate the three-phase reconnaissance game as a partially observable Markov game and couple an ImpLSTM+GAN opponent-trajectory predictor with Actor-Critic reinforcement learning, reporting higher round-robin scores and lower variance than DDPG, MADDPG, MATD3, and MAPPO together with sim-to-real TurtleBot3 experiments whose success rate degrades from 0.89 to 0.74 as the reconnaissance dwell time grows from 3 s to 20 s [P079]."
