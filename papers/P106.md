## 2. Research Background
Reach-avoid differential games (a.k.a. two-target games, perimeter/target-defense games) ask whether pursuers can protect a target set against evaders that try to reach it, and are motivated by protection of critical infrastructures (buildings, airports) against malicious drones. Four methodological families exist: Hamilton–Jacobi (HJ) reachability analysis (general but low-dimensional), the method of characteristics (closed forms when singular surfaces are known), computational geometry (Voronoi diagrams, Apollonius circles, dominance regions — for simple-motion players), and learning-based approaches (empirically strong, no guarantees). Almost all reach-avoid results assume obstacle-free domains; obstacles add both motion and visibility constraints and are handled in the pursuit-only literature by dominance-region analysis (Oyler et al.), visibility-based pursuit, or sufficiency results on pursuer numbers (Zhou et al.: three pursuers suffice in bounded arenas). This paper brings winning-region-based, computationally cheap guarantees to multiplayer reach-avoid games in bounded polygonal domains with general polygonal obstacles, continuing the first author's line of matching-based multiplayer reach-avoid games ([39], [40]).

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
- **Problem**: $N_p$ pursuers $\mathcal{P}$ cooperate to protect a convex polygonal goal region $\Omega_{\text{goal}}$ inside a polygonal region $\Omega$ containing pairwise-disjoint simple-polygon obstacles $\mathcal{O}$; $N_e$ evaders starting in the play region $\Omega_{\text{play}} = \Omega \setminus (\cup_O O \cup \Omega_{\text{goal}})$ try to reach $\Omega_{\text{goal}}$ through $\Omega_{\text{free}} = \Omega_{\text{play}} \cup \Omega_{\text{goal}}$. The pursuers must guarantee capture of, or eternal delay of, as many evaders as possible against arbitrary evader strategies, under a state-feedback information structure (all current positions known to both teams; speeds, capture radii, and map known).
- **[Authors' claim]** Current obstacle reach-avoid approaches suffer from at least one of: curse of dimensionality (HJ discretization, ≈5-state limit citing [5]), heavy computational burden, model approximations (discrete-time/LQ approximations [1], LTL synthesis [32]), and lack of winning-region construction; "an efficient approach for computing winning regions and strategies for environments with obstacles is missing, and it becomes even harder for multiplayer games" (p. 816).
- **[Verified from cited text]** The one-pursuer-one-evader obstacle-free basis Lemma 1 is explicitly from Dorothy et al. [9] (Automatica 2024, "One Apollonius circle is enough for many pursuit-evasion games") — the paper honestly attributes it. The safe-distance/evasion-region machinery (Definition 7, eq. (7)) and the $\dot\varrho \ge 0$ argument are adapted from Yan et al. [40, Th. 3.1, Lemma 3.3] (Automatica 2022, 3D heterogeneous games, obstacle-free); the paper says the proof of [40, Th. 3.1] is "followed" and [40, Lemma 3.3] is "easily adapted" — these transplants are plausible given [40]'s identical $f_{ij}$ form, but the obstacle-free 3D-to-2D adaptation for "at most two pursuers" (Remark 6) is asserted, not re-proven here. ESP/wavefront representations (Lemmas 6–7) are from Hershberger–Suri's optimal planar ESP algorithm [16] (SIAM J. Comput. 1999) — standard. The gap claim that prior multiplayer work [6] (Chen–Zhou–Tomlin, TAC 2017) fused only 1-vs-1 pairwise outcomes via maximum matching is accurate, and this paper's coalition-level (many-vs-one) subgames plus hierarchical allocation is a genuine extension.
- **Gap filled**: first construction of pursuit winning regions and strategies for reach-avoid games with general polygonal obstacles, fused into a multiplayer receding-horizon strategy with a provably increasing lower bound on the number of defeated evaders.

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Theory (subgame winning regions)**: three pairs of pursuit winning regions + strategies for a coalition $P_c$ vs. one evader $E_j$ in bounded polygonal environments with general polygonal obstacles: *onsite* winning (Theorem 1/Lemma 2, expanded Apollonius circles), *goal-visible close-to-goal* winning (Theorem 2, convex GCPs), *nongoal-visible close-to-goal* winning (Theorem 4, ESP + anchored safe distance).
2. **Modeling tools**: new geometric constructs — convex goal-covering polygons (Definition 5) with existence/consistency (Lemma 4), direction range (Definition 6), first-visible obstacle vertices (Definition 9) and a constructive GCP-enlargement theorem (Theorem 3), ESP reachable region/wavefront/circular-sector decomposition (Definitions 11, 12, 14), and the anchored ESP-based safe distance (Definition 13).
3. **Algorithm**: the multiplayer onsite and close-to-goal (MOCG) receding-horizon strategy (Algorithm 1) with a four-stage hierarchical optimal task allocation — capture matching (BIP (22) over a conflict graph), enhanced, nondominated (polynomial-time maximum bipartite matching), and closest matchings — plus a check routine (Algorithm 2) and an ESP-based evasion-winning certificate (Lemma 9) used to prune hopeless assignments.
4. **Guarantee**: Theorem 5 — the number of guaranteed defeated evaders $\lvert E_{\text{capture}}\rvert + \lvert M_{\text{defeat}}\rvert$ is a valid lower bound at every iteration and is nondecreasing over iterations.
5. **Experiments**: four simulated scenarios (up to 8 pursuers, 6 evaders) illustrating each winning mode and the increasing bound; all games finish in <1 min since winning regions are closed-form or need small convex programs.

## 5. Key Mathematical Results (exhaustive)

### 5.0 Game model and information structure (Sec. II, pp. 816–817)
- **Dynamics (eq. (1))**: simple (Isaacs) holonomic motion, $\dot x_{Pi}(t) = v_{Pi} u_{Pi}(t)$, $\dot x_{Ej}(t) = v_{Ej} u_{Ej}(t)$, $x_{Pi}, x_{Ej} \in \mathbb{R}^2$, controls $u \in \mathcal{U} = \{u : [0,\infty) \to \mathbb{S}^2 \mid u \text{ piecewise smooth}\}$. Speed ratio $\alpha_{ij} = v_{Pi}/v_{Ej} > 1$ (faster pursuers). Capture radius $r_i \ge 0$; **capture condition**: Euclidean distance $\le r_i$ AND the connecting segment is obstacle-free ("visual radius capture") — obstacles hence also shield capture.
- **Environment**: obstacles $O_i \subset \Omega$ simple polygons with boundaries excluded, disjoint closures; $\Omega_{\text{goal}} = \{x \mid A_i x + b_i \ge 0,\ i \in I_{\text{goal}}\}$ convex, disjoint from obstacles. State-feedback information: each team decides controls from all players' current positions.
- **Winning (Definition 1, p. 817)**: coalition $P_c$ wins against $E_j$ at state $X_{cj}$ if some $U_c \in \mathcal{U}_c$ guarantees, for any evader strategy, either (1) capture in $\Omega_{\text{play}}$, or (2) $E_j$ is delayed from entering $\Omega_{\text{goal}}$ forever unless captured. Winning states form the **pursuit winning region**. Termination for an evader: entering $\Omega_{\text{goal}}$ (evader win) or capture.
- **Decomposition principle (p. 817)**: the multiplayer game is split into many-vs-one subgames $X_{cj} = (X_c, x_{Ej})$, solved separately and fused by task allocation; contrast with [6] which used 1-vs-1 subgames and maximum matching.

### 5.1 Lemma 1 (Expanded-Apollonius pursuit strategy; p. 817–818; due to [9])
For $X_{ij}$ define (eq. (2)) $R_A = \frac{\alpha_{ij}\|x_{Pi}-x_{Ej}\|_2}{\alpha_{ij}^2-1}$, $x_A = \frac{\alpha_{ij}^2 x_{Ej} - x_{Pi}}{\alpha_{ij}^2 - 1}$; Apollonius disk $\mathcal{A} = \{x \mid \|x - x_A\|_2 \le R_A\}$ and expanded disk $\mathcal{A}_\delta = \{x \mid \|x - x_A\|_2 \le R_A + \delta\}$, $\delta > 0$ (eq. (3)). **Statement** (obstacle-free): if $P_i$ adopts $u_{Pi}(\tau) = z(\tau)/\|z(\tau)\|_2$ with $z(\tau) = \alpha_{ij}(R_A(t)+\delta-R_A(\tau))\frac{x_{Ej}(\tau)-x_{Pi}(\tau)}{\|x_{Ej}(\tau)-x_{Pi}(\tau)\|_2} + x_A(\tau) - x_A(t)$, then (i) $\mathcal{A}(\tau) \subset \mathcal{A}_\delta(t)$ for all $\tau \ge t$; (ii) $E_j$ is captured inside $\mathcal{A}_\delta(t)$ in finite time under $r_i = 0$. *Remark 1*: for $r_i > 0$ the exact reachable-difference set is a Cartesian oval (degenerating to the Apollonius circle at $r_i=0$); whether a Lemma-1-type strategy exists for Cartesian ovals is open (left as future work); capture at $r_i=0$ implies capture at $r_i>0$. *Remark 2*: $\delta$ is needed because $P_i$ does not observe $E_j$'s current control, so both could head to different boundary points of $\mathcal{A}$; expanding by $\delta$ lets $P_i$ aim at points it reaches strictly earlier.

### 5.2 Onsite winning region construction and Theorem 1 (p. 818, eqs. (4)–(5))
Tangency construction: $x_{T1}, x_{T2}$ with $\|x_{Tk} - x_A\|_2 = R_A + \delta$ and $(x_{Tk} - x_{Pi})^\top (x_{Tk} - x_A) = 0$, explicitly
$x_{T1} = x_A + \frac{\ell_1 (x_{Pi} - x_A)}{\|x_{Pi}-x_A\|_2^2} + \frac{\ell_2 (x_{Pi}-x_A)^\circ}{\|x_{Pi}-x_A\|_2^2}$, $x_{T2}$ likewise with $-\ell_2$ term, where $x^\circ = [y,-x]^\top$ for $x = [x,y]^\top$, $\ell_1 = (R_A+\delta)^2$, $\ell_2 = (R_A+\delta)\sqrt{\|x_{Pi}-x_A\|_2^2 - \ell_1}$ (verified against rendered p. 818). The **onsite region** is $R^{\text{onsite}}_{ij} = R_{\text{tri}}(x_{Pi}, x_{T1}, x_{T2}) \cup \mathcal{A}_\delta$ (eq. (4)) — the disk plus the triangle of tangent lines from the pursuer.

- **Theorem 1 (Onsite pursuit winning; p. 818, condition (5))**: if at time $t$, $R^{\text{onsite}}_{ij}(t) \cap O = \emptyset$ for all $O \in \mathcal{O}$ and $\mathcal{A}_\delta(t) \cap \Omega_{\text{goal}} = \emptyset$, then the Lemma-1 strategy for all $\tau \ge t$ guarantees capture of $E_j$ inside $\mathcal{A}_\delta(t)$.
  - *Assumptions*: $\alpha_{ij} > 1$; the whole triangle-union obstacle-free; expanded disk disjoint from goal.
  - *Proof approach*: (1) obstacle-free case is Lemma 1, extended from $r_i = 0$ to $r_i \ge 0$ by "larger capture range". (2) With obstacles, it suffices that $x_{Pi}(\tau) \in R^{\text{onsite}}_{ij}(t)$ until capture, since that region is obstacle-free and $\mathcal{A}_\delta(t)$ avoids $\Omega_{\text{goal}}$ (so $E_j$ cannot slip to the goal while confined). (3) Key step: using $x_A(\tau) - x_{Pi}(\tau) = \frac{\alpha_{ij}^2 (x_{Ej}(\tau) - x_{Pi}(\tau))}{\alpha_{ij}^2 - 1}$, the direction $z(\tau)$ is rewritten (eq. (6)) as $u_{Pi}(\tau) = \frac{x^\star_{Pi}(\tau) - x_{Pi}(\tau)}{\|x^\star_{Pi}(\tau) - x_{Pi}(\tau)\|_2}$ where $x^\star_{Pi}(\tau) = x_{Pi}(\tau) + (x_A(\tau) - x_{Pi}(\tau)) + \frac{\|x_A(\tau)-x_{Pi}(\tau)\|_2 (x_A(\tau) - x_A(t))}{\alpha_{ij}(R_A(t)+\delta - R_A(\tau))}$ — i.e., $P_i$ always walks toward the point $x^\star_{Pi}(\tau)$. (4) Decisive inequality: since $\mathcal{A}(\tau) \subset \mathcal{A}_\delta(t)$, $\|x_A(\tau) - x_A(t)\|_2 \le R_A(t) + \delta - R_A(\tau)$, hence $\|x^\star_{Pi}(\tau) - x_A(\tau)\|_2 = \frac{\|x_A(\tau)-x_{Pi}(\tau)\|_2 \|x_A(\tau)-x_A(t)\|_2}{\alpha_{ij}(R_A(t)+\delta-R_A(\tau))} \le \frac{\|x_A(\tau)-x_{Pi}(\tau)\|_2}{\alpha_{ij}} = \frac{\alpha_{ij}\|x_{Pi}(\tau)-x_{Ej}(\tau)\|_2}{\alpha_{ij}^2 - 1} = R_A(\tau)$, so $x^\star_{Pi}(\tau) \in \mathcal{A}(\tau)$: the pursuer always moves toward the *current* Apollonius disk, whose points lie in $R^{\text{onsite}}_{ij}(t)$; with $x_{Pi}(t) \in R^{\text{onsite}}_{ij}(t)$ the straight path never exits the (tangent) region. □
  - *Meaning*: obstacles are irrelevant if the whole tangent wedge + expanded dominance disk is clear — capture occurs "onsite" before the evader can exploit any obstacle. Local (state-dependent) condition; analytic; how obstacles modify the classical picture: the free-space winning condition "start in $\mathcal{A}$-complement dominance" becomes "start with an obstacle-free tangent-neighborhood of $\mathcal{A}_\delta$".

- **Lemma 2 (Onsite winning for coalitions; p. 819)**: if (5) holds for every $i \in c$, then all pursuers running Lemma 1 guarantee capture in $\bigcap_{i \in c} \mathcal{A}_{\delta,i}(t)$. Proof: each $P_i$ alone wins (Theorem 1), hence the coalition wins in the intersection. *Remark 3*: adding pursuers shrinks (or preserves) the guaranteed capture region and shortens capture time; since (5) and the strategy are decoupled across pursuers, the onsite layer is **distributable**. Fig. 2(b) illustrates capture in $\cap_{i=1}^3 \mathcal{A}_{\delta,i}$.

### 5.3 Goal-visibility toolkit (Sec. IV-A, pp. 819–820)
- **Definition 3** (goal-visible point): $x \in \Omega_{\text{free}}$ seeing every $y \in \Omega_{\text{goal}}$ along obstacle-free segments. **Definition 4** (pair of minimum-covering points): $(x_1,x_2) \in \Omega^2_{\text{goal}}$ with $\Omega_{\text{goal}} \subset R_{\text{sec}}(x, x_1, x_2)$; computable by checking vertex pairs $x_1, x_2 \in V_{\text{goal}}$ (convexity). **Lemma 3** (verification): $x$ is goal-visible iff $x \in \Omega_{\text{goal}}$, or ($x \in \Omega_{\text{play}}$ and $R_{\text{tri}}(x,x_1,x_2) \cap O = \emptyset$ for all $O$); proof reduces segment-vs-obstacle tests to the triangle spanned by the covering pair. **Definition 5** (convex GCP): polygon $R$ that is convex, contains $x$, lies in $\Omega_{\text{free}}$, and covers $\Omega_{\text{goal}}$. **Lemma 4** (existence & consistency): a GCP always exists for a goal-visible $x$ (construct $R$ from the vertex set $S \cup \{x, x_1, x_2\}$, where $S$ collects goal vertices outside $R_{\text{tri}}(x,x_1,x_2)$); and every point of a convex GCP is goal-visible — the invariance property that makes strategies sustainable. **Definition 6** (direction range $DR(x) \subseteq [0,2\pi)$) and **Remark 5**: $DR(x) = \mathbb{D}[\theta_L, \theta_U]$ (boundary case) or $[0,2\pi)$ (interior case).

### 5.4 Safe distance and Theorem 2 (Sec. IV-B, pp. 820–821)
- **Definition 7** (evasion region, from [40]): closure $\mathbb{E}(c,j) = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^2 \mid f_{ij}(x, X_{ij}) \ge 0,\ i \in c\}$ (eq. (7)) with $f_{ij}(x, X_{ij}) = \|x - x_{Pi}\|_2 - \alpha_{ij}\|x - x_{Ej}\|_2 - r_i$; bounded, strictly convex; $\mathcal{A}$ is its boundary at $r_i = 0$.
- **Definition 8 (safe distance $\varrho(X_{cj})$; convex program $\mathcal{P}(X_{cj})$, eq. (8))**: minimize $\|x - y\|_2$ over $(x,y) \in \mathbb{R}^2 \times \mathbb{R}^2$ s.t. $f_{ij}(x,X_{ij}) \ge 0\ \forall i \in c$ and $A_m y + b_m \ge 0\ \forall m \in I_{\text{goal}}$; with solution $(x_I, x_G)$: $\varrho = -\infty$ if $x_I = x_G$ and ($f_{ij}(x_I,X_{ij}) > 0\ \forall i$, or $A_m x_I + b_m > 0\ \forall m$) — i.e., the sets overlap — else $\varrho = \|x_I - x_G\|_2$ (signed distance between $\mathbb{E}(c,j)$ and $\Omega_{\text{goal}}$). Note $\varrho$ ignores obstacles (pure geometry of evasion region vs. goal).
- **Lemma 5 (consistent goal-visible pursuit)**: if $x_{Pi}(t)$ is goal-visible and the input keeps heading toward some point of a convex GCP $R_i(\tau)$, i.e., $\theta_i(\tau) \in DR_i(\tau)(x_{Pi}(\tau))$, then $x_{Pi}(\tau)$ stays goal-visible forever. Proof: convexity keeps the next position inside the current GCP; Lemma 4 re-exports a GCP; induction on small $\Delta t$.
- **Theorem 2 (Goal-visible pursuit winning; p. 820–821, eqs. (9)–(11))**: if 1) every $x_{Pi}$ is goal-visible and 2) $\varrho(X_{cj}) \ge 0$, then strategy $u_{Pi}(\tau) = [\cos\theta_i(\tau), \sin\theta_i(\tau)]^\top$ (eq. (9)), $\theta_i(\tau) = \arg\min_{\theta \in DR_i(\tau)(x_{Pi}(\tau))} |\theta - \sigma(x_{Pi}(\tau), x_I(\tau))|$ (eq. (10)) — head toward the closest evasion-region point $x_I$ if it lies in the direction range, otherwise slide along the GCP edge — guarantees $P_c$ wins (capture or eternal delay).
  - *Proof approach*: (1) goal-visibility consistency by Lemma 5. (2) *Case 1* ($\theta^\star_i(\tau) \in DR_i$ for all $i$): strategy becomes $u_{Pi} = \frac{x_I - x_{Pi}}{\|x_I - x_{Pi}\|_2}$ (eq. (11)); uniqueness of $(x_I,x_G)$ from strict convexity of $\mathbb{E}(c,j)$ and convexity of $\Omega_{\text{goal}}$; write the **KKT system (12)**: $0 = \nabla_x d + \sum_i \lambda_i \nabla_x f_{ij}$, $0 = \nabla_y d + \sum_m \lambda_m A_m$, with sign/complementarity conditions; complementary slackness splits $I_{\text{goal}}$ into $I^{=0}_{\text{goal}}$ ($A_m x_G + b_m = 0$, $\lambda_m \le 0$) and $I^{>0}_{\text{goal}}$ ($A_m x_G + b_m > 0$, $\lambda_m = 0$) (eq. (13)). (3) Decisive computation: $\dot\varrho(X_{cj}) = \frac{d}{dt} d(x_I, x_G) = -\sum_{i \in c} \lambda_i \nabla_x^\top f_{ij}(x_I, X_{ij}) \dot x_I$, because the goal terms drop: for $m \in I^{=0}_{\text{goal}}$, $x_G$ stays on the face $A_m x_G + b_m = 0$ so $A_m^\top \dot x_G = 0$; then "following the similar argument in the proof of [40, Th. 3.1]", $\dot\varrho \ge 0$ under (11) — the safe distance never strictly decreases, so $\varrho \ge 0$ invariance holds. (4) *Case 2* (some $\theta^\star_i \notin DR_i$): clamped pursuers may let $\dot\varrho < 0$ temporarily, but goal-visibility implies the direction to any goal point lies in $DR_i$, so before $\mathbb{E}(c,j)$ could penetrate $\operatorname{int}\Omega_{\text{goal}}$ there is $\tau'$ with $\theta^\star_i(\tau') \in DR_i(\tau')$ for all $i$ — returning to Case 1. □
  - *Proof gap (minor)*: Case 2's "there must exist $\tau'$ … before $\mathbb{E}(c,j)$ intersects with the interior of $\Omega_{\text{goal}}$" is argued qualitatively; no explicit bound on the transient $\varrho$ decrease nor a measure-zero-touching argument (touching $\partial\Omega_{\text{goal}}$ with $\varrho = 0$ still satisfies the winning definition via delay, so the argument is plausible but the interchange of limits is not formalized, p. 821).
  - *Remark 6*: adapting [40, Lemma 3.3] (stated in 3D: at most three pursuers) to the plane shows **at most two pursuers** are needed for goal-visible winning — the basis for restricting coalitions to size ≤ 2 in the allocation.
  - *Meaning*: in the goal-visible regime the obstacle complexity is fully absorbed into the direction-range clamp; the pursuers behave like [40]'s obstacle-free guards restricted to a visibility cone. Local, analytic + one convex program (8) per step.

### 5.5 GCP enlargement — Theorem 3 (pp. 822–823, eq. (17))
- **Definition 9 (first-visible obstacle vertices)**: $(y_L, y_U) \in V^2_{\text{obs}}$ minimize the clockwise/counterclockwise angular gap from the covering rays, subject to spans $\le \pi$ and obstacle-free segments (eqs. (15)–(16)); if infeasible, $y_L = 2x - x_U$ (resp. $y_U = 2x - x_L$), mirror points about $x$.
- **Theorem 3 (constructing convex GCPs)**: for goal-visible $x_{Pi}$: if $x_{Pi} \in \Omega_{\text{goal}}$, inflate $\Omega_{\text{goal}}$ slightly along outward normals to get a GCP with $DR = [0, 2\pi)$; if $x_{Pi} \in \Omega_{\text{play}}$, there is a GCP with (eq. (17)) $DR(x_{Pi}) = \mathbb{D}[\theta_L, \theta_U]$ if $|\mathbb{D}[\theta_L,\theta_U]| \le \pi$, else $\mathbb{D}[\theta_L, \theta_L + \pi]$ or $\mathbb{D}[\theta_U - \pi, \theta_U]$, where $\theta_L = \sigma(x_{Pi}, y_L)$, $\theta_U = \sigma(x_{Pi}, y_U)$.
  - *Proof approach*: polygonal case analysis. Case 1 ($\le \pi$): extend the farther goal boundary through $x_L$ to hit $x_{Pi}y_L$ at $x''$; define nested polygons $R_0$ (vertices $x_{Pi}, x_L, x', x_U$ — a natural GCP), $R_1$ ($x_{Pi}, x'', x', x_U$), $R_2$ ($x_{Pi}, x'', x_L$); $R_1 = R_0 \cup R_2$; obstacle-freeness of $R_2$ by contradiction — any obstacle inside would expose a visible vertex with a smaller (15)-objective, and any edge penetrating $x''x_{Pi}$ would contradict $y_L$'s visibility; collinear degeneracy ($x_{Pi}, x_L, x'$) handled by extending $x_U x'$. Adding $y_U$ gives $R_3, R_4$ with $DR_{R_4} = \mathbb{D}[\theta_L, \theta_U]$. Case 2 ($> \pi$): triangles $R_5$ ($x_{Pi}, y_L, x_L$) and expansions yield GCPs with half-plane direction ranges $\mathbb{D}[\theta_L, \theta_L+\pi]$, $\mathbb{D}[\theta_U-\pi, \theta_U]$. □
  - *Purpose*: when $\Omega_{\text{goal}}$ is small, the minimal GCP (eq. (14): $DR = \mathbb{D}[\sigma(x_{Pi},x_1), \sigma(x_{Pi},x_2)]$) has a tiny angle span and Theorem 2 degenerates to hovering at the goal; Theorem 3 buys the maximal provably-safe maneuver range up to $\pi$.

### 5.6 ESP machinery and nongoal-visible winning (Sec. V, pp. 823–825)
- **Definitions 10–12** (from [16]): ESP $\mathcal{P}_{\text{ESP}}(x_1,x_2)$ = minimum-length obstacle-free path; ESP distance $d_{\text{ESP}}$; ESP reachable region $\mathcal{R}_{\text{ESP}}(x,\ell) = \{z \in \Omega_{\text{free}} \mid d_{\text{ESP}}(x,z) \le \ell\}$; wavefront $\mathcal{W}_{\text{ESP}}(x,\ell)$ = points at ESP distance exactly $\ell$.
- **Lemma 6** ([16]): an ESP is an ordered set of obstacle vertices plus endpoints — finite representation. **Lemma 7** ([16]): a nonempty wavefront is a disjoint union of *wavelets* — circular arcs centered at obstacle vertices (or $x$), each a tuple $(y, \epsilon, \theta_1, \theta_2)$. **Remark 8**: wavelets with span $> \pi$ are split in two so that each induces a **convex** circular sector.
- **Definition 14 (circular sectors)**: $R^k_{\text{sec}} = \{z \in \mathbb{R}^2 \mid \|z - y\|_2 \le \epsilon,\ [-\sin\theta_1, \cos\theta_1]^\top z \ge 0,\ [-\sin\theta_2, \cos\theta_2]^\top z \le 0\}$ (as printed, p. 824). *Notation caveat*: the two half-plane tests are printed on $z$, not on $z - y$; since the sector is centered at $y$, the literal formula is presumably a typo for $[-\sin\theta_1,\cos\theta_1]^\top (z - y) \ge 0$ etc. — the proof only uses convexity of $R^k_{\text{sec}}$ and $d_k = \max_{z \in R^k_{\text{sec}}} \|z - s\|_2$.
- **Definition 13 (anchored ESP-based safe distance $\varrho_A(X_{ij}, s)$, eq. (18))**: for a goal-visible obstacle vertex ("anchor") $s \in V^{\text{gv}}_{\text{obs}}$: minimize $\|x - y\|_2$ over $(\bar x, x, y) \in \mathbb{R}^6$ s.t. $\bar x \in \mathcal{R}_{\text{ESP}}(x_{Ej}, d_{\text{ESP}}(x_{Pi}, s)/\alpha_{ij})$, $f_{ij}(x, s, \bar x) \ge 0$ (evasion region for pursuer frozen at $s$, evader at $\bar x$), $A_m y + b_m \ge 0\ \forall m$. *Remark 9 (two-stage semantics)*: stage 1 — $P_i$ walks the ESP to $s$ in time $d_{\text{ESP}}(x_{Pi},s)/v_{Pi}$ while $E_j$ roams anywhere in the (generally **nonconvex**) region $\mathcal{R}_{\text{ESP}}$; stage 2 — $\varrho_A$ = the minimal safe distance over all evader positions $\bar x$ in that region. This is exactly the worst-case guarantee available when $P_i$ arrives at $s$. Solving (18) exactly is a nonconvex program.
- **Lemma 8 (nonnegative anchored safe distance; p. 824, eq. (19))**: let $\mathcal{I}, \mathcal{R}_{\text{sec}}$ be the wavelet index set / sectors of $\mathcal{W}_{\text{ESP}}(x_{Ej}, d_{\text{ESP}}(x_{Pi},s)/\alpha_{ij})$. If 1) $\alpha_{ij} d_{\text{ESP}}(x_{Ej}, x) \ge d_{\text{ESP}}(x_{Pi}, s)$ for all $x \in V_{\text{goal}}$ (evader cannot reach any goal vertex before $P_i$ reaches $s$), and 2) $\min_{k \in \mathcal{I}} J^\star_k \ge 0$, where $J^\star_k$ is the optimal value of the **convex** program: minimize $\|x - y\|_2$ s.t. $\bar x \in R^k_{\text{sec}}$, $A_m y + b_m \ge 0\ \forall m$, $\|x - \bar x\|_2 \le (d_k - r_i)/(\alpha_{ij} - 1)$, $\|x - s\|_2 \le (\alpha_{ij} d_k - r_i)/(\alpha_{ij}-1)$; then $\varrho_A(X_{ij}, s) \ge 0$.
  - *Proof approach*: (1) condition 1 ⇒ for each sector the center's ESP distance to $x_{Ej}$ is $\le d_{\text{ESP}}(x_{Pi},s)/\alpha_{ij}$ (no premature goal entry). (2) Convexity: $R^k_{\text{sec}}$ convex (Remark 8) + convex constraints ⇒ (19) convex. (3) $\cup_k R^k_{\text{sec}} \subseteq \mathcal{R}_{\text{ESP}}(\cdot)$ via Definitions 11/12/14 and Lemma 7. (4) Decisive relaxation: $f_{ij}(x, s, \bar x) \ge 0$ (i.e., $\|x-s\| \ge \alpha_{ij}\|x - \bar x\| + r_i$) is implied by the two looser constraints via the (reverse) triangle inequalities: $\|x - \bar x\|_2 + \|\bar x - s\|_2 - \alpha_{ij}\|x-\bar x\|_2 - r_i \ge 0 \Rightarrow \|x-\bar x\|_2 \le \frac{\|\bar x - s\|_2 - r_i}{\alpha_{ij}-1} \le \frac{d_k - r_i}{\alpha_{ij}-1}$ and $\|x - s\|_2 - \alpha_{ij}(\|x-s\|_2 - \|s - \bar x\|_2) - r_i \ge 0 \Rightarrow \|x-s\|_2 \le \frac{\alpha_{ij}\|s-\bar x\|_2 - r_i}{\alpha_{ij}-1} \le \frac{\alpha_{ij} d_k - r_i}{\alpha_{ij}-1}$; so the feasible set of (19) contains that of the slice of (18), making $J^\star_k$ a lower bound of the slice's true safe distance; a nonnegative bound on every sector yields $\varrho_A \ge 0$. □
  - *Critical observations*: (a) condition 1 tests only goal **vertices** — for a convex polygon the ESP-minimizing entry point can be an edge-interior point, so the vertex check is a practical heuristic (cf. the analogous remark after Lemma 9, p. 825: "we practically check the evasion winning condition (21) over finitely many critical points"); the lemma's proof indeed only uses the vertex statement, so condition 1 as stated is what is proven, but its sufficiency for "no premature goal entry" relies on that heuristic step; (b) the coverage $\cup_k R^k_{\text{sec}} \subseteq \mathcal{R}_{\text{ESP}}$ is printed as a strict subset, while the conclusion over all of $\mathcal{R}_{\text{ESP}}$ tacitly uses equality of the tiling — benign if the wavefront decomposition is exact.
- **Theorem 4 (Nongoal-visible pursuit winning; p. 825, eq. (20))**: if 1) $x_{Pi}$ is not goal-visible and 2) some $s \in V^{\text{gv}}_{\text{obs}}$ has $\varrho_A(X_{ij}, s) \ge 0$, then $P_i$ wins by the two-stage strategy: follow $\mathcal{P}_{\text{ESP}}(x_{Pi}(t), s)$ for $t \le \tau \le t + d_{\text{ESP}}(x_{Pi}(t),s)/v_{Pi}$, then switch to the goal-visible strategy (9). *Proof*: by Definition 13 the safe distance at arrival is nonnegative for any evader behavior; $s$ is goal-visible; apply Theorem 2. One-pursuer statement only (no coalition version); Algorithm 2 accordingly tests Theorem 4 for $|c| = 1$.

### 5.7 Multiplayer fusion (Sec. VI, pp. 825–826)
- **Lemma 9 (ESP-based evasion winning; eq. (21))**: if some $x \in \Omega_{\text{goal}}$ satisfies $d_{\text{ESP}}(x_{Pi}, x) - r_i > \alpha_{ij} d_{\text{ESP}}(x_{Ej}, x)$, then $E_j$ reaches $\Omega_{\text{goal}}$ before capture by moving along $\mathcal{P}_{\text{ESP}}(x_{Ej}, x)$. *Proof by contradiction*: assume capture at $\tau' \le t + d^E_{\text{ESP}}/v_{Ej}$ under some $u'_{Pi}$; visual capture ⇒ the segment $x_{Pi}(\tau')x_{Ej}(\tau')$ is obstacle-free, so $P_i$ has an obstacle-free path $P'$ (go to $x_{Pi}(\tau')$, jump the free segment, then follow the tail of the evader's ESP to $x$) with length chain: $d(P') - d^P_{\text{ESP}} \le v_{Pi}(\tau'-t) + r_i + d^E_{\text{ESP}} - v_{Ej}(\tau'-t) - d^P_{\text{ESP}} < (1 - 1/\alpha_{ij}) v_{Pi}(\tau'-t) + r_i + (d^P_{\text{ESP}} - r_i)/\alpha_{ij} - d^P_{\text{ESP}} = (1-1/\alpha_{ij})(v_{Pi}(\tau'-t) + r_i - d^P_{\text{ESP}}) \le (1-1/\alpha_{ij})(v_{Pi} d^E_{\text{ESP}}/v_{Ej} + r_i - d^P_{\text{ESP}}) < 0$, using (21) at the second and fourth inequalities — contradicting ESP optimality of $d^P_{\text{ESP}}$. Practical check: (21) is verified only at finitely many critical points of $\Omega_{\text{goal}}$ (e.g., vertices) — an acknowledged heuristic sufficiency.
- **Definition 15 (ESP-pure pursuit strategy)**: head along the ESP toward the evader.
- **Algorithm 1 (MOCG, receding horizon with step $\Delta$)** — four sequential matchings per iteration:
  1. *Capture matching*: bipartite graph $G$ with $V_P$ = all nonempty coalitions of size ≤ 2 (justified by Remarks 3, 6 and the single-pursuer Theorem 4), $V_E$ = live evaders; edge $e_{cj}$ iff `Check_pursuit_winning` (Algorithm 2) returns $T_{cj} \ge 1$ ($T = 1$: onsite, $T = 2$: goal-visible, $T = 3$: nongoal-visible; $T = 0$ none). Maximizing defeated evaders = **BIP (22)**: $\max \sum_{e_{cj} \in E} a_{cj}$ s.t. $\sum_{P_c \in V_P} a_{cj} \le 1\ \forall E_j$, $\sum_{E_j \in V_E} a_{cj} \le 1\ \forall P_c$, and $a_{cj} + a_{pq} \le 1$ for every conflicting pair $(e_{cj}, e_{pq}) \in \bar E$ of the conflict graph $C$ (edges share a pursuer). Matched pursuers run Lemma 1/2 ($T=1$), Theorem 2 ($T=2$), or (20) ($T=3$). The best matching so far is kept ($M_{\text{defeat}}$).
  2. *Enhanced matching*: extra unassigned pursuers with onsite wins join an assigned evader (shrinks the capture region, Remark 3).
  3. *Nondominated matching*: on $\bar G$, edge iff the evader cannot certify evasion winning (21); a maximum bipartite matching (polynomial time, e.g., max-flow [10]); matched pursuers use ESP-pure pursuit — "no pursuer is assigned to an evader that it cannot win against".
  4. *Closest matching*: leftover pursuers chase the closest unmatched evader (or closest evader) via ESP-pure pursuit.
- **Algorithm 2 (`Check_pursuit_winning`)**: $T_{cj} = 1$ if (5) holds for all $i \in c$; else $T_{cj} = 2$ if Theorem 2's conditions hold; else $T_{cj} = 3$ if $|c|=1$ and Theorem 4's conditions hold; else 0.
- **Theorem 5 (increasing lower bound; p. 826)**: under MOCG, the pursuit team guarantees at least $|E_{\text{capture}}| + |M_{\text{defeat}}|$ simultaneously defeated evaders at every iteration, regardless of evader strategy, and this number is nondecreasing over iterations. *Proof*: one line — inheritance from Theorems 1, 2, 4 plus the update rule "the matching changes only if the new one defeats more evaders". *Observation*: the bound's strict increase is not guaranteed (it is "increasing" in the non-strict sense), and Theorem 5 gives no capture-time bound; the delay-mode win is also not decidable in finite time (eternal delay is an asymptotic certificate).

### 5.8 Summary of quantitative objects
All winning tests are closed-form (onsite: tangent geometry; goal-visible: convex QP-like distance program (8)/(19) with second-order-cone-type constraints; ESP distances by [16]) — no discretization anywhere; the only combinatorial step is the BIP (22) over $\le 2$-size coalitions. Dynamics (1); winning conditions per Definition 1; capture per visual-radius condition (Sec. II-A); termination: capture or goal entry; cost: none — this is a qualitative game, no payoff functional is optimized (maximizing the count of guaranteed wins is the objective).

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
- **Author-stated** (p. 828): future work on distributed games, games with limited visible areas, and reconnaissance games with obstacles [20]; Remark 1: extension of Lemma 1 to Cartesian ovals for $r_i > 0$ "is not straightforward, and we leave it for future work"; the onsite winning targets only "scenarios where players are close to each other with no obstacles nearby".
- **Heuristic checks presented as conditions**: evasion-winning test (21) and Lemma 8's condition 1) are verified only at finitely many critical points (goal vertices), although the minima of ESP distance to a convex polygon need not be at vertices — the papers' own wording ("practically check", "sufficient and efficient method", p. 825) concedes this; a formal version would need edge-projection checks.
- **Theorem 2, Case 2** (p. 821): the return-to-Case-1 argument ("there must exist $\tau'$") is qualitative — no bound on the transient drop of $\varrho$, no handling of grazing contact $\varrho = 0$; a proof gap in rigor, not necessarily in truth.
- **Conservatism**: all three winnings are sufficient conditions — the onsite region requires the entire tangent triangle obstacle-free; the nongoal-visible test freezes the pursuer at the anchor for the worst case (eq. (18)) and then relaxes further (eq. (19)); the true winning regions are certainly larger, and no completeness/characterization claim is made (contrast: winning regions [17]-style). Winning Definition 1's "delay forever" mode has no finite-time verification.
- **Coalition restriction**: capture matching considers only $|c| \le 2$; Theorem 4 is single-pursuer; the claim of completeness of this restriction leans on Remarks 3/6 (which adapt [40, Lemma 3.3] without reproof) — for obstacle environments with visibility constraints this transplants the obstacle-free argument and is not independently justified here.
- **Centralization**: Remark 3 notes the onsite layer is distributed, but the BIP (22), the conflict graph, and the hierarchical allocation are centralized; no communication/consensus analysis, despite "distributed games" being future work.
- **Model scope**: simple motion, exact positions, perfect map knowledge, no sensing noise, no dynamic obstacles; evader capture requires line-of-sight (visual radius), so obstacles can also hide evaders — exploited by neither strategy explicitly beyond the winning-region conditions.
- **Experimental gap**: no quantitative evaluation (runtime, bound tightness, capture times, comparison with HJ or [6]); all parameters unreported, hindering reproduction.

## 8. Citation Value
This is the reference point for **reach-avoid games under geometric obstacles via computational geometry**: it is the first TAC-level work to construct obstacle-aware pursuit winning regions (expanded Apollonius + tangent-wedge onsite region; GCP-based goal guarding; ESP-anchored safe distance) and to fuse many-vs-one coalition outcomes into a hierarchical receding-horizon allocation with a monotone defeat-count guarantee. For a new paper it serves as (i) the canonical citation for "winning strategies/winning regions for reach-avoid games with obstacles" alongside the obstacle-free matching literature [6], [40]; (ii) the source of reusable tools — convex goal-covering polygons, first-visible obstacle vertices, direction-range clamping, anchored ESP-based safe distance — for any obstacle-laden pursuit, target-defense, or perimeter-defense model; and (iii) the natural baseline (plus its admitted heuristics) for learning-based or distributed obstacle reach-avoid approaches. Suggested citation sentences: "Winning regions and pursuit strategies for reach-avoid games with polygonal obstacles were constructed in [P106] via expanded Apollonius circles, convex goal-covering polygons, and Euclidean shortest paths, yielding a receding-horizon multiplayer strategy whose guaranteed number of defeated evaders is nondecreasing [P106]." or "Unlike [P106], which requires the entire tangent wedge of the Apollonius disk to be obstacle-free, we …".
