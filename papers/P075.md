## 2. Research Background
Multiplayer reach-avoid differential games (MRADG) model conflicts where a pursuer team must intercept all evaders before any evader reaches a target set; applications span autonomous robots, air traffic management, military operations and network security (p. 81, refs. 1–4). Moving the analysis from 2D to 3D matters practically (aerial vehicles operate in 3D) but explosively increases analytic difficulty: the state space grows to 3(N+M), HJI-based numerical methods suffer the curse of dimensionality and become "unfeasible" in 3D (p. 81–82), and geometric tools (Apollonius circle, Voronoi diagrams) had been developed almost exclusively for 2D planes (p. 82, refs. 11–14). Prior 3D work was limited — e.g., Yan et al.'s evasion-space (ES) concept for 3D heterogeneous MRADG (ref. 19) provided no analytic barrier or closed-form optimal strategies (authors' characterization). The paper positions itself as the first to derive closed-form (analytic) barrier surfaces for 3D reach-avoid games with multiple players, up to two-pursuer-one-evader cooperation.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
**Problem.** N pursuers vs M evaders with simple motion in R³, heterogeneous speeds, target plane = xy-plane. Evader team wins if ≥1 evader reaches the plane; pursuer team wins only if ALL evaders are captured. Three questions are posed explicitly (p. 83–84): Problem 1 (game of kind): determine the winner and winning conditions from initial positions; Problem 2 (game of degree): for x ∈ W_P, what strategy guarantees the pursuer's win and with what value; Problem 3: solve the assignment α_ij to maximize payoff.
**Gap [authors' claim]:** numerical HJI methods (level set, ref. 6; path-defense approximation, ref. 7) cannot scale beyond 2 players nor to 3D; geometric methods (Apollonius circle, Voronoi, dominance regions, refs. 11–14) are 2D-only; 2D MRADG solutions exist (Chen et al. ref. 17 — pairwise outcomes but "no cooperation between the pursuers"; Garcia et al. ref. 18 — Apollonius-circle assignment but "requires enumerating all feasible assignments, leading to extreme computational complexity"); Yan et al. ref. 19 studied 3D MRADG with an ES concept but "did not provide analytical solutions for barriers or optimal strategies"; hence "analytical solutions are notably absent" in 3D (refs. 20, 21). 
**Verified from cited text:** the 2D characterizations of refs. 17/18 are accurate (ref. 17's pairwise-outcome reduction indeed sidesteps pursuer cooperation; ref. 18's assignment does enumerate pairwise outcomes); ref. 19 (Yan et al., Automatica 2022) indeed delivers matching-based capture conditions and assignment but no closed-form 3D barrier surface, so the stated gap — no analytic 3D barrier for even the 2-vs-1 cooperative case — is real. The claimed novelty is scoped to 3D analytic barriers; note the authors' own prior work (ref. 22, Ruan et al., Research 2023, Harris's-hawks-inspired 3D MRADG) is cited as the origin of the Apollonius-sphere idea ("we innovatively introduce the concept of the Apollonius sphere ... to solve the proposed problem.22", p. 84), so the concept itself is not new here — the new part is the closed-form two-sphere-intersection barrier and the barrier-measure-based assignment.

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Theory (game of kind):** introduction of the **Apollonius sphere** in 3D; closed-form optimal interception points (OIP) and **analytic barrier expressions for 1-vs-1 (eq. (13)) and 2-vs-1 (Theorem 2, eqs. (15)–(17))**, claimed as "the first successful derivation of the analytical solution in 3D space" (abstract, p. 81).
2. **Theory (game of degree):** optimal feedback headings for pursuers and evaders inside the pursuer winning region W_P (eq. (32)), plus Theorem 1 (straight-line optimal trajectories via PMP, eqs. (9)–(10)). The contributions list additionally claims "the proof that the value function is continuously differentiable and satisfies the HJI partial differential equations is presented" (p. 82) — **not substantiated in the paper body** (see §7).
3. **Algorithm:** the optimal assignment is posed as maximization of a barrier-measure sum Θ_ρ(x) (eq. (33)) over feasible assignments and solved by a **two-level Hungarian (bipartite maximum matching) approximation algorithm** on a graph of pursuer *groups* (singletons and pairs) vs evaders, avoiding enumeration of all feasible assignments (contrast with ref. 18).
4. **Experiment:** numerical 3-vs-3 and 6-vs-4 simulations with ablation over {optimal, nonoptimal} control × assignment for each side (Figs. 5–6), showing any deviation from optimality degrades the deviator's outcome.

## 5. Key Mathematical Results

### 5.1 Model, information structure, termination (pp. 82–83)
- **Dynamics (eq. (1)), simple motion in R³:** ẋ_Pi = V_Pi u_Pi, ẏ_Pi = V_Pi v_Pi, ż_Pi = V_Pi w_Pi; ẋ_Ej = V_Ej u_Ej, ẏ_Ej = V_Ej v_Ej, ż_Ej = V_Ej w_Ej. Controls are instantaneous headings u_Pi = (u,v,w)_Pi ∈ R³ (similarly u_Ej); players are holonomic; global state x = {x_Pi, x_Ej} ∈ R^{3(N+M)}. Speeds V_Pi > 0, V_Ej > 0, heterogeneous allowed.
- **Assumption 1 (p. 83):** every pursuer faster than every evader: V_Pi > V_Ej; speed ratio γ_ij = V_Ej/V_Pi < 1.
- **Information structure (p. 83):** each player knows only opponents' positions and speeds (not controls); strategies are state-feedback based. Assignment binary α_ij = 1 iff P_i is assigned to E_j; one pursuer per evader; **pursuers firmly commit to their initial assignment without changing**. Point capture: ‖x_Pi − x_Ej‖₂ = 0.
- **Terminal set (eqs. (2)–(4)):** D := D_P ∪ D_E; D_P = {x : x_Pi = x_Ej, ∀j, ∃i, α_ij = 1} (all evaders captured); D_E = {x : z_Ej = 0, ∃j} (some evader on the target plane). Winning rules: evader team wins iff one evader reaches the plane; pursuer team wins iff all evaders are captured.
- **Game of kind (eqs. (5)–(6)):** barrier surface B := {x : B(x) = 0}; winning regions W_P := {x : B(x) > 0}, W_E := {x : B(x) < 0}.
- **Game of degree (eqs. (7)–(8)), Mayer payoff:** J(u_P, u_E, x_0) := Σ_{j=1}^M z_Ej(t_f) (sum of evaders' terminal heights above the plane — the threat posed to the plane), and V(x_0) := max_{u_P} min_{u_E} J. So pursuers maximize total terminal height of evaders; evaders minimize it.

### 5.2 Theorem 1 (p. 83, eqs. (9)–(10)) — straight-line optimality
- **Statement.** For MRADG (1)–(8), the optimal headings of all players are constant and the optimal trajectories are straight lines.
- **Proof approach (PMP).** Hamiltonian (9): H = Σ_i V_Pi(λ^x_Pi u_Pi + λ^y_Pi v_Pi + λ^z_Pi w_Pi) + Σ_j V_Ej(λ^x_Ej u_Ej + λ^y_Ej v_Ej + λ^z_Ej w_Ej), costate λ := (λ^x_Pi, λ^y_Pi, λ^z_Pi, λ^x_Ej, λ^y_Ej, λ^z_Ej) ∈ R^{3(N+M)}. Stationarity under min_{u_E} max_{u_P} H gives (10): u*_Pi = (λ^x_Pi, λ^y_Pi, λ^z_Pi)/‖λ_Pi‖ and u*_Ej = −(λ^x_Ej, λ^y_Ej, λ^z_Ej)/‖λ_Ej‖. Costate dynamics λ̇ = −∂H/∂x = 0 (H has no explicit x-dependence under simple motion) ⇒ λ constant ⇒ headings constant ⇒ straight-line trajectories.
- **Decisive step:** eq. (10) (costate-normalized headings) + λ̇ = 0.
- **Nature/locality:** analytic, global within each assigned pair's engagement; physical meaning: with simple motion and Mayer-type payoff, no player ever benefits from turning.
- **Proof gap (minor, p. 83):** the argument is the standard informal Isaacs/PMP reasoning: the saddle condition min_{u_E} max_{u_P} H is asserted rather than verified, no transversality conditions at the terminal manifold (capture or plane arrival) are analyzed, and the single terminal time t_f in (7) is shared across pairs whose capture times differ; none of this is formalized. Also the max–min order in (8) vs the min–max in the proof is not reconciled (no existence/saddle-value argument).

### 5.3 Apollonius sphere, OIP, 1-vs-1 barrier (p. 84, eqs. (11)–(14))
- **Apollonius sphere S_ij of (P_i, E_j) (eqs. (11)–(12)):** center C_ij = (x_Ej − γ²_ij x_Pi, y_Ej − γ²_ij y_Pi, z_Ej − γ²_ij z_Pi)/(1 − γ²_ij); radius R_ij = γ_ij ‖x_Pi − x_Ej‖₂/(1 − γ²_ij). The closed ball bounded by S_ij is E_j's safe region: points that E_j reaches no later than P_i (equivalently ‖X − x_Ej‖ ≤ γ_ij‖X − x_Pi‖); "The region enclosed within the Apollonius sphere is the safe region of E_j, where E_j is guaranteed not to be captured regardless of P_i's best efforts" (p. 84). This is the exact 3D lift of the planar Apollonius circle.
- **OIP (eq. (14)):** I*_ij = (x*_ij, y*_ij, z*_ij) = (x_cij, y_cij, z_cij − R_ij) — the lowest point of S_ij (the capture point realizing the best possible outcome for the evader / worst guaranteed outcome for the pursuer).
- **1-vs-1 barrier (eq. (13)):** B(x) = z_cij − R_ij, i.e., the z-coordinate of I*_ij. Meaning: if the whole safe sphere lies strictly above the plane (B > 0), any evader path to the plane must exit the sphere and is intercepted above the plane ⇒ W_P = {B > 0}, W_E = {B < 0} via (5)–(6); on B, capture occurs exactly on the plane (tangency of S_ij with z = 0). Value of the 1-vs-1 game = B (summand of (7)); no formal theorem is stated for this value equality, it is implicit in the strategy construction.

### 5.4 Theorem 2 (p. 84, eqs. (15)–(18); proof pp. 84–86, eqs. (19)–(31)) — the 2-vs-1 analytic barrier (core result)
- **Statement.** Consider two pursuers (P_i1, P_i2) vs one evader E_j under (1)–(8). Let Υ denote the boundary of the intersection region (spatial convex lens) of the two Apollonius spheres S_i1j and S_i2j — the lens is E_j's safe region against both pursuers. Let I*_i1j, I*_i2j be the single-pursuer OIPs (14) and I*_Υ the OIP on Υ. Then, with B: R⁹ → R:
  - If (I*_i1j, I*_i2j) ∉ Υ, the barrier is **B(x) = C^z_o − r_o Φ** (eq. (15)), where
    C^z_o = [(R²_i2j + D² − R²_i1j)/(2D²)] z_ci1j + [(R²_i1j + D² − R²_i2j)/(2D²)] z_ci2j,
    D = ‖C_i1j − C_i2j‖₂ = sqrt((x_ci1j − x_ci2j)² + (y_ci1j − y_ci2j)² + (z_ci1j − z_ci2j)²),
    r_o = sqrt(4D²R²_i1j − (R²_i1j + D² − R²_i2j)²)/(2D),
    Φ = sqrt((x_ci1j − x_ci2j)² + (y_ci1j − y_ci2j)²)/D,
    with C's and R's from (11)–(12).
  - If I*_i1j ∈ Υ: **B(x) = z_ci1j − R_i1j** (eq. (16)); if I*_i2j ∈ Υ: **B(x) = z_ci2j − R_i2j** (eq. (17)).
  Barrier surface and winning regions then follow from (5)–(6).
- **Geometry (proof, pp. 84–86).** The two spheres intersect in a spatial circle C with center C_0 and radius r_0; E_j's safe region is the lens whose boundary Υ is made of the two spherical caps seamed at C. When neither single-pursuer OIP lies on Υ, the lowest point of Υ is the lowest point of C, taken as OIP I*_Υ = (x*_Υ, y*_Υ, z*_Υ) (eq. (18)): x*_Υ = C^x_o + (r_o/D)(z_ci2j − z_ci1j)(x_ci2j − x_ci1j)/sqrt((x_ci1j − x_ci2j)² + (y_ci1j − y_ci2j)²); y*_Υ analogous with (y_ci2j − y_ci1j); z*_Υ = C^z_o − r_o Φ. At I*_Υ the evader is simultaneously captured by both pursuers (simultaneous arrival on both Apollonius spheres).
- **Decisive intermediate steps (all printed):** (i) circle radius (19): r_0 = R_i1j sqrt(1 − cos²∠AC_i1j C_i2j) = sqrt(4D²R²_i1j − (R²_i1j + D² − R²_i2j)²)/(2D); (ii) circle center (20): C_0 = C_i1j + [(R²_i1j + D² − R²_i2j)/(2D²)](C_i2j − C_i1j), substituted with (11) to get (C^x_o, C^y_o, C^z_o) (21); (iii) subtracting the sphere equations (22)–(23) yields the radical plane of C (24) with normal n = (n1, n2, n3) = C_i2j − C_i1j, ‖n‖₂ = D (25); (iv) n cannot be parallel to the z-axis in the current case, because then x_ci1j = x_ci2j and y_ci1j = y_ci2j and necessarily I*_i1j ∈ Υ or I*_i2j ∈ Υ, contradicting the case assumption (p. 85); (v) with z_0 = (0,0,1) and r_0 the vector C_0 → I*_Υ (coplanar with n, z_0): cos φ = (n·z_0)/(‖n‖₂‖z_0‖₂) (26) and sin φ = sqrt((‖n‖₂² − n3²)/‖n‖₂²) = sqrt((x_ci1j − x_ci2j)² + (y_ci1j − y_ci2j)²)/D, so **sin φ = Φ** (27); the projection of r_0 onto the z-axis is r_oΦ, giving z*_Υ = C^z_o − r_oΦ (28); (vi) the tilt angle θ of the (⊥xy) plane containing r_0, z_0, n: [cos θ, sin θ] = [n1, n2]/sqrt(n1² + n2²) (29), projections x_lx = r_0 cos φ cos θ = r_0 n3n1/(‖n‖₂ sqrt(n1² + n2²)) (30) and y_lx = r_0 cos φ sin θ = r_0 n3n2/(‖n‖₂ sqrt(n1² + n2²)) (31); assembling x*_Υ = C^x_o + x_lx, y*_Υ = C^y_o + y_lx yields (18). Toolset: elementary spatial geometry + vector algebra; purely analytic, closed-form, global in the 9-dimensional state (it is a static geometric function of the current positions, hence usable as feedback barrier).
- **Cap-dominated cases (p. 86).** If I*_i1j ∈ Υ, then I*_i1j lies below the seam circle and is the OIP: E_j is captured by P_i1 at I*_i1j, "the game is transformed into the one vs one case", barrier (16); symmetrically (17). Physical meaning: when one pursuer's Apollonius ball engulfs the other's lowest point, the weaker (higher) constraint is irrelevant and the pair behaves as a single pursuer.
- **Extensibility/verification notes.** (a) The three cases are exhaustive: if neither OIP is on Υ, the lens's lowest point provably lies on the seam circle (both caps are "cut" above their sphere's lowest point); if a ball is nested in the other (no proper intersection), the inner sphere's OIP lies on Υ and (16)/(17) apply. (b) The simultaneous case I*_i1j ∈ Υ AND I*_i2j ∈ Υ is nongeneric: it forces Δ_xy = 0 and R_i2j = R_i1j + Δz (internally tangent at the common lowest point) — the paper does not discuss this degeneracy (**proof gap: Theorem 2 never states the circle-existence condition |R_i1j − R_i2j| < D < R_i1j + R_i2j under which r_o in (15) is real**, p. 84–85). (c) Independently checked: for vertically aligned centers, the higher sphere's OIP is always inside the lower ball, confirming the paper's claim used at step (iv). (d) The value interpretation V = Σ_j B under the chosen assignment is used implicitly (weights, Fig. 4) but never proven as a formal theorem.
- **Optimal strategies (Section 3.2, p. 86, eq. (32)).** For x ∈ W_P: u*_Pi = (I*_O − x_Pi)/‖I*_O − x_Pi‖₂, u*_Ej = (I*_O − x_Ej)/‖I*_O − x_Ej‖₂, where I*_O = I*_ij for P_i vs E_j and I*_O = I*_Υ for (P_i1, P_i2) vs E_j. Both opponents head straight to the OIP: this is the saddle configuration — the pursuer guarantees capture at a point with z ≥ B (any point of the closed safe region is at height ≥ its lowest point), and the evader cannot be captured below B (the OIP is the lowest point of its safe-region boundary, its best reachable terminal height). No separate theorem is stated; correctness rests on Theorems 1–2 and the geometry above.

### 5.5 Optimal assignment (Section 3.2, p. 86, eq. (33) + steps (1)–(4))
- **Objective (eq. (33)):** for each feasible assignment Λ_ρ (ρ = 1, …, ρ̄; ρ̄ = number of feasible assignments, i.e., those in which "the pursuers can successfully capture evaders"), Θ_ρ(x) = Σ_{j=1}^M α^ρ_ij 𝒵_ij(x), where 𝒵_ij(x) = I*_ij if P_i captures E_j and 𝒵_ij(x) = I*_Υ if (P_i1, P_i2) capture E_j (as printed, 𝒵_ij is the OIP **point**; the scalar intended is evidently the barrier value z(I*_ij), i.e., (13)/(15) — see §7). Optimal assignment Λ*_ρ = arg max_ρ Θ_ρ(x): maximize the total barrier height = the pursuer-winning value Σ_j z_Ej(t_f).
- **Algorithm (approximate, avoids enumeration).** (1) Build weighted undirected bipartite graph G = (U_p ∪ U_e, E): U_p = pursuer groups P¹_c (singletons) and P²_c (pairs), |U_p| = N̂; U_e = evaders, |U_e| = M; edge e_cj exists iff group P_c can cooperatively capture E_j and "the capture is not guaranteed in the absence of any individual pursuer within P_c" (genuinely cooperative pairs only); edge weight ω_cj = (13) if |P_c| = 1, (15) if |P_c| = 2. (2) First-level subgraph G¹ = (U¹_p ∪ U_e, E¹) with the single-pursuer groups; Hungarian algorithm → maximum(-weight) matching A_1; matched sets L¹_p, L¹_e; L̄¹_p := vertex set containing L¹_p. (3) Second-level subgraph G² = (U²_p ∪ U²_e, E²) with U²_p = {P¹_c \ L¹_p; P²_c \ L̄¹_p} (unmatched singletons plus pairs not using matched pursuers) and U²_e = U_e \ L¹_e; Hungarian → A_2. (4) Output A_1 ∪ A_2. Handles equal or unequal team sizes.
- **Status:** presented as an approximation to reduce computational burden (contribution (3), p. 82); no approximation-ratio or global-optimality guarantee is given for the two-level greedy ordering (singles first). Feasibility gate in implementation (Fig. 4, p. 87): a grouping is kept only if B(x) > 0; otherwise "discard this grouping and recalculate the other pursuer-evader pairs".

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
**Author-stated:** none explicitly beyond scope; the conclusion (p. 90) claims effectiveness/robustness and "lays a foundation for future research" without listing limitations.
**Critical observations (with page/eq):**
1. **Overclaimed HJI result:** contribution (2) states "the proof that the value function is continuously differentiable and satisfies the HJI partial differential equations is presented" (p. 82) — no such proof exists in the body; Section 3.2 only gives strategy formulas (32) and the assignment. The barrier is derived geometrically; C¹-smoothness of V and HJI satisfaction are never established (and smoothness fails on the barrier itself by construction).
2. **Theorem 1 informality (p. 83):** PMP argument without transversality analysis on D_P ∪ D_E (eqs. (3)–(4)); Isaacs saddle condition asserted; common t_f in (7) glosses over pairwise capture times.
3. **Theorem 2 completeness (pp. 84–86):** the intersection-circle existence condition |R_i1j − R_i2j| < D < R_i1j + R_i2j (needed for real r_o, Φ in (15)) is not stated; the nongeneric double-tangency case (both OIPs on Υ) is not discussed; nested-ball geometry is handled silently by (16)/(17).
4. **Notational gap (p. 86, eq. (33)):** 𝒵_ij(x) is defined as the OIP point I*_ij (a vector in R³) yet Θ_ρ(x) = Σ α^ρ_ij 𝒵_ij(x) is maximized over ρ; the intended scalar is evidently the barrier value z(I*_ij) — consistent with the edge weights ω_cj "given by (13)/(15)" — but this is never written explicitly.
5. **Cooperation capped at two pursuers:** the group set U_p contains only singletons P¹_c and pairs P²_c (p. 86); coalitions of ≥3 pursuers per evader are excluded, so "N pursuers vs M evaders" is really a pairwise/2-vs-1 decomposition.
6. **Assignment is heuristic:** the two-level Hungarian scheme (singles matched first) has no optimality or approximation guarantee for the group-to-evader weighted matching; feasibility depends on the B(x) > 0 gate (Fig. 4, p. 87).
7. **Open-loop vs feedback tension:** Theorem 1 proves straight-line (open-loop) optimality and pursuers "firmly commit to their initial assignment" (p. 83), yet the implementation "constantly updates the OIP" (p. 90, Fig. 6(b)) — i.e., a receding-horizon feedback law whose equivalence to the stated value is not analyzed; no reassignment/role-switch dynamics are permitted.
8. **Simulation reporting:** speeds, γ_ij, platform, integrator, time step, runtimes all UNREPORTED (pp. 86–90); results are single deterministic runs (no Monte Carlo, no noise, no delays); baselines are generic (pure pursuit, potential field) rather than state-of-the-art 3D MRADG methods (refs. 18, 19, 21).
9. **Minor:** refs. 8–10 (coauthor's invariant-ellipsoid/homogeneous-control papers) are cited under "Geometric approaches employ various tools" (p. 82) with little thematic fit; ref. 5 printed as "Inter. Game Theory Rev. 12(1), 1–17 (2021)" (volume/year mismatch suspicious, recorded as printed).
