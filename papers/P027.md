## 2. Research Background

The paper studies a three-role reach-avoid game among an evader E, a target T, and (M−2) pursuers P_i, where the target and pursuers form a cooperative TP coalition: the target acts as a **bait** that flees the evader while moving toward the pursuers, and the pursuers approach the target so as to intercept the evader that chases it (p. 2). The authors motivate this by protection scenarios — repelling birds/malicious drones near airports and repelling malicious flying vehicles in crowds (p. 1). The distinguishing feature is the **asymmetric information structure**: the TP coalition observes the evader only through noisy measurements and holds uncertain state information, whereas the evader observes the TP coalition perfectly (Assumption 2, p. 4). Because decisions are generated from observation information, each coalition effectively plays its own **subjective game** built from what it perceives, so the two coalitions may converge to *different* NEs; the paper makes this information gap explicit and quantifies it (p. 2, Section 5.1.2). Methodologically the work sits at the intersection of receding-horizon game-theoretic planning (IBR methods [11]–[13]), stochastic/chance-constrained MPC ([18]–[20]), and distributed optimization via ADMM ([22]–[24]), and it is the direct forerunner of the same group's journal extension on asymmetric-observation reach-avoid differential games with geometric safe-region tools (P028, IEEE TASE 2025).

## 3. Research Problem and Gap

**Problem.** Design a game-theoretic, real-time, cooperative path-planning algorithm for the TP coalition (1 target + M−2 pursuers, all double-integrator players in 3D) that captures an evader with superior information, i.e., computes TP trajectories that are robust to the evader's state uncertainty while the evader plays its own perfect-information strategy. Termination: TP wins when a pursuer is within capture distance r_p of the evader; the evader wins when it is within attack distance r_e of the target and not captured (p. 2). Both coalitions solve receding-horizon optimization problems (6)–(7), and each constructs a subjective game: G1 = ({J_q}_{q∈V\{E}}, J_E) for the evader (perfect information), G2 = ({J_q}_{q∈V\{E}}, Ĵ_E) for the TP coalition, where Ĵ_E is the TP's *perceived* evader problem built from filtered information (Table 1, pp. 3–4).

**Gap, as stated by the authors (with verification status):**
- [Authors' claim, p. 1–2]: perfect-information RAG solutions are either nongame-theoretic (proportional navigation [5], [6], LOS guidance [7], MPC [8]) — which "do not consider players as intelligent decision-makers" — or game-theoretic (geometric partitions [9], zero-sum multiplayer formulations [10], differential games in general), but "differential game methods cannot handle inequality constraints unless they are incorporated as soft constraints in the objective function" and are "numerically intractable for high dimensional problem" — **authors' characterization of the field; the inequality-constraint criticism is a known HJI/analytical-solution limitation and is consistent with the cited geometric works, though it is stated without proof**.
- [Authors' claim, p. 2]: prior imperfect-observation RAG works (entropy/stochastic-temporal belief [14], noisy interception point [15], KF + sensitivity-enhanced IBR [16]) "do not investigate the impact of players' observation abilities on their subjective games and decision outcomes" — **authors' claim; plausible for [14]–[16] as summarized; the paper itself substantiates the gap empirically via the σ_θ-sweep Monte Carlo study (Figures 4–5), not theoretically**.
- [Authors' claim, p. 2]: "Due to differences in observation abilities and lack of communication between opposing players, it is possible that they may not have the same NE" — **this is the paper's conceptual gap statement; it is validated only by simulation statistics in Section 5.1.2 (no formal proof of subjective-NE divergence is given)**.
- [Authors' claim, p. 6]: existing NE-seeking methods "require convexity and regularity conditions, which is not met by our problem, and are too computationally intensive for online implementation" [21] — **authors' claim; consistent with the fact that their problem (8) is nonconvex due to collision avoidance (5), which they themselves state on p. 7**.

## 4. Core Contributions

1. **Modeling — asymmetric-information multiplayer RAG with a bait role** (Sections 2–3, pp. 2–6): a 3D reach-avoid game with a TP coalition (target + pursuers) vs. one evader; the TP coalition has imperfect observation of the evader while the evader has perfect observation; each coalition solves its own subjective game (G1 perfect-information for the evader, G2 imperfect-information for the TP coalition).
2. **Imperfect-observation mechanism — virtual evader with chance constraints** (Section 3.2, pp. 5–6): the TP coalition runs a Kalman filter on the evader, forms a *virtual evader* whose state is the filtered mean plus a Gaussian perturbation with the posterior covariance, and predicts its trajectory via a chance-constrained optimization problem whose feasible set is *inflated* relative to the actual evader's constraints (eqs. (11)–(19)), so the TP's counter-strategy is robust to estimation uncertainty; scenario programming converts the chance constraint into a deterministic sampled problem with the sample-complexity guarantee of Lemma 1 [20].
3. **Cooperative mechanism design** (Section 2.1, pp. 2–3): objective functions engineered so the target baits (term1 minus term2 in (1)), pursuers capture/protect (2), and the evader's target-seeking dominates its avoidance by Assumption 1's coefficient conditions; plus a linearized intra-coalition collision-avoidance constraint (5), (22) handled through trajectory copies.
4. **Algorithm — IBR-DADMM distributed NE seeking** (Section 4, pp. 6–8): an outer iterative-best-response loop between the virtual evader and the TP coalition (25)–(26) and an inner distributed ADMM loop (27)–(28), with Prediction/Coordination/Mediation/Communication phases (Algorithm 1), that computes the NE of the subjective game G2 from the TP coalition's perspective in real time with fixed iteration caps.
5. **Experiments** (Section 5, pp. 8–13): numerical simulation (case 1), Rflysim UAV simulation (case 2), and a 4-AGV motion-capture hardware experiment (case 3), including a 500-run Monte Carlo study linking measurement noise σ_θ to subjective-game divergence and capture percentage.

### 5.0 Game model, dynamics, information structure, termination

- **Players and coalitions (p. 2, Table 1 p. 3)**: V = {T, E, P_i}, i = 1,…,M−2 (M players total); TP coalition {T, P_1,…,P_{M−2}} vs. evader coalition {E}; same-coalition communication is allowed. States x_j^k = [x_j^k, y_j^k, z_j^k, v_{x,j}^k, v_{y,j}^k, v_{z,j}^k]^T ∈ X_j ⊂ ℝ^{2n}, positions y_j^k = [x_j^k, y_j^k, z_j^k]^T ∈ Y_j ⊂ ℝ^n, actions u_j^k = [a_{x,j}^k, a_{y,j}^k, a_{z,j}^k]^T ∈ U_j ⊂ ℝ^n (accelerations).
- **Dynamics (p. 4, eq. (4))**: discrete double integrator, x_j^{k+1} = A x_j^k + B u_j^k, y_j^k = C x_j^k, ∀j ∈ V, with A = [[I_n, T_s I_n],[0, I_n]] ∈ ℝ^{2n×2n}, B = [[(T_s²/2) I_n],[T_s I_n]] ∈ ℝ^{2n×n}, C = [I_n, 0] ∈ ℝ^{n×2n}.
- **Winning/termination conditions (p. 2)**: "the TP coalition wins the game if a pursuer is close enough to the evader (within the capture distance r_p), and … the evader wins if the evader is close enough to the target (within the attack distance r_e) and is not captured by a pursuer." In all experiments r_p = r_e = 1 m (p. 8). No formal capture-time or winnability region analysis is given — outcomes are emergent from the receding-horizon plays.
- **Collision-avoidance constraint (p. 4, eq. (5))**: g_ql(y_q^k, y_l^k) = ‖y_q^k − y_l^k‖ − Δ_q ≥ 0 for all TP pairs (q,l), q ≠ l, with safety distance Δ_q (1 m in experiments). **Only intra-TP collision avoidance is imposed** — the evader imposes none in (7), and TP-evader proximity is the objective, not a constraint.
- **Receding-horizon problems (p. 4, eqs. (6)–(7))**: TP coalition centralized problem: min over {u_{0:H−1}^q}_q of Σ_{q∈V\{E}} Σ_{k=0}^H J_q^k(y_q^k, y_{−q}^k, y_E^k, u_q^k), s.t. (6b) y_q(0) initial condition and y_q^k ∈ Y_q, u_q^k ∈ U_q; (6c) dynamics (x^k, u^k) ∈ C_q; (6d) collision avoidance ∀k ∈ {1,…,H}. Evader problem (7): min Σ_{k=0}^H J_E^k s.t. (7b)–(7c), no collision-avoidance constraint. Only controls are decision variables; states derive from the dynamics (p. 4).
- **Compact forms and NE (p. 4, eqs. (8)–(10))**: TP problem (8): min_{U_q} Σ_q J_q(U_q, U_{−q}, U_E) s.t. h_q(U_q) = 0 (8b), g_q(U_q) ≤ 0 (8c); evader problem (9) analogous. **NE definition (10)**: U* = (U*_{−E}, U*_E) is a NE of the subjective game if U*_{−E} ∈ argmin_{U_{−E}} Σ_q J_q(U_q, U_{−q}, U*_E) and U*_E ∈ argmin_{U_E} J_E(U_E, U*_{−E}); "A NE lies at the intersection of solutions to the set of problems (8) and (9) (fixed-point of best-response map)" (p. 4). **Assumption 2 (p. 4)**: the evader knows the TP's optimization problem and full state; the TP knows the evader's optimization problem but only has uncertain observation of the evader's state.

### 5.1 Objective functions and Assumption 1 (convexity mechanism)

- **Target/bait cost (p. 2–3, eq. (1))**: J_T^k(y_T^k, {y_{P_i}^k}, y_E^k, u_T^{k−1}) = Σ_{i=1}^{M−2} ‖y_T^k − y_{P_i}^k‖²_{Q1_T} **−** ‖y_T^k − y_E^k‖²_{Q2_T} + ‖u_T^{k−1}‖²_{R_T}. (Verified from the rendered page: term2 enters with a **minus** sign; the plain-text layer suggests "+" but the PDF shows "−".) Physical meaning: term1 attracts the target to pursuers, the negative term2 repels it from the evader, term3 penalizes control effort.
- **Pursuer cost (p. 3, eq. (2))**: J_{P_i}^k = ‖y_{P_i}^k − y_E^k‖²_{Q1_P} + ‖y_{P_i}^k − y_T^k‖²_{Q2_P} + ‖u_{P_i}^{k−1}‖²_{R_P} — approach the evader (capture) and the target (protect). All-plus, hence convex.
- **Evader cost (p. 3, eq. (3))**: J_E^k = ‖y_E^k − y_T^k‖²_{Q1_E} **−** Σ_{i=1}^{M−2} ‖y_E^k − y_{P_i}^k‖²_{Q2_E} + ‖u_E^{k−1}‖²_{R_E} — chase the target, avoid pursuers. (Minus sign verified from the rendered page.)
- **Assumption 1 (p. 3)**: Q1_T = τ1_T I_n, Q2_T = τ2_T I_n, Q1_E = τ1_E I_n, Q2_E = τ2_E I_n, with **(M−2)τ1_T > τ2_T > 0 and τ1_E > (M−2)τ2_E > 0**. This is exactly the positive-definiteness condition of the Hessians: ∇²_{y_T} J_T = 2((M−2)τ1_T − τ2_T)I_n and ∇²_{y_E} J_E = 2(τ1_E − (M−2)τ2_E)I_n (the cross terms are linear in the own position variable), so citing [17] (Boyd, convex optimization) the paper concludes J_T, J_{P_i}, J_E are convex in y_T, y_{P_i}, y_E respectively (p. 3). **Physical/modeling meaning**: without the minus terms the bait/avoidance behaviors would be impossible; with them, convexity survives only under Assumption 1, which also encodes the behavioral priority "the target has stronger intention to reach the target [sic — the *evader* has stronger intention to reach the target] when this contradiction happens" (p. 3). **Caveat**: convexity holds per-player in its own variable; joint convexity in (U_{−E}, U_E) does not hold, and no joint-NE existence argument is provided.

### 5.2 Imperfect-observation modeling: KF belief and the virtual evader (pp. 5–6)

- **Process and measurement models (p. 5, eqs. (11)–(12))**: x_E^{k+1} = A x_E^k + B u_E^k + ω^k, ω^k ≈ N(0_{2n}, Σ_ω^k), Σ_ω^k = σ_ω^k I_{2n}; y_E^k = C x_E^k + ϑ^k, ϑ^k ≈ N(0_{2n}, Σ_ϑ^k), Σ_ϑ^k = σ_ϑ^k I_{2n}. **Printed inconsistencies**: the text declares ϑ^k ⊂ ℝ^n while (12) uses 0_{2n} and I_{2n} (an n-dimensional measurement noise); "⊂" is misused for "∈" throughout.
- **Belief (p. 5, eq. (13))**: b^k = Pr(x_E^k | y_E^0,…,y_E^k, u_E^0,…,u_E^k), realized as b^k = (x̂_E^k, Σ_{x_E}^k) with Σ_{x_E}^k printed as (σ_{x_E}^k)_{2n}^T I_{2n} — evidently an isotropic covariance (σ_{x_E}^k)² I_{2n}; the printed exponent/subscript is garbled.
- **KF recursion (p. 5, eqs. (14a)–(14e))**: given a nominal trajectory b̄ = {b̄^k = (x̄_E^k, Σ̄_{x_E}^k), k = 1,…,H} and nominal inputs {ū_E^0,…,ū_E^{H−1}} satisfying x̄_E^{k+1} = A x̄_E^k + B ū_E^k: prior mean x̂̃_E^{k+1} = A x̂_E^k + B u_E^k (14a); prior covariance Σ̃_{x_E}^{k+1} = A Σ_{x_E}^k A^T + Σ_ω^k (14b); Kalman gain K^{k+1} = Σ̃_{x_E}^{k+1} C^T (C Σ̃_{x_E}^{k+1} C^T + Σ_ϑ^{k+1})^{−1} (14c); posterior mean x̂_E^{k+1} = x̂̃_E^{k+1} + K^{k+1}(y^k − C x̂̃_E^{k+1}) (14d); posterior covariance Σ_{x_E}^{k+1} = (I − K^{k+1} C) Σ̃_{x_E}^{k+1} (14e). **Two observations**: (i) (14d) uses the *current* measurement y^k although the preceding text says "the observation at the next time step y_E^{k+1}" — a standard KF would use y^{k+1}; (ii) during receding-horizon *prediction* over future steps, measurements (14c)–(14e) do not exist — only (14a)–(14b) are realizable — yet the paper presents (14) wholesale as the belief-propagation device. This is a conceptual gap, not elaborated.
- **Virtual evader (p. 5, eq. (15))**: x_E^k ≈ x̂_E^k + η^k with η^k ≈ N(0_{2n}, Σ_{x_E}^k); prediction dynamics used by the target: x_E^{k+1} = A(x̂_E^k + η^k) + B u_E^k, y_E^{k+1} = C x_E^{k+1}. The uncertainty enters as an additive Gaussian state perturbation scaled by the current posterior covariance.
- **Batch prediction form (p. 5–6, eqs. (16a)–(16d))**: with F = H = [CA; CA²; …; CA^H] ∈ ℝ^{nH×2n} (16a) — **the paper defines F and H as the *same* matrix**, so the η-term and the mean-term share structure, Y_{E+} = [y_E^{k+1}; …; y_E^{k+H}] ∈ ℝ^{nH}, U_E = [u_E^k; …; u_E^{k+H−1}] ∈ ℝ^{nH} (16d):
  **Y_{E+} = F x̂_E^k + G U_E + H η^k (16c)**, with G ∈ ℝ^{nH×nH} the lower-block-Toeplitz matrix with first block column [CB; CB; …; CA^{H−1}B] and diagonal-shifted blocks CAB, …, CB (16b). Because F = H, (16c) = F(x̂_E^k + η^k) + G U_E. **Modeling observation**: a *single* Gaussian vector η^k is propagated over the whole horizon; per-step process-noise accumulation along the horizon does not appear as independent samples in (16c) (any accumulation is only implicit if Σ_{x_E}^k is read as a propagated Σ̄_{x_E}^k, which the text does not pin down). The uncertainty quantification within a planning horizon is therefore that of an initial-condition perturbation — optimistic over long H, mitigated by receding-horizon replanning.
- **Belief/NE loop closure (p. 5 and Remark 2, p. 8)**: the KF prediction step needs u_E^k, unknown to the TP coalition; the TP coalition instead takes "the part of evader's decision in the subjective game's NE as the control input of KF" — i.e., the virtual evader's NE strategy from G2 is fed back as the KF's input estimate (Remark 2: Algorithm 1's virtual-evader prediction in the NE of G2 "is then used in KF for the update of the evader's belief").

### 5.3 Chance-constrained problem, scenario programming, Lemma 1 (pp. 5–6)

- **Chance-constrained problem (p. 6, eq. (17))**: min_{U_E} E[Ĵ_E(U_E, U_{−E}, η^k)] (17a) s.t. ĥ_E(U_E, η^k) = 0 (17b), ℙ(ĝ_E(U_E, η^k) ≤ 0) ≥ 1 − ε (17c), with ε the violation probability. The uncertainty set is the **2σ confidence interval −2σ_{x_E}^k ≤ η^k ≤ 2σ_{x_E}^k**; "we allow the optimization problem to be solved even if it violates the confidence interval with a certain probability [18]" (p. 6). Rationale given: considering all possibilities "can be too conservative or leads to infeasibility and computation burden" (unbounded-support Gaussians).
- **Uncertain constraints (p. 6, eq. (18))**: the chance constraint specializes to the position box constraints G U_E − Y_E^max + F x̂_E^k + H η^k ≤ 0 and −G U_E + Y_E^min − F x̂_E^k − H η^k ≤ 0, with Y_E^max, Y_E^min ∈ ℝ^{nH} the horizon bounds. **Key modeling sentence (p. 6)**: "the chance constraint (17c) allows a larger feasible set for the virtual evader compared with the actual evader's optimization problem. The TP coalition obtains the robust response to the evader's strategy by solving (17) under uncertainty." This inflated decision domain of the virtual evader is the paper's robustness mechanism — the TP plans against the *worst-direction-inflated* reachable set of the evader.
- **Scenario program (p. 6, eq. (19))**: min_{U_E} (1/L) Σ_{s=1}^L Ĵ_E(U_E, U_{−E}, η_k^s) (19a) s.t. ĥ_E(U_E, η_k^s) = 0 (19b), ĝ_E(U_E, η_k^s) ≤ 0 for s = 1,…,L (19c), where each scenario η_k^s is a random sample of the 2σ interval. The expectation and probability constraint are replaced by sample averages/pointwise constraints; "a robust prediction of the evader's strategy is achieved by finding the predicted mean of all N_ibr virtual evaders' strategies" (p. 6) — **the printed "N_ibr" is inconsistent with the L scenarios of (19) (N_ibr is elsewhere the IBR iteration cap); L is evidently meant**.
- **Lemma 1 [imported from ref. [20], Micheli & Lygeros, ECC 2022] (p. 6, eqs. (20)–(21))**: Fix violation probability ε ∈ [0,1], confidence parameter β ∈ [0,1], and let d be the number of decision variables in U_E. Further assume the SP (19) is always feasible and its optimal solution is unique. If N_m is chosen such that
  **Σ_{s=0}^{d−1} C(L, i) ε^s (1−ε)^{L−s} ≤ β (20)** [as printed: the summation index is s but the binomial's lower index is printed i, and "N_m" collides with the DADMM iteration cap — the intended statement is: if the number of samples L satisfies (20)],
  then with probability at least (1−β), the optimal solution to the sampled deterministic SP (19) is a feasible solution for the chance-constrained Problem (17). The required sample number is given as **L = (2/ε)(ln(1/β) + d) (21)** (verified from the rendered page). Proof approach: none given — this is cited machinery ([20], in the Campi–Garatti scenario-approach lineage). Physical meaning: it converts the chance constraint into a deterministic convex program with a probabilistic feasibility certificate; with the case-1 parameter choice ε = β = 0.1 and d = nH = 60, (21) gives L = 20(ln 10 + 60) ≈ 1246, though the paper never reports the L actually used in case 1. **Extensibility**: the lemma applies to any convex scenario program; here the SP (19) is convex because Ĵ_E and the constraints are convex in U_E under Assumption 1 — but note Lemma 1's uniqueness assumption interacts poorly with the non-strict convexity of quadratic costs.

### 5.4 Subjective game G2, IBR, and the DADMM inner loop (pp. 6–8)

- **Nonconvexity and linearization (p. 7, eq. (22))**: problem (8) is nonconvex due to (5), "thus we cannot guarantee the uniqueness of an optimal solution. For online implementation, we chose a local optimization strategy, thus potentially sacrificing optimality for the sake of increasing speed" (p. 7). First-order Taylor linearization near the previous-time solution: **ḡ_ql(y_q^k, y_l^k) = γ^T[(y_q^k − y_l^k) − (y_q^{k−1} − y_l^{k−1})] − Δ_q**, with γ = (y_q^{k−1} − y_l^{k−1})/‖y_q^{k−1} − y_l^{k−1}‖. **Proof gap (modeling, p. 7)**: the genuine first-order expansion of ‖y_q − y_l‖ at (y_q^{k−1}, y_l^{k−1}) is γ^T(y_q − y_l) − γ^T(y_q^{k−1} − y_l^{k−1}) + ‖y_q^{k−1} − y_l^{k−1}‖, so the printed (22) **omits the constant term ‖y_q^{k−1} − y_l^{k−1}‖**; as printed, the constraint is not the stated Taylor expansion (it presumes zero previous separation). **Sign inconsistencies**: (5)/(6d) impose g_ql ≥ 0, (8c)/(23c) impose ḡ_ql ≤ 0, and Algorithm 1 line 9 imposes ḡ_ql(a w_q, a w_{q→l}) ≥ 0 — the convention flips across the paper.
- **Copy-restructured TP problem (p. 7, eq. (23))**: min over {U_q, w_q}_q, {w_{q→l}}_{q,l} of Σ_{q∈V\{E}} J_q(X_q, Y_{−q}, Ŷ_E, U_q) s.t. h_q(U_q) = 0 (23b); ḡ_ql(w_q, w_{q→l}) ≤ 0 (23c) — now on the *copies*, not the true trajectories; w_q = Y_q, w_{q→l} = Y_l (23d) consensus constraints. "The feasible set of the linearized problem (23) is an inner approximation of the non-convex problem's (8) feasible set. This curtailment … causes the trajectories … to be more conservative" (p. 7). Ŷ_E is the virtual evader's predicted trajectory.
- **Best response and IBR (p. 7, eqs. (24)–(26))**: BR(U_{−E}) = {U*_E ∈ 𝒰_E | J_E(U_E, U*_{−E}) = min_{U_E∈𝒰_E} J_E(U_E, U*_{−E})} (24) [as printed; intended: U*_E attains the minimum]. The TP-side IBR process is: **U_E^p ∈ BR(U_{−E}^p) (25)** — realized by solving the scenario program (19) for the virtual evader — and **U_{−E}^{p+1} ∈ BR(U_E^p) (26)** — realized by solving the centralized problem (23) via DADMM. "The action of one coalition is optimized while the strategy of the opponent coalition remains fixed. The fixed point reached by the iteration is the NE" (p. 7). **No convergence proof of (25)–(26) is given**; the fixed-point/NE correspondence is attributed to the IBR literature [11]–[13].
- **DADMM master problem (p. 7, eq. (27))**: min_{U_q, w} f({U_q}) + s(w) s.t. Y_q − w_q! = 0 (27b), with (27c) "(Y_q, U_q, w_q!) satisfy (23b) to (23d)", where f({U_q}) = Σ_{q∈V\{E}} [J_q(X_q, Y_{−q}, Ŷ_E, U_q) + ℐ_{C_q}(X_q, U_q)] and s(w) = Σ_{q∈V\{E}} [Σ_{l∈V\{q,∅}} ℐ_{ḡ_ql(w_q,w_{q→l})≥0}(w_q)]. Indicator functions: ℐ_{C_q}(X_j, U_q) := 0 if (x_q, u_q) ∈ C_q, ∞ otherwise; **ℐ_{ḡ_ql(w_q,w_{q→l})≥0}(w_q) := 0 if ḡ_ql(w_q, w_{q→l}) ≤ 0, ∞ otherwise** — the subscript and the body state opposite inequalities (≥0 vs ≤0), an internal contradiction. The index set "V\{q,A}" / "V\{q,∅}" (also in (28) and Algorithm 1 line 1) uses a symbol **A that is never defined**; presumably the TP-coalition member set minus {q}.
- **Augmented Lagrangian (p. 7, eq. (28))**: L_ρ({U_q}, w, λ) = f({U_q}) + s(w) + Σ_q^{V\{E}} [M_q + Σ_{l∈V\{q,A}} M_ql], with M_q = λ^T ε_q + (ρ/2)‖ε_q‖²₂, ε_q = Y_q − w_q; M_ql = λ^T ε_ql + (ρ/2)‖ε_ql‖²₂, ε_ql = Y_l − w_{q→l}. "The DADMM solves (27) by alternatingly searching for a saddle point of the augmented Lagrangian" (p. 7); mechanism attributed to [22] (Boyd et al.) and the linearized-constraint ADMM of [23] (Rey et al.) and [24] (Van Parys & Pipeleers). **No convergence statement for (27)–(28) under the linearized nonconvex constraints is provided.**
- **Algorithm 1 (IBR-DADMM, p. 8)**: Initialization of copies w_q, w_{q→l}, w_{l→q}, multipliers λ_q, λ_{q→l}, λ_{l→q}, states X_q^0, controls U_q^0 for q ∈ V\{E}, l ∈ V\{q,A}. **Outer IBR loop** p = 0,…,N_ibr: (i) *Prediction* — the target predicts the evader's strategy with Y_{−E}^p, η^p by (19), obtaining Ŷ_E^p; (ii) *Communication* — send Ŷ_E^p to pursuers. **Inner DADMM loop** a = 0,…,N_m, for each player q: (iii) *Y/U-update* — argmin_{U_q,Y_q} J_q({}^a X_q^p, {}^a Y_{−q}^p, Ŷ_E^p, {}^a U_q^p) + λ^T({}^a Y_q^p − {}^a w_q) + (ρ/2)‖{}^a Y_q^p − {}^a w_q‖² + Σ_l [λ^T({}^a Y_q^p − {}^a w_{l→q}) + (ρ/2)‖{}^a Y_q^p − {}^a w_{l→q}‖²] s.t. (X_q, U_q) ∈ C_q; (iv) *Communication* of {}^{a+1} Y_q^p; (v) *Coordination* — argmin_{w_q, w_{q→l}} of the same quadratic penalty terms s.t. ḡ_ql({}^a w_q, {}^a w_{q→l}) ≥ 0; (vi) *Mediation* — dual ascent {}^{a+1} λ_q ← {}^a λ_q + ρ({}^{a+1} Y_q^p − {}^{a+1} w_q), {}^{a+1} λ_{q→l} ← {}^a λ_{q→l} + ρ({}^{a+1} Y_l^p − {}^a w_{q→l}); (vii) *Communication* of multipliers and proposals. After the inner loop: X_q^{p+1} ← {}^{a+1} X_q^p, U_q^{p+1} ← {}^{a+1} U_q^p; return at maximum iterations. **Remark 1 (p. 8)**: full convergence "comes with significant computational burden, so in practical applications it is not necessary to wait for the algorithm to converge to a NE" — fixed caps N_ibr, N_m are used instead (following [24], [25]). **Remark 2 (p. 8)**: the algorithm yields the virtual evader's prediction in the NE of G2, which feeds the KF belief update (loop closure of Section 3.2.1).

### 5.5 Summary of analytic status

The only formal guarantee in the paper is **Lemma 1** (probabilistic feasibility of the scenario solution of (17), eqs. (20)–(21)). Everything else is definitional or algorithmic: convexity of the per-player costs under Assumption 1 (Hessian count, one line, citing [17]); inner-approximation claim for (23) vs (8) (asserted, p. 7); NE = fixed point of IBR (asserted, p. 7, credited to [11]–[13]); DADMM saddle-point iteration (cited machinery [22]–[24]). **There is no proof of NE existence for G1/G2, no convergence proof for the IBR loop (25)–(26), and no convergence proof for DADMM on the linearized problem** — the paper explicitly sidesteps this by capping iterations (Remark 1). "Local/analytic" status: all constructions are local (linearized collision constraint (22), per-horizon receding optimization); nothing global about the RAG (barriers, winning sets) is derived — in sharp contrast to the geometric reach-avoid literature the same group engages in P028.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues

**Author-stated (pp. 8, 12–13):**
- Full IBR-DADMM convergence is too slow for real time; fixed iteration caps N_ibr, N_m are used, "potentially sacrificing optimality" (Remark 1, p. 8; Section 4.1, p. 7).
- Measurement noise cannot be eliminated; the σ_θ study is offered as a design tool for balancing observer accuracy against capture rate (pp. 9–10).
- The linear double-integrator model (4) mismatches the Ackermann-dynamics AGVs, producing the ~0.02 m average / ~0.05 m max tracking errors of Table 6 (p. 13).
- Future work: game-theoretic algorithms on physical UAVs (p. 13); "Research data are not shared" (p. 13).

**Critical observations (with page/eq.):**
1. **No theoretical guarantees for the core game mechanism** (pp. 6–8): NE existence for the subjective games G1/G2 is never established (the negative quadratic terms of (1)/(3) make the game nonconvex jointly even though Assumption 1 fixes per-player convexity); IBR convergence (25)–(26) is asserted via "fixed points … correspond to the generalized NE" (p. 2, credited to [11]–[13]) but not proved; DADMM convergence on the linearized nonconvex problem (23) is not proved (only [22]–[24] cited). All claimed guarantees beyond Lemma 1 are empirical.
2. **Lemma 1's guarantee is not actually met in practice** (pp. 6, 12): case 3 uses L = 10 with H = 15, Nibr = 3 (p. 12), far below (21)'s prescription (d = nH = 30 → L = 20(ln 10 + 30) ≈ 646 for ε = β = 0.1); the probabilistic feasibility certificate is thus void in the hardware experiments, and the virtual evader's "robustness" there rests only on the 2σ sampling heuristic. Case 1's L is UNREPORTED.
3. **Eq. (22) omits the constant norm term** of the first-order Taylor expansion of ‖y_q − y_l‖ (p. 7) — the printed linearization is exact only if the previous separation is zero; combined with the ≥0/≤0 sign inconsistencies ((5)/(6d) vs (8c)/(23c) vs Algorithm 1 line 9 vs the indicator subscript/body contradiction on p. 7), the collision-avoidance machinery as printed is internally inconsistent, though the intended constraint (safety distance on copies) is unambiguous.
4. **KF belief propagation mixes prediction and correction** (p. 5, eqs. (14a)–(14e)): (14c)–(14e) require the future measurement y^{k+1} ("y^k" as printed in (14d), itself inconsistent with the text), which does not exist during horizon prediction; the intended open-loop predictor is presumably only (14a)–(14b), but the paper does not say so.
5. **Single-sample uncertainty propagation** (p. 5–6, eqs. (15)–(16c)): one Gaussian vector η^k with the current covariance is propagated through the whole horizon (F = H in (16a)); process-noise accumulation along the horizon never enters the scenario set as independent per-step uncertainty — the robustness inflation is that of an initial-condition perturbation only.
6. **Symbol/notation defects**: Lemma 1 says "If N_m is chosen such that Σ_{s=0}^{d−1} C(L, i) ε^s(1−ε)^{L−s} ≤ β" (p. 6) — N_m collides with the DADMM cap and the binomial index i with the summation index s; "predicted mean of all N_ibr virtual evaders' strategies" (p. 6) should read L; undefined index sets "V\{q,A}" / "V\{q,∅}" in s(w), (28), and Algorithm 1 line 1 (pp. 7–8); ϑ^k ⊂ ℝ^n vs N(0_{2n}, σ_ϑ I_{2n}) dimension mismatch (p. 5); Σ_{x_E}^k = (σ_{x_E}^k)_{2n}^T I_{2n} garbled (p. 5); "TD coalition" appears twice for "TP coalition" in the Conclusion (p. 13); "NOVOK"/"NOKOV" (p. 11–12); reference [25] is an exact duplicate of [16] (Spica et al., IEEE T-RO 2020, 36, 1389) while [12] is a second Spica et al. T-RO 2020 paper (36, 1403) — reference list was not de-duplicated (p. 14).
7. **No comparison baselines and no geometric analysis** (Section 5): the empirical claims rest on within-method statistics; no ablation isolates the value of the chance-constrained virtual evader versus a plain KF-mean prediction, and no winnability/capture-time analysis connects to the geometric RAG literature (contrast P028).
8. **Information-structure idealization**: the evader's perfect observation and its exact knowledge of the TP's optimization problem (Assumption 2, p. 4) plus identical objective-function knowledge on both sides are strong; the reported subjective-NE gap is driven purely by the TP's measurement noise, not by model mismatch of the games themselves.

## 8. Citation Value

**Paragraph role.** P027 is the reference implementation of *asymmetric-information cooperative pursuit as receding-horizon game solving*: it shows how a disadvantaged coalition converts a Kalman-filter belief into a virtual opponent with an inflated (chance-constrained) decision set, and how IBR + distributed ADMM turns that subjective game into real-time, collision-free coalition trajectories validated from simulation down to motion-capture hardware. In the game-paper knowledge base it plays four roles: (i) the canonical citation for *subjective games / divergent NEs under imperfect observation* in multiplayer RAGs — together with its geometric journal extension P028 (Fang et al., IEEE TASE 2025) it anchors the "imperfect observation" branch of the reach-avoid literature; (ii) the worked example of *chance-constrained scenario programming inside a pursuit game* (Lemma 1/(20)–(21) sample complexity) — useful when contrasting worst-case barrier-based robustness (Apollonius/Cartesian-oval works, HJI barriers) with probabilistic robustness; (iii) the source for *IBR-DADMM as a distributed NE-seeking pattern* for multi-vehicle teams with coupled collision constraints (linearization (22), copies (23), Lagrangian (28), Algorithm 1); and (iv) an experimental benchmark template (Rflysim; NOKOV AGVs; tracking-error tables; σ_θ-vs-capture-rate Monte Carlo).

**Suggested citation sentences.**
- "Fang et al. [P027] model the information disadvantage of a target–pursuer coalition as a subjective game against a 'virtual evader' whose decision set is inflated by a chance constraint built from the Kalman-filter posterior, and solve the resulting game in real time with an IBR–DADMM scheme, showing that the divergence between the two coalitions' subjective Nash equilibria — and the capture rate — degrades monotonically with the measurement noise σ_θ."
- "The target-as-bait cooperative mechanism, in which the objective functions (1)–(3) jointly encode attraction to teammates and repulsion from the evader under the convexity conditions of Assumption 1, was introduced in [P027] and later complemented by geometric safe-region analysis under asymmetric observations in [P028]."
