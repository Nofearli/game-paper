## 2. Research Background

Reach-avoid differential games model safety-critical multi-agent decision problems such as autonomous driving, obstacle avoidance, path planning, and disaster rescue (cited to [1]–[5]). The classical machinery for solving them — barriers and winning regions — is built either from geometric methods or from Hamilton–Jacobi–Isaacs (HJI) equations under **complete information** ([6], [7]; barrier structures [12]–[14]). In practice, pursuers have finite sensing range and cannot access global state, while the HJI equation under a time-varying (partial) information structure is generally intractable, and complete-information barriers/winning regions do not automatically remain valid when information is partial. Recent partial-information work is limited and simplified: [8] (Shishika et al., ICRA 2021) assumes a circular concentric sensing geometry, and [9] (Maity et al., ACC 2024) lets the evader buy global information at a cost. Separately, 3-D reach-avoid games ([10] Yan et al., Automatica 2022; [11] Lee & Bakolas, TAC 2024) are built on HJI equations under complete information. This paper sits at the intersection: N pursuers vs. one evader, a general convex target region in 3-D, and a strict information asymmetry (evader global, pursuers sensing-limited).

## 3. Research Problem and Gap

**Problem.** In infinite 3-D Euclidean space split into a convex target region Ωtar and a play region Ωplay, Np homogeneous pursuers with speed vP and sensing radius rs (sensing ball Si = {z : ‖z − xPi‖₂ ≤ rs}, team sensing region S = ∪Si) try to capture a single evader (speed vE, capture radius rc) before it reaches Ωtar. The evader holds **global** state information; each pursuer only senses the evader inside S (pursuers communicate, so the team information set is pooled). The evader cannot even access rs (p. 2). Payoff J = dist(xE(tf), Ωtar); pursuit maximizes, evasion minimizes.

**Verified gap addressed**: no prior work in the cited corpus constructs the *winning region of a reach-avoid game in which the defense side has provably valid barriers under partial information*, for a general convex target set in 3-D. The paper fills this by (i) a two-phase decomposition (deployment/capture) around the detection instant tc, (ii) a capture strategy that provably keeps the evader inside S, and (iii) sufficient sensing-radius conditions under which the complete-information barrier remains the valid boundary under partial information.

## 4. Core Contributions

1. **(Modeling)** A many-vs-one reach-avoid game in 3-D with a convex target region and an asymmetric information structure: evader global, pursuit team limited to S with pooled communication; non-anticipative strategy sets (2); the defense strategy is split into an open-loop deployment strategy ΠdP (before detection) and a closed-loop capture strategy ΠcP (after detection), with the feasible capture set Π̄cP required to keep xE(t) ∈ S for all t ∈ [tc, tf] (p. 3).
2. **(Strategy/algorithm — Theorem 1, p. 4, eqs. (6)–(7))** A geometric cooperative capture strategy under partial information with three cases, guaranteeing the partial-information value is bounded below by the complete-information value at the detection instant, V(x) ≥ ‖ϑ*(tc) − χ(tc)‖₂; includes an explicit speed-ratio threshold αc = (√(8rs² + rc²) − rc)/(2rs) under which the complete-information optimal strategy is itself feasible under partial information.
3. **(Theory — Theorem 2, p. 6, eq. (9)–(10))** A tight sufficient sensing-radius condition for a single pursuer to have its barrier covered by its sensing ball, RS = ((α+1)/α)·max dist(x0Pi, Ωtar) − rc/α, proved via tangency of the evasion dominance space with Ωtar plus a normal-cone tightness argument; with **Corollary 2** (optimal single-pursuer deployment = center of the minimum enclosing sphere of Ωtar) and **Corollary 3** (multi-pursuer extension via a Voronoi partition Γi of the target boundary, RsV = max_i{((α+1)/α)·max dist(x0Pi, Vi) − rc/α}, Vi = conv(Γi)).
4. **(Concept — Definition 1, p. 5, and eq. (8))** The **fuzzy region** F = W̃P \ S — the part of the complete-information pursuit winning region from which neither side can *guarantee* a win under partial information — and the partial-information pursuit winning region WP = W̃P if B̃ ⊂ int(S), else S \ W̃E.
5. **(Experiment)** A 3-pursuer single-evader 3-D simulation against a tetrahedral target validating the capture strategy and the two-phase game evolution (Fig. 8).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results

### 5.0 Dynamics, information structure, payoff, termination (pp. 2–3)

- **Dynamics (eq. (1))**: simple motion, ẋPi = vP·uPi, ẋE = vE·uE, controls in U = {u ∈ R³ : ‖u‖₂ = 1 or u = 0} (unit heading or full stop). Homogeneous pursuers; augmented state x = [xP⊤, xE⊤]⊤.
- **Termination**: game ends at tf when ‖xPi − xE‖₂ ≤ rc for some i (capture) or xE reaches Ωtar.
- **Information**: IP(t) = {xPi, vP}ᴺᵖᵢ₌₁ ∪ {xE, uE, vE : xE ∈ S}; IE(t) = {xPi, vP}ᴺᵖᵢ₌₁ ∪ {xE, vE}; non-anticipative admissible strategies (2): ΠP = {uP(·) : uP(t) ∈ UP(IP(t)) ∀t}, likewise ΠE. The evader cannot access rs.
- **Assumption 1 (speed ratio)**: α = vP/vE > 1. **Assumption 2 (initial deployment)**: initial positions randomly generated; x0E ∈ Ωplay \ S (game not trivially over at t = 0).
- **Payoff / value**: J(x, uP, uE) = dist(xE(tf), Ωtar) (evader minimizes); complete-information value Ṽ(x) = sup_{uP∈ΠP} inf_{uE∈ΠE} J; partial-information capture-phase value V(x) = sup_{uP∈Π̄cP} inf_{uE∈ΠE} J (p. 3). Since Ωtar is convex, ProjΩtar(z) and dist(z, Ωtar) are well defined (unique projection).
- **Phases**: deployment phase = evader outside S (open-loop ΠdP); capture phase begins at first detection instant tc (closed-loop ΠcP); the evader may leave S and the game returns to deployment (Fig. 3); the feasible capture set Π̄cP requires xE(t) ∈ S ∀t ∈ [tc, tf].

### 5.1 Evasion dominance space and potential function (pp. 2–3, eqs. (3)–(4))

- **Potential function (eq. (3))**: φi(z) = ‖z − xPi‖₂ − α‖z − xE‖₂ − rc; dominance region Di = {z : φi(z) > 0}, boundary ∂Di = {z : φi(z) = 0} (eq. (4)). This is the offset-Apollonius construction ("dominance region" of the (Pi, E) pair). Overall evasion dominance space D = ∩ᴺᵖᵢ₌₁ Di (Fig. 2) — all positions the evader can reach before capture regardless of pursuit. Strict convexity of D is [authors' claim] taken from [10] (Yan et al., Automatica 2022). D, ∂D depend only on dynamics/parameters, not on rs or strategies — this is what later lets the barrier analysis decouple from the information structure.

### 5.2 Complete-information benchmark geometry (p. 3, eq. (5), Corollary 1, Fig. 4)

- ϑ* ∈ arg min_{z∈∂D} dist(z, Ωtar) (boundary point of D closest to the target); χ = ProjΩtar(ϑ*). At this regular boundary point, define the **active pursuer set** A* as a minimum-cardinality subset of {Pi : φi(ϑ*) = 0} such that (eq. (5)): ϑ* − χ = Σ_{Pi∈A*} λi ∇zφi(ϑ*), λi > 0. (Sign check verified: ∇zφi points into Di hence into D, and ϑ* − χ points from the target side toward the boundary, i.e., inward — consistent.)
- **Corollary 1 (p. 3)**: In 3-D there exists an active set A* with |A*| ≤ 3. *Proof (2 steps, printed)*: (i) first-order optimality at ϑ* puts ϑ* − χ in the conic hull of the tight-constraint gradients; (ii) conic Carathéodory in R³ reduces to at most 3 generators. *Local result, analytic, dimension-specific (bounds = 3 in R³; d in R^d).*
- **Complete-information optimal strategies** (derivation credited to [7], Lee & Bakolas CDC 2021): pursuers in A* drive toward ϑ*, ũ*Pi = (ϑ* − xPi)/‖ϑ* − xPi‖₂; the evader also heads to ϑ*; the game value equals ‖ϑ* − χ‖₂ (Fig. 4). The HJI equation is thereby solved geometrically.

### 5.3 Theorem 1 — capture strategy under partial information (p. 4, eqs. (6)–(7))

**Preliminary sufficient sensing condition (p. 4, unnumbered).** For sensing pursuer Ps = {Pi : xE ∈ Si}, di = ‖xE − xPi‖₂, ei = (xE − xPi)/di: ḋi = vE·e⊤i uE − vP·e⊤i uPi. Hence if some sensing pursuer keeps α e⊤i uPi ≥ e⊤i uE (possible because uE is in IP(t)), then ḋi ≤ 0 and since di(tc) ≤ rs the evader stays in Si forever after. This is the analytic engine of the whole proof.

**Theorem 1.** Let Ps be the sensing pursuer set and A* a selected active set at ϑ*. The capture strategy under partial information is:
- **Case 1: Ps ⊄ A*** (eq. (6)): pick Pk ∈ Ps \ A*, set uPk = ek and uPi = ũ*Pi for Pi ∈ A*.
- **Case 2: Ps ⊆ A*** (eq. (7)): uPi = ũ*Pi, Pi ∈ A*, if α ≥ αc; otherwise uPi = (ϑ† − xPi)/‖ϑ† − xPi‖₂, Pi ∈ A†, if α < αc, where αc = (√(8rs² + rc²) − rc)/(2rs) and ϑ† ∈ ∂D is the intersection of the ray xE + λuE (λ > 0) with ∂D, A† a minimum-cardinality subset of {Pi : φi(ϑ†) = 0} with uE = Σ_{Pi∈A†} μi ∇zφi(ϑ†), μi > 0 (|A†| ≤ 3 by conic Carathéodory).

**Claim**: V(x) ≥ ‖ϑ*(tc) − χ(tc)‖₂ — i.e., from the detection instant on, the pursuit team guarantees at least the complete-information game value; the evader can never get closer to Ωtar than ϑ*(tc).

**Proof approach (printed, 3 cases; p. 4–5):**
1. *Case 1*: the extra sensing pursuer Pk drives straight at the evader: ḋk = vE e⊤k uE − vP ≤ vE − vP = vE(1 − α) ≤ 0. So dk stays ≤ rs while the active pursuers play the complete-information optimum; strategy feasible and value-preserving.
2. *Case 2, α ≥ αc*: all sensing pursuers are active and play ũ*Pi. With ρi = ‖ϑ* − xPi‖, ρE = ‖ϑ* − xE‖, the boundary relation ρi = αρE + rc holds. Decisive chain: minimizing α e⊤i ũ*Pi over the geometry yields α e⊤i ũ*Pi ≥ [rc + √((α²−1)(α²di² − rc²))]/(αdi); the condition ḋi ≤ 0 for all admissible uE is equivalent to α e⊤i ũ*Pi ≥ 1, which holds whenever α ≥ (√(8di² + rc²) − rc)/(2di); since di ≤ rs after detection, the uniform condition α ≥ αc = (√(8rs² + rc²) − rc)/(2rs) guarantees the evader stays inside at least one sensing ball.
3. *Case 2, α < αc*: pursuers in A† mirror the evader toward ϑ†: under uE = (ϑ† − xE)/sE, uPi = (ϑ† − xPi)/sPi (sE = ‖ϑ† − xE‖, sPi = ‖ϑ† − xPi‖ = αsE + rc), the paper asserts ṡE = −vE, ṡPi = −vP, hence d/dt(sPi − αsE − rc) = 0 (boundary relation preserved — note the typo "perserves", p. 5), consequently D(t) ⊆ D(tc) and V(x) ≥ ‖ϑ*(tc) − χ(tc)‖₂. Finally, differentiating di² = s²Pi + s²E − 2 sPi sE cos βi gives the decisive inequality di ḋi = −vE[(α²+1)sE + αrc − (2αsE + rc)cos βi] ≤ 0 because cos βi ≤ 1 and α > 1, so the evader remains sensed. (I verified this last differentiation is algebraically exact given sPi = αsE + rc and ṡE = −vE, ṡPi = −αvE.)

**Independent verification of the α ≥ αc branch (my check).** The correct pursuer-side identity (law of cosines at the pair (xE, ϑ*)): ρE² = ρi² + di² − 2 di ρi (e⊤i ũ*Pi), i.e., α e⊤i ũ*Pi = α[(α²−1)ρE² + 2αρE rc + rc² + di²]/(2di(αρE + rc)). Minimizing over ρE (stationary point ρE* = [√((α²−1)(α²di²−rc²)) − rc(α²−1)]/(α(α²−1))) gives exactly min α e⊤i ũ*Pi = [rc + √((α²−1)(α²di² − rc²))]/(αdi) — the printed bound. The threshold α ≥ (√(8di²+rc²) − rc)/(2di) is exactly the root where this bound crosses 1: substituting α = (√(8s²+c²)−c)/(2s) into α³s² − α(c²+2s²) + 2sc annihilates it identically (I verified symbolically), so the printed αc is the *exact*, not merely sufficient, speed ratio at which the sensing condition closes (given di ≥ rc, which holds at the threshold). Behavior: αc → √2 ≈ 1.414 as rs → ∞ and αc → 0 as rs → 0; αc depends on rs and rc only — notably **not** on the target region size. In the printed proof, however, the law-of-cosines line is written as ρi² = di² + ρE² + 2diρE e⊤i ũ*Pi, which with the given definitions of ũ*Pi and ei is not a valid identity (it becomes one only if e⊤i ũ*Pi is read as the projection of the evader-side unit vector w = (ϑ* − xE)/ρE; with that reading the subsequent minimization would not produce the printed bound either). **Proof gap (presentational, p. 4, Case 2)**: the intermediate identity/derivation as printed does not support the printed bound; the bound and threshold are nevertheless correct per the independent derivation above.

**Proof gap 2 (p. 5, Case 2, α < αc)**: ṡE = −vE and ṡPi = −vP implicitly treat ϑ†(t) as fixed; rigorously ṡE = w⊤ϑ̇† − vE and ṡPi = ũ⊤Pi ϑ̇† − vP, and the orthogonality of ϑ̇† to both line-of-sight directions is not established. The invariance of the boundary relation and the monotonicity D(t) ⊆ D(tc) are therefore asserted rather than proven.

**Proof gap 3 (p. 4, A† decomposition)**: for the exit point of a ray from an interior point of the convex set D, the exit direction lies in the *outward* normal cone of D at ϑ†, i.e., uE = −Σ μi∇zφi(ϑ†); the printed uE = +Σ μi∇zφi(ϑ†), μi > 0, is sign-inconsistent with eq. (5)'s own convention (verified on a planar Apollonius example: evader at origin-side, exit direction has strictly negative inner product with every active ∇zφi). The subsequent proof does not use the decomposition, and |A†| ≤ 3 (conic Carathéodory) is unaffected.

**Physical meaning / extensibility.** Theorem 1 says the *only* price of partial information is a speed-ratio surcharge: if α is large relative to sensing (α ≥ αc, e.g., ≥ √2 for large rs), nothing is lost; below that, the pursuers must react to the evader's instantaneous control (Remark 1: feasible because uE ∈ IP(t), but "may not be easy to obtain in practice"). The value bound is global (any detection state), analytic, and the case split is driven by set inclusion Ps ⊆ A* — directly extensible to any dimension d by replacing the Carathéodory bound 3 with d, and to any convex target since only ProjΩtar is used. Note the guarantee is a value lower bound (target denied); finite-time capture is implicit, not proven (see §7).

### 5.4 Barrier, fuzzy region, and winning region under partial information (p. 5, Definition 1, eq. (8))

- **Barrier (complete information)**: B̃(xP) = {xE ∈ R³ : Ṽ(xP, xE) = 0} — a 2-D manifold in 3-D partitioning the game space into pursuit winning region W̃P and evasion winning region W̃E; equivalently the set of evader positions from which pursuer and evader reach the same target-boundary point simultaneously under optimal play (p. 5).
- **Key monotonicity argument (p. 5)**: the sensing constraint limits only the pursuit team; the evader keeps global information, so the evasion winning region cannot shrink or grow: WE = W̃E. From Theorem 1, if the evader enters S *before* reaching B̃, it can never enter W̃E. Hence the whole question reduces to: does the evader reach B̃ before detection?
- **Definition 1 (fuzzy region)**: F = W̃P \ S. From states in F the evader may reach the barrier undetected (Fig. 5) — yet if S covers the barrier entirely (Fig. 6), detection is unavoidable and even fuzzy-region states are pursuit-winning. Note: "fuzzy" here is a set-theoretic ambiguity region, not fuzzy logic; in it *neither* side has a guaranteed win (pursuers might stumble on the evader open-loop, but cannot guarantee it).
- **Winning region (eq. (8))**: WP = W̃P if B̃ ⊂ int(S); WP = S \ W̃E otherwise. The partial-information pursuit winning region therefore depends only on the relative position of the sensing union and the barrier. (S is a moving set; the statement is quasi-static — evaluated for the current/initial sensing geometry; the paper does not elaborate the time-varying version.)

### 5.5 Theorem 2 — sensing radius that preserves the barrier (p. 6, eqs. (9)–(10))

**Theorem 2.** For a pursuer Pi with initial position x0Pi ∈ R³, there exists RS = ((α+1)/α)·max dist(x0Pi, Ωtar) − rc/α such that for all rs > RS, B̃(x0Pi) ⊂ int(Si), where max dist(x0Pi, Ωtar) is the maximum distance from x0Pi to points of the target region.

**Proof approach (printed, 4 steps; p. 6):**
1. *Barrier characterization*: B̃ is the set of evader positions whose induced evasion dominance space is tangent to Ωtar; for x0E ∈ B̃(x0Pi) there is a tangent point xT ∈ ∂Ωtar satisfying the decisive equation (9): ‖xT − x0Pi‖₂ − α‖xT − x0E‖₂ − rc = 0, hence ‖xT − x0E‖₂ = (‖xT − x0Pi‖₂ − rc)/α.
2. *Triangle inequality*: ‖x0Pi − x0E‖₂ ≤ ‖x0Pi − xT‖₂ + ‖xT − x0E‖₂ = ((α+1)/α)‖xT − x0Pi‖₂ − rc/α, with equality iff x0Pi, xT, x0E collinear with xT on the segment.
3. *Decisive inequality (10)*: since xT ∈ ∂Ωtar and Ωtar is convex, ‖xT − x0Pi‖₂ ≤ max dist(x0Pi, Ωtar); hence dist(x0Pi, B̃) ≤ ((α+1)/α)·max dist(x0Pi, Ωtar) − rc/α (eq. (10)), so any rs above this bound covers B̃.
4. *Tightness (normal-cone argument)*: if, at the equality case, ‖xT − x0Pi‖₂ < max dist(x0Pi, Ωtar) held, then strict convexity of the dominance space gives a unique supporting hyperplane at xT whose outward normal is parallel to x0Pi − xT; tangency makes this a supporting hyperplane of Ωtar, so xT − x0Pi ∈ NΩtar(xT). But the strict inequality implies another point xmax ∈ Ωtar with (xmax − xT)⊤(xT − x0Pi) > 0, contradicting the normal-cone definition. Hence the bound (10) is attained and RS is the *minimum* sensing radius.
- *Analytic, global, exact (tight)*; dimension-free argument (works in R^n for any convex compact Ωtar); the constant (α+1)/α → 1 as α → ∞, so RS → max dist − 0: faster pursuers need sensing only up to the target's far extent. My note: tightness can be seen directly — the farthest point xmax* of Ωtar from x0Pi satisfies (xmax* − x0Pi) ∈ NΩtar(xmax*) by first-order optimality of max ‖z − x0Pi‖² over the convex set, so the collinear barrier placement at that tangent point attains equality in (10); the paper's contradiction route is a roundabout version of this.
- *Physical meaning*: the sensing radius needed to make partial information "invisible" (barrier preserved) scales with the target's angular extent as seen from the deployment point, inflated by the factor (α+1)/α and deflated by rc/α.

### 5.6 Corollary 2 — optimal single-pursuer deployment (p. 6)

**Corollary 2.** Determining the minimum sensing radius for one pursuer in R³ to cover B̃ is equivalent to finding the minimum enclosing sphere (MES) of Ωtar. Deploying at xopt = MES center of Ωtar, the minimum radius guaranteeing B̃(xopt) ⊂ int(S) is RS = ((α+1)/α)·max dist(xopt, Ωtar) − rc/α. *No proof printed* ("directly leads to"); the equivalence is immediate because RS(xP) is increasing in max dist(xP, Ωtar), whose minimization over xP ∈ R³ is precisely the 1-center/MES problem (max dist(xopt, Ωtar) = MES radius). A clean bridge from barrier validity to a classical facility-location object.

### 5.7 Corollary 3 — multi-pursuer Voronoi extension (p. 7, unnumbered display)

**Corollary 3.** With Np pursuers and initial state x0P, a sufficient lower bound on the sensing radius covering the barrier is RsV = max_{i=1..Np} { ((α+1)/α)·max dist(x0Pi, Vi) − rc/α }, where Γi = {z ∈ ∂Ωtar : ‖z − xPi‖₂ ≤ ‖z − xPj‖₂ ∀j ≠ i} is the Voronoi partition of the target boundary and Vi = conv(Γi) the dominant subregion. If rs > RsV, the team sensing region covers the barrier.
- *Justification printed (not a proof)*: "a pursuer can only be active on its own dominant subregion — for any other boundary patch Γj, pursuer Pj can capture the evader moving toward Γj no later than pursuer Pi" (p. 7); Fig. 7 illustrates the 3-D partition and the sectional view, with the barrier "jointly determined by the corresponding pursuers" through intersection curves/points.
- *Proof gap*: no formal proof that the team barrier is covered by the union of per-pursuer covered patches; taking Vi = conv(Γi) (hull of the patch rather than the patch) is conservative but the reduction from the joint barrier ∂(∩Di) to per-pursuer tangency sets is asserted. The corollary inherits the exactness of Theorem 2 only per-subregion.

### 5.8 Summary of the analytic skeleton

Complete-information geometry (potential (3)–(4), active set (5), Cor. 1) → partial-information capture strategy with exact speed threshold αc (Thm. 1, eqs. (6)–(7)) → barrier-preserving sensing radius, exact for one pursuer (Thm. 2, eqs. (9)–(10)) → deployment optimality via MES (Cor. 2) → team extension via Voronoi subregions (Cor. 3) → winning-region dichotomy and fuzzy region (Def. 1, eq. (8)). No HJI equation is ever solved numerically; the entire value analysis is geometric.

- Fig. 8(b) (t = 9.99 s): detection instant; P1, P2 become active (blue) and drive toward ϑ* (marked ×) per the complete-information optimal strategy; wireframe sensing spheres shown. Validates the phase switch at tc and the strategy (6)–(7).

- Fig. 8(d) ("Distance relations"): blue = min_i ‖xE − xPi‖ (m, left axis), red = dist(xE, Ωtar) (m, right axis); dashed blue horizontal at 5 m = rs; dashed black at rc = 0.5 m; vertical magenta marker at capture. Initial evader-target distance ≈ 12.7 m (read from figure). Validates the two central claims: (i) after detection at t ≈ 9.99 s the minimum pursuer–evader distance never exceeds rs again (evader retained in S — the Theorem 1 sensing invariant, eq. (7) branch α ≥ αc); (ii) the blue curve falls to rc at t ≈ 11.91 s while the red curve simultaneously reaches 0 — capture occurs just before the evader reaches Ωtar, i.e., the value bound V ≥ ‖ϑ*(tc) − χ(tc)‖₂ > 0 is honored and the game terminates in capture rather than evasion success.

**Metrics**: minimum pursuer–evader distance and evader–target distance versus time. **No** comparison against complete-information-only strategies, no sensing-noise/model-error robustness tests, no parameter sweeps.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues

**Author-stated (p. 7, Conclusions)**:
1. For α < αc the capture strategy needs the evader's control input uE (feasible under the adopted information structure but hard in practice); future work: strategies independent of uE.
2. Optimal deployment positions of pursuers under partial information in multiplayer games remains open (Corollary 2 solves only Np = 1; Corollary 3 gives a feasibility bound, not an optimal placement).

**Critical observations (with locations)**:
3. **Finite-time capture not proven.** Theorem 1 concludes V(x) ≥ ‖ϑ*(tc) − χ(tc)‖₂ > 0 (target denied) and di nonincreasing (evader retained in S), but termination of the game by capture in finite time in the unbounded play region Ωplay is nowhere established (termination definition, p. 2; proof, pp. 4–5).
4. **Presentational proof gaps in Theorem 1.** (i) Case 2, p. 4: the law-of-cosines identity ρi² = di² + ρE² + 2diρE e⊤i ũ*Pi is inconsistent with the printed definitions of ei and ũ*Pi (it holds only for the evader-side unit vector toward ϑ*), and the printed minimization does not follow from the printed intermediate expression; the final bound [rc + √((α²−1)(α²di²−rc²))]/(αdi) and threshold αc are nonetheless exactly correct (verified independently, §5.3). (ii) Case 2, α < αc, p. 5: ṡE = −vE, ṡPi = −vP ignore the motion of the boundary point ϑ†(t) (typo "perserves" also on p. 5). (iii) p. 4: the A† gradient decomposition uE = Σ μi∇zφi(ϑ†), μi > 0 carries a sign inconsistent with the outward-normal-cone geometry of a ray exit point (eq. (5)'s convention); not used downstream.
5. **Corollary 3 is unproven.** The reduction of the joint team barrier to per-pursuer barriers on Voronoi subregions is justified only by an "observation" (p. 7); conv(Γi) over-covers the boundary patch Γi (conservative); no error bound on the conservatism.
6. **Deployment strategy ΠdP is never designed.** The two-phase architecture presumes an open-loop deployment strategy, but nothing specifies what pursuers do before detection; the simulations effectively use stationary deployment (Fig. 8(a) pursuers inactive/gray; Fig. 5 caption "If the pursuit team remains stationary…"). The winning-region statement (8) is therefore quasi-static: S is a moving set and its evolution during the deployment phase is not analyzed.
7. **Consequence of eq. (8) is severe but understated**: unless B̃ ⊂ int(S), the partial-information pursuit winning region shrinks to S \ W̃E — and since Assumption 2 places the evader outside S at t = 0, the pursuit team can guarantee capture *only if the sensing union already covers the barrier at t = 0*; otherwise initial states in F = W̃P \ S have no guaranteed winner. The paper's practical message is essentially a sensing-radius design rule (Theorem 2/Corollary 3), not a strategy for arbitrary initial conditions.
8. **Idealized models**: simple motion with no inertia or actuation limits beyond the unit sphere (with a stop control), homogeneous pursuers, static target, perfect sensing within Si, noiseless pooled communication; the evader knows all pursuer positions and vP globally (but not rs) — a deliberately evader-favorable asymmetry whose robustness to estimation error is untested.
9. **Single toy simulation**: one 3-vs-1 scenario, one tetrahedron, no baselines, no statistics; the α ≥ αc branch exercised (αc ≈ 1.365 < 1.6) — the harder α < αc strategy (Case 2, eq. (7) lower branch) is never demonstrated numerically.
10. **αc is target-size-independent** (depends only on rs, rc via (√(8rs²+rc²) − rc)/(2rs), saturating at √2) — an interesting, possibly conservative artifact of checking the sensing condition only through di ≤ rs around ϑ*.

## 8. Citation Value

**Role in the literature**: this is a current-generation IEEE TAC paper that pushes barrier/winning-region analysis from complete to partial information, from planar/circular targets to a general convex 3-D target, and from 1-vs-1 to Np-vs-1 — the missing quadrant relative to [8], [9] (partial information, simplified geometry) and [10]–[14] (3-D/convex targets, complete information). It is the canonical reference for three reusable devices: (i) the two-phase deployment/capture decomposition for time-varying information sets, (ii) the exact speed-ratio threshold αc = (√(8rs²+rc²) − rc)/(2rs) under which complete-information pursuit strategies stay feasible under sensing constraints, and (iii) the "fuzzy region" F = W̃P \ S together with the dichotomy WP = W̃P iff B̃ ⊂ int(S). The sensing-radius formula RS = ((α+1)/α)·max dist(x0Pi, Ωtar) − rc/α and its MES-deployment corollary turn barrier validity into a concrete sensor-specification and placement tool. In the corpus it pairs naturally with the same group's 1-vs-1 partial-information precursor (P067/P068, Ma & Liu, IEEE TCST 2025 — not cited by this paper) and with P100 ([12]-type analytical barriers). 

**Suggested citation sentences**: "Under sensing-limited defense, Ma et al. prove that the complete-information barrier of a convex 3-D target remains valid once the sensing region covers it, deriving the tight radius RS = ((α+1)/α)·max dist(xP, Ωtar) − rc/α and the exact speed-ratio threshold αc = (√(8rs²+rc²) − rc)/(2rs) beyond which no strategy modification is needed [P062]." Or: "The fuzzy region W̃P \ S of [P062] formalizes the states from which neither pursuit nor evasion is guaranteed under partial information, yielding the winning-region dichotomy WP = W̃P if B̃ ⊂ int(S) and WP = S \ W̃E otherwise."
