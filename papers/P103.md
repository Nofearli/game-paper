## 2. Research Background

The paper studies a 3D multiplayer reach-avoid differential game in which a plane T separates the Euclidean space into a play region Ω_play = {z > 0} and a goal region Ω_goal = {z ≤ 0}. A team of Np pursuers with heterogeneous speeds and heterogeneous nonzero capture radii defends the goal region against Ne evaders with heterogeneous speeds that try to penetrate T and enter Ω_goal; equivalently, evaders try to escape the play region while avoiding adversaries treated as dynamic obstacles. Such games are motivated by region protection/surveillance, robot competition, dynamic collision avoidance, and path planning through exits (citing Bullo et al. 2011; Duan et al. 2020; Oyler et al. 2016; Chen et al. 2017; Yan et al. 2019). Direct analysis is hard because of high dimensionality and multiple stages: level-set solutions of the HJI equation suffer the curse of dimensionality (a 12-state subgame already needs ~10^12 grid points at 10 points/dimension, Remark 3.2), and prior multiplayer analytical work (Chen et al. 2017; Yan et al. 2020; Agharkar et al. 2015; Shishika & Kumar 2018; Shishika & Paley 2019) mostly handles zero capture radius, homogeneous speeds, or non-cooperative/semi-open-loop strategies. The paper builds an analytical pipeline: subgame decomposition via Isaacs' evasion space, a coalitional game-of-kind criterion, an HJI-to-convex-program reduction, and a combinatorial matching layer with complexity analysis.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap

**Problem.** Design feedback capture strategies for the pursuit team that maximize the number of evaders prevented from entering Ω_goal, for 3D simple-motion players with heterogeneous speeds v_Pi, v_Ej and heterogeneous capture radii r_i ≥ 0, and extend to a bounded convex play region with a planar exit.

**Gaps as claimed by the authors** (p. 2, "Literature review"/"Contributions"):
1. [Authors' claim] Existing multiplayer reach-avoid analyses are restricted to zero capture radius and homogeneous players (Agharkar et al. 2015; Yan et al. 2019, 2020; Shishika & Kumar 2018); nonzero capture radii "greatly increases the complexity of explicit computation" (p. 5). — [Verified from the cited-text discussion as summarized in the paper: Chen et al. 2017 is described as suboptimal, numerical/approximate one-to-one basic solutions with semi-open-loop strategies and no cooperation beyond matching; Yan et al. 2020 solved assignment by 0–1 integer programming "without complexity analysis and polynomial-time algorithms". These characterizations are the authors' reading of those papers, not re-verified here against the originals.]
2. [Authors' claim] For constrained matching, prior work (Itai et al. 1978; Darmann et al. 2011; Öncan et al. 2013; Thomas 2016) established complexity results for various constrained-matching variants "however, none of the aforementioned results addresses our problems. Thus, to the best of our knowledge, this paper is the first that attempts to prove the NP-hardness and design approximation algorithms for the MBMC" (p. 2). — [Verified as the paper's own novelty claim; the MBMC with the specific conflict structure (29) of coalitional edges is indeed formulated fresh in this paper; no earlier MBMC NP-hardness reference is cited.]
3. [Authors' claim] Solving HJI equations in multiplayer games is intractable numerically; the paper offers "a new perspective on solving HJI equations ... by showing that for a special subgame the associated HJI equation can be solved via convex programming" (p. 2). — [Verified within the paper: Theorem 3.2 proves this for the special subgame of degree (19)–(21).]
4. [Stated open case] Speed ratios α_ij = 1 or α_ij < 1 (with r_i > 0) "require the separate analysis and we leave it for future work" (p. 4, end of Section 2.2). The paper assumes **all pursuers strictly faster than all evaders**.

## 4. Core Contributions

1. **(Modeling)** A 3D heterogeneous multiplayer reach-avoid game formulation (Section 2): simple motion (holonomic, instantaneous heading changes, controls on the unit sphere S²), plane-separator goal/play regions, heterogeneous capture sets C_i = {x : ‖x − x_Pi‖₂ ≤ r_i}, state-feedback information structure (deliberately not non-anticipative, since opponents' current inputs are unavailable; under feedback the game "may not necessarily admit a value", citing Cardaliaguet 1996, 1997 and Krasovskii et al. 1987, p. 3), Assumption 2.1 (generic initial deployment) and Assumption 2.2 (α_ij = v_Pi/v_Ej > 1 for all pairs).
2. **(Theory — subgame of kind)** Evasion-space (ES) machinery for a pursuit coalition vs. one evader: the generalized (capture-radius-bearing) Apollonius surface as zero level set of the potential f_ij (eq. (2)); strict convexity and boundedness of the closed ES (Lemma 3.1); interception point I(s,j) and its structural properties (Lemmas 3.2, 3.3); a constructive guaranteed winning feedback strategy (Theorem 3.1); a game-of-kind trichotomy and the headline result that **at most three pursuers are ever necessary** to defend the goal against one evader in 3D (Corollary 1, Lemma 3.3).
3. **(Theory — subgame of degree)** Proof that the value function of a special subgame of degree (evader maximizes capture proximity to the goal, pursuers the opposite; payoff J = z_Ej(t_f), eq. (19)–(20)) equals the optimal value of a convex program (Theorem 3.2, eq. (21)), i.e., the HJI equation is solved via KKT conditions of a convex program (Remark 3.1).
4. **(Algorithm/complexity)** Whole-game assembly via matching of coalitions (size ≤ 3, by Corollary 1) to evaders: bipartite graph (28) + conflict graph (29) + binary integer program (30) defining the Maximum Bipartite Matching with Conflict graph (MBMC); proof that MBMC is NP-hard via reduction from 3-dimensional matching (Theorem 4.1); polynomial-time Sequential Matching Algorithm (Algorithm 1) with 1/3-approximation (1/2 without 3-pursuer coalitions, exact without 2- and 3-pursuer coalitions) guarantees (Theorem 4.2); MBMC is APX-complete (Corollary 2).
5. **(Algorithm — closed loop)** Receding-horizon capture strategy (Algorithm 2): re-solve (exactly or approximately) the matching each step Δ, keep the previous matching unless a strictly larger one appears, apply ES-based strategies to matched coalitions; monotone improvement of the guaranteed capture count but no global optimality over the whole horizon (Remark 4.2).
6. **(Extension)** Bounded convex play region Ω^b_play = {x : z ≥ 0, g(x) ≥ 0} with planar exit T^b (eq. (31)): redefined interception point via convex program (32) with the extra constraint g(x) ≥ 0; uniqueness (Lemma 5.1) and the analogous ES-based winning strategy (Lemma 5.2); matching results carry over (Remark 5.1).
7. **(Experiment)** Two Matlab numerical studies (Section 6): Np = 8 vs Ne = 9 unbounded (receding horizon captures 8/9 vs. 7/9 for a sticky-matching baseline) and Np = 7 vs Ne = 7 bounded convex region (6 captured, 1 escapes).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results (exhaustive)

### 5.0 Game model, information and winning conditions

- **Dynamics (p. 3, eq. (1))**: ẋ_Pi = v_Pi u_Pi, x_Pi(0) = x0_Pi; ẋ_Ej = v_Ej u_Ej, x_Ej(0) = x0_Ej; controls u_Pi, u_Ej ∈ S² (instantaneous heading, simple motion). Positions in R³; plane T = {z = 0}, Ω_goal = {z ≤ 0}, Ω_play = {z > 0}.
- **Capture set (p. 3)**: C := ∪_{i=1}^{Np} C_i, C_i = {x ∈ R³ : ‖x − x_Pi‖₂ ≤ r_i}; evader Ej is captured when its distance to at least one pursuer equals that pursuer's radius; coalition capture set C_s = ∪_{i∈s} C_i (p. 4).
- **Information structure (p. 3, Section 2.2)**: state feedback — each player chooses u based on the information set {x_Pi, x_Ej} for all players. Non-anticipative structures (Elliott–Kalton; Margellos & Lygeros 2011; Mitchell et al. 2005; Yan et al. 2020) are rejected as too strong (they instantiate Stackelberg play); the authors note the feedback game may not admit a value (Cardaliaguet 1996, 1997; Krasovskii et al. 1987, Section 3) but construct one-sided guaranteed strategies.
- **Assumption 2.1 (Initial Deployment, p. 3)**: (1) distinct pursuer positions; (2) distinct evader positions; (3) ‖x0_Ej − x0_Pi‖₂ > r_i for all pairs (no initial capture); (4) x0_Ej ∈ Ω_play for all evaders, x0_Pi ∈ R³ unrestricted.
- **Assumption 2.2 (Speed Ratio, p. 3)**: α_ij := v_Pi/v_Ej > 1 for all i, j — every pursuer strictly faster than every evader. Cases v_Pi < v_Ej with r_i > 0, or v_Pi = v_Ej, are explicitly deferred to future work (p. 4).
- **Termination/winning (p. 4)**: For coalition Ps vs. evader Ej, Ps wins iff it captures Ej (x_Ej ∈ C_s) before Ej reaches Ω_goal; otherwise Ej wins. Whole-game objective: maximize the number of evaders captured before they enter Ω_goal.

### 5.1 Evasion space and potential function (Section 3.2)

- **Definition 3.1 (Evasion Space, p. 4)**: E(s,j) = set of positions Ej can reach without being captured by Ps "regardless of Ps' control input"; ∂E(s,j) is the BES (boundary of evasion space). Based on Isaacs (1965, Section 6.7).
- **Definition 3.2 (Potential Function, p. 4, eq. (2))**: for ‖x_Ej − x_Pi‖₂ > r_i,
  f_ij(x) = ‖x − x_Pi‖₂ − α_ij‖x − x_Ej‖₂ − r_i, with gradient (eq. (3)) ∇f_ij(x) = (x−x_Pi)/‖x−x_Pi‖₂ − α_ij (x−x_Ej)/‖x−x_Ej‖₂.
- **ES as superlevel set (p. 4, eq. (4))**: E(i,j) = {x ∈ R³ : f_ij(x) > 0}, ∂E(i,j) = {f_ij(x) = 0}, closure Ē(i,j) = {f_ij(x) ≥ 0}. If r_i = 0 the BES is "the Apollonius circle in 3D" (i.e., the Apollonius sphere, Isaacs 1965); for r_i > 0 "the analysis is far beyond the scope of Apollonius circle" (p. 5).
- **Coalition ES (p. 5, per Isaacs 1965 Section 6.8)**: E(s,j) = {x : f_ij(x) > 0, i ∈ s} = ∩_{i∈s} E(i,j); ∂E(s,j) ⊆ ∪_{i∈s} ∂E(i,j); Ē(s,j) = {x : f_ij(x) ≥ 0, i ∈ s}.

### 5.2 Lemma 3.1 (ES for One Pursuer, p. 4)

**Statement**: For the ES E(i,j) w.r.t. Pi ∈ P and Ej ∈ E (α_ij > 1, ‖x_Ej − x_Pi‖₂ > r_i), the closure Ē(i,j) is **bounded and strictly convex**.

**Proof approach** (pp. 4–5):
1. Polar coordinates centered at the evader: x = x_Ej + ρe, ρ ∈ R+, e ∈ S², with e parametrized by rotations θ ∈ [0,π], ψ ∈ [0,2π] and arbitrary initial rotations θ0 ∈ [0,π], ψ0 ∈ [0,2π].
2. On the BES, f_ij = 0 becomes ‖x_Ej + ρe − x_Pi‖₂ − α_ij ρ − r_i = 0, solved as **eq. (5)**: ρ = (h1 + h2)/(α²_ij − 1), with **eq. (6)**: h1(θ,ψ) = (x_Ej − x_Pi)⊤e − α_ij r_i, h2(θ,ψ) = √(h1² + (α²_ij − 1)(‖x_Ej − x_Pi‖₂² − r_i²)). Since α_ij > 1 and ‖x_Ej − x_Pi‖₂ > r_i, h2 > 0, hence ρ is bounded and positive ⇒ Ē(i,j) bounded.
3. Chain rule gives **eq. (7)**: ∂h2/∂ψ = (h1/h2) ∂h1/∂ψ, and **eq. (8)**: first/second derivatives of ρ w.r.t. ψ.
4. **Decisive intermediate inequality (p. 5, unnumbered, between (8) and Lemma 3.2)**: ρ² + 2(∂ρ/∂ψ)² − ρ ∂²ρ/∂ψ² ≥ ((h2+h1)²/(α²_ij−1)²)(1 − (1/h2)∂²h1/∂ψ²), and h2 > ∂²h1/∂ψ² = −h1 − α_ij r_i is proven by two cases: if h1 > −α_ij r_i trivial since h2 > 0; if h1 ≤ −α_ij r_i then h2² − (∂²h1/∂ψ²)² = (α²_ij−1)(‖x_Ej−x_Pi‖₂² − r_i²) − α_ij r_i(2h1 + α_ij r_i) > α²_ij r_i² ≥ 0.
5. Hence ρ² + 2(ρ′)² − ρ ρ″ > 0 for all ψ; by **Lemma A.1** (polar-coordinate curvature criterion, eq. (36), p. 11) Ē(i,j) is strictly convex for each fixed θ; because any admissible θ0, ψ0 can be chosen and θ sweeps [0,π], strict convexity holds globally.
- **Physical meaning**: the closed guaranteed-safe region of the evader against one faster pursuer is a strictly convex "ball-like" set (Fig. 2(a)) — a capture-radius-deformed Apollonius sphere. **Extensibility**: intersection of finitely many such sets is bounded and strictly convex, giving Ē(s,j) for coalitions (p. 5).

### 5.3 Lemma 3.2 (Properties of the Interception Point, p. 5)

**Setup**: **Definition 3.3 (Interception Point, p. 5)**: if Ē(s,j) ∩ Ω_goal = ∅, I(s,j) ∈ Ē(s,j) is the unique point closest to Ω_goal (i.e., minimal z since Ω_goal = {z ≤ 0}).

**Statement**: (i) I(s,j) ∈ ∂E(s,j). (ii) For |s| = 3 with Ej and the three pursuers non-coplanar, if I(s,j) ∈ ∩_{i∈s} ∂E(i,j) then there is a plane in which I(s,j) is an intersection point of two strictly convex closed curves. (iii) For |s| = 3 non-coplanar, ∩_{i∈s} ∂E(i,j) contains **at most 4 points**.

**Proof approach** (p. 5):
1. (i) from strict convexity of Ē(s,j) and Definition 3.3.
2. (ii) Writing I(s,j) = x_Ej + ρe, membership in all three BESs gives **eq. (9)**: ‖x_Ej − x_Pi + ρe‖₂ = α_ij ρ + r_i, ∀i ∈ s, equivalently **eq. (10)**: ρ² = c_i + ρ(m_i⊤e − b_i) with m_i = 2(x_Ej−x_Pi)/(α²_ij−1), b_i = 2α_ij r_i/(α²_ij−1), c_i = (‖x_Ej−x_Pi‖₂² − r_i²)/(α²_ij−1).
3. Eliminating ρ² pairwise yields **eq. (11)**: ((c2−c3)m1 + (c3−c1)m2 + (c1−c2)m3)⊤e = (c3−c2)(b2−b1) − (c2−c1)(b3−b2) — a linear (planar) constraint on e; non-coplanarity makes m1, m2, m3 linearly independent, so I(s,j) lies in a plane; intersecting that plane with ∂E(1,j) and ∂E(2,j) gives two strictly convex closed curves.
4. (iii) Given ρ, **eq. (12)**: e = [m1 m2 m3]^{-⊤}[(ρ²−c1)/ρ + b1, (ρ²−c2)/ρ + b2, (ρ²−c3)/ρ + b3]; ‖e‖₂ = 1 becomes a **quartic equation in ρ** ⇒ at most 4 solutions.
- **Physical meaning**: the candidate "hand-off" points where three capture surfaces meet are finite (≤ 4), which powers the ≤3-pursuer degeneration.

### 5.4 Lemma 3.3 (Degeneration of the Interception Point, p. 6)

**Statement**: For any Ps ∈ [P]+ and Ej with Ē(s,j) ∩ Ω_goal = ∅, there exists a subcoalition s1 ⊆ s with **|s1| ≤ 3** and I(s1,j) = I(s,j).

**Proof approach** (p. 6, Fig. 3):
1. Trivial for |s| ≤ 3; assume |s| ≥ 4 and consider a triple Ps1 = {P1,P2,P3} whose interception point depends on all three (I(s1,j) ∈ ∩_{i∈s1} ∂E(i,j)).
2. **Case 1 (non-coplanar)**: by Lemma 3.2(ii)–(iii) I(s1,j) is one of ≤ 4 points H1–H4 where two strictly convex curves meet in a plane. Adding a fourth pursuer P4 contributes another strictly convex curve in the same plane. If I(s,j) = H3 the fourth is redundant; if I(s,j) = H2 (resp. H4) the new curve must cross from inside to outside ∩_{i∈s1} Ē(i,j) at H2, and then the interception point is already determined by {P1, P3, P4} (resp. by the set replacing P1), so P2 (resp. P1) is redundant; I(s,j) = H1 is impossible because both curves decrease (in z) at H1. Hence a fourth pursuer never increases the number of pursuers the interception point necessarily depends on.
3. **Case 2 (coplanar)**: m1, m2, m3 linearly dependent; system **eq. (13)**: [m1 m2 m3]⊤e = [(ρ²−c1)/ρ + b1, (ρ²−c2)/ρ + b2, (ρ²−c3)/ρ + b3]. If (13) admits solutions, all intersection points of ∂E(1,j) and ∂E(2,j) lie on ∂E(3,j) ⇒ P3 redundant; if not, some Pi ∈ Ps1 has I(s1,j) ∉ ∂E(i,j) ⇒ Pi redundant.
- **Proof gap (minor, critical observation)**: the Case-1 argument that "the z-coordinate of the points on these two curves decreases along the arrow" and the H1/H2/H4 redundancy reasoning are argued pictorially via Fig. 3 (p. 6) rather than by an explicit analytic inequality; the geometric monotonicity claim is stated, not formalized. All other steps are constructive.

### 5.5 Theorem 3.1 (ES-Based Strategies for Multiple Pursuers, p. 6) — the guaranteed winning feedback strategy

**Statement**: For any Ps and Ej with Ē(s,j) ∩ Ω_goal = ∅, let s1 ⊆ s with I(s1,j) = I(s,j), |s1| ≤ 3 (exists by Lemma 3.3). If every Pi ∈ Ps1 adopts the feedback strategy
u_Pi = (I(s1,j) − x_Pi)/‖I(s1,j) − x_Pi‖₂  (heading straight at the interception point),
then Ps1 guarantees that Ē(s,j) does not approach Ω_goal, i.e., **ż_{I(s1,j)} ≥ 0 for any u_Ej ∈ S²**; moreover ż_{I(s1,j)} = 0 **iff** Ej adopts u_Ej = (I(s1,j) − x_Ej)/‖I(s1,j) − x_Ej‖₂. (ES-based strategies are pure feedback; the strategies of Chen et al. 2017 are semi-open-loop, p. 6.)

**Proof approach** (pp. 6–7):
1. x_I = [x_I y_I z_I]⊤ is the unique solution of the convex program minimize z subject to f_ij(x) ≥ 0 ∀i ∈ s1 (displayed above eq. (14), p. 6).
2. **KKT conditions (eq. (14))**: [0 0 −1]⊤ = Σ_{i∈s1} λ_i ∇f_ij(x_I), f_ij(x_I) ≥ 0, λ_i ≤ 0, λ_i f_ij(x_I) = 0; **slackness classification (eq. (15))**: active set s1^=0 = {i : f_ij(x_I) = 0, λ_i ≤ 0} and inactive set s1^>0 = {i : f_ij(x_I) > 0, λ_i = 0} (may be empty).
3. Differentiate the active constraints f_ij(x_I(t)) = 0 in time, using (1)–(3) and that x_Pi, x_Ej in f_ij are time-varying — **eq. (16)**: d f_ij(x_I)/dt = 0 ⇒ (x_I−x_Pi)⊤(ẋ_I − v_Pi u_Pi)/‖x_I−x_Pi‖₂ = α_ij (x_I−x_Ej)⊤(ẋ_I − v_Ej u_Ej)/‖x_I−x_Ej‖₂.
4. **Decisive inequality (eqs. (17)–(18), p. 7)**: with u_Pi = (x_I−x_Pi)/‖x_I−x_Pi‖₂, ∇f_ij(x_I)⊤ẋ_I = v_Pi − v_Pi (x_I−x_Ej)⊤u_Ej/‖x_I−x_Ej‖₂ ≥ v_Pi − v_Pi = 0, for **any** u_Ej ∈ S²; equality iff u_Ej = (x_I−x_Ej)/‖x_I−x_Ej‖₂ (since (x_I−x_Ej)⊤u_Ej ≤ ‖x_I−x_Ej‖₂ by Cauchy–Schwarz).
5. Combine with (14)–(15): −ż_I = [0 0 −1]ẋ_I = Σ_{i∈s1} λ_i ∇f_ij(x_I)⊤ẋ_I = Σ_{i∈s1^=0} λ_i (∇f_ij(x_I)⊤ẋ_I) ≤ 0 (inactive terms vanish by λ_i = 0; active terms are ≤ 0 because λ_i ≤ 0 and the factor ≥ 0) ⇒ ż_I ≥ 0, with equality iff the evader heads to I.
- **Local/global**: global in the winning state set {Ē(s,j) ∩ Ω_goal = ∅}; analytic. **Physical meaning**: each defending pursuer simply chases the (common, unique, convexly computable) interception point; the lowest point of the evader's safe region then recedes from the goal plane no matter what the evader does. **Extensibility**: the KKT-sign argument is reused verbatim for the bounded region (Lemma 5.2) and for the value function (Theorem 3.2).

### 5.6 Corollary 1 (Game of Kind, p. 7)

**Statement**: The winner between coalition Ps and evader Ej is determined by the cardinality of Ē(s,j) ∩ Ω_goal: **empty ⇒ Ps wins; more than one element ⇒ Ej wins; a unique element ⇒ tied**. Moreover, "the maximum number of pursuers required to capture an evader before the evader reaches the goal region, is three."
- Proof: "directly follows from Definition 3.1 and Theorem 3.1" (p. 7). [Critical observation: the pursuit-winning and ≤3 branches are established constructively (Definition 3.1 + Theorem 3.1 + Lemma 3.3), while the evader-winning branch (|Ē(s,j) ∩ Ω_goal| > 1) rests on Isaacs' ES semantics (Definition 3.1) rather than a constructive evader strategy proof in this paper; the tie case corresponds to a single entry point on T.]
- **This is the paper's winning-region characterization**: coalition Ps guards the goal against Ej **iff |Ē(s,j) ∩ Ω_goal| ≤ 1**, and the ES — intersection of ≤ 3 generalized Apollonius closed balls — is the certificate. This ≤1 condition is exactly the edge rule in the matching graph (28).

### 5.7 Theorem 3.2 (Value Function, p. 7) — HJI equation solved by convex programming

**Subgame of degree (p. 7, eq. (19)–(20))**: among winning states, terminal set Ψ = {(x_s, x_Ej) : ∃ i ∈ s, x_Ej ∈ C_i}; payoff J(u_s, u_Ej; x0_s, x0_Ej) = z_Ej(t_f) (height at capture); the evader tries to be captured as close to the goal as possible, the coalition seeks the opposite: V(x0_s, x0_Ej) = max_{u_s} min_{u_Ej} J.

**Statement**: For the game (1), (19), (20) with Ē(s,j) ∩ Ω_goal = ∅, at every state (x_s, x_Ej) where V is differentiable,
V(x_s, x_Ej) = minimize_{x∈R³} z subject to f_ij(x) ≥ 0, ∀ i ∈ s  **(eq. (21))** — i.e., V is the z-coordinate of the (coalition) interception point, computable as a convex program.

**Proof approach** (pp. 7–8):
1. On the differentiability set V satisfies the HJI equation **eq. (22)**: −∂V/∂t = max_{u_s} min_{u_Ej} {Σ_{i∈s} (∂V/∂x_Pi)⊤ v_Pi u_Pi + (∂V/∂x_Ej)⊤ v_Ej u_Ej} (Isaacs 1965, Ch. 4); time-independence of Ψ and J (Isaacs' main equation 1, eq. 4.2.1 with formulas 2.1.1, 2.3.1, 2.4.1) rewrites it as **eq. (23)**: 0 = max_{u_s} min_{u_Ej} dV/dt.
2. Let x_I solve (21) and V = z_I. KKT **eq. (24)** (same form as (14) but over all i ∈ s); V̇ = ż_I = [0 0 1]ẋ_I = −Σ_{i∈s} λ_i ∇f_ij(x_I)⊤ẋ_I **(eq. (25))**; slackness splits s into s^=0 (active) and s^>0 (inactive) **(eq. (26))**.
3. For active i, **eq. (27)**: ∇f_ij(x_I)⊤ẋ_I ≥ v_Pi (x_I−x_Pi)⊤u_Pi/‖x_I−x_Pi‖₂ − α_ij v_Ej (x_I−x_Ej)⊤u_Ej/‖x_I−x_Ej‖₂.
4. **Decisive equation (p. 8, unnumbered chain after (27))**: since the u_s-term and u_Ej-term separate, max_{u_s} min_{u_Ej} V̇ = max_{u_s} Σ_{i∈s^=0} λ_i (−v_Pi (x_I−x_Pi)⊤u_Pi/‖·‖) + min_{u_Ej} Σ_{i∈s^=0} λ_i α_ij v_Ej (x_I−x_Ej)⊤u_Ej/‖·‖ = −Σ_{i∈s^=0} λ_i v_Pi + Σ_{i∈s^=0} λ_i α_ij v_Ej = 0, using λ_i ≤ 0 and the maximizers/minimizer u_Pi = (x_I−x_Pi)/‖·‖, u_Ej = (x_I−x_Ej)/‖·‖, and v_Pi = α_ij v_Ej. Hence V = z_I satisfies the HJI equation (23).
5. **Terminal condition**: at capture ‖x_Ej − x_Pi‖₂ = r_i for some i, which forces Ē(i,j) = {x_Ej} (for x ≠ x_Ej, f_ij(x) < 0 by the triangle inequality and α_ij > 1), so the program (21) has the unique solution x_I = x_Ej and V = z_I = z_Ej, matching (19).
- **Scope caveat (authors', p. 7)**: V need not be differentiable across barrier surfaces (Lewin 1994, Section 5.3) or singular surfaces (Lewin 1994, Section 9.1); finding the differentiability set is "quite hard and beyond the scope of this paper"; empirically V is "highly likely to be differentiable over the winning space of one player/team" (Garcia et al. 2019, 2020). So the result is **local to the differentiable region of the pursuit-winning domain**, analytic, and exact (no gridding).
- **Remark 3.1 (p. 8)**: the KKT–HJI connection ("strategies are the gradients of the value function... obtained through the optimal solution to a convex optimization problem") is proposed as a method for more general differential games.
- **Remark 3.2 (p. 8)**: a 3-pursuer coalition game has 12 states; a level-set solve at 10 grid points/dimension needs 10^12 grid points, whereas (21) is a small convex program — accuracy independent of grid resolution.

### 5.8 Matching layer: bipartite graph, conflict graph, MBMC (Section 4.1)

- **Coalition count (p. 8)**: Np(Np² + 5)/6 = Np + Np(Np−1)/2 + Np(Np−1)(Np−2)/6 vertices No (all coalitions of size ≤ 3; Corollary 1 makes larger ones unnecessary; footnote 1: extendable to any fixed coalition size "rather routinely").
- **Bipartite graph (p. 8, eq. (28))**: U = [P]³, V = E, E = { e_sj : Ps ∈ U, Ej ∈ V, |Ē(s,j) ∩ Ω_goal| ≤ 1, ∀ s1 ⊊ s, |Ē(s1,j) ∩ Ω_goal| > 1 } — an edge exists iff Ps can capture Ej in Ω_play or at T (win-or-tie per Corollary 1) **and Ps is minimal** with this property. Note |U| = No, |V| = Ne.
- **Conflict graph (p. 9, eq. (29))**: C = (E, Ē) with Ē = {(e_sj, e_pq) : s ≠ p, s ∩ p ≠ ∅} — two coalitions sharing a pursuer cannot both be used.
- **BIP formulation of MBMC (p. 9, eq. (30))**: maximize Σ_{e_sj∈E} a_sj subject to Σ_{s∈U} a_sj ≤ 1 (∀ Ej ∈ V), Σ_{j∈V} a_sj ≤ 1 (∀ Ps ∈ U), a_sj + a_pq ≤ 1 (∀ (e_sj, e_pq) ∈ Ē), a_sj ∈ {0,1}, a_sj = 0 for non-edges.
- **Remark 4.1 (p. 9)**: if the team plays the optimal matching a* to the end, exactly N* evaders are captured after time t — an **open-loop** guarantee (each edge = a guaranteed win-or-tie by Theorem 3.1, so any feasible matching's size is a guaranteed capture count).

### 5.9 Theorem 4.1 (Hardness of the Matching, p. 9)

**Statement**: MBMC (30) is **NP-hard**.

**Proof approach** (p. 9):
1. Polynomial reduction from the NP-complete 3-dimensional matching problem (Karp 1972): instance I = (X, Y, Z, T), |X| = |Y| = |Z| = m, triples T ⊆ X×Y×Z; question: exists M ⊆ T, |M| = m, no two triples agreeing in any coordinate?
2. Construct G: U = X×Y, V = Z, E = {((i,j),k) : (i,j,k) ∈ T}; conflict graph Ē = {(((i1,j1),k1), ((i2,j2),k2)) : i1 = i2 or j1 = j2}. Interpretation: a game with 2m pursuers (X ∪ Y) and m evaders (Z) where evader k can be captured only by the cooperative pair (i, j) with (i,j,k) ∈ T; 1- and 3-pursuer coalitions absent.
3. **Decisive equivalence**: G with C has a complete matching ⇔ I has a 3-dimensional matching. (⇒ conflict graph forbids shared X/Y coordinates, matching forbids shared Z; ⇒ immediate.)
4. Since deciding completeness reduces to computing the maximum matching, MBMC is NP-hard.
- Note the reduction uses **only 2-pursuer coalitions**, which is what makes the APX argument in Corollary 2 work.

### 5.10 Algorithm 1 (Sequential Matching Algorithm, p. 9) and Theorem 4.2 (p. 9–10)

**Algorithm 1** (Input: bipartite graph G with U = [P]³, V = E; Output: matching M):
1. G1 over 1-pursuer vertices: compute maximum matching M1 by **maximum network flow** (Ford & Fulkerson 1962); let A1, B1 be its pursuer/evader sets.
2. Remove all vertices containing a player of A1 ∪ B1; G2 over ≤ 2-pursuer vertices of the remainder: compute M2 by **local search maximum with size-2 swaps** (Hurkens & Schrijver 1989; Cygan 2013).
3. Remove players of A1 ∪ A2 ∪ B1 ∪ B2; G3 over the remaining 3-pursuer vertices: compute M3 by local search with size-2 swaps.
4. Return M = M1 ∪ M2 ∪ M3.

**Theorem 4.2 (Constant-Factor Approximation, p. 9–10)**: Algorithm 1 is polynomial time and: (i) a **1/3-approximation** for MBMC; (ii) a **1/2-approximation** if the MBMC optimum uses no 3-pursuer coalitions; (iii) **exact** if the optimum uses no 2- or 3-pursuer coalitions.

**Proof (Appendix B, pp. 12–13)**, with M* = M1* ∪ M2* ∪ M3* optimal, M_i* edges incident to i-pursuer vertices:
1. **Eq. (37)**: |M1*| ≤ |M1| = |A1| = |B1| ≤ |M| (M1 is a maximum matching of the 1-pursuer subgraph).
2. Re-inserting one removed pursuer P_i ∈ A1 into U2 raises the subgraph maximum by ≤ 1; local search with size-2 swaps is a 1/2-approximation for 3-uniform-like set packing (each edge involves 3 players), so |M2′| ≤ 2|M2| + 1; adding all of A1 and B1: |M2″| ≤ 2|M2| + |A1| + |B1|; since G2″ contains all 1- and 2-pursuer edges, **eq. (38)**: |M1*| + |M2*| ≤ |M2″| ≤ 2|M2| + 2|M1| ≤ 2|M|.
3. Analogously for G3: |M3′| ≤ |M3| + 1 per re-inserted player, and **eq. (39)**: |M*| ≤ 3|M3| + |A1| + |A2| + |B1| + |B2| = 3|M3| + 2|M1| + 3|M2| ≤ 3|M|, using |A1| = |B1| = |M1| and |A2| = 2|B2| = 2|M2|.
4. (37)–(39) give |M*| ≤ 3|M|.
- **Corollary 2 (Class of Complexity, p. 10)**: MBMC is **APX-complete** — APX-hardness because APX-complete 3-dimensional matching (Kann 1991) reduces to MBMC; membership in APX from Theorem 4.2's constant-factor approximation.

### 5.11 Algorithm 2 (Receding Horizon Strategy, p. 10) and Remark 4.2

**Algorithm 2**: Input P, E, all positions, M_a ← ∅, L_c ← 0. Repeat: (i) M ← Max(P, E, states) — exact (e.g., cutting-plane methods) or approximate (Algorithm 1) solution of (30); (ii) if |M| > |M_a| or L_c == 1 then M_a ← M, L_c ← 0 ("sticky" matching: never switch unless strictly more evaders can be captured); (iii) assign coalitions of M_a, apply ES-based strategies (Theorem 3.1) for a short duration Δ, unmatched pursuers and all evaders play "some (any) strategy"; (iv) update positions; (v) remove captured/arrived evaders from E, set L_c ← 1 upon any capture; until E = ∅.
- **Guarantee structure**: at every re-matching instant the current matching size is a guaranteed open-loop capture count from that instant (Remark 4.1); switching only to strictly larger matchings makes the guaranteed count **monotonically non-decreasing** (Remark 4.2). [Critical observation] The step-Δ application of the ES strategy means the winning certificate Ē(s,j) ∩ Ω_goal = ∅ is evaluated at re-matching instants; between updates, feasibility is maintained by Theorem 3.1's feedback property (ż_I ≥ 0 for any evader input).
- **Remark 4.2 (authors' limitation)**: the rolling one-step greedy strategy "does not come with global optimality guarantees" — maximality of the total number captured over the entire horizon is open, both for exact and approximate matchings.

### 5.12 Bounded convex play region (Section 5)

- **Setup (p. 10, eq. (31))**: Ω^b_play = {x : z ≥ 0, g(x) ≥ 0} closed convex, exit T^b = {x ∈ Ω^b_play : z = 0}, g differentiable with {g ≥ 0} convex; Assumption 2.1(4) becomes x0_Pi, x0_Ej ∈ Ω^b_play.
- **Interception point (p. 10, eq. (32))**: if Ē(s,j) ∩ T^b = ∅, I_b(s,j) = solution of minimize z subject to f_ij(x) ≥ 0 ∀i ∈ s, g(x) ≥ 0 (the z ≥ 0 constraint "holds naturally").
- **Lemma 5.1 (Uniqueness, p. 10)**: I_b(s,j) is unique. Proof (p. 11): if the unconstrained interception point I(s,j) lies in Ω^b_play (Fig. 4(a)) then I_b = I; otherwise sweep a plane T1 parallel to T^b from T^b into the play region — the first tangency between the moving strictly convex section of Ē(s,j) and the convex section of Ω^b_play defines the unique tangent point I_b (Fig. 4(b)).
- **Lemma 5.2 (ES-Based Strategy, p. 11)**: if Ē(s,j) ∩ T^b = ∅ and every Pi ∈ Ps plays u_Pi = (I_b(s,j) − x_Pi)/‖I_b(s,j) − x_Pi‖₂, then ż_{I_b(s,j)} ≥ 0 for any u_Ej ∈ S², with equality iff u_Ej = (I_b(s,j) − x_Ej)/‖·‖. Proof (p. 11): the boundary motion satisfies dg(x_I^b)/dt = 0 ⇒ ∇g⊤ẋ_I^b = 0 **(eq. (33))**; KKT with boundary multiplier **(eq. (34))**: [0 0 −1]⊤ = Σ_{i∈s} λ_i ∇f_ij(x_I^b) + λ_g ∇g(x_I^b), λ_i, λ_g ≤ 0, complementary slackness; active-constraint inequality **(eq. (35))**: ∇f_ij(x_I^b)⊤ẋ_I^b ≥ 0; hence −ż_I^b = Σ_{i∈s} λ_i ∇f_ij⊤ẋ_I^b + λ_g ∇g⊤ẋ_I^b ≤ 0 (the λ_g term vanishes by (33)).
- **Remark 5.1 (game of kind, p. 11)**: Ē(s,j) ∩ T^b empty ⇒ Ps wins; more than one element ⇒ Ej wins; unique ⇒ tie. Since Ē(s,j) ∩ Ω^b_play ⊆ Ē(s,j), at most three pursuers remain necessary and the matching results of Section 4 apply verbatim.

### 5.13 Appendix A: Lemma A.1 (Convexity of Sets in Polar Coordinates, p. 11)

**Statement**: For a twice differentiable simple 2D closed curve ρ : [0,2π] → R+ with ρ(0) = ρ(2π), the set consisting of the curve and its interior is strictly convex if ρ² + 2(dρ/dψ)² − ρ d²ρ/dψ² > 0 for all ψ **(eq. (36))**.
**Proof (p. 12)**: the polar curvature formula κ = (ρ² + 2(ρ′)² − ρ ρ″)/(ρ² + (ρ′)²)^{3/2} (Abbena et al. 2017, Lemma 3.7) gives κ > 0; by Toponogov (2006, Problem 1.7.6) the curve is convex; strict positivity excludes line segments, hence strict convexity of the curve and then of the enclosed set (supporting hyperplane at every boundary point, closed, nonempty interior). This lemma is the analytic engine behind Lemma 3.1.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues

**Author-stated**:
1. All pursuers must be strictly faster than all evaders (Assumption 2.2, α_ij > 1); the equal-speed case and slower-pursuer-with-radius case are "left for future work" (p. 4).
2. Theorem 3.2 holds only where the value function is differentiable; "finding the set of states at which V is differentiable is quite hard and beyond the scope of this paper" — barrier and singular surfaces are not handled (p. 7, citing Lewin 1994).
3. The receding-horizon strategy has no global optimality guarantee over the whole horizon (Remark 4.2, p. 10); the monotonicity argument guarantees only non-decreasing open-loop capture counts.
4. MBMC is NP-hard and APX-complete, so exact matching scales poorly; the polynomial algorithm is only a 1/3-approximation in general (Theorem 4.2, Corollary 2).
5. The open-loop matching guarantee (Remark 4.1) counts win-or-tie edges; the objective is capture-count maximization "partially dealing with this game" (p. 1) — the whole game is not solved in closed form.
6. Pursuers beyond the matched coalitions, and all evaders, play "some (any) strategy" in Algorithm 2 — evader behavior is not modeled adversarially inside the receding-horizon loop beyond the ES worst-case guarantee (p. 10, line 7).

**Critical observations (mine, with page/eq. anchors)**:
7. Lemma 3.3's Case 1 (the headline "three pursuers suffice in 3D") is argued through Fig. 3's qualitative curve-monotonicity ("the z-coordinate ... decreases along the arrow", p. 6); a fully analytic version of the H1–H4 case analysis is not given — this is the weakest link in an otherwise KKT/convexity-based chain.
8. Corollary 1's evader-winning branch (|Ē(s,j) ∩ Ω_goal| > 1 ⇒ Ej wins) and the tie branch are asserted "directly" from Definition 3.1/Theorem 3.1 (p. 7); no constructive evader strategy or rigorous proof that two safe goal entries cannot both be neutralized is provided.
9. The ES semantics "reachable without being captured regardless of Ps' control input" (Definition 3.1) is taken from Isaacs (1965, Sections 6.7–6.8); the paper does not re-prove that the intersection of pairwise ESs is the coalition ES (it cites Isaacs 6.8, p. 5), and E(s,j) = ∩_i E(i,j) implicitly assumes the evader can handle pursuers one at a time — a subtlety inherited, not re-derived.
10. In Algorithm 2 the ES certificate is computed at discrete instants with step Δ; feasibility between updates relies on feedback continuity of the ES strategy, but no explicit bound relating Δ to the margin ‖x_I − T‖ is derived (p. 10).
11. Experiments are two single-run illustrations with unreported speeds, radii, Δ and initial conditions (Section 6) — reproducibility is limited, and no timing data substantiates Remark 3.2's scalability claim against level-set methods.
12. The matching layer is memoryless about coalitions: an edge in (28) requires strict minimality (all proper subcoalitions lose), so a coalition that wins only because of a favorable transient (e.g., an evader near T) is handled only through re-matching; interplay between the sticky rule and minimality is not analyzed.

## 8. Citation Value

**Paragraph role**: This is the canonical reference for **analytical 3D heterogeneous multiplayer reach-avoid games with nonzero capture radii**: it supplies (a) the capture-radius-generalized Apollonius/evasion-space certificate Ē(s,j) = {f_ij ≥ 0, i ∈ s} as the win/tie/lose criterion for a pursuer coalition vs. one evader; (b) the "at most three pursuers are necessary in 3D" degeneration theorem that collapses the coalition space to O(Np³); (c) the HJI-equals-convex-program result (KKT–Isaacs bridge) for the subgame of degree; (d) the MBMC formulation with NP-hardness, APX-completeness, and a 1/3-approximation sequential algorithm; and (e) the receding-horizon pursuit architecture with monotone guaranteed capture counts, transferable to bounded convex domains with exits. It is the direct 3D/nonzero-radius successor of Chen et al. (2017, TAC) and Yan et al. (2020, TRO), and a natural building block for later work on multiplayer reach-avoid reviews, enclosure-based pursuit, and matching-based defense.

**Suggested citation sentences**:
- "Yan et al. decomposed the 3D heterogeneous multiplayer reach-avoid game into pursuer-coalition-versus-single-evader subgames certified by generalized Apollonius (evasion-space) surfaces, proved that at most three pursuers are ever needed to guard the goal against one evader, and showed that the associated subgame-of-degree HJI equation reduces to a convex program whose KKT conditions recover the Isaacs equation."
- "To assign coalitions to evaders, the authors formulated the maximum bipartite matching problem with conflict graph (MBMC), proved its NP-hardness and APX-completeness via reduction from 3-dimensional matching, and embedded a polynomial-time 1/3-approximation into a receding-horizon capture strategy with monotonically non-decreasing guaranteed capture counts."
