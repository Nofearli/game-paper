## 2. Research Background
Geometric solutions of reach-avoid games (attacker vs. defender protecting a target) are mature for simple-motion players, where Apollonius circles (or Cartesian ovals with nonzero capture radius) describe dominance regions and optimal trajectories are straight lines. Real vehicles, however, are second-order systems: speed cannot jump, turning requires acceleration, and damping bounds the terminal speed. Recent work moved toward richer dynamics — Dubins cars and differential-drive robots, double integrators (Coon & Panagou [31]; Wei et al. [32]), first-order missile models [33], and damped double integrators for pursuit–evasion via Hamilton–Jacobi–Isaacs equations (Li, Wang & Xie [34], IEEE TAC 2023). The damped double integrator is arguably the simplest model capturing speed saturation ($\|v_i\|\to u_{im}/\mu$) and curved optimal paths, but reach-avoid games under this dynamics had not been studied, and even the geometry of a damped-double-integrator player's motion under an optimal strategy was unknown. This paper fills that gap for the canonical one-attacker/one-defender game with a static point target, in the defender-winning regime.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
**Problem.** One attacker $A$ and one defender $D$, both with damped double integrator dynamics (eq. (1), p.2):
$$\dot x_i = v_{ix},\ \dot y_i = v_{iy},\ \dot v_{ix} = -\mu v_{ix} + u_i\cos\theta_i,\ \dot v_{iy} = -\mu v_{iy} + u_i\sin\theta_i,\quad i\in\{A,D\},$$
with $u_i\in[0,u_{im}]$, $\theta_i\in[0,2\pi)$, common damping $\mu>0$, and the defender's acceleration advantage $\alpha = u_{Am}/u_{Dm} < 1$. The attacker wants to reach the static target at $[0,0]^T$; the defender wants point capture ($g(\mathbf x_{Af},\mathbf x_{Df}) = \sqrt{(x_{Af}-x_{Df})^2+(y_{Af}-y_{Df})^2} = 0$, eq. (2)) **as far from the target as possible**. Terminal payoff $J = \Phi(\mathbf x_{Af}) = \sqrt{x_{Af}^2+y_{Af}^2}$ (eqs. (3)–(4)); saddle-point condition (5) with attacker minimizing and defender maximizing; value $V = \min_{u_A,\theta_A}\max_{u_D,\theta_D} J$ (6). Complete information; strategies are state feedbacks $u_i(\mathbf x_A,\mathbf x_D,\mathbf v_A,\mathbf v_D)$, $\theta_i(\cdot)$. Scope: **defender-winning scenarios only** (attacker cannot reach the target); attacker-win conditions are not characterized.

**Gap.** [Authors' claim, p.2]: "reach-avoid games of damped double integrator dynamics have not been considered in existing literature, and the properties of a damped double integrator player's motion under optimal strategy derived by differential game theory remain to be studied." [Verified from cited text as described in the paper]: [34] solves pursuit–evasion (no target to defend) with damped double integrator via HJI; [31] and [32] treat reach-avoid/active-defense with **undamped** double integrators; [33] uses first-order missile models. So the claimed gap is consistent with the cited literature: the combination "reach-avoid + damping" and the associated motion geometry (isochrones, multiple arrival strategies) is genuinely new. A second, subtler gap identified by the authors: with curved optimal trajectories, "attacker arrives earlier at point $x$ than the defender" no longer implies "attacker is safe en route to $x$" (Remark 2, p.8), which invalidates the classical simple-motion dominance-region logic.

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Motion analysis under the optimal strategy** (Sec. 3.1–3.2): via the Maximum Principle, optimal play is maximum acceleration with constant heading (eqs. (7)–(14)); isochrones are circles (15); the **multiple reachable region (MRR)** $\mathcal M_i$ — points reachable at three distinct times, hence by three strategies — is constructed analytically through self-overlapping isochrones (Lemma 2), with boundary curves $\mathcal B_I^i,\mathcal B_II^i$ (26), cusp time $t_u$ (30)–(31), and barrier time $t_{is}$ (25). Circumscribing/inscribing times of the two players' isochrones: at least one, at most three (Theorem 1), computable by a bracketed root-finding scheme (Algorithm 1).
2. **New typology of the attacker's dominance region** (Sec. 3.3): typical ADR (Def. 3) split into $\mathcal R_I$ (safe, eq. (34)) and $\mathcal R_II = \mathrm{ADR}\setminus\mathcal R_I$; and a **third type $\mathcal R_{III}$ inside $\mathcal M_D$** (Def. 4, Theorem 2) — points with $t_{D1}<t_A<t_{D2}$ that the attacker can reach only by *deliberately reducing* acceleration (the "MRR strategy", p.11), a phenomenon impossible for simple motion.
3. **Necessary condition for optimality** (Sec. 3.4, Theorem 3): the Hamiltonian vanishes ($H=0$) at a local minimal capture point on $\mathcal L$ iff the attacker's and defender's arrival-time categories match (both local-minimum type, or both the middle $t_{i2}$ type); where it fails, the attacker improves by decreasing $u_A$.
4. **Initially-at-rest case** (Sec. 3.5): ADR boundary is the Apollonius circle (Lemma 5) and the optimal capture point is unique and satisfies $H=0$ (Theorem 4) — recovering the classical simple-motion answer as a degenerate case.
5. **Simulations** (Sec. 4, MATLAB 2023a): three strategy-I games (incl. the rest case) showing the saddle property against pure-pursuit deviations, plus two special cases demonstrating that ignoring $\mathcal R_{III}$ breaks strategy I.

### 5.1 Model, information, payoff, termination
- **Dynamics** (1), p.2 (above). Implicit standing assumption (p.4): $\|\mathbf v_{i0}\| \le u_{im}/\mu$, consistent with the speed saturation $\|v_i(t)\| \le \|v_{i0}\|e^{-\mu t} + \frac{u_{im}}{\mu}(1-e^{-\mu t}) \to u_{im}/\mu$ (p.4).
- **Termination**: point capture, terminal equality constraint (2); value = final attacker–target distance (3)–(4). Defender-winning scenario assumed: target is outside the ADR.
- **Information**: complete state feedback (p.3). Cost is terminal-only; no running cost.
- **Hamiltonian** (7), p.3: $H = \lambda_1 v_{Dx}+\lambda_2 v_{Dy}+\gamma_1 v_{Ax}+\gamma_2 v_{Ay}+\lambda_3(-\mu v_{Dx}+u_D\cos\theta_D)+\lambda_4(-\mu v_{Dy}+u_D\sin\theta_D)+\gamma_3(-\mu v_{Ax}+u_A\cos\theta_A)+\gamma_4(-\mu v_{Ay}+u_A\sin\theta_A)$, with defender co-state $\lambda$, attacker co-state $\gamma$.
- **Co-state solutions** (8)–(11), pp.3–4: $\dot\lambda_1=\dot\lambda_2=0$, $\dot\lambda_3=-\lambda_1+\mu\lambda_3$, $\lambda_{3f}=\lambda_{4f}=0$, $\lambda_{1f} = \nu\frac{x_{Df}-x_{Af}}{\|\mathbf x_{Af}-\mathbf x_{Df}\|}$, $\lambda_{2f}=\nu\frac{y_{Df}-y_{Af}}{\|\mathbf x_{Af}-\mathbf x_{Df}\|}$, whence $\lambda_1,\lambda_2$ constant and $\lambda_3 = \frac{\lambda_1}{\mu}(1-e^{-\mu(t_f-t)})$ (10); for the attacker $\gamma_1 = \frac{x_{Af}}{\sqrt{x_{Af}^2+y_{Af}^2}} + \nu\frac{x_{Af}-x_{Df}}{\|\mathbf x_{Af}-\mathbf x_{Df}\|}$, $\gamma_3 = \frac{\gamma_1}{\mu}(1-e^{-\mu(t_f-t)})$ (11).
- **Optimal controls** (12), p.4: $u_A^*=u_{Am}$, $u_D^*=u_{Dm}$; $\cos\theta_A^* = -\gamma_3/\sqrt{\gamma_3^2+\gamma_4^2}$, $\sin\theta_A^* = -\gamma_4/\sqrt{\gamma_3^2+\gamma_4^2}$ (attacker steers against $\gamma$; it minimizes $H$), $\cos\theta_D^* = \lambda_3/\sqrt{\lambda_3^2+\lambda_4^2}$, $\sin\theta_D^* = \lambda_4/\sqrt{\lambda_3^2+\lambda_4^2}$ (defender steers along $\lambda$; it maximizes $H$). Since $\gamma_3/\gamma_4=\gamma_1/\gamma_2$ and $\lambda_3/\lambda_4=\lambda_1/\lambda_2$ are time-invariant, **both optimal headings are constant**, although the trajectories (13) are curved: $v_i(t) = \mathbf v_{i0}e^{-\mu t} + \frac{u_i}{\mu}(1-e^{-\mu t})[\cos\theta_i,\sin\theta_i]^T$, $x_i(t) = \mathbf x_{i0} + \frac{\mathbf v_{i0}}{\mu}(1-e^{-\mu t}) + \frac{u_i[\cos\theta_i,\sin\theta_i]^T}{\mu}\left(t-\frac{1-e^{-\mu t}}{\mu}\right)$ (13). The feedback form (14): for a chosen terminal point $\mathbf x_f$, $\cos\theta_i^* = \dfrac{x_f - x_{i0} - \frac{v_{ix0}}{\mu}(1-e^{-\mu t_f})}{\frac{u_i}{\mu}\left(t_f-\frac{1-e^{-\mu t_f}}{\mu}\right)}$ (and similarly for $\sin$) — a genuine state feedback once $t_f$/$\mathbf x_f$ is re-solved from the current state.

### 5.2 Lemma 1 (p.4, eq. (15)) — isochrones are exact reachable sets
**Statement.** Given $\mathbf x_{i0},\mathbf v_{i0}$, $\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^2 \mid \|\mathbf x-\mathbf x_{ic}(t)\| \le r_{ic}(t)\}$ is exactly the set of points player $i$ can reach at time $t$, where the isochron (circle) has radius and center $r_{ic}(t) = \frac{u_{im}}{\mu}\left(t-\frac{1-e^{-\mu t}}{\mu}\right)$, $\mathbf x_{ic}(t) = \mathbf x_{i0} + \frac{\mathbf v_{i0}}{\mu}(1-e^{-\mu t})$ (15) (isochrone definition and formulas "as derived in [34]").
**Proof approach** (Appendix A, pp.17–18 — note: Appendix A is *titled* "Proof of Lemma 3" but its content is this proof; a mislabel, see §5.9): (i) decompose $\mathbf v_i(t) = \mathbf v_{i0}e^{-\mu t} + \mathbf q(t)$ with $\dot q_x = -\mu q_x + u_i(t)\cos\theta_i(t)$, $q(0)=0$ (49), so $\mathbf x_i(t)-\mathbf x_{ic}(t) = \int_0^t \mathbf q\,d\tau$ (50); (ii) $\|\mathbf x_i-\mathbf x_{ic}\| = \|\int\mathbf q\| \le \int\|\mathbf q\|$ (51); (iii) from (49), $\dot q_x^2+\dot q_y^2 + 2\mu\|\mathbf q\|\frac{d\|\mathbf q\|}{dt} + \mu^2\|\mathbf q\|^2 = u_i(t)^2$ (52), and the Lagrange identity $\big(\tfrac{d\|\mathbf q\|}{dt}\big)^2 = \dot q_x^2+\dot q_y^2 - \frac{(q_y\dot q_x - q_x\dot q_y)^2}{q_x^2+q_y^2}$ (53) yields the decisive differential inequality $\frac{d\|\mathbf q\|}{dt} + \mu\|\mathbf q\| \le u_i(t)$; (iv) **Gronwall–Bellman** gives $\|\mathbf q\| \le \frac{u_{im}}{\mu}(1-e^{-\mu t})$, hence $\|\mathbf x_i(t)-\mathbf x_{ic}(t)\| \le \int_0^t \frac{u_{im}}{\mu}(1-e^{-\mu\tau})d\tau = r_{ic}(t)$ (54); (v) converse: any $\mathbf x$ with $\|\mathbf x-\mathbf x_{ic}(t)\| \le r_{ic}(t)$ is reached by the reduced constant acceleration $\hat u_i = \frac{\mu}{t-\frac{1-e^{-\mu t}}{\mu}}\|\mathbf x_{ic}(t)-\mathbf x\|$ (p.18) (radius scales linearly in $u_i$ at fixed $t$; center (15) is $u_i$-independent). Global, analytic. **Meaning**: max-acceleration/constant-heading play sweeps exact disks; "reaching time" questions reduce to circle geometry. **Extensibility**: the inequality chain only uses the linear damped dynamics + bounded input, so it carries to time-varying $u_i$ and to any $\mu>0$.

### 5.3 Theorem 1 (p.4) + Corollary 1 (p.5) — tangency counting; Algorithm 1 (p.8)
**Statement.** $\mathcal I_A(t)$ and $\mathcal I_D(t)$ inscribe (circumscribe) **at least once and at most three times**. *Assumptions*: distinct initial positions ($\|\Delta\mathbf x\| > 0$, p.18); dynamics (1); strategies $u_i = u_{im}$, constant headings. *Proof* (Appendix B, pp.18–19): circumscribing condition $o(t)-p(t)=0$ with (55) $o(t) = \|\Delta\mathbf x\|^2 + \frac{\|\Delta\mathbf v\|^2}{\mu^2}(1-e^{-\mu t})^2 + \frac{2}{\mu}\Delta\mathbf x^T\Delta\mathbf v(1-e^{-\mu t})$, $p(t) = \frac{(u_A+u_D)^2}{\mu^2}\big(t-\frac{1-e^{-\mu t}}{\mu}\big)^2$ (this is $\Gamma_{out}=0$ in (16); the inscribing case $\Gamma_{in}=0$ is "proven similarly"). Steps: (i) endpoints: $o(0)-p(0) = \|\Delta\mathbf x\|^2>0$, $o(+\infty)-p(+\infty)<0$ ($p\sim t^2$ dominates the bounded $o$) — existence by IVT; (ii) derivative table (56): $o' = 2e^{-\mu t}(\frac{\|\Delta\mathbf v\|^2}{\mu}(1-e^{-\mu t}) + \Delta\mathbf x^T\Delta\mathbf v)$, $o'' = 2e^{-\mu t}(\|\Delta\mathbf v\|^2(2e^{-\mu t}-1)-\mu\Delta\mathbf x^T\Delta\mathbf v)$, $o^{(3)} = 2\mu e^{-\mu t}(\mu\Delta\mathbf x^T\Delta\mathbf v - \|\Delta\mathbf v\|^2(4e^{-\mu t}-1))$, and $p', p'', p^{(3)}$ for $p$; (iii) **type 1** ($\|\Delta\mathbf v\|^2+\mu\Delta\mathbf x^T\Delta\mathbf v<0$): $o'<0$, $p'\ge0$ ⇒ $o-p$ strictly decreasing ⇒ 1 zero; (iv) **type 2** ($\|\Delta\mathbf v\|^2+\mu\Delta\mathbf x^T\Delta\mathbf v\ge0$, $\Delta\mathbf x^T\Delta\mathbf v\le0$): show $o''-p''$ has ≤1 zero — assume two sign changes $t_1<t_2<t_M$ ($t_M = \frac1\mu\ln\frac{2\|\Delta\mathbf v\|^2}{\|\Delta\mathbf v\|^2+\mu\Delta\mathbf x^T\Delta\mathbf v}$, the zero of $o''$), then **Lagrange's mean value theorem** gives $t_3$ with $p^{(3)}(t_3)<o^{(3)}(t_3)<0$; the ratio $p^{(3)}/o^{(3)} = \frac{\frac{(u_A+u_D)^2}{\mu^2}(4(1-e^{-\mu t})-\mu t)}{\mu(\|\Delta\mathbf v\|^2+\mu\Delta\mathbf x^T\Delta\mathbf v-4\|\Delta\mathbf v\|^2 e^{-\mu t})}$ has a monotonically increasing denominator and (where $p^{(3)}<0$) monotonically decreasing numerator, forcing $p^{(3)}<o^{(3)}$ for all $t>t_3$, i.e. $p''<o''$ for $t>t_2$, contradicting $p''(t_M)>o''(t_M)=0$; hence $o'-p'$ has ≤2 zeros $t_{o1},t_{o2}$ and $o-p$ decreases–increases–decreases ⇒ **≤3 zeros**; (v) **type 3** ($\Delta\mathbf x^T\Delta\mathbf v>0$): if $\|\Delta\mathbf v\|^2\le\mu\Delta\mathbf x^T\Delta\mathbf v$, $o''<0$ ∀t ⇒ 1 zero; else with $k = \mu\Delta\mathbf x^T\Delta\mathbf v/\|\Delta\mathbf v\|^2\in(0,1)$, the decisive scalar inequality $p^{(3)}(t_M) \propto (2-k) + \ln\frac{1+k}{2} > 0$ (57) (printed as "$2(1-k)+\ln\frac{1+k}{2}>0$", p.19; see §5.9) ⇒ $p''$ increases on $[0,t_M)$ ⇒ $o''-p''$ has one zero ⇒ $o'-p'$ one zero ⇒ $o-p$ one zero. Analytic; the inscribing case mirrors it. **Corollary 1** (p.5): any static point $\mathbf x$ is reachable by a player with **at least one and at most three** strategies — proved by the degenerate trick $u_A=0$, $\mathbf v_{A0}=\mathbf 0$, $\mathbf x_{A0}=\mathbf x$, which turns "$\mathcal I_D$ passes $\mathbf x$" into a circumscribing event of Theorem 1. **Meaning**: $\Gamma_{in},\Gamma_{out}$ (16) are non-monotone in general, so capture windows ($t_{out}$ earliest capture, $t_{in}$ latest capture [34]) can occur multiple times; the whole subsequent ADR construction lives in $[0,t_{in}]$. **Algorithm 1** (p.8) is a numerical companion: using the sign conditions on $\Delta\mathbf x^T\Delta\mathbf v$ and $\|\Delta\mathbf v\|^2+\mu\Delta\mathbf x^T\Delta\mathbf v$ plus the inflection structure ($\Gamma''$ zero $t_{d\text{-}max}$, then $\Gamma'$ extrema $t_{min},t_{max}$), it splits $t>0$ into intervals containing **at most one** zero of $\Gamma$ and applies a single-root finder on each, returning all 1–3 circumscribing/inscribing times. Local analytic structure + numerical root isolation. **Proof gap (minor)**: in type 2 the claim "$o^{(3)}(t_3)<0$" and the ratio argument are stated without full sign bookkeeping (p.18); the conclusion is nevertheless consistent with the endpoint contradiction at $t_M$.

### 5.4 MRR machinery: Definitions 1–2, Lemma 2, barrier time, Lemma 3 (pp.5–7)
- **Def. 1 (MRR)**: $\mathcal M_i$ = set of points reachable by *multiple* (i.e., three) strategies; **Def. 2 (Reaching time)**: $t_{Aj}(\mathbf x)$, $j\in\{1,2,3\}$ on $\mathcal M_A$ ($j=0$ outside, written $t_A$), ordered $t_{i1}<t_{i2}<t_{i3}$; all continuous on their domains (p.5).
- **Reaching-time gradient** (17), p.5: $\frac{dt_{ij}}{d\mathbf x} = \frac{[\cos\theta_i,\ \sin\theta_i]^T}{v_{ix}\cos\theta_i+v_{iy}\sin\theta_i}$ — inverse radial speed along the arrival heading; it **fails to exist exactly on $\partial\mathcal M_i$**, equivalently at the tangency condition (18): $v_{iy0}\sin\theta_i + v_{ix0}\cos\theta_i + \frac{u_i}{\mu}(e^{\mu t}-1) = 0$ (derived from $\frac{v_{iy}(t,\theta_i)}{v_{ix}(t,\theta_i)} = -\cot\theta_i$, i.e., trajectory tangent to isochron).
- **Lemma 2 (p.6)**: if $\mathcal I_i$ self-overlaps at $t$ (def.: $\exists T$ s.t. $\forall\Delta t\in(0,T)$, $\mathcal I_i(t)\cap\mathcal I_i(t-\Delta t)\neq\emptyset$; intersection points $\mathbf x_{int}^{\pm}(\Delta t,t)$ symmetric about $\mathbf v_{i0}$, wedge-product sign convention, Remark 1 handles $\Delta t\to0$), then two extremal trajectories are tangent to $\mathcal I_i(t)$ at $\lim_{\Delta t\to0}\mathbf x_{int}^{\pm}(\Delta t,t)$. *Proof*: WLOG $v_{iy0}=0$, $\mathbf x_{i0}=0$; first-order expansion of $r_{ic}(t-\Delta t), x_{ic}(t-\Delta t)$ (19); subtracting the two circle equations (20) gives $-2(x-x_{ic}(t))\Delta t\, v_{ix0}e^{-\mu t} = 2r_{ic}(t)\frac{u_i}{\mu}\Delta t(1-e^{-\mu t}) + o(\Delta t)$ (21); the **decisive limit** (22) $-(x-x_{ic}(t))v_{ix0}e^{-\mu t} = r_{ic}(t)\frac{u_i}{\mu}(1-e^{-\mu t})$ yields (23) $x_{int}^{\pm}(t)-x_{ic}(t) = -r_{ic}(t)\frac{u_i}{\mu}\frac{1-e^{-\mu t}}{v_{ix0}e^{-\mu t}}$ and (24) $\cos\theta_i = -\frac{u_i}{\mu}\frac{1-e^{-\mu t}}{v_{ix0}e^{-\mu t}}$, which is then verified to satisfy the tangency condition (18). Local, analytic.
- **Barrier time** (25), p.6 (term from [34]): $t_{is} = \frac1\mu\ln\frac{\sqrt{v_{ix0}^2+v_{iy0}^2}+u_{im}}{u_{im}}$, after which isochrones no longer self-overlap (nested family).
- **Boundary structure** (26)–(31), pp.6–7: $\mathcal B_I^i = \{t_{i1}=t_{i2}\}$, $\mathcal B_II^i = \{t_{i2}=t_{i3}\}$; $\mathbf x_{i0}\in\mathcal B_I^i$ ($t_{i1}=t_{i2}=0$) and $\mathbf x_s \in \mathcal B_II^i$ (the single tangency point at $t_{is}$, where (24) gives $\cos\theta_i=-1$). Boundary tangent (27) $\frac{d\mathbf x}{dt} = \mathbf v_i(t,\theta_i) + \frac{u_i}{\mu}\big(t-\frac{1-e^{-\mu t}}{\mu}\big)\frac{d\theta_i}{dt}[-\sin\theta_i,\cos\theta_i]^T$ with the two tangency solutions (28) $\sin\theta_i = \frac{-v_{iy0}\frac{u_i}{\mu}(e^{\mu t}-1) \pm v_{ix0}M}{v_{ix0}^2+v_{iy0}^2}$, $\cos\theta_i = \frac{-v_{ix0}\frac{u_i}{\mu}(e^{\mu t}-1) \mp v_{iy0}M}{v_{ix0}^2+v_{iy0}^2}$, $M = \sqrt{v_{ix0}^2+v_{iy0}^2 - \frac{u_i^2}{\mu^2}(e^{\mu t}-1)^2}$, and (29) $\frac{d\theta_i}{dt} = \pm\frac{e^{\mu t}u_i}{M}$ (verified by differentiating (18) along the curve: $(v_{iy0}\cos\theta_i - v_{ix0}\sin\theta_i)\frac{d\theta_i}{dt} = -u_ie^{\mu t}$ and $v_{iy0}\cos\theta_i - v_{ix0}\sin\theta_i = \mp M$). At the cusps $\mathbf x_{u1},\mathbf x_{u2}$, $d\mathbf x/dt=0$ gives (30) $\frac{u_i}{\mu}\big(t_u-\frac{1-e^{-\mu t_u}}{\mu}\big)\frac{e^{\mu t_u}u_i}{M} = \|\mathbf v_i(t_u,\theta_i)\| = Me^{-\mu t_u}$ (the printed norm is the 2-norm, not squared — verified against the rendered equation), using $\|\mathbf v_i(t,\theta_i)\|^2 = \|\mathbf v_{i0}\|^2e^{-2\mu t} - \frac{u_i^2}{\mu^2}(1-e^{-\mu t})^2$ (which follows from (18)); hence the scalar equation for the cusp time (31): $\frac{u_i^2}{\mu}\big(t_u-\frac{1-e^{-\mu t_u}}{\mu}\big) = \|\mathbf v_{i0}\|^2e^{-2\mu t_u} - \frac{u_i^2}{\mu^2}(1-e^{-\mu t_u})^2$. Ordering: on $[0,t_u]$ the intersections trace $\mathcal B_I^i$ ($t_{i1}=t_{i2}$), on $[t_u,t_{is}]$ they trace $\mathcal B_II^i$ ($t_{i2}=t_{i3}$); for $\mathbf x\in\mathcal M_i$: $t_{i1}\le t_u \le t_{i3}$, $t_{i2}\le t_{is}$. One-sided continuity (32): approaching $\mathcal B_I^i$ from outside $\mathcal M_i$, $t_i\to t_{i3}$; approaching $\mathcal B_II^i$, $t_i\to t_{i1}$.
- **Lemma 3 (p.7)**: $\forall \mathbf x\in\mathcal M_i$, $\forall t\in(t_{i2}(\mathbf x),t_{i3}(\mathbf x))$: the player **cannot** be at $\mathbf x$ at $t$ under any strategy ($\|\mathbf x-\mathbf x_{ic}(t)\| > r_{ic}(t)$, inherited from Theorem 1's proof); while for $t\in(t_{i1},t_{i2})$ it can. *Proof*: 3 lines, via Lemma 1. **Meaning**: $\mathcal M_i$ is the "loitering zone" — a point inside it is reachable early, then *blocked* for a positive time window, then reachable again; this non-convex time structure is the algebraic root of the new dominance-region type below.

### 5.5 ADR typology: Def. 3, $\mathcal R_I,\mathcal R_II$, Lemma 4, Def. 4 + Theorem 2, MRR strategy (pp.7–11)
- **Def. 3 (typical ADR, eq. (33), p.7)**: $\mathrm{ADR} = \{\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^2 \mid \|\mathbf x-\mathbf x_{Ac}(t_{Aj})\| = r_{Ac}(t_{Aj}),\ \|\mathbf x-\mathbf x_{Dc}(t_{Aj})\| > r_{Dc}(t_{Aj}) \text{ for some } j\in\{0,1,2,3\}\}$ — the attacker reaches $\mathbf x$ at one of its arrival times while $\mathbf x$ is outside the defender's isochron at that instant. **$\mathcal R_I$ (eq. (34), p.7)**: points reachable with $u_{Am}$ and a constant heading $\theta_A^*$ while staying outside $\mathcal I_D(t)$ for **all** $t<t_{Aj}$ — provably safe. **$\mathcal R_II = \mathrm{ADR}\setminus\mathcal R_I$** (p.8): there exists a defender strategy capturing the attacker en route despite the attacker arriving earlier at the endpoint. **Remark 2 (p.8)**: for simple motion Def. 3 collapses to the classical dominance region [20]; for damped double integrators the optimal trajectory is curved, so endpoint dominance ≠ path dominance, and $\mathcal R_II$ is nonempty in general. It is inferred from Lemma 3 that $\mathcal R_I\cup\mathcal R_II$ = points with $t_{Aj}<\min t_D$ or $t_{D2}<t_{Aj}<t_{D3}$ (p.8); the intersection curve $\mathcal L$ of isochrones (where $t_{Aj}=t_{Dk}$) forms part of its boundary; points on $\mathcal B_I^A$ are excluded (attacker's and defender's times never equal there, so they are not feasible *capture* points). Working window: $t<t_{in}$, first inscribing time (p.8).
- **Lemma 4 (p.9)**: if $t_{Aj}=t_{Dk}$ on part of $\mathcal L$, the magnitude order of $t_{Aj}$ vs. $t_{Dk}$ **flips** across $\mathcal L$. *Proof*: along $\mathcal L$ the tangential derivatives coincide (35)–(37) $\mathbf b^T\frac{dt_{Dk}}{d\mathbf x} = \mathbf b^T\frac{dt_{Aj}}{d\mathbf x}$ ($\mathbf b$ = tangent of $\mathcal L$); if the order did not flip, the normal derivatives would also coincide (38), so the full gradients (17) would be parallel — but by (17) the two gradients are parallel iff $\mathcal I_A$ and $\mathcal I_D$ are tangent (inscribe/circumscribe), which happens only finitely often (Theorem 1). Clean differential argument; local.
- **Def. 4 (p.10)**: $\mathcal R_{III}$ = regions in $\mathcal M_D$ with $t_{D1}(\mathbf x)<t_A(\mathbf x)<t_{D2}(\mathbf x)$ (or $t_{D1}<t_{A1}<t_{D2}$) **that contract continuously to a point as $u_A$ decreases** — the contraction clause ensures every point of $\mathcal R_{III}$ lies in $\mathcal R_I(u_A^*)\cup\mathcal R_II(u_A^*)$ for some $u_A^*<u_{Am}$, i.e., is truly reachable when the attacker "throttles down".
- **Theorem 2 (p.10)**: a region satisfying either condition is $\mathcal R_{III}$: **(1)** its boundary consists solely of isochrone-intersection arcs between the first two circumscribing times $t_{o1},t_{o2}$, with $\|\mathbf x_{Ac}(t)-\mathbf x_{Dc}(t)\| > r_{Dc}(t)$ on $[t_{o1},t_{o2}]$ (region $\mathcal P_1$, Fig. 5(a)); **(2)** its boundary consists solely of $\mathcal B_II^D$ and the part of $\mathcal L$ where $t_{A1}(\mathbf x)=t_{D2}(\mathbf x)$ (resp. $t_A=t_{D2}$ outside $\mathcal M_A$) (region $\mathcal P_2$, Fig. 5(b)). *Proof*: (i) $o(t),p_{out}(t)$ in (16) are continuous in $(u_A,t)$, so $t_{o1},t_{o2}$ move continuously as $u_A\downarrow$, with $t_{o1}(u_A)\ge t_{o1}(u_{Am})$, $t_{o2}(u_A)\le t_{o2}(u_{Am})$; since $\|\mathbf x_{Ac}-\mathbf x_{Dc}\|>r_{Dc}$ on the window, for $u_A=0$ the isochrones never meet, so there is $u_A^*$ with exactly one external tangency $t_{o1}(u_A^*)=t_{o2}(u_A^*)$: $\mathcal P_1$ shrinks to that point and vanishes — hence $\forall\mathbf x\in\mathcal P_1\ \exists u_A\in[u_A^*,u_{Am}]$ with $\mathbf x\in\mathcal R_II(u_A)\cup\mathcal R_I(u_A)$; the time order inside $\mathcal P_1$ must be $t_{D1}<t_A<t_{D2}$ (the alternative $t_A>t_{D3}$ would keep $\mathbf x$ inside $\mathcal I_D$ forever, contradicting the existence of $u_A^*$). (ii) For $\mathcal P_2$: the tangent points $\mathbf x_{tg1},\mathbf x_{tg2} = \mathcal B_II^D\cap\mathcal L$ (where $t_A=t_{D2}=t_{D3}$) move continuously as $u_A$ decreases — otherwise $t_A$ on $\mathcal P_2$ would jump discontinuously in $u_A$, contradicting the continuity of $t_A(\mathbf x)$ while $\mathcal P_2\neq\emptyset$; decreasing $u_A$ increases $t_{A1},t_A$, pushing $\mathbf x_{tg}$ along $\mathcal B_II^D$ into the $t_A<t_{D2}$ side and shrinking the $\mathcal L$-arc, so $\mathcal P_2\to\emptyset$; the inner time order $t_{D1}<t_A<t_{D2}$ follows from Lemma 4 applied across the adjacent $t_A=t_{D3}$ curve. Constructive-geometric proof; global for the stated configurations. **Honesty clauses (p.11)**: regions failing both conditions may still be reachable (Fig. 5(c)) but "there is still no criterion to guarantee" it (counterexample mechanism: $\|\mathbf x_{Ac}(t)-\mathbf x_{Dc}(t)\|<r_{Dc}(t)$ can make the isochrone center itself unreachable); and $t_{D1}<t_A<t_{D2}$ alone does **not** imply reachability (Fig. 5(d): with the attacker initially at rest, the $\mathcal L$-arc with $t_{A1}=t_{D1}$ only drifts toward $\mathbf x_{A0}$ instead of vanishing). This is the paper's most candid limitation statement.
- **MRR strategy (p.11)**: to reach $\mathbf x\in\mathcal R_{III}$ (where the defender cannot), the attacker solves $t_{D2}(\mathbf x)$ via Algorithm 1 and picks the **reduced** acceleration $\hat u_A$ from $\frac{\hat u_A}{\mu}\Big(t_{D2}(\mathbf x)-\frac{1-e^{-\mu t_{D2}(\mathbf x)}}{\mu}\Big) = \|\mathbf x-\mathbf x_{Ac}(t_{D2}(\mathbf x))\|$ — arrive exactly when the defender's middle-time isochron does, outside $\mathcal I_D$ meanwhile. **Fig. 6** shows full ADRs: $\mathcal R_{III}$ can be connected (6(a)) or partly isolated inside $\mathcal R_I\cup\mathcal R_II$ (6(c), type-1 condition). Consequence (p.11): (12) from the Maximum Principle **does not hold** when the attacker enters or passes $\mathcal R_{III}$ — the PMP strategy is not globally optimal, motivating §5.6.

### 5.6 Theorem 3 (p.11–13) — necessary condition for strategy I
**Statement.** Let **strategy I** = "head to the point of $\mathcal L$ closest to the target, with $u_i=u_{im}$ and constant heading". The necessary PMP condition $\max_{u_D,\theta_D}\min_{u_A,\theta_A} H = H(\mathbf x_i,\theta_i^*,u_i^*,\lambda,\gamma,t) = 0$ holds at a **local minimal point** $\mathbf x\in\mathcal L$ **if** $t_{Dk}(\mathbf x)=t_{Aj}(\mathbf x)$ with $k\in\{0,1,3\},j\in\{0,1,3\}$, **or** $t_{D2}(\mathbf x)=t_{A2}(\mathbf x)$, **and** the gradients (17) of both times exist at $\mathbf x$. *Assumptions*: both players on $\mathcal L$ (equal arrival times), non-tangency point of $\mathcal L$ with $\partial\mathcal M_A,\partial\mathcal M_D$, defender-winning regime.
*Proof approach* (pp.11–13): (i) auxiliary **Local Minimal (Maximal) Time Problem**: single damped-DI player minimizing/maximizing arrival time at $\mathbf x$; Hamiltonian (39) with co-state $\kappa_i$, $\kappa_{i3}=\kappa_{i4}=0$ at $t_f$; citing [4], $\kappa_i(t) = \pm\partial t_i(\mathbf x,\mathbf x_i(t),\mathbf v_i(t))/\partial\mathbf x_i(t)$ is constant, so (40) $\kappa_i = \pm\partial t_i(\mathbf x,\mathbf x_{i0},\mathbf v_{i0})/\partial\mathbf x_{i0}$ (＋: local min, −: local max); (ii) differentiating (13) gives $\partial t_i/\partial\mathbf x + \partial t_i/\partial\mathbf x_{i0} = 0$, and inserting (17) yields $H_{aux} = \pm\mathbf v_{if}^T\frac{\partial t_i}{\partial\mathbf x_{i0}} = \mp1$ (41): **$t_{i2}$ is the local-maximum type ($H_{aux}=+1$), all other reaching times the local-minimum type ($H_{aux}=-1$)** — geometrically because $\mathbf x$ is inside isochrons on $[t_{i1},t_{i2}]$ and outside on $[t_{i2},t_{i3}]$; (iii) any same-category pair satisfies the compatibility identity (42) $\mathbf v_{Af}^T\frac{\partial t_A}{\partial\mathbf x_{A0}} - \mathbf v_{Df}^T\frac{\partial t_D}{\partial\mathbf x_{D0}} = 0$; (iv) at a local minimum $\mathbf x_f$ of $\Phi$ on $\mathcal L$, stationarity with multiplier $\sigma$: (43)–(44) $\frac{d}{d\mathbf x_f}\sqrt{x_f^2+y_f^2} - \sigma\big(\frac{\partial t_A}{\partial\mathbf x_{A0}} - \frac{\partial t_D}{\partial\mathbf x_{D0}}\big) = 0$; (v) define (45) $\hat\lambda = -\sigma\frac{\partial t_D}{\partial\mathbf x_{D0}}$, $\hat\gamma = \sigma\frac{\partial t_A}{\partial\mathbf x_{A0}}$; then (46) $\hat\gamma^T\mathbf v_{Af} + \hat\lambda^T\mathbf v_{Df} = 0$ (equivalent to $H=0$ at $t_f$, where $\lambda_3=\lambda_4=\gamma_3=\gamma_4=0$); (vi) identify $\hat\lambda = \frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\mathbf x_D^T}+\nu\frac{\partial g}{\partial\mathbf x_D^T}$ using $\sigma = |\nu|(v_{Dxf}\cos\theta_D + v_{Dyf}\sin\theta_D)$ (positive for minimal-time arrivals since $\frac{d}{dt}(\|\mathbf x_f-\mathbf x_{Dc}(t_f)\|-r_{Dc}(t_f))<0$), and check $\hat\lambda,\hat\gamma$ reproduce (10)–(11) including $\lambda_1+\gamma_1 = \frac{x_f}{\sqrt{x_f^2+y_f^2}}$, $\lambda_2+\gamma_2 = \frac{y_f}{\sqrt{x_f^2+y_f^2}}$; (vii) the induced strategies (47) $\theta_A^* = \arg\min_{\theta_A}\sigma\frac{\partial t_A}{\partial\mathbf x_{A0}^T}[\cos\theta_A,\sin\theta_A]^T$, $\theta_D^* = \arg\max_{\theta_D}\sigma\frac{\partial t_D}{\partial\mathbf x_{D0}^T}[\cos\theta_D,\sin\theta_D]^T$ coincide with (12). *Converse direction* (p.13, case analysis): at local minima where the categories **mismatch** ($t_{D2}=t_{Aj}$, $j\in\{0,1,3\}$ or $t_{Dk}=t_{A2}$, $k\in\{0,1,3\}$), $H=0$ fails and the attacker has a improving move — across $t_{D2}=t_{A1}$, decreasing $u_A$ makes $t_{D2}(\mathbf x)<t_{A1}(\mathbf x)$, converting the region into $\mathcal R_I\cup\mathcal R_II$; for $t_{D2}=t_{A3}$ either $t_{A1}<t_{D1}$ (better point exists on $\mathcal L$) or decreasing $u_A$ inflates $t_{A3}$ into ADR; for $t_{D3}=t_{A2}$, decreasing $u_A$ decreases $t_{A2}$, moving $\mathcal L$ into the $t_{D3}<t_{A2}$ side. All equations presuppose $u_i = u_{im}$, so a player using the MRR strategy (reduced $u_A$) voids $H=0$ — exactly why the optimal point can jump into $\mathcal R_{III}$. **Physical meaning**: the saddle point of the capture-position game is the target-closest point of $\mathcal L$ *provided both players are "same-type" arrivals*; the middle-time branch $t_{i2}$ is a local *maximum* time, and only matched types give stationarity. **Extensibility**: the category-matching argument is dynamics-agnostic — any model with a continuous, piecewise-smooth arrival-time function with one local-max branch gets an analogous condition. **Proof gap (regularity)**: step (iv) assumes the capture point is a regular point of $\mathcal L$ (constraint qualification) and that the value equals a *local* minimum of $\Phi|_{\mathcal L}$; global optimality over multiple local minima is not proven (acknowledged implicitly in Sec. 5: "since the dominance region is not convex, we will explore the situations involving multiple minimal points").

### 5.7 Lemma 5 (p.13, eq. (48)) and Theorem 4 (pp.13–14) — players initially at rest
**Lemma 5.** If both players start at rest ($t_{is}=0$, no self-overlap), the ADR boundary is an **Apollonius circle**: isochrones (48) $r_{ic} = \frac{u_{im}}{\mu}\big(t-\frac{1-e^{-\mu t}}{\mu}\big)$, $\mathbf x_{ic}=\mathbf x_{i0}$ are concentric with radius ratio $r_{Ac}/r_{Dc} = \alpha$; the intersection set $\{\mathbf x\mid \|\mathbf x-\mathbf x_{A0}\|/\|\mathbf x-\mathbf x_{D0}\| = \alpha\}$ has center $\mathbf x_b = \frac{1}{1-\alpha^2}(\mathbf x_{A0}-\alpha^2\mathbf x_{D0})$ and radius $r_b = \frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha^2}\|\mathbf x_{A0}-\mathbf x_{D0}\|$ — identical to the simple-motion dominance circle. **Theorem 4.** In defender-winning scenarios with both at rest, the minimal point is **unique and satisfies $H=0$**. *Proof*: target outside ADR; uniqueness from convexity of the Apollonius circle and continuity/uniqueness of minimal arrival times (no MRR); then Theorem 3 applies. Consequently the optimal play of both players is to run straight (in the acceleration-heading sense) to the **nearest point of the Apollonius circle to the target — the intersection of the circle with the line joining target and center** (p.14). Global and analytic; the cleanest result of the paper, and a sanity bridge to the simple-motion literature.

### 5.8 Standing interpretations
- $t_{out}$ (first circumscribing) = earliest possible capture; $t_{in}$ (first inscribing) = latest capture under maximal attacker effort [34]; all ADR analysis is confined to $[0,t_{in}]$ (p.8).
- Fig. 4 (p.9) catalogs the three possible tangential orders of $\mathcal I_A,\mathcal I_D$ (1+1, 3+3, 3 circumscriptions before first inscription), which determine the topology of the ADR.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
**Author-stated** (p.17, Sec. 5): (i) no strategy yet for reaching the **second type** region $\mathcal R_II$ ("might be caught" — no sharp characterization); (ii) the dominance region is **not convex**, so situations with **multiple minimal points** are unresolved (global optimality open); (iii) alternative techniques such as **retrogressive path equations** remain to be explored. Also implicit: scope restricted to defender-winning scenarios, 1v1, static point target, point capture, and to the window $t<t_{in}$ (p.8); attacker-win barrier is not constructed.
**Critical observations** (with loci): (a) optimality rests on PMP necessary conditions plus the local category-matching condition of Theorem 3 — no verification theorem/value function, so "optimal strategy" claims outside the rest case are local and simulation-supported (pp.11–14); (b) strategy I is implemented open-loop in simulations while marketed as state feedback (p.14 vs. p.3) — feedback re-solving (Algorithm 1 at each step, as done in Fig. 10's jumping-point experiment) is costlier and only partially demonstrated; (c) Algorithm 1's root-finder robustness/complexity is not analyzed, and gradient nonexistence on $\partial\mathcal M$ (p.5) and tangency points $\mathbf x_{tg1},\mathbf x_{tg2}$ (p.10) sit exactly where Theorem 3's hypotheses fail; (d) the sufficient conditions of Theorem 2 do not *characterize* all reachable regions (the paper's own Figs. 5(c)/(d) counterexample discussion, p.11); (e) typos of §5.9 (Appendix A label; (56) $p'$ exponent and $p^{(3)}$ prefactor; p.19 bracket) — all immaterial to the results but worth knowing when quoting equations; (f) the common damping $\mu$ and equal-form dynamics for both players is assumed; heterogeneous damping is untouched; (g) no comparison against HJI numerical solutions or the strategies of [31]–[34], so the practical advantage over simpler baselines (beyond pure pursuit) is unquantified.

## 8. Citation Value
This is the first paper to carry reach-avoid geometric game theory from simple motion to damped double integrator dynamics, and its durable contributions are the motion primitives — isochrone reachability disks with Gronwall-certified tightness (Lemma 1), the multiple reachable region with its $\mathcal B_I/\mathcal B_II$ boundary anatomy and barrier time (Theorem 1, Lemma 2, eqs. (25)–(32)), and the three-type attacker's dominance region culminating in $\mathcal R_{III}$, where the attacker must *decelerate* to win (Def. 4, Theorem 2, MRR strategy) — plus the category-matching necessary condition $H=0$ (Theorem 3) and the Apollonius-circle degenerate case (Lemmas 5, Theorem 4). Cite it as: the bridge reference between Isaacs-style geometric reach-avoid solutions [4,20] and second-order damped dynamics [34]; as the source of the observation that endpoint-dominance no longer implies path-safety under non-straight optimal trajectories (Remark 2); and as evidence that PMP-derived constant-heading strategies are only locally optimal when the attacker's reachable set has internal time structure. Suitable citation sentences: "Lyu et al. introduced the multiple reachable region and the third-type attacker's dominance region for reach-avoid games with damped double integrator dynamics, showing that the attacker may need to reduce its acceleration to remain uncapturable inside the defender's multiple reachable region [P061]"; or "Unlike simple-motion reach-avoid games where dominance regions are Apollonius circles, damped double-integrator players admit a three-part dominance-region typology in which parts of the defender's reachable set become attacker-dominant only under non-maximal acceleration [P061]."
