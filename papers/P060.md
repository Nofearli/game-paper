## 2. Research Background
Maritime security tasks — intercepting smuggling vessels and protecting civilian harbors — are increasingly modeled as reach-avoid games for unmanned surface vehicles (USVs) (p. 1, citing Shalumov 2022; Siddhardha & Ratnoo 2023; Wu et al. 2024; Zheng et al. 2023). Existing USV interception works based on nonlinear control (optimal sliding-mode guidance, adaptive backstepping, fast-marching path planning) design only the defender side and rarely analyze the attacker's winning conditions (p. 1–2). HJI-based differential-game methods give barriers but suffer from the curse of dimensionality, are hard to validate, and are "rarely adopted in real robots" (p. 2); learning methods inherit the same deployment gap. Geometric approaches (Apollonius circles, Voronoi diagrams, Cartesian ovals — Salmon et al. 2020; Yan et al. 2019; Garcia 2021; Shi et al. 2025) offer real-time computation but, per the authors, omit capture radii or omit proofs that the oval-based reachable region is actually invariant against deviating opponents. This paper positions itself as the bridge: a geometric two-defender reach-avoid solution with capture radii, provable non-inferiority, and a full USV hardware deployment chain.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
**Problem** (p. 3): a 2D reach-avoid game with two defenders ($D_1, D_2$) and one attacker ($A$), circular target region $\mathcal T$ centered at $\mathbf p_T$ with radius $\rho_T = \rho_T^0 + \rho_A$ (original protected radius inflated by the attacker's interception radius $\rho_A$, so $\rho_A$ never appears again). Each agent has a maximum speed $v^{\max}_{(\cdot)}$; each defender has capture radius $\rho_{D_i}$ (interception = distance $\le \rho_{D_i}$). Termination at $t_f$: (1) defenders win if they intercept $A$ before $A$ reaches $\partial\mathcal T$; (2) attacker wins if it reaches $\mathcal T$ first (p. 3). Decision outputs are reference speed $v^{\mathrm{ref}}$ and heading $\psi^{\mathrm{ref}}$. With fixed initial defender positions, the DDR is the set of attacker initial positions guaranteed interceptable under optimal defense, the ADR the set from which $A$ guarantees intrusion. Payoffs: in DDR, defenders maximize / attacker minimizes $d_{A\mathcal T}(t_f) = d_{AT} - \rho_T$; in ADR, defenders minimize / attacker maximizes $\min_{i=1,2}\{d_{D_iA}(t_f) - \rho_{D_i}\}$ (p. 3). Objectives: strategies for both regions for all agents, a barrier separating DDR/ADR, and USV application.

**Gap as claimed by the authors** (p. 2, contribution list (a)–(c)):
- [authors' claim] (a) Existing geometric studies (Fang et al. 2022; Liang et al. 2019; Salmon et al. 2020; Yan et al. 2020) neglect the defender's capture radius, which "is non-negligible in practical engineering"; Garcia (2021) and Shi et al. (2025) do use a nonzero capture radius but "directly adopted the Cartesian oval as the boundary of the reachable region without analyzing whether the attacker can reach positions outside this region by adjusting its strategy" — i.e., no invariance/proof layer.
- [authors' claim] (b) Existing models (Liang et al. 2020; Shi et al. 2025; Yan et al. 2025, 2020) "default that all agents move at their respective constant maximum speeds", so defenders cannot respond to an attacker moving slower within its constraints.
- [authors' claim] (c) Yan et al. (2019)'s ADR defense only covers the case where attacker and defenders move toward their respective predicted optimal points, with no response to deviations and no guaranteed distance indicator at $t_f$.

**Verified from cited text**: the cited baselines are correctly characterized at title/venue level — Garcia (2021, Automatica) is "Cooperative target protection from a superior attacker" (Cartesian-oval construction), Shi et al. (2025, Neurocomputing) is "Determination of barrier surface in target-attacker-defender game with capture radius for superior pursuer", and Yan et al. (2019, IEEE TCYB) is "Reach-avoid games with two defenders and one attacker: an analytical approach" (the direct ancestor, also the source of the game-level baselines in Sec. 4.1). The substantive criticism — that prior oval constructions lack a closed-loop proof that the attacker cannot break out of the oval under arbitrary headings/speeds — is a fair reading of these works' construction-style (as opposed to proof-style) presentations, and the paper does supply that missing layer (its Lemma 3). Claim (b) is the weakest: dominance-region geometry defined at max speed remains valid for slower opponents (a slower attacker has a *smaller* reachable region, so the defenders' guarantee is only weakened conservatively); the genuinely new element is the closed-form speed-adaptive heading law (2) with the ratio $\beta_i v_A / v^{\max}_{D_i}$, not the removal of a correctness flaw.

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Theory (reachability)**: a scalar *time-difference function* $f_{\mathrm{diff},i}(\mathbf p_R) = (d_{D_iR}-\rho_{D_i})/\beta_i - d_{AR}$ (p. 4) as a per-point reachability criterion (Lemma 1), a Lipschitz-type property of the oval's direction map (Lemma 2), and a closed-loop invariance proof that under the proposed oval-based interception strategy (2) the attacker is confined to the Cartesian oval $\mathcal R_i$ (Lemma 3), extended to two defenders by set intersection (Theorem 1). This is exactly the proof layer claimed missing in Garcia (2021)/Shi et al. (2025).
2. **Theory (game values)**: non-inferiority ("no alternative strategy can do better") theorems giving the DDR value $d_{MT}(t_0)-\rho_T$ (Theorem 2) and the ADR value $\Delta_{D_1D_2N}$ (Theorem 3), the latter via a *virtual auxiliary capture radius* $\rho^{\mathrm{aux}}_{D_i} = \rho_{D_i} + \Delta_{D_1D_2N}$.
3. **Modeling/algorithm**: speed-adaptive defender heading laws (2) (DDR) and (5) (ADR) embedding the attacker's real speed ratio via sine/arcsine composition; attacker strategies (3)/(4) heading to the farthest reachable point $\mathbf p_M$ and to the fΔ-maximizing target point $\mathbf p_N$; a constructive barrier-tracing algorithm (Sec. 3.4) with closed-form tangency angle $\phi_i = -\arcsin(d_{D_iT}\sin\theta_i/(\beta_i d_{D_iF}))$ and a two-defender case analysis (dominated arcs + oval-intersection arcs).
4. **Experiment (implementation)**: integration of the strategies as guidance-layer decision modules into the USV NGC closed loop with a 3-DOF surface-vessel model (eq. (6)) and the backstepping controller of Lin et al. (2024); validation by 3000-run Monte Carlo simulation and real lake trials (Jimei Lake, Xiamen) with one attacker USV and two defender USVs in both DDR and ADR scenarios.

## 5. Key Mathematical Results (MOST IMPORTANT — exhaustive)

### 5.1 Setup, information structure, winning conditions (p. 3)
- Agents: $D_1, D_2, A$ with positions $\mathbf p_{(\cdot)} = [x,y]^\mathsf T$, speed $v = \|\dot{\mathbf p}\| \le v^{\max}$; defender capture radii $\rho_{D_i}$; target $\mathcal T$: circle center $\mathbf p_T$, radius $\rho_T = \rho_T^0 + \rho_A$ (attacker radius absorbed, p. 3).
- Termination (p. 3): defenders win iff $A$ intercepted ($\exists i: d_{D_iA} \le \rho_{D_i}$) before $d_{A\mathcal T} = 0$; attacker wins iff it reaches $\partial\mathcal T$ first.
- Information structure: game-level strategies consume the real-time position, heading and surge speed of the own vehicle and the counterpart(s) (p. 8) — perfect-information state feedback; no partial observation.
- DDR/ADR definitions and payoffs as in Sec. 3 above; in DDR the defenders' goal is to intercept $A$ "as far as possible from $\mathcal T$", in ADR to be "as close as possible to $A$ when $A$ reaches $\mathcal T$" (p. 3).

### 5.2 Cartesian oval reachable region (pp. 3–4, unnumbered display + eq. (1))
- **Statement**: with speed ratio $\beta_i = v^{\max}_{D_i}/v^{\max}_A > 1$ and capture radius $\rho_{D_i}$, the boundary $\mathrm{br}_i$ of $A$'s reachable region $\mathcal R_i$ under $D_i$'s interception is a Cartesian oval centered at $A$ (attributed to Garcia 2021). In polar form about $\mathbf p_A$, with $\delta_A$ the angle between $A$'s heading ray and the LOS angle $\sigma_{D_iA} = \mathrm{atan2}(y_A - y_{D_i}, x_A - x_{D_i})$:
$$f_{r_A}(\delta_A) = \frac{1}{\beta_i^2 - 1}\Big\{\big[(d^2_{D_iA} - \rho^2_{D_i})(\beta_i^2 - 1) + (\beta_i\rho_{D_i} - d_{D_iA}\cos\delta_A)^2\big]^{1/2} + d_{D_iA}\cos\delta_A - \beta_i\rho_{D_i}\Big\}$$
  and the corresponding defender-side radius/angle (eq. (1), p. 4):
$$r_{D_i} = \beta_i r_A + \rho_{D_i},\qquad \delta_{D_i} = f_{\delta D}(\delta_A, \beta_i, \rho_{D_i}) = \arcsin\!\Big(\frac{f_{r_A}(\delta_A,\beta_i,\rho_{D_i})}{\beta_i f_{r_A}(\delta_A,\beta_i,\rho_{D_i}) + \rho_{D_i}}\sin\delta_A\Big) \in (-\tfrac{\pi}{2}, \tfrac{\pi}{2}).$$
- **Verification (mine)**: the oval condition $d_{D_iC} = \beta_i r_A + \rho_{D_i}$ (attacker flight time to $C$ equals defender time to reach the $\rho_{D_i}$-ball around $C$) plus the law of cosines $d^2_{D_iC} = d^2_{D_iA} + r_A^2 + 2 d_{D_iA} r_A \cos\delta_A$ gives a quadratic in $r_A$ whose positive root is exactly $f_{r_A}$ as printed; the $\delta_{D_i}$ formula is the sine rule in triangle $D_iAC$ ($r_A \sin\delta_{D_i} = r_A^{\mathrm{opp}}$... precisely $r_A/\sin\delta_{D_i} = r_{D_i}/\sin\delta_A$). Analytic, exact, global; reduces to the classic oval for $\rho_{D_i} = 0$.
- **Physical meaning**: $\mathcal R_i = \{f_{\mathrm{diff},i} > 0\}$ is the set of points $A$ can still win the race to; the defender-side map $f_{\delta D}$ tells $D_i$ where to head to cover the boundary point in direction $\delta_A$.

### 5.3 Lemma 1 (p. 4, proof p. 11 §1.1): reachability criterion
- **Statement**: for any position $\mathbf p_R$: (1) if $f_{\mathrm{diff},i}(\mathbf p_R) > 0$ then $A$ can reach $\mathbf p_R$ before being intercepted by $D_i$; (2) if $f_{\mathrm{diff},i}(\mathbf p_R) < 0$ *consistently*, $A$ cannot reach $\mathbf p_R$ before interception.
- **Proof approach**: minimum-time comparison $t_A = d_{AR}/v^{\max}_A$ vs. $t_{D_i} = (d_{D_iR} - \rho_{D_i})/v^{\max}_{D_i} = (d_{D_iR} - \rho_{D_i})/(\beta_i v^{\max}_A)$ — decisive equation $(d_{D_iR} - \rho_{D_i})/\beta_i > d_{AR} \Leftrightarrow t_A < t_{D_i}$. Part (2): from $d_{D_iR} \le \beta_i d_{AR} + \rho_{D_i}$ and the triangle inequality $d_{AR} \ge d_{D_iA} - d_{D_iR} \ge d_{D_iA} - (\beta_i d_{AR} + \rho_{D_i})$; then "Before $A$ is intercepted, $d_{D_iA} > \rho_{D_i}$ always holds. Substituting this into the inequality above yields $d_{AR} > 0$".
- **Proof gap (as printed, p. 11)**: the last step is a compressed contradiction argument — the intended reading is $(1+\beta_i) d_{AR} \ge d_{D_iA} - \rho_{D_i} > 0$ at any pre-interception time, and if $A$ were at $\mathbf p_R$ ($d_{AR} = 0$) the invariant would force $d_{D_iA} \le \rho_{D_i}$, i.e., already intercepted. The printed sentence "yields $d_{AR} > 0$, thus it also implies that $A$ cannot reach $\mathbf p_R$" does not by itself exclude arrival; the contradiction must be supplied by the reader. Also, the qualifier "consistently" silently converts a static inequality into a dynamic invariant; the invariance is actually proven only for boundary points (Lemma 3), and exterior-point guarantees are inherited from the initial-configuration reading used by Theorem 1.
- **Extensibility**: $f_{\mathrm{diff}}$ is the standard "time-to-go difference" generalized by the capture radius; it works for any $\beta_i > 0$ and is the paper's main reusable tool.

### 5.4 Lemma 2 (p. 4, proof p. 11 §1.2): contraction of the direction map
- **Statement**: for $f_{\delta D}$ defined in (1), $\left|\partial f_{\delta D}(\delta_A)/\partial\delta_A\right| < 1$.
- **Proof approach**: the boundary-point geometry satisfies $r_{D_i}\sin\delta_{D_i} = r_A\sin\delta_A$ and $r_{D_i}\cos\delta_{D_i} = d_{D_iA} + r_A\cos\delta_A$; implicit differentiation w.r.t. $\delta_A$ and elimination gives the decisive equation
$$\frac{\partial\delta_{D_i}}{\partial\delta_A} = \frac{r_A\big(\beta_i\cos(\delta_A - \delta_{D_i}) - 1\big)}{r_{D_i}\big(\beta_i - \cos(\delta_A - \delta_{D_i})\big)},$$
  then $\left|\partial\delta_{D_i}/\partial\delta_A\right| \le r_A/r_{D_i} < 1$ since $r_{D_i} = \beta_i r_A + \rho_{D_i} > r_A$.
- **Proof gap (hidden sub-inequality)**: the bound $\left|(\beta_i\cos\Delta - 1)/(\beta_i - \cos\Delta)\right| \le 1$ is used but not shown; it does hold for $\beta_i > 1$, $\Delta \in [-\pi,\pi]$ (equality only at $\cos\Delta = \pm1$ endpoints), and $f_{\delta D}$ is $C^1$ with $|f'| < 1$ pointwise, so on the compact interval between any two arguments the sup is $< 1$ — the use made of it in Lemma 3 (a mean-value-theorem step) is therefore valid.
- **Physical meaning**: the map from the attacker's heading offset to the required defender heading offset is non-expansive — heading corrections do not oscillate; this is what makes the closed-loop invariant provable.

### 5.5 Defender interception strategy (2) + Lemma 3 (p. 4, proof p. 11 §1.3) — the paper's core mechanism
- **Strategy (2)** (verified from 600-dpi render; the arcsin argument is a **product**, not a fraction):
$$v^{\mathrm{ref}}_{D_i} = v^{\max}_{D_i},\qquad \psi^{\mathrm{ref}}_{D_i} = \sigma_{D_iA} + \arcsin\!\Big(\sin\big(f_{\delta D}(\psi_A - \sigma_{D_iA})\big)\,\beta_i v_A / v^{\max}_{D_i}\Big).$$
  Note $\beta_i v_A/v^{\max}_{D_i} = v_A/v^{\max}_A \in [0,1]$ — literally "the ratio of the attacker's actual speed to its speed limit" of contribution (b): at $v_A = v^{\max}_A$ the defender aims exactly at the oval boundary point in the attacker's heading direction; as $v_A \to 0$ the heading degenerates to pure pursuit $\psi^{\mathrm{ref}} \to \sigma_{D_iA}$.
- **Lemma 3 statement**: $A$ can reach any position within $\mathcal R_i$ irrespective of $D_i$'s strategies, and cannot reach any position outside $\mathcal R_i$ before interception when $D_i$ applies strategy (2).
- **Proof approach**: for a fixed boundary point $\mathbf p_C \in \mathrm{br}_i$ ($f_{\mathrm{diff},i}(\mathbf p_C) = 0$ at $t_0$), with direction angles $\delta_A + \sigma_{D_iA}$ (from $A$) and $f_{\delta D}(\delta_A) + \sigma_{D_iA}$ (from $D_i$): $\dot d_{AC} = -v_A\cos(\psi_A - \sigma_{D_iA} - \delta_A)$, $\dot d_{D_iC} = -v^{\max}_{D_i}\cos(\psi^{\mathrm{ref}}_{D_i} - \sigma_{D_iA} - f_{\delta D}(\delta_A))$. From (2): $\sin(\psi^{\mathrm{ref}}_{D_i} - \sigma_{D_iA}) = \frac{\beta_i v_A}{v^{\max}_{D_i}}\sin(f_{\delta D}(\psi_A - \sigma_{D_iA}))$ and $\cos(\psi^{\mathrm{ref}}_{D_i} - \sigma_{D_iA}) \ge \cos(f_{\delta D}(\psi_A - \sigma_{D_iA})) \ge 0$, $\cos(f_{\delta D}(\delta_A)) \ge 0$. Decisive chain:
$$\dot d_{D_iC} \le -v^{\max}_{D_i}\cos(f_{\delta D}(\psi_A - \sigma_{D_iA}))\cos(f_{\delta D}(\delta_A)) - \beta_i v_A\sin(f_{\delta D}(\psi_A - \sigma_{D_iA}))\sin(f_{\delta D}(\delta_A)) \le -\beta_i v_A\cos\big(f_{\delta D}(\psi_A - \sigma_{D_iA}) - f_{\delta D}(\delta_A)\big),$$
  where the first inequality uses $v^{\max}_{D_i} - \beta_i v_A \ge 0$ and the second rewrites $-v^{\max}_{D_i}c_1c_2 - \beta_i v_A s_1s_2 \le -\beta_i v_A(c_1c_2 + s_1s_2)$. Then
$$\dot f_{\mathrm{diff},i}(\mathbf p_C) = \dot d_{D_iC}/\beta_i - \dot d_{AC} \le -v_A\Big(\cos\big(f_{\delta D}(\psi_A - \sigma_{D_iA}) - f_{\delta D}(\delta_A)\big) - \cos(\psi_A - \sigma_{D_iA} - \delta_A)\Big) \le 0,$$
  the last step by Lemma 2 + mean value theorem ($|\psi_A - \sigma_{D_iA} - \delta_A| \ge |f_{\delta D}(\psi_A - \sigma_{D_iA}) - f_{\delta D}(\delta_A)|$) and cosine non-increasing on $[0,\pi]$. Hence $f_{\mathrm{diff},i}(\mathbf p_C) \le 0$ is invariant for every initial boundary point; since $\mathrm{br}_i$ is a closed curve, $A$ can never exit $\mathcal R_i$; interior reachability is Lemma 1(1) applied pointwise.
- **Typo (p. 11)**: the intermediate line prints $\cos(\psi^{\mathrm{ref}}_{D_i} - \sigma_{D_iA} - f_{\delta D}(\delta_A))$ as the second cosine argument of $\dot f_{\mathrm{diff}}$, but the subsequent step of the same proof uses $\cos(\psi_A - \sigma_{D_iA} - \delta_A)$, which is the correct quantity (it is $-\dot d_{AC}/v_A$); the chain is recoverable and consistent with strategy (2)'s product form.
- **Physical meaning**: the defender always covers the oval boundary point in the attacker's instantaneous heading direction, damped by the attacker's speed ratio; the attacker's reachable set can never grow beyond the initial oval. This is the invariance proof the authors claim Garcia (2021)/Shi et al. (2025) lack.
- **Extensibility**: the argument is per-defender and independent of the second defender; it carries over verbatim to the enlarged ovals used in Theorem 3.

### 5.6 Theorem 1 (p. 4): two-defender reachable region and DDR/ADR criterion
- **Statement**: suppose $v^{\max}_A < v^{\max}_{D_1}$ and $v^{\max}_A < v^{\max}_{D_2}$ (i.e., $\beta_1, \beta_2 > 1$); then $A$ cannot reach any position outside $\mathcal R_{1,2} = \mathcal R_1 \cap \mathcal R_2$ when $D_1, D_2$ execute strategy (2), but can reach any position inside $\mathcal R_{1,2}$ before interception, regardless of the defenders' strategies.
- **Proof**: 6 lines. For $\mathbf p_R \notin \mathcal R_1 \cap \mathcal R_2$, either $f_{\mathrm{diff},1}(\mathbf p_R) < 0$ or $f_{\mathrm{diff},2}(\mathbf p_R) < 0$, so (Lemma 1) that defender intercepts first; for $\mathbf p_R \in \mathcal R_1 \cap \mathcal R_2$ both $f_{\mathrm{diff},i} > 0$, so $A$ wins both races. Relies on the initial-configuration/"consistently" reading of Lemma 1; no separate dynamic argument (acceptable given Lemma 3 confines $A$ to the initial ovals).
- **DDR/ADR criterion (stated as bullets, p. 4, not a numbered theorem)**: $\mathcal R_{1,2}(t_0) \cap \mathcal T = \emptyset \Rightarrow A$ is in the DDR; $\mathcal R_{1,2}(t_0) \cap \mathcal T \ne \emptyset \Rightarrow A$ is in the ADR. Correctness follows from Theorem 1 + the definitions; the tangency case (boundary touch) is the barrier, Sec. 5.9.
- **Physical meaning**: the two-defender extension costs nothing structurally — no task allocation is needed because each defender unilaterally enforces its own oval; the protected region is exactly the intersection.

### 5.7 Strategy (3) + Theorem 2 (pp. 4–5): DDR game value
- **Strategy (3)** (attacker, DDR): $v^{\mathrm{ref}}_A = v^{\max}_A$, $\psi^{\mathrm{ref}}_A = \mathrm{atan2}(y_M - y_A, x_M - x_A)$, where $\mathbf p_M$ is the closest point of $\mathcal R_{1,2}(t_0)$ to $\mathcal T$ (p. 5, Fig. 4). Defenders keep (2).
- **Theorem 2**: when $A$ is in the DDR: (i) if defenders execute (2) perfectly, $d_{A\mathcal T}(t_f) \ge d_{MT}(t_0) - \rho_T$ regardless of $A$'s strategy; (ii) if $A$ executes (3) perfectly, $d_{A\mathcal T}(t_f) \le d_{MT}(t_0) - \rho_T$ regardless of the defenders' strategies. So $d_{MT}(t_0) - \rho_T$ is the DDR zero-sum value and (2)/(3) are non-inferior (Remark 2, p. 5).
- **Proof**: (i) by Theorem 1, $A$ stays inside $\mathcal R_{1,2}(t_0)$, whose closest point to $\mathcal T$ is $\mathbf p_M$, so at interception $d_{A\mathcal T} \ge d_{MT}(t_0) - \rho_T$ (the proof line prints $d_{MT}(t_f)$ — typo for $t_0$). (ii) at $t_0$, $f_{\mathrm{diff},i}(\mathbf p_M) = 0$ (for the defender(s) whose oval defines $\mathbf p_M$; $\ge 0$ for the other — the proof writes $= 0$ for both, a harmless imprecision), and along play
$$\dot f_{\mathrm{diff},i}(\mathbf p_M) = \dot d_{D_iM}/\beta_i + v^{\max}_A \ge -v^{\max}_{D_i}/\beta_i + v^{\max}_A = 0,$$
  the decisive inequality (uses only $|\dot d_{D_iM}| \le v^{\max}_{D_i}$, i.e., holds for *any* defender motion; $v^{\max}_{D_i}/\beta_i = v^{\max}_A$). Hence $\min_i f_{\mathrm{diff},i}(\mathbf p_M) \ge 0$ is maintained and $A$ reaches $\mathbf p_M$ (Lemma 1(1)/Lemma 3), where $d_{A\mathcal T} = d_{MT}(t_0) - \rho_T$ exactly; "the distance cannot be greater at interception" — implicitly assuming $A$ keeps approaching $\mathcal T$ afterward (minor unstated monotonicity assumption).
- **Analytic/global**: exact value statement, no numerics; local in the sense that $\mathbf p_M$ and $\Delta$ are computed from initial configuration.

### 5.8 Strategies (4)–(5) + Theorem 3 (p. 5, proof p. 12 §1.4): ADR game value
- **Predicted-distance functional**: assuming $A$ runs at max speed to an arbitrary $\mathbf p_B \in \mathcal T$, $f_\Delta(\mathbf p_B) = \min_{i=1,2}\{d_{D_iB} - \beta_i d_{AB} - \rho_{D_i}\}$ (p. 5); define $\mathbf p_N = \arg\max_{\mathbf p_B \in \mathcal T} f_\Delta(\mathbf p_B)$ and $\Delta_{D_1D_2N} = f_\Delta(\mathbf p_N)$ — the best distance margin the attacker can promise itself, equivalently the worst-case closeness the defenders must accept.
- **Strategy (4)** (attacker): $v^{\mathrm{ref}}_A = v^{\max}_A$, $\psi^{\mathrm{ref}}_A = \mathrm{atan2}(y_N - y_A, x_N - x_A)$; "(x_A, y_A) and (x_N, y_N) are the coordinates of $A$'s *initial* position and $\mathbf p_N$" (p. 5) — an initial-configuration (open-loop reference) design; online recomputation policy is unspecified.
- **Strategy (5)** (defenders): same product form as (2) but with a **virtual auxiliary capture radius** $\rho_{D_i} + \Delta_{D_1D_2N}$ inside $f_{\delta D}$:
$$v^{\mathrm{ref}}_{D_i} = v^{\max}_{D_i},\qquad \psi^{\mathrm{ref}}_{D_i} = \sigma_{D_iA} + \arcsin\!\Big(\beta_i v_A / v^{\max}_{D_i} \times \sin\big(f_{\delta D}(\psi_A - \sigma_{D_iA},\, \beta_i,\, \rho_{D_i} + \Delta_{D_1D_2N})\big)\Big).$$
- **Theorem 3**: when $A$ is in the ADR: (i) if defenders execute (5) perfectly, $\min_{i}\{d_{D_iA}(t_f) - \rho_{D_i}\} \le \Delta_{D_1D_2N}$ when $A$ reaches $\mathcal T$, regardless of $A$; (ii) if $A$ executes (4) perfectly, $\min_i\{d_{D_iA}(t_f) - \rho_{D_i}\} \ge \Delta_{D_1D_2N}$, regardless of the defenders. Non-inferiority of (5)/(4) in the ADR (Remark 3).
- **Proof (ii) — clean**: $A$ reaches $\mathbf p_N$ at $t = d_{AN}/v^{\max}_A$; $D_i$ can travel at most $\beta_i d_{AN}$; triangle inequality gives $d_{D_iA}(t_f) \ge d_{D_iN} - \beta_i d_{AN}$, hence $\min_i\{d_{D_iA}(t_f) - \rho_{D_i}\} \ge \min_i\{d_{D_iN} - \beta_i d_{AN} - \rho_{D_i}\} = f_\Delta(\mathbf p_N) = \Delta_{D_1D_2N}$ — decisive equation.
- **Proof (i) — constructive but terse**: introduce $\rho^{\mathrm{aux}}_{D_i} = \rho_{D_i} + \Delta_{D_1D_2N}$ and the virtual region $\mathcal R^{\mathrm{aux}}_{1,2}$ (Fig. 5); "following the derivation logic of Lemma 3 and Theorem 1", under (5) $A$ cannot exit $\mathcal R^{\mathrm{aux}}_{1,2}$ before the interception distance drops to $\rho^{\mathrm{aux}}_{D_i}$. Since $\Delta_{D_1D_2N} = \max_{\mathbf p_B \in \mathcal T} f_\Delta$, no point of $\mathcal T$ lies inside $\mathcal R^{\mathrm{aux}}_{1,2}$ (membership $f^{\mathrm{aux}}_{\mathrm{diff},i} > 0$ is exactly $f_\Delta$'s $i$-term $> \Delta$), so $A$ cannot reach $\mathcal T$ until $\min_i\{d_{D_iA} - \rho_{D_i}\} \le \Delta_{D_1D_2N}$.
- **Proof gap (minor, p. 12)**: the step from "cannot reach $\mathcal T$ before the distance drops to $\Delta$" to "$d_{D_iA}(t_f) - \rho_{D_i} \le \Delta$ at $t_f$" assumes the defenders maintain the approach afterward (no explicit monotonicity argument), and the invariance for the enlarged ovals is invoked by analogy rather than re-proven; equality/non-strict cases are not treated.
- **Physical meaning**: in the ADR the defenders do not try to prevent intrusion (impossible) but to cap the attacker's safety margin at exactly $\Delta_{D_1D_2N}$ by pretending their capture radius is larger by $\Delta$ — an elegant way to reuse the DDR machinery.

### 5.9 Barrier computation (pp. 5–6, Sec. 3.4) — constructive, not a theorem
- **Single defender**: for $\mathbf p_{E_i} \in \mathcal B_i$ (barrier = locus of attacker positions where $\mathcal R_i$ is tangent to $\mathcal T$ at $\mathbf p_F$), define angles $\theta_i = \angle(\overrightarrow{TF}, \overrightarrow{TD_i})$, $\phi_i = \angle(\overrightarrow{FE_i}, \overrightarrow{TF})$, $\lambda_i = \angle(\overrightarrow{TE_i}, \overrightarrow{TD_i})$ (Fig. 6a), with
$$d_{D_iF} = \sqrt{d^2_{D_iT} + \rho_T^2 - 2 d_{D_iT}\rho_T\cos\theta_i},\qquad d_{FE_i} = \sqrt{\rho_T^2 + d^2_{TE_i} - 2\rho_T d_{TE_i}\cos(\lambda_i - \theta_i)}.$$
  Tangency point condition (Lemma 1): $G_i = d_{D_iF} - \rho_{D_i} - \beta_i d_{FE_i} = 0$; stationarity $\partial G_i/\partial\theta_i = 0$ with $\partial d_{D_iF}/\partial\theta_i = d_{D_iT}\rho_T\sin\theta_i/d_{D_iF}$ and $\partial d_{FE_i}/\partial\theta_i = \rho_T d_{TE_i}\sin(\theta_i - \lambda)/d_{FE_i} = -\rho_T\sin\phi_i$ yields the closed form
$$d_{FE_i} = \frac{d_{D_iF} - \rho_{D_i}}{\beta_i},\qquad \phi_i = -\arcsin\!\Big(\frac{d_{D_iT}\sin\theta_i}{\beta_i\, d_{D_iF}}\Big);$$
  sweeping $\theta_i \in [0, 2\pi]$ traces $\mathcal B_i$ (Fig. 6b). Barrier points outside $\mathcal R_i$'s tangency satisfy $G_i \le 0$.
- **Two defenders**: for a candidate tangency point $\mathbf p_F$ from $D_1$'s construction: **Case 1** $d_{D_2F} - \rho_{D_2} - \beta_2 d_{FE_1} > 0$ — $D_2$ cannot intercept $A$ running from $\mathbf p_{E_1}$ to $\mathbf p_F$; interception is $D_1$-dominated, $\mathbf p_F \in \mathcal R_2$, $\mathbf p_F \in \mathrm{br}_{1,2}$, and $\mathrm{br}_{1,2}$ coincides with $\mathrm{br}_1$ near $\mathbf p_F$; $\mathbf p_{E_1} \in \mathcal B_{1,2}$. **Case 2** $\le 0$: $\mathbf p_{E_1}$ rejected. Symmetrically, $\mathbf p_{E_i} \in \mathcal B_{1,2}$ iff $d_{D_{2-i}F} - \rho_{D_{2-i}} - \beta_{2-i} d_{FE_i} > 0$, $\theta_i \in [0,2\pi]$. **Intersection arcs** where the tangency point lies on both $\mathrm{br}_1$ and $\mathrm{br}_2$: angles $\theta_1^*, \theta_2^*$ (possibly two solution pairs) solve $G_1 = G_2 = 0$ plus sign conditions $\partial G_1/\partial\theta_1|_{\theta_1^*} \ge 0 \ \&\ \partial G_2/\partial\theta_2|_{\theta_2^*} \le 0$ (or both reversed); remaining arc points satisfy $\phi_1(\phi_2)$ between $-\arcsin(d_{D_1T}\sin\theta_1/(\beta_1 d_{D_1F}))$ and $-\arcsin(d_{D_2T}\sin\theta_2/(\beta_2 d_{D_2F}))$ and $d_{FE_{1,2}} = (d_{D_1F} - \rho_{D_1})/\beta_1 = (d_{D_2F} - \rho_{D_2})/\beta_2$ (Fig. 7c: closed curve around $\mathcal T$, ADR inside, DDR outside).
- **Nature and gaps**: this is a numerical tracing algorithm (parameter sweep + root conditions), not a formal theorem: the derivative-sign conditions, the "possibly two solution pairs" claim, and the assertion that the traced $\mathcal B_{1,2}$ exactly separates DDR from ADR are stated without proof. Still, the construction is the only one in the paper that handles the mixed regime where neither defender alone dominates.

### 5.10 USV dynamics and physical implementation chain (p. 8, eq. (6); p. 10, Fig. 17)
- **USV model (3-DOF, per agent $(\cdot) \in \{D_1, D_2, A\}$)**:
$$\dot x = v\cos\psi - s\sin\psi,\quad \dot y = v\sin\psi + s\cos\psi,\quad \dot\psi = r,$$
$$m_1\dot v = m_2 s r - X_u v + T + b_u,\quad m_2\dot s = -m_1 v r - Y_v s + b_v,\quad J\dot r = -N_r r + \tau + b_r,$$
  with surge/sway/yaw disturbances bounded $|b_u| \le B_v$, $|b_v| \le B_v$, $|b_r| \le B_r$ (as printed — $B_v$ doubles as the surge bound; p. 8). $T$, $\tau$ are thrust/torque control inputs.
- **Implementation chain**: game strategy (guidance layer) consumes real-time $\mathbf p, \psi, v$ of own and counterpart USVs and outputs $(v^{\mathrm{ref}}, \psi^{\mathrm{ref}})$; the lower-level controller (backstepping design of Lin et al. 2024, parameters $k_1, k_2, k_3, \epsilon$, Table 4) uses feedback $(\mathbf p, \psi, v, s, r)$ to compute $(T, \tau)$; the whole NGC loop runs in MATLAB/Simulink on an off-board computer, streaming commands over radio at 10 Hz (100 ms interval) to three transceivers, one per USV (p. 10, Figs. 10, 17). GNSS at 5 Hz with centimeter accuracy is the only mandatory sensor; no target-perception sensors are carried (p. 9–10) — states are shared, so the information structure remains perfect information via the radio link.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
**Author-stated**: (1) the game-level strategy assumes perfect control; when embedded in the closed loop, control errors "may arise, potentially reducing efficiency" (p. 8) — addressed only empirically via Monte Carlo; (2) no target-perception sensors are deployed, so real-world adversarial sensing (an attacker would not broadcast its state) is out of scope (pp. 9–10); (3) only one trial per scenario.

**Critical observations** (with page/eq. anchors):
1. **Minimal defender cooperation**: each defender unilaterally plays the single-defender oval law (2)/(5); Theorem 1 is a pure set-intersection argument (p. 4). There is no role/sector assignment and no exploit of the second defender beyond shrinking $\mathcal R_{1,2}$ — the price of the clean value theorems is that mixed regimes (e.g., one slower defender, $\beta_i \le 1$) are simply excluded by the Theorem 1 hypothesis.
2. **Proof gap in Lemma 1(2)** (p. 11): the triangle-inequality chain ends with the non-sequitur-as-printed "yields $d_{AR} > 0$"; the contradiction must be reconstructed (see 5.3), and the "consistently" qualifier hides the static-to-dynamic conversion that Lemma 3 performs only for boundary points.
3. **Hidden sub-inequality in Lemma 2** (p. 11): $|(\beta_i\cos\Delta - 1)/(\beta_i - \cos\Delta)| \le 1$ used without proof (valid for $\beta_i > 1$).
4. **Typos**: Theorem 2 proof prints $d_{MT}(t_f) - \rho_T$ where $d_{MT}(t_0) - \rho_T$ is meant (p. 5); Lemma 3 proof prints $\cos(\psi^{\mathrm{ref}}_{D_i} - \sigma_{D_iA} - f_{\delta D}(\delta_A))$ where the next line uses the correct $\cos(\psi_A - \sigma_{D_iA} - \delta_A)$ (p. 11); disturbance bound $B_v$ serves for both $|b_u|$ and $|b_v|$ (p. 8); $f_{\delta D}$'s argument list is inconsistent between (1)/(2) (implicit $\beta_i, \rho_{D_i}$) and (5) (explicit, p. 5).
5. **Unstated monotonicity in the value theorems**: Theorem 2(ii) ("the distance cannot be greater at interception") and Theorem 3(i) implicitly assume the attacker keeps closing on $\mathcal T$ and the defenders keep closing on $A$ after the value point/distance cap is reached (pp. 5, 12).
6. **Barrier is an algorithm, not a result** (pp. 5–6): no theorem states that the traced $\mathcal B_{1,2}$ is the exact DDR/ADR boundary; the derivative-sign conditions at oval intersections and the "possibly two solution pairs" claim are asserted; tangency-measure/regularity issues (e.g., when $\mathbf p_F$ degenerates) are not discussed.
7. **ADR strategy is an initial-configuration design**: $\mathbf p_N$ and $\Delta_{D_1D_2N}$ are computed from $t_0$ positions (p. 5); whether they are refreshed online during play is unspecified, and Theorem 3 is a $t_0$-statement — under heavy maneuvering the guaranteed $\Delta$ may not track the current configuration.
8. **Attacker radius folded away** ($\rho_T = \rho_T^0 + \rho_A$, p. 3): neat, but it bakes the attacker's size into the target and means the attacker "wins" by its own interception disc touching the protected disc — the barrier geometry depends on this convention.
9. **Evaluation asymmetry**: game-level and Monte Carlo comparisons use capture-radius-blind / PN / direct-rush baselines, which are known-weak for this setting; no comparison against the capture-radius-aware works the paper criticizes (Garcia 2021; Shi et al. 2025), so the claimed superiority within that family is argued theoretically, not measured.
10. **Scale and statistics of the trials**: speeds ≤ 1.1 m/s, target radius 21 m, single runs, and the identical 9.03 m defender distances in the ADR trial (p. 10) leave robustness (wind gusts at 10 m/s, radio latency at 100 ms) unquantified.

## 8. Citation Value
This is the collection's cleanest "theory-to-hull" arch: a geometric reach-avoid solution (Cartesian oval + time-difference function + invariance proof + two value theorems) that is actually run on three physical USVs in a lake, with the full NGC integration (guidance-layer strategy → backstepping controller → 3-DOF model) spelled out. In an archive, it plays four roles: (i) as the engineering counterpart of Yan et al. (2019)-style two-defender geometry, showing what changes when capture radii and non-maximal attacker speeds are taken seriously (P060's Lemma 1/3 layer); (ii) as a source of two reusable tricks — the speed-ratio-damped arcsine heading law (2) and the virtual auxiliary capture radius of Theorem 3; (iii) as a rare worked barrier-construction algorithm for the two-defender/circular-target case (Sec. 3.4); and (iv) as the canonical citation for USV reach-avoid field validation methodology (off-board Simulink NGC, 5 Hz GNSS, 10 Hz command loop, Jimei Lake trials). Suggested citation sentences: "Lin et al. derive Cartesian-oval reach-avoid strategies with nonzero capture radii and prove their non-inferiority for two-defender-one-attacker games, validating them in Monte Carlo and lake trials with three USVs [P060]." or "Geometric reach-avoid strategies have been deployed as guidance-layer modules in the closed-loop NGC system of physical USVs and verified in field experiments [P060]."
