## 2. Research Background
Unmanned surface vehicles are small, cheap and low-detectable, and a canonical adversarial mission is for one USV to penetrate a defended region safely. In reach-avoid (RA) games the geometric/Apollonius approach avoids solving the Hamilton–Jacobi–Isaacs PDE for nonlinear kinematics and yields Nash-equilibrium feature points directly from reachable sets. The authors observe that the existing geometric RA literature overwhelmingly analyzes the *advantaged* side — pursuers with superior speed, superior number, or obstacle assistance (their refs. [19]–[28]) — while the disadvantaged (inferior) penetrator, which is outnumbered and slower, is acknowledged as important but under-studied. For marine applications the target region is additionally an arbitrary polygon (possibly non-convex), not the disk or line segment of classical formulations. The paper builds a complete penetration pipeline — penetration-point selection, penetration condition, and a yaw-rate-feasible two-stage guidance law — for a single slower USV against multiple faster defensive USVs.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
**Problem.** A penetrative USV E must reach the boundary ∂Ω_goal of a polygonal target region in the plane before being intercepted by any of J defensive USVs P_j; every defender is faster: λ_j = u_{P_j}/u_E ∈ (1, +∞) (eq. (4d), p. 4). Both sides have bounded yaw rate (assumption (e): r_E = K_E u_E/R_E, r_{P_j} = K_{P_j} u_{P_j}/R_{P_j}), interception is ‖x_E − x_{P_j}‖₂ ≤ 2r (eq. (4f)) and successful reach is ‖x_E − x‖₂ ≤ r for some x ∈ ∂Ω_goal (eq. (4g)).

**Gap — authors' claim** (pp. 1–3): prior RA-game research "mainly focuses on the advantage side of the game" (velocity, number, obstacle assistance); the strategy of the disadvantaged side is "an essential but challenging aspect"; and prior methods do not handle *complex* (convex and non-convex) target regions together with yaw-velocity constraints, so the penetrator's strategy is incomplete under the twin constraints of interception avoidance and safe arrival.
**Verified from cited text**: the works cited to support the gap do match the description at the title level — Yan et al. (TAC 2024, homicidal-chauffeur RA games, ref. [20]) and Li et al. (TAC 2024, damped double integrators, ref. [19]) analyze pursuer-winning conditions; Ramana & Kothari (CDC 2015, ref. [22]) encircle a *high-speed evader* with multiple pursuers; Wasz et al. (MED 2019, ref. [23]) and Vlassakis & Pachter (IFAC 2020, ref. [24]) study two-on-one pursuit with pursuer speed advantage or parity; Sun et al. (JMSE 2022, ref. [26]) and Chen et al. (Ocean Eng. 2024, ref. [27]) are USV pursuit/interception from the pursuer side (Chen et al. is the group's companion paper on *inferior USVs intercepting a superior USV*). The claim that the slower, outnumbered *evader* side with polygonal targets is comparatively neglected is fair. However, the single-faster-defender Apollonius barrier for a slower evader is classical (Isaacs, ref. [30]), so the genuine novelty is narrower: (i) the complex-target-region (convex vs non-convex) treatment of the penetration point, and (ii) closed-loop yaw-rate-feasible guidance with provable alignment stage — both incremental but concrete.

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Penetration condition (PC) under complex target region** (theory): a geometric condition (eq. (20)) — the closed joint safe region S̃ (intersection of Apollonius disks of all defenders) intersects the target boundary and the signed set distance is non-increasing under the Nash guidance — proved via a KKT argument on the minimum-distance problem (eqs. (16)–(19)), with the indirect point y_F and penetration point x_I defined separately for **non-convex** (Definitions 1, eq. (10)) and **convex** (Definition 2, eq. (11)) target regions.
2. **Two-stage penetration strategy with two yaw-velocity constraints** (modeling + algorithm): Stage 1 converts the non-penetration-angle case (NPC, θ_E ≠ φ_E) into the penetration-angle case (PAC, θ_E = φ_E) using a bang-bang yaw law (eq. (42)) whose sufficiency requires 2r/R_E ≥ h₃(λ_j) (eq. (39)); Stage 2 is the Nash-equilibrium guidance K_E = G(X)ᵀ[cos φ_E, sin φ_E]ᵀ (eqs. (26)–(28)) whose yaw feasibility is captured by the necessary-and-sufficient condition (eq. (31)). Algorithm 1 (p. 10) pseudocodes the per-step pipeline.
3. **Simulation + Monte Carlo evidence** (experiment): two 6-defender scenarios (non-convex and convex target regions) and a 11200-run Monte Carlo comparison against a DWA-based method (ref. [33]) over distance/quantity/velocity/yaw-velocity ratio sweeps, reporting higher success rate and lower time for the proposed strategy.

## 5. Key Mathematical Results (exhaustive)

**Kinematics and information structure.** Eq. (1)–(2), p. 3: each USV is a 3-DOF surface vessel reduced by ignoring sway (v_{P_j} = v_E = 0, justified by large sway damping): η̇_E = R_E ω_E with η_E = [x_E, θ_E]ᵀ, ω_E = [u_E, v_E, r_E]ᵀ, R_E the planar rotation matrix; same for P_j with yaw ψ_{P_j}. Hence ẋ_E = u_E[cos θ_E, sin θ_E]. *Notation clash*: R_E denotes both the rotation matrix in (2) and a scalar yaw-constraint constant in (e)/(27)–(31)/Table 1 (R_E = 0.5); likewise the state X_j(t) = (x_E, θ_E, x_{P_j}, ψ_{P_j}) (p. 7). Assumptions (4a)–(4g), p. 4: (a) mutual defender separation ‖x_{P_i0} − x_{P_j0}‖₂ > 2r; (b) ‖x_{E0} − x_{P_j0}‖₂ > 2r; (c) all initial positions in Ω_play; (d) λ_j = u_{P_j}/u_E ∈ (1, +∞) (defenders strictly faster — E is the *inferior* agent in speed and number); (e) yaw-rate linear in surge: r_E = K_E u_E/R_E, r_{P_j} = K_{P_j} u_{P_j}/R_{P_j} with K ∈ [−1,1] the normalized control; (f) interception when ‖x_E − x_{P_j}‖₂ ≤ 2r; (g) reach when ‖x_E − x‖₂ ≤ r, x ∈ ∂Ω_goal. Information structure: full/perfect — "all USVs have real-time access to each other's positional and speed information via physical sensors" and the target boundary equation is known (p. 11). Target region modeling (eq. (3), p. 3): Ω_goal is a polygon whose edges are lines g_i(x,y) := y − m_i x − n_i, i = 1,…,I, and ∂Ω_goal is formed by joining the segments head-to-tail; Ω_play = ℝ².

**Apollonius safe region (eqs. (5)–(7), pp. 4–5).** f_j(x) = ‖x − x_{P_j}‖₂ − λ_j‖x − x_E‖₂; the safe region Ω_SRE = {x ∈ ℝ² : f_j(x) > 0} is the set E reaches strictly before P_j (contains x_E; it is the disk bounded by the Apollonius circle). Joint safe region (6): S̃ = ∩_{j=1}^J Ω̃_SRE^j; note the paper prints ∂S̃ = ∩_{j=1}^J ∂Ω̃_SRE^j, which conflates "boundary of intersection" with "intersection of boundaries" (the figures, e.g. Fig. 3, use the boundary of the intersection — a closed curve around E). Apollonius circle (7): center o_{Aj} = (λ_j² x_E − x_P)/(λ_j² − 1), radius r_{Aj} = λ_j‖x_P − x_E‖₂/(λ_j² − 1) (printed with x_P, an inconsistent shorthand for x_{P_j}). Two cases of joint region (Fig. 3): a single dominant circle, or an intersection over P, citing [13] (Von Moll et al., border defense).

**Signed set distance (eq. (8), p. 5).** l(Ω₁,Ω₂) = ‖x−y‖₂ for x ∈ Ω₁, y ∈ Ω₂ when Ω₁ ∩ Ω₂ = ∅, and −‖x−y‖₂ otherwise — a signed distance positive when disjoint.

**Definition 1 — Indirect point, non-convex case (eq. (9)–(10), p. 5).** The line i attaining the minimum, i = argmin_i l(∂S̃, g_i(x,y)) with g_i ∩ ∂S̃ ≠ ∅ (eq. (9)); y_F is "the unique point on ∂Ω_goal with minimum distance to ∂S̃", given as the perpendicular-foot construction through the Apollonius center o_{Aj}: x_F = [m_i/(1−λ²)]·[y_E − m̄_i x_E − λ²(y_P − m̄_i x_P) − (1−λ²)n_i]/(m̄_i²+1), y_F = [m̄_i²/(1−λ²)]·[y_E − m̄_i x_E − λ²(y_P − m̄_i x_P) + ((1−λ²)/m̄_i²)n_i]/(m̄_i²+1), where m̄_i, n̄_i are the gradient/intercept of the line orthogonal to g_i through o_{Aj}. *Defects*: λ carries no subscript j; n̄_i is declared but unused; the derivation is not shown; as printed it cannot be cross-checked against the line convention (see (11) below).

**Definition 2 — Boundary intersection, convex case (eq. (11), p. 6).** When the closest point would fall on the *extension* of an edge (convex region), y_F is taken as the vertex of two edges i, j: x_F = (n_i − n_j)/(m_i − m_j), y_F = m_i·(n_i − n_j)/(m_i − m_j) − n_i, s.t. g_i ∩ ∂S̃, g_j ∂S̃ ≠ ∅. **Sign error (verified against rendered p. 6)**: with the line definition g_i(x,y) := y − m_i x − n_i (eq. (3), p. 3, and the Fig. 3 labels "y = m_i x + n_i"), the vertex of lines i and j is x = (n_j − n_i)/(m_i − m_j), y = m_i(n_j − n_i)/(m_i − m_j) + n_i; the printed (11) instead matches lines y = m_i x − n_i. One of (3)/(11) is inconsistent — a concrete erratum affecting implementation of the convex-case penetration point.

**Penetration point (eqs. (12)–(14), p. 6, Definition 3).** x_C := f(x) − g_i(x,y) subject to l(∂S̃, g_i) ≤ 0 denotes the (one or two) intersection point(s) of the Apollonius circle with edge line g_i; τ₁ = (x_{C1} − o_{Aj})/‖x_{C1} − o_{Aj}‖₂, τ₂ analogously (13) — *as printed these are unit vectors, but they are used as angles τ ∈ [τ₁, τ₂] in (14); the intended meaning is the polar angles of the two intersection points about o_{Aj} (notation defect)*. Definition 3 / eq. (14): x_I = (λ_j² x_E − x_{P_j})/(λ_j² − 1) + [λ_j‖x_{P_j} − x_E‖₂/(λ_j² − 1)]·[cos τ, sin τ], s.t. τ ∈ [τ₁, τ₂] — i.e., the point of the (binding) Apollonius circle arc closest to ∂Ω_goal, realizing min distance between ∂S̃ and ∂Ω_goal. **Complex-target-region modeling, recorded precisely**: for a *non-convex* region the foot of the closest approach lies on an actual edge, and y_F is the perpendicular foot (Definition 1); for a *convex* region the closest approach may lie beyond an edge endpoint, so y_F snaps to a vertex (Definition 2); x_I is then the arc point of the binding Apollonius circle in the angular span subtended by the intersection chord (τ₁, τ₂). x_I depends on **one** Apollonius circle only (p. 13: "the calculation of the penetration point is only related to one of the Apollonius circle"), but the rule selecting the binding defender j from the joint region ∩_j is not formalized.

**Definition 4 — Saddle point / Nash guidance (eq. (15), p. 6).** E^Nes := [cos φ_E, sin φ_E] = (x_I − x_E)/‖x_I − x_E‖₂ and P_j^Nes := [cos φ_{P_j}, sin φ_{P_j}] = (x_I − x_{P_j})/‖x_I − x_{P_j}‖₂: both players head to x_I. Because f_j(x_I) = 0 (x_I on the Apollonius circle), ‖x_I − x_{P_j}‖ = λ_j‖x_I − x_E‖, so arrival times coincide: ‖x_I − x_E‖/u_E = ‖x_I − x_{P_j}‖/u_{P_j} — the classic simultaneous-arrival property (attributed to [30], Isaacs). Fig. 5 (p. 7) shows the payoff surface over (φ_E, φ_{P_j}) with the saddle point marked.

**Penetration condition (eqs. (16)–(20), pp. 6–7).** Optimize min l(x_I, y_F) s.t. f_j(x_I) ≥ 0, g_i(y_F) (17); KKT (18): 0 = ∇_x l + ϑ₁∇_x f_j(x_I), 0 = ∇_y l + ϑ₂∇_y g_i(y_F), f_j(x_I) ≥ 0, g_i(y_F), ϑ₁,ϑ₂ ≥ 0, ϑ₁f_j(x_I) = 0, ϑ₂g_i(y_F) = 0. Time derivative (19): d/dt l(∂S̃, ∂Ω_goal) = −ϑ₁∇_xᵀ f_j (dx_I/dt) − ϑ₂∇_yᵀ g_i (dy_F/dt). Argument: y_F stays on ∂Ω_goal so g_i(y_F) ≡ 0 and the second term vanishes; "if E adopts E^Nes, then ∇_xᵀ f_j (dx_I/dt) ≥ 0 holds. Therefore (16) holds [20]" — the decisive step ∇_xᵀ f_j ẋ_I ≥ 0 is **asserted with a citation to Yan et al. [20] rather than derived** (proof gap, p. 7). The PC (20) is the triple: l(∂S̃, ∂Ω_goal) ≤ 0; d/dt l(∂S̃, ∂Ω_goal) ≤ 0; ∂S̃ = ∩_{j=1}^J ∂Ω̃_SRE^j. Definition 5 (p. 7): X_j satisfies PC if it satisfies (20). Definition 6: **PAC** if φ_E = θ_E, **NPC** if φ_E ≠ θ_E.

**Stage 2 — PAC strategy (eqs. (21)–(28), p. 8).** Trajectory curvature (21): dθ_E/ds_E = (ÿ_E ẋ_E − ẍ_E ẏ_E)/u_E³, s_E = u_E t. Azimuth components (25): x = (x_{P_j} − x_E)/‖x_{P_j} − x_E‖₂, y = (y_{P_j} − y_E)/‖x_{P_j} − x_E‖₂ ∈ [−1,1]. Under PC and PAC the paper claims E "cannot be intercepted by P_j no matter what strategy P_j adopts" (p. 8) — this invariance claim for *arbitrary* defender play is not proven; only the Nash-pair behavior is analyzed.

**Algorithm 1 (p. 10)** — Two-stage penetration strategy when X_j(t) satisfies PC and NPC: per step, solve (5)–(6) for ∂S̃; if the target region is non-convex solve (10) for y_F (the pseudocode prints "Solve Eq.(10)" for both branches; the convex branch should read (11) — minor erratum), else solve (11); solve (12) and (14) for x_I; if PC holds: if NPC and 2r/R_E satisfies (39) return (42); else if PAC and 2r/R_E satisfies (31) return (27); otherwise return null (PC violated — no guarantee).

**Extensibility.** All quantities are closed-form in the current states; the Apollonius + polygon machinery extends to any J, to time-varying x_I (recomputed per step), and the yaw-feasibility conditions (31)/(39) are explicit parameter screens (λ_j, R_E, r, d) usable as admit/deny tests before committing a penetration attempt.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
**Author-stated (p. 14)**: PC is "the main limitation" of the strategy (when the joint safe region does not reach the target region the algorithm returns null); real obstacles are not considered; future work — cooperative multi-penetrator strategies when PC fails, and obstacle effects in the RA game.
**Critical observations (with locus)**:
1. **Eq. (37) is invalid as printed** (p. 10): the exact expansion of (14) gives ‖x_I − x_E‖₂² = d²[(1+λ_j²) + 2λ_j cos Δ]/(λ_j² − 1)² whose minimum over the circle is d²/(λ_j + 1)²; (37) drops the cross term and uses (λ_j² − 1) unsquared. Since (39) and (41) are obtained by substituting (37), the Stage-1 sufficiency chain is unsound as written (numerical check above: printed h₃ ≈ 2.13 passes with Table 1, corrected requirement ≈ 13.8 fails).
2. **Inequality-direction defect in (30)/(31)** (pp. 8–9): "max K_E ≥ 1" cannot coexist with the constraint K_E ∈ [−1,1]; the corrected "≤" reading is the one consistent with the simulated geometries. The inner min/max over K_E ∈ [−1,1] in (29)–(30) is vacuous, and the reduction from (28) to (29)–(30) is not derived.
3. **Sign error in (11)** (p. 6) relative to the line convention (3) and the Fig. 3 labels "y = m_i x + n_i": the vertex formula matches lines y = m_i x − n_i instead.
4. **Eq. (24) differentiation gap** (p. 8): the radial ḋ[cos τ, sin τ] term of ẋ_I is absent; the closed forms (26)–(28) inherit this unverified step.
5. **Eq. (13) type mismatch**: τ₁, τ₂ printed as unit vectors but used as angles in (14).
6. **Notation clashes**: R_E is both rotation matrix (2) and scalar constraint (e)/Table 1; x_P vs x_{P_j} in (7); unsubscripted λ in (10); Algorithm 1 line 6 prints "Eq.(10)" where the convex branch requires (11).
7. **Guarantee scope**: the claim "no matter what strategy P_j adopts, E cannot be intercepted" (p. 8) is not proven for arbitrary defender controls — the invariance d/dt l ≤ 0 is argued only along the Nash pair (and even there ∇_xᵀ f_j ẋ_I ≥ 0 is asserted via [20]); the strict ordering between reach (4g) and interception (4f) at tangency (l = 0, simultaneous arrival at x_I) is not analyzed — in the simulations separation is maintained with visible margin (Figs. 7b/8b).
8. **Binding-defender selection**: x_I is computed from a single Apollonius circle, but the rule choosing which j's circle attains the joint boundary is informal (p. 13 remark only).
9. **Baseline fairness**: the only comparison is against DWA, a non-game-theoretic local planner; no comparison against game-based penetrators (e.g., pure Apollonius closest-boundary-point pursuit without the two-stage/yaw-feasibility machinery), so "superiority" claims are confined to that baseline. Constant speeds, perfect information, no environmental disturbances (currents/waves), and known boundary equations are assumed throughout (p. 11).

## 8. Citation Value
This is an *application-oriented RA-game paper*: it imports the standard Apollonius-circle reachable-set machinery (Isaacs; Von Moll et al. border defense) into a marine-USV penetration setting and adds two genuinely useful engineering elements — a convex/non-convex polygonal target-region treatment of the closest penetration point (Definitions 1–3), and yaw-rate-feasible, two-stage closed-loop guidance with explicit admit conditions (31)/(39) on the speed ratio λ_j, radius-to-yaw-gain ratio 2r/R_E, and range ‖x_{P_j} − x_E‖. It is best cited as the "disadvantaged-side / complex-target-region" representative in RA-game surveys, as a USV kinematics instantiation (surge–yaw model with bounded yaw rate) of geometric reach-avoid games, and as evidence that Apollonius-based penetration points degrade gracefully with defender count (quantity-ratio-insensitive Monte Carlo results). Its analytic chain contains several printable defects ((37), (30)–(31), (11), (24) — see Section 7), so cite it for the problem formulation, the two-stage architecture, and the experimental methodology rather than as a rigorous source for the yaw-feasibility bounds. Suggested citation sentence: "For penetration of a polygonal (possibly non-convex) target region by a slower, outnumbered USV against multiple faster defenders, Yang et al. [P107] combine an Apollonius-circle joint safe region with a two-stage, yaw-rate-feasible Nash guidance law and report Monte Carlo success-rate and time advantages over a DWA baseline."
