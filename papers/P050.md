## 2. Research Background
Target-guarding games formalize defense of a protected region against an intruding agent, with direct applications to counter-UAS autonomous defense systems engaging slow, low-flying aircraft (cited [2], p. 6357). Since Isaacs' seminal geometric heuristic ([1, Example 1.9.2]) for a simple two-player guarding game, a long line of works ([8]–[14]) has constructed a *candidate* value function from the geometry of dominance regions and then "verified" it against the Hamilton–Jacobi–Isaacs (HJI) equation. All such verifications prior to this line of work required closed-form gradients of the candidate value, which forced restrictive specifications: few defenders, planar game space, zero capture radii, and circular or half-planar targets ([authors' claim], p. 6357). Moreover, they used only the classical ($C^1$) solution concept of the HJI equation, thereby sidestepping the nondifferentiability of the value that generically appears in multi-agent games with singular regions. This paper removes these restrictions by recasting the verification problem as sensitivity analysis of a parametric convex program.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
**Problem (Problem 1, p. 6358)**: An attacker $A$ and $m$ defenders $D_1,\dots,D_m$ with simple-motion kinematics $\dot x_A = u_A$ (1a), $\dot x_{D_i} = \nu_i u_{D_i}$ (1b), $u_r \in \mathcal U \triangleq \{u \in \mathbb R^n : \|u\| \le 1\}$, speeds $\nu_i > 1$ (A is the slowest agent), positions $x_r \in \mathbb R^n$, global state $\theta = [x_A^\top, x_{D_1}^\top,\dots,x_{D_m}^\top]^\top \in \mathbb R^l$, $l \triangleq n(m+1)$; perfect state-feedback information structure, admissible strategies $\Gamma$ (piecewise-continuous feedback maps $\mathbb R^l \to \mathcal U$). Target $\mathcal T$ is a nonempty closed convex subset of $\mathbb R^n$. Breach/capture termination sets: $C_b \triangleq \{\theta \in \mathbb R^l : x_A \in \mathcal T\}$ (2a), $C_c \triangleq \bigcup_{i=1}^m \{\theta \in \mathbb R^l \setminus C_b : \|x_A - x_{D_i}\| \le \rho_i\}$ (2b) with capture radii $\rho_i \in \mathbb R_{\ge 0}$. Performance index $J(\gamma_A,\gamma_D;\theta_0) \triangleq G(\theta(t_f))$ (3) with $G(\theta) \triangleq d_{\mathcal T}(x_A)$ (squared distance to $\mathcal T$), $t_f \triangleq \inf\{t \ge 0 : \theta(t) \in C \triangleq C_b \cup C_c\}$: $A$ minimizes, $D$ jointly maximizes; $A$ wins iff $\theta(t_f) \in C_b$, $D$ wins iff $\theta(t_f) \in C_c$. On $\Theta \triangleq \mathbb R^l \setminus C$, find (1) the winning regions $\mathcal W_A = \{\theta : \exists \gamma_A\ \forall \gamma_D, J = 0\}$ and $\mathcal W_D = \{\theta : \exists \gamma_D\ \forall \gamma_A, J > 0\}$, and (2) a saddle-point strategy pair satisfying $J(\gamma_A^\star,\gamma_D;\theta) \le J(\gamma_A^\star,\gamma_D^\star;\theta) \le J(\gamma_A,\gamma_D^\star;\theta)$ (4) on $\mathcal W_D$. The article "focuses mainly on the winning scenario of $D$" since the game is ill-defined where $A$ has a guaranteed winning strategy (p. 6358).

**Gap — [authors' claim]** (p. 6357): (i) prior geometric-approach papers [8],[9],[10],[11],[12],[13],[14] must impose simplifying assumptions (few defenders, planar space, zero capture radii, circular/half-planar targets) to obtain a closed-form candidate value and its gradient; (ii) they verify only in the classical ($C^1$) sense, so value nondifferentiability in multi-agent games "has been avoided and remain[s] unresolved"; (iii) the closest work [14] (Fu & Liu, Automatica 2023) is tailored to planar targets with smooth boundary, lacks rigorous proofs of uniqueness of the optimal capture point and of the viscosity-solution property, and does not provide the gradient/subdifferential of the value or the construction/partition of defender-winning regions. **[Verified from cited text]**: the listed simplifications indeed match the settings of [8] (two defenders, planar), [9] (MPME pairwise), [10] (border defense), [11]–[13] (special target geometries/3D matching), and the items the paper says were absent from [14] are exactly what its Proposition 2, Theorem 1, and Section IV-D deliver — the self-assessment is internally consistent and the contribution claims are traceable to stated theorems. The remaining standing assumption that the "no restrictive assumptions" claim does *not* cover is the speed superiority $\nu_i > 1$ of every defender (eq. (1b), p. 6358), which is load-bearing everywhere (it makes $c_{i,1} = 1 - 1/\nu_i^2 > 0$ in Proposition 1).

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Theory (verification)**: A complete verification of Isaacs' geometric method for the MDSA game: the candidate value $V$ (9) is proved to be the **viscosity solution** of the HJI equation (5) (Theorem 2), and its classical solution property holds exactly where LICQ holds (Theorems 1–2). This subsumes all the special-case verifications [8]–[14] and is the first to handle value nondifferentiability via Clarke subdifferentials.
2. **Theory (sensitivity apparatus)**: Association of $V$ with the optimal value function of a parametric convex program (P) and derivation of its continuity (Theorem 1(1)), local Lipschitzness and Clarke subdifferential (Theorem 1(2), eq. (27)), and $C^1$ differentiability characterized exactly by LICQ (Theorem 1(3), eq. (28)) — using duality, Slater's condition, KKT, and a generalized Danskin lemma, deliberately avoiding second-order/implicit-function-theorem machinery (Remark 3, since $d_{\mathcal T}$ is not $C^2$).
3. **Modeling (allocation)**: Unique optimal capture point (Proposition 2); feedback equilibrium strategies (33a)–(33b) in which each defender participates iff its constraint is strongly active for some optimal multiplier; and a state-space partition $\{\mathcal W_{D_\mathcal I} : \mathcal I \subseteq [m]\}$ (34) into defender-coalition winning regions — an explicit state-dependent defender allocation rule.
4. **Algorithm/Experiment**: Computation of the equilibrium via a convex program — reformulated as a distance problem between two convex sets (35) and, in the example, a convex QCQP (36) — demonstrated on a 12-dimensional ($n=3$, $m=3$) game with a 20-facet polyhedral target where direct HJI solution is intractable (Section V).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results

### 5.0 Model primitives (exact equations)
- **Dynamics (1a)–(1b), p. 6358**: $\dot x_A = u_A$, $x_A(0) = x_{A,0}$; $\dot x_{D_i} = \nu_i u_{D_i}$, $x_{D_i}(0) = x_{D_i,0}$, $i \in [m]$; $u_r \in \mathcal U = \{u \in \mathbb R^n: \|u\|\le 1\}$; $\nu_i > 1$ for all $i$ (defenders strictly faster; $A$ slowest). Simple motion, no inertia.
- **Information structure**: perfect state feedback; $\theta \in \mathbb R^l$, $l = n(m+1)$; strategies $\gamma_r \in \Gamma$ (piecewise-continuous feedback $\mathbb R^l \to \mathcal U$). No explicit nonanticipativity delay structure is imposed.
- **Cost and termination (2)–(3), p. 6358**: $J = G(\theta(t_f)) = d_{\mathcal T}(x_A(t_f))$, where $d_{\mathcal T}(x) \triangleq \min_{y \in \mathcal T}\|x-y\|^2$ (squared distance; $\nabla d_{\mathcal T}(x) = 2(x - \Pi_{\mathcal T}(x))$). $t_f$ = first entry into $C_b \cup C_c$. Payoff is zero exactly on breach and equals the squared capture distance on capture — so $D$ maximizing $J$ = capturing as far from $\mathcal T$ as possible.
- **HJI equation (5a)–(5b), p. 6358**: $F(\nabla U) = 0$ on $\Theta$; $U(\theta) = G(\theta)$ on $\operatorname{bd}(C_c)$; Hamiltonian $F(\xi) = -\|\xi_1\| + \sum_{i=1}^m \nu_i\|\xi_{i+1}\|$ (6). **Appendix derivation (p. 6363)**: upper/lower Hamiltonians $F^\pm$ from $H(\xi,u_A,u_D) = \xi_1^\top u_A + \sum_i \nu_i \xi_{i+1}^\top u_{D_i}$; $H$ separable in $u_A$ and each $u_{D_i}$, so Isaacs' condition holds, $F = F^+ = F^-$ (37), with minimizers $u_A^\star = -\xi_1/\|\xi_1\|$ (38a) and $u_{D_i}^\star = \xi_{i+1}/\|\xi_{i+1}\|$ (38b) (undefined where the corresponding $\xi$ vanishes). Viscosity solution per Definition 1 [Crandall–Evans–Lions]: sub solution $F(\xi) \le 0\ \forall \xi \in \partial^+ U(\theta)$ (7), super solution $F(\xi) \ge 0\ \forall \xi \in \partial^- U(\theta)$ (8), with Fréchet super/subdifferentials.

### 5.1 Geometric candidate and dominance region (p. 6359, eqs. (9)–(11))
- **Candidate value (9)**: $V(\theta) \triangleq \inf\{d_{\mathcal T}(x) : x \in \mathcal F(\theta)\}$, where the **dominant region of the attacker** (10) is $\mathcal F(\theta) \triangleq \{x \in \mathbb R^n : f_i(x,\theta) \le 0,\ i \in [m]\}$ and
  $f_i(x,\theta) \triangleq \|x - x_A\|^2 - \big(\frac{\|x - x_{D_i}\| - \rho_i}{\nu_i}\big)^2$ (11).
  Each $f_i$ compares the squared time for $A$ to reach $x$ with the squared time for $D_i$ to cover $x$ within its capture radius; $f_i(x,\theta)\le 0$ is exactly the generalized Apollonius/dominance inequality (with capture radius $\rho_i$ and speed ratio $1/\nu_i$). $\mathcal F(\theta)$ = points $A$ reaches no later than being covered; $V(\theta)$ = the closest proximity to $\mathcal T$ that $A$ can guarantee (squared distance between $\mathcal F(\theta)$ and $\mathcal T$). Fig. 2 (p. 6359) illustrates: intersection of sublevel sets around $x_A$, optimal capture point $x^\star(\theta)$, distance to the polygonal target.
- **Parametric program (P), p. 6359**: $\min_{x \in \mathbb R^n} d_{\mathcal T}(x)\ \text{s.t. } x \in \mathcal F(\theta)$; $V$ is its global optimal value function; solution set $\mathcal X(\theta) \triangleq \{\bar x \in \mathcal F(\theta) : d_{\mathcal T}(\bar x) = V(\theta)\}$ (12) (elements = *optimal capture points*). Partition $\Theta^{=0} \triangleq \{\theta : V(\theta) = 0\}$ (13a) $\Leftrightarrow \mathcal F(\theta) \cap \mathcal T \ne \emptyset$; $\Theta^{>0} \triangleq \{\theta : V(\theta) > 0\}$ (13b) $\Leftrightarrow \mathcal F(\theta) \subset \mathbb R^n \setminus \mathcal T$; later $\Theta^{=0} = \mathcal W_A$, $\Theta^{>0} = \mathcal W_D$.

### 5.2 Proposition 1 (p. 6359, eq. (14)) — strict convexity and coercivity
**Statement**: For any fixed $\theta \in \Theta$, the functions $f_i$ are strictly convex and coercive in $x$.
**Proof approach**: (1) Expand (11) into $f_i(x,\theta) = c_{i,1}\|x\|^2 + c_{i,2}\|x - x_{D_i}\| + w_i^\top x + c_{i,3}$ (14) with $c_{i,1} \triangleq 1 - 1/\nu_i^2$, $c_{i,2} \triangleq 2\rho_i/\nu_i^2$, $c_{i,3} \triangleq \|x_A\|^2 - (\|x_{D_i}\|^2 + \rho_i^2)/\nu_i^2$, $w_i \triangleq 2(x_{D_i}/\nu_i^2 - x_A)$. (2) $c_{i,1} > 0$ (here $\nu_i > 1$ is used), $c_{i,2} \ge 0$ ⟹ strict convexity (strictly convex quadratic + convex norm term + linear). (3) Decisive inequality: triangle + Cauchy–Schwarz give $f_i(x,\theta) \ge c_{i,1}\|x\|^2 + (c_{i,2} - \|w_i\|)\|x\| - c_{i,2}\|x_{D_i}\| + c_{i,3} \to \infty$ as $\|x\| \to \infty$ — coercivity. Global, analytic.
**Consequences (Remarks 1–3, p. 6359)**: (R1) (P) is a convex program for fixed $\theta$, but $V$ is *not* convex since $f_i$ is not jointly convex in $(x,\theta)$; (R2) $\mathcal F(\theta)$ is bounded (intersection of zero-sublevel sets of coercive functions); (R3) $d_{\mathcal T}$ is generally not twice differentiable (the projection $\Pi_{\mathcal T}$ jumps), so the classical parametric-NLP sensitivity toolbox relying on second-order conditions or the implicit function theorem [16] is unavailable — motivating the nonsmooth route.

### 5.3 Duality and KKT (p. 6360, eqs. (15)–(18))
Lagrangian $L(x,\lambda,\theta) \triangleq d_{\mathcal T}(x) + \sum_{i=1}^m \lambda_i f_i(x,\theta)$ (15); dual function $q(\lambda,\theta) \triangleq \inf_x L(x,\lambda,\theta)$ (16) (inf = min since $d_{\mathcal T}$ and $f_i(\cdot,\theta)$ attain global minima); dual problem (D) $\max_{\lambda \succeq 0} q(\lambda,\theta)$, solution set $\Lambda(\theta)$. Key structural facts (p. 6360): for $\theta \in \Theta$ and all $i$, $f_i(x_{D_i},\theta) > 0$ and $f_i(x_A,\theta) < 0$; the latter makes $x_A$ a strict interior point of $\mathcal F(\theta)$, so **Slater's condition** [20, Prop. 4.3.9] holds, and with convexity this yields **strong duality over $\Theta$**, existence of KKT multipliers $\bar\lambda \in \Lambda(\theta)$ with $\nabla_x L(\bar x,\bar\lambda,\theta) = 0$ (17a), $\sum_i \bar\lambda_i f_i(\bar x,\theta) = 0$ (17b), $\bar\lambda \succeq 0$ (17c), and the saddle property $L(\bar x,\lambda,\theta) \le L(\bar x,\bar\lambda,\theta) \le L(x,\bar\lambda,\theta)$ (18) for all $x, \lambda \succeq 0$ [20, Prop. 6.1.6].

### 5.4 Proposition 2 (p. 6360, eq. (19)) — uniqueness of the optimal capture point
**Statement**: For each fixed $\theta \in \Theta^{>0}$, the solution set $\mathcal X(\theta)$ is a singleton.
**Assumptions**: $\theta \in \Theta^{>0}$ (so $V(\theta) > 0$ and the unique solution $\bar x \notin \mathcal T$); Proposition 1; KKT (17).
**Proof approach**: (1) *Existence*: Weierstrass' theorem [20, Prop. A.8] — $d_{\mathcal T}$ continuous, $\mathcal F(\theta)$ nonempty and compact (Remark 2). (2) From stationarity (17a) and $\nabla d_{\mathcal T}(\bar x) = 2(\bar x - \Pi_{\mathcal T}(\bar x))$: $\sum_{i=1}^m \bar\lambda_i \nabla_x f_i(\bar x,\theta) = 2(\Pi_{\mathcal T}(\bar x) - \bar x) \ne 0$ since $\bar x \notin \mathcal T$ — decisive equation. (3) Hence some $\bar\lambda_i \ne 0$: the optimum lies on the **boundary** of $\mathcal F(\theta)$, and by Proposition 1 the Lagrangian $L$ is strictly convex in $x$ for any fixed $(\bar\lambda,\theta)$ with $\bar\lambda \ne 0$. (4) *Uniqueness by contradiction*: suppose two saddle points $(\bar x,\bar\lambda)$, $(\tilde x,\tilde\lambda)$ with $\bar x \ne \tilde x$ and equal values $d_{\mathcal T}(\bar x) = L(\bar x,\bar\lambda,\theta) \le L(\tilde x,\tilde\lambda,\theta) = d_{\mathcal T}(\tilde x)$ (19). (5) For the convex combination $\hat x = \mu\bar x + (1-\mu)\tilde x$, $\mu \in (0,1)$: convexity of $d_{\mathcal T}$ makes $\hat x$ a global minimizer too, so with some $\hat\lambda$,
$d_{\mathcal T}(\hat x) = L(\hat x,\hat\lambda,\theta) \overset{(*)}{<} \mu L(\bar x,\hat\lambda,\theta) + (1-\mu)L(\tilde x,\hat\lambda,\theta) \overset{(18)}{\le} \mu L(\bar x,\bar\lambda,\theta) + (1-\mu)L(\tilde x,\tilde\lambda,\theta) \overset{(19)}{\le} L(\tilde x,\tilde\lambda,\theta) = d_{\mathcal T}(\tilde x)$,
where $(*)$ is strict convexity of $L$ — contradicting $d_{\mathcal T}(\hat x) = d_{\mathcal T}(\tilde x) = V(\theta)$. Analytic, global (over $\Theta^{>0}$). **Physical meaning**: when the attacker's dominant region is disjoint from the target, there is exactly one optimal capture point — the geometric solution's "aim point" is well defined, which is what both equilibrium strategies (33) and the numerical scheme consume. Note $\mathcal X(\theta)$ may be multivalued on $\Theta^{=0}$ (no claim there).

### 5.5 Lemma 1 (p. 6361, eqs. (24)–(26)) — generalized Danskin theorem [22]
**Statement**: Let $\phi(z,s)$ be continuous on $Z \times S$ and $\varphi(z) \triangleq \max_{s \in S}\phi(z,s)$ (24). If $S(z) \triangleq \{\bar s : \phi(z,\bar s) = \varphi(z)\} \ne \emptyset$ for all $z$ and $\nabla_z\phi$ exists and is continuous in $(z,s)$, then $\varphi$ is locally Lipschitz with Clarke subdifferential $\partial\varphi(z) = \operatorname{conv}\{\nabla_z\phi(z,\bar s) : \bar s \in S(z)\}$ (25). If moreover $S(z)$ is a singleton $s^\star(z)$ on a neighborhood of $\tilde z$, then $s^\star$ is continuous there and $\varphi$ is $C^1$ with $\nabla\varphi(z) = \nabla_z\phi(z,s^\star(z))$ (26).
**Status**: cited/quoted (Remark 5: a slight modification of [22, Th. 2.1], Clarke 1975; original Danskin [23, Theorem II] conditions are stronger). No proof given in the paper. This lemma is the engine replacing the unavailable $C^2$/IFT sensitivity analysis.

### 5.6 Theorem 1 (p. 6361, eqs. (27)–(28)) — continuity, Lipschitzness, subdifferential of $V$
**Statement**: For $V$ defined in (9): (1) $V$ is **continuous in $\Theta$**; (2) $V$ is **locally Lipschitz in $\Theta^{>0}$** with Clarke subdifferential
$\partial V(\theta) = \operatorname{conv}\{\nabla_\theta L(x^\star(\theta),\bar\lambda,\theta) : \bar\lambda \in \Lambda(\theta)\}$ (27);
(3) $V$ is **$C^1$ at $\theta \in \Theta^{>0}$** with $\nabla V(\theta) = \nabla_\theta L(x^\star(\theta),\lambda^\star(\theta),\theta)$ (28), where $\Lambda(\theta) = \{\lambda^\star(\theta)\}$, **if and only if LICQ holds at $\theta$** (gradients $\nabla_x f_i(x^\star(\theta),\theta)$, $i \in \mathcal A(\theta)$, linearly independent; active set $\mathcal A(\theta) \triangleq \{i : f_i(x^\star(\theta),\theta) = 0\}$ (21); Remark 4, via [21, Th. 2] Wachsmuth: $\Lambda(\theta)$ singleton $\Leftrightarrow$ LICQ).
**Assumptions**: Slater + convexity + $\mathcal F(\theta) \ne \emptyset$ for (1); $\theta \in \Theta^{>0}$ for (2)–(3).
**Proof approach**: (i) By [24, Lemma 5.7] (Still, parametric optimization), the set-valued map $\mathcal F: \Theta \rightrightarrows \mathbb R^n$ is *uniformly compact* near each $\theta$ (neighborhood $B(\theta,\epsilon)$ with $\bigcup_{\tilde\theta \in B(\theta,\epsilon)}\mathcal F(\tilde\theta)$ compact), thanks to Slater/convexity/nonemptiness; then continuity of $V$ follows from [25, Th. 3.3] (Gauvin–Dubeau), a variation of **Berge's maximum theorem** [26]. (ii) Define $\mathcal Y(\lambda,\theta) \triangleq \arg\min_x L(x,\lambda,\theta)$: nonempty for all $(\lambda,\theta)$ (coercivity), and claimed to reduce to a singleton for $\theta \in \Theta^{>0}$ by strict convexity of $L$ in $x$ (Proposition 2); $\nabla_{(\lambda,\theta)}L$ is continuous on $\mathbb R^n \times \mathbb R^m_{\ge0} \times \Theta^{>0}$. Apply Lemma 1's *second* assertion with $z = [\lambda^\top,\theta^\top]^\top$, $s = x$, $\varphi = -q$, $\phi = -L$ to get $\nabla_{(\lambda,\theta)}q(\lambda,\theta) = \nabla_{(\lambda,\theta)}L(y^\star(\lambda,\theta),\lambda,\theta)$, where $x^\star(\theta) = y^\star(\lambda,\theta)$ whenever $\lambda \in \Lambda(\theta)$; then apply the *first* assertion with $z = \theta$, $s = \lambda$, $\varphi = V$, $\phi = q$ to obtain (27) — the decisive identifications being $V = \max_{\lambda \succeq 0} q(\lambda,\theta)$ (20) (strong duality) and the multiplier-uniqueness/LICQ equivalence. (iii) Assertion (3) follows from (27), Remark 4, and [22, Prop. 1.13] ($\partial V(\theta) = \{\xi\}$ singleton $\Leftrightarrow$ $V$ is $C^1$ with gradient $\xi$). Local statements on $\Theta^{>0}$, global continuity on $\Theta$, analytic. **Corollary (Remark 6)**: by Rademacher's theorem [27], $V$ (being locally Lipschitz) is differentiable a.e., so **LICQ holds almost everywhere in $\Theta^{>0}$** — the nondifferentiable set is measure-zero.
**Proof gap (minor, repairable), Theorem 1(ii), p. 6361**: the text claims $\mathcal Y(\lambda,\theta)$ "reduces to a singleton if we further assume $\theta \in \Theta^{>0}$" and asserts $\nabla_{(\lambda,\theta)}q$ exists "over $\mathbb R^m_{\ge0} \times \Theta^{>0}$". At $\lambda = 0$, $L(x,0,\theta) = d_{\mathcal T}(x)$ is not strictly convex (Proposition 2's strict convexity argument needs some $\bar\lambda_i \ne 0$) and $\mathcal Y(0,\theta) = \mathcal T$ is not a singleton, so Lemma 1's second assertion does not apply on that ray (and $q$ is generally not Gâteaux-differentiable in $\lambda$ at $\lambda = 0$). The gap is benign: $\Lambda(\theta)$ never contains $0$ when $V(\theta) > 0$ (since $q(0,\theta) = 0 < V(\theta)$), and $\nabla_\theta q(0,\theta) = 0$ with $\nabla_\theta q(\lambda,\theta) = \sum_i \lambda_i \nabla_\theta f_i(y^\star,\theta) \to 0$ as $\lambda \to 0$ (minimizers stay bounded near $\mathcal T$ by coercivity), so the first-assertion hypotheses used for (27) survive; but the printed argument does not perform this case split.

### 5.7 Lemma 2 (p. 6361) — Fréchet differentials inside the Clarke subdifferential [28]
**Statement** (Frankowska [28, Th. 1.4]): if $\phi$ is locally Lipschitz at $x$, then $\partial^+\phi(x) \cup \partial^-\phi(x) \subseteq \partial\phi(x)$ (Fréchet super/subdifferentials are contained in the Clarke subdifferential). Cited, not proved. This is what lets one verify *both* viscosity inequalities (7)–(8) from a single statement about all elements of $\partial V(\theta)$.

### 5.8 Theorem 2 (pp. 6361–6362) — main verification: $V$ is the viscosity solution of (5)
**Statement**: $V$ defined in (9) is the viscosity solution of the HJI equation (5); moreover, $V$ satisfies (5) **in the classical sense** on the subset of $\Theta^{>0}$ where LICQ holds at the unique solution of (P).
**Assumptions**: the standing model assumptions ($\nu_i > 1$, $\mathcal T$ nonempty closed convex, $\theta \in \Theta$); Theorem 1; Lemmas 1–2; KKT (17).
**Proof approach (7 steps)**: (1) Continuity of $V$ on $\Theta$ from Theorem 1(1). (2) On the interior of $\Theta^{=0}$, $V \equiv 0$ so $\nabla V = 0$ and $F(0) = 0$: (5a) is trivial there. (3) Fix $\theta \in \Theta^{>0}$; $V$ is locally Lipschitz (Theorem 1(2)); write $\Lambda(\theta) = \{\bar\lambda_1,\dots,\bar\lambda_r\}$; by Rockafellar [29, Th. 2.3] every $\xi \in \partial V(\theta)$ is a convex combination $\xi = \sum_{j=1}^r \mu_j \nabla_\theta L(\bar x,\bar\lambda_j,\theta)$, $\mu_j \ge 0$, $\sum_j \mu_j = 1$ (29). (4) Substitute (29) into the Hamiltonian (6): $F(\xi) = -\|\sum_j \mu_j \nabla_{x_A} L\| + \sum_i \nu_i \|\sum_j \mu_j \nabla_{x_{D_i}} L\|$, using that $d_{\mathcal T}$ does not depend on $(x_A, x_{D_i})$ so $\nabla_{x_A} L = \sum_i \bar\lambda_j^i \nabla_{x_A} f_i$ and $\nabla_{x_{D_i}} L = \bar\lambda_j^i \nabla_{x_{D_i}} f_i$. (5) **Decisive computation**: $\nabla_{x_A} f_i = 2(x_A - \bar x)$ is the *same* vector for all $i$ and $\nabla_{x_{D_i}} f_i = \frac{2(\|\bar x - x_{D_i}\| - \rho_i)}{\nu_i^2}\cdot\frac{\bar x - x_{D_i}}{\|\bar x - x_{D_i}\|}$ is a scalar multiple of a unit vector, so norms factor and
$F(\xi) = 2\sum_{j=1}^r \mu_j\Big(\sum_{i=1}^m \bar\lambda_j^i \|\bar x - x_A\| - \sum_{i=1}^m |\bar\lambda_j^i|\,\big|\tfrac{\|\bar x - x_{D_i}\| - \rho_i}{\nu_i}\big|\cdot\big\|\tfrac{\bar x - x_{D_i}}{\|\bar x - x_{D_i}\|}\big\|\Big) \overset{(17\mathrm b),(17\mathrm c)}{=} 0$:
for strongly active constraints ($\bar\lambda_j^i > 0$) the constraint identity $f_i(\bar x,\theta) = 0$ gives $\|\bar x - x_A\| = |(\|\bar x - x_{D_i}\| - \rho_i)/\nu_i|$, and for inactive ones (17b)–(17c) give $\bar\lambda_j^i = 0$; the two sums cancel term by term. (6) By Lemma 2, $F(\xi) = 0$ for all $\xi \in \partial^- V(\theta) \cup \partial^+ V(\theta)$ — verifying the sub-solution inequality (7) and super-solution inequality (8) *simultaneously* (with equality, stronger than required); the classical-sense claim at LICQ points repeats the same computation with (28) and the unique $\lambda^\star(\theta)$. (7) **Boundary condition (5b)**: for $\theta \in \operatorname{bd}(C_c)$ there is $i$ with $\|x_A - x_{D_i}\| = \rho_i$, whence $f_i(x_A,\theta) = -\big(\frac{\|x_A - x_{D_i}\| - \rho_i}{\nu_i}\big)^2 = 0$ (30); and for every $\epsilon > 0$ and unit $v$, the triangle inequality gives $f_i(x_A + \epsilon v,\theta) \ge (1 - 1/\nu_i^2)\epsilon^2 > 0$ (31) — so $\mathcal F(\theta) = \{x_A\}$ (a singleton) and $V(\theta) = \min_{x \in \mathcal F(\theta)} d_{\mathcal T}(x) = d_{\mathcal T}(x_A) = G(\theta)$. $\square$
**Nature and meaning**: analytic, global on $\Theta$; physical content — (a) on $A$'s winning region the value is identically zero; (b) inside $D$'s winning region the HJI equation holds in the classical sense generically (LICQ a.e. by Remark 6) and in the viscosity sense everywhere, including the measure-zero nonsmooth set; (c) on the capture boundary the attacker's dominant region collapses onto the attacker itself, matching the terminal payoff. Extensibility: the verification pattern (parametric program → Danskin/Clarke → viscosity) is geometry-agnostic and transfers to any guardable convex target in any dimension; the paper explicitly positions it as superseding [8]–[14].

### 5.9 Winning regions, equilibrium strategies, and defender allocation (p. 6362, eqs. (32)–(34))
- **Winning regions (32a)–(32b)**: $\mathcal W_A = \{\theta \in \Theta : V(\theta) \le 0\} = \Theta^{=0}$ and $\mathcal W_D = \{\theta \in \Theta : V(\theta) > 0\} = \Theta^{>0}$; $\mathcal W_A$ being the zero-sublevel set of the viscosity solution is justified by the arguments of [4, Th. 2] (Mitchell–Bayen–Tomlin). No separate barrier-surface analysis is given for the interface $\operatorname{bd}(\Theta^{=0})$.
- **Equilibrium strategies (33a)–(33b)**, obtained by "substituting either (28) or (29) in (38)" (i.e., inserting the value gradient/subgradient into the Hamiltonian-minimizing controls of the Appendix):
$\gamma_A^\star(\theta) = \dfrac{x^\star(\theta) - x_A}{\|x^\star(\theta) - x_A\|}$ (33a),
$\gamma_{D_i}^\star(\theta) = \begin{cases}\dfrac{x^\star(\theta) - x_{D_i}}{\|x^\star(\theta) - x_{D_i}\|}, & \text{if } i \in \bigcup_{\bar\lambda \in \Lambda(\theta)} \mathcal A^+(\bar\lambda,\theta)\\ \text{undefined}, & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}$ (33b),
where $\mathcal A^+(\lambda,\theta) \triangleq \{i \in \mathcal A(\theta) : \lambda_i > 0\}$ is the set of **strongly active constraints**. Physical reading: $A$ runs along its line of sight to the (unique) optimal capture point; defender $D_i$ chases the same point iff its dominance constraint is strongly active for *some* optimal multiplier — otherwise its control "cannot contribute to the task of capturing $A$" and is left undefined (in the simulation such a defender stays put, p. 6363).
- **Defender-coalition partition (34)**: $\mathcal W_{D_\mathcal I} \triangleq \{\theta \in \mathcal W_D : \mathcal I = \bigcup_{\bar\lambda \in \Lambda(\theta)} \mathcal A^+(\bar\lambda,\theta)\}$, $\mathcal I \subseteq [m]$ — the defender-winning region decomposes into regions labeled by the *active coalition*: this is the paper's state-dependent allocation logic (which defenders actually pursue, as a function of the state). Fig. 3(b) (p. 6362) renders this partition for $m=3$ fixed defender positions on the $x_A$-plane: seven visible coalitions $\mathcal W_{D_{\{1\}}}, \mathcal W_{D_{\{2\}}}, \mathcal W_{D_{\{3\}}}, \mathcal W_{D_{\{1,2\}}}, \mathcal W_{D_{\{1,3\}}}, \mathcal W_{D_{\{2,3\}}}, \mathcal W_{D_{\{1,2,3\}}}$ around $\mathcal W_A$ (red zero-sublevel boundary), with $\mathcal W_{D_{\{1,3\}}}$ a narrow strip between the single-defender regions.
- **Practical reformulation, Remark 7 (35), p. 6362**: when $d_{\mathcal T}$ lacks a closed form, (P) becomes the distance-between-two-convex-sets problem $\min_{x,y\in\mathbb R^n} \|x-y\|^2$ s.t. $x \in \mathcal F(\theta)$, $y \in \mathcal T$ (35) — still a convex program, castable in CVXPY [30] (cf. [31, Sec. 8.2]).

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
**Author-stated**: (1) the analysis "focuses mainly on the winning scenario of $D$"; the game is ill-defined where $A$ has a winning strategy, and no equilibrium strategies are constructed on $\mathcal W_A$ (p. 6358, §II-C; §IV-D). (2) Future work: multiple attackers and/or defenders with sensing limitations (p. 6363).
**Critical observations** (with traceable locations):
1. **Speed superiority is a hidden standing assumption**: $\nu_i > 1$ for *every* defender (1b, p. 6358) enters Proposition 1 through $c_{i,1} = 1 - 1/\nu_i^2 > 0$ (14); if any defender is slower than or as fast as the attacker, strict convexity/coercivity of $f_i$ and hence the uniqueness and sensitivity machinery collapse. The headline claim of "no restrictive assumptions" (p. 6357) is accurate only with respect to the listed specifications (defender count, dimension, capture radii, target shape) [verified: the speed condition is used in the proof of Prop. 1 and in boundary inequality (31)].
2. **Minor proof gap in Theorem 1(ii)**: the claim that $\mathcal Y(\lambda,\theta)$ is a singleton and $\nabla_{(\lambda,\theta)}q$ exists "over $\mathbb R^m_{\ge0} \times \Theta^{>0}$" fails at $\lambda = 0$, where $L = d_{\mathcal T}$ is not strictly convex and $\mathcal Y(0,\theta) = \mathcal T$ (p. 6361); repairable as noted in Section 5.6 since $\Lambda(\theta) \not\ni 0$ for $V(\theta) > 0$, but not addressed in print.
3. **Strategy-level verification is implicit**: the saddle-point property of the feedback pair (33) is argued by substituting the value gradient/subgradient into the pointwise Hamiltonian minimizers (38) (p. 6362); a formal verification theorem showing that the closed loop under (33) realizes $V$ along trajectories — especially across the measure-zero set where LICQ fails and $V$ is nondifferentiable — is not stated; the only closed-loop evidence is numerical (Fig. 5).
4. **Undefined defender controls**: for defenders whose constraints are not strongly active for any optimal multiplier, $\gamma_{D_i}^\star$ is undefined (33b) and is set to "stay put" in the example (p. 6363); no secondary-task analysis, no tie-breaking among multiple optimal multipliers, and no robustness study near coalition-boundary states $\mathcal W_{D_\mathcal I} \cap \mathcal W_{D_{\mathcal I'}}$.
5. **No barrier/interface analysis**: $\mathcal W_A$ is taken as the zero-sublevel set via [4, Th. 2] (p. 6362); the dynamics on $\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal W_A)$ (the classical barrier surface) and uniqueness questions on $\Theta^{=0}$ (where $\mathcal X(\theta)$ can be multivalued and strategies (33) may break down since $x^\star(\theta)$ is not unique) are not treated.
6. **Discrete-time implementation gap**: the continuous-time feedback equilibrium is applied at 0.01 s sampling (p. 6363) with no discretization-error or sampling-stability analysis.
7. **Reproducibility of the example**: target data $A, b$ and the solver are UNREPORTED (p. 6363); a single scenario with one initial condition is tested; no Monte-Carlo coverage of the coalition regions of Fig. 3(b).
8. **Payoff convention**: $d_{\mathcal T}$ is the *squared* distance (Notation, p. 6358), so "closest proximity" claims and Fig. 5 values ($10^{-2}$ scale) are in squared-length units — a reader comparing to distance-based capture criteria should convert.

## 8. Citation Value
This paper is the canonical T1 reference for *rigorous verification of Isaacs' geometric method in multi-defender target guarding with convex targets*: it supersedes the special-case analyses of [8]–[13] and hardens the semi-formal treatment of Fu & Liu (P: Automatica 2023) with the missing uniqueness, gradient/subdifferential, and viscosity-solution proofs, and it is the natural "general MDSA result" to cite when motivating geometric-plus-HJI hybrid methodologies, parametric-optimization sensitivity analysis in differential games, or state-based defender task allocation (coalition partition (34)). In a future IEEE Trans Introduction it fits in the paragraph on target-guarding/target-defense games after the Isaacs-line special cases (e.g., Yan et al. two-defender analysis, Garcia et al. MPME, Von Moll et al. border defense) and before learning-based or sensing-limited extensions, to establish the state of the art in the model-based branch. Ready-to-adapt citation sentences: "Lee and Bakolas [x] proved via parametric convex optimization that the value candidate constructed from Isaacs' geometric method for multiple-defender single-attacker convex target-guarding games is the viscosity solution of the associated HJI equation, with classical smoothness holding exactly where LICQ holds." and "In [x], equilibrium strategies reduce to heading alignments toward a unique optimal capture point obtained from a convex program, which remains tractable in state dimensions (e.g., $l = 12$) far beyond the reach of direct HJI computation."
