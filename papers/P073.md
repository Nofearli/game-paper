## 2. Research Background

Reach-avoid differential games formalize scenarios where one team drives the state into its own target set while keeping the opponent out of its target set; the qualitative solution ("which team wins") is a prerequisite for quantitative payoff optimization. The game-of-kind machinery goes back to the two-target qualitative game of Getz & Leitmann [8] and Isaacs' barrier method [9], while the modern reachability formulation defines the barrier as the zero sublevel set of a minimax value function obtained by solving an HJI partial differential equation [10]–[15] — accurate but cursed by dimensionality (here the joint state space is $\mathbb R^{12}$). A complementary line uses geometry (Voronoi partitions [16], [17], Apollonius circles [18], line-of-sight arguments [19], "paths of defense" [20]) to obtain closed-form qualitative and quantitative solutions for specific game setups, and recent work analyzes reach-avoid games in specific regions: Capture-the-Flag in a convex domain [21], a defense game in a circular region [22], and a confinement–escape problem [23]. This paper belongs to the geometric line and lifts the authors' own planar analysis (their ref [18] = library P099) to three-dimensional space: three cooperating pursuers must stop one evader from crossing a planar target interface into a goal subspace.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap

**Problem [authors' formulation, pp. 1–2]**: a three-pursuer ($P_1,P_2,P_3$) one-evader ($E$) reach-avoid game in $\mathbb R^3$. A plane $\mathcal T$ splits the space into a goal subspace $\Omega_{\mathrm{goal}}$ and a play subspace $\Omega_{\mathrm{play}}$ (eq. (1), p. 2). All four players are mass points with **equal unit speed** and simple motion (instantaneous direction change), eq. (2). $E$ starts in $\Omega_{\mathrm{play}}$ and wins by reaching $\Omega_{\mathrm{goal}}$ (necessarily crossing $\mathcal T$) before capture; capture means the distance from $E$ to the closest pursuer equals zero, and the pursuers win by capturing $E$ in $\Omega_{\mathrm{play}}$. **Problem 1 (Game of kind, p. 2)**: given $K, b$ and the initial configuration $K^T x_E^0 > b$, $x_{P_i}^0 \in \mathbb R^3$ ($i=1,2,3$), which team can guarantee its own winning — "successful capture or successful safe arrival ... when both team adopt their optimal strategies?" The pursuers may initially be anywhere in $\mathbb R^3$ (including $\Omega_{\mathrm{goal}}$), and the four players start from pairwise different positions.

**Gap [authors' claim, p. 1]**: the HJI/reachability route to the barrier ([10]–[15]) "bears the curse of dimensionality"; geometric tools exist (Voronoi, Apollonius, pairwise outcomes [20]) but a **complete closed-form 3D barrier for a cooperating pursuer team** was not available. [Verified from cited text] The HJI-dimensionality claim is standard and consistent with [10], [11], [13] (TAC reach-avoid formulations computed numerically); [20] (Chen, Zhou, Tomlin, TAC 2017) indeed solves multiplayer reach-avoid only *pairwise*, i.e., without the full cooperation structure; the authors' [18] (P099) is planar. The specific gap claimed — barrier for **three (and by extension $n$) cooperating equal-speed pursuers in 3D with a planar target** — is plausibly novel at writing time and is exactly what the paper delivers; no 3D multiplayer barrier construction is cited prior to this work. [Authors' claim, abstract] the analytic solution "require[s] a little memory without computational burden and allow[s] for real-time updates, beyond the capacity of traditional Hamilton-Jacobi-Isaacs method" — true by construction (closed-form sets), though never benchmarked (no experiment exists, see §6).

## 4. Core Contributions

As stated on p. 1 and verified in the body:

1. **Theory — contribution/contribution classification conditions**: necessary and sufficient conditions determining *which pursuers* contribute to the barrier — Lemma 2 (eq. (8), two pursuers, p. 3) and Lemma 3 (eqs. (11)–(13), three pursuers, p. 4), introducing the notions of **active pursuer** and of barriers dependent on one/two/three pursuers.
2. **Theory — complete closed-form 3D barrier and winning subspaces with all pursuer cooperations**: Lemma 1 + eq. (7) (one pursuer: point/ray geometry), Theorem 1 + eqs. (9)–(10) (two active pursuers: mirror points + circular arc), Theorem 2 + eq. (14) (three collinear-projection pursuers: union of two two-pursuer barriers), Theorem 3 + eqs. (15)–(16) (three noncollinear-projection pursuers: **spherical patch** over the projection triangle). By these, the initial-configuration space is partitioned into pursuer-winning subspace $\mathcal W_{\mathrm P}$, evader-winning subspace $\mathcal W_{\mathrm E}$, and barrier — the game winner is decided before play starts.
3. **Algorithm — extension to $n$ pursuers**: Algorithm 1 (p. 6) constructs the barrier for *any number* of pursuers and any initial configuration by filtering **active pursuers**, grouping them into **active triple-pursuer coalitions**, and taking unions of barriers and $\mathcal W_{\mathrm P}$ / intersection of $\mathcal W_{\mathrm E}$ over coalitions (Fig. 6: six pursuers → four coalitions → barrier made of parts of four spheres, "$\mathcal W_{\mathrm P}$ ... bounded by a hexagonal prism").
4. **Modeling reframing**: the game is equivalently "an evader escaping a subspace through its planar boundary while avoiding three moving obstacles" (p. 1), and everything is reduced to a *static* geometric construction at $t=0$ (evasion space, §III-B).
5. **Experiment**: **none.** The paper contains no simulation, no numerical example, and no experiment section (see §6).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results (exhaustive)

### 5.0 Model, information structure, termination (p. 2)

- **Game space (eq. (1))**: $\mathcal T=\{z\in\mathbb R^3 \mid K^Tz=b\}$ (target plane, TP), $\Omega_{\mathrm{goal}}=\{z\mid K^Tz<b\}$, $\Omega_{\mathrm{play}}=\{z\mid K^Tz>b\}$, with known $K\in\mathbb R^3$, $K\ne0$, $b\in\mathbb R$.
- **Dynamics (eq. (2))**: $\dot x_{P_i}(t)=p_i(t),\ x_{P_i}(0)=x_{P_i}^0$, $i=1,2,3$; $\dot x_E(t)=e(t),\ x_E(0)=x_E^0$; controls are instantaneous unit headings $p_i, e \in \mathcal U=\{u\in\mathbb R^3 \mid \|u\|_2=1\}$ ("Eculidean norm" [sic]). **Equal unit speed** for all four players; simple motion. Whole state space $\mathbb R^{12}$.
- **Termination / winning conditions**: capture $\iff$ distance from $E$ to the *closest* pursuer $=0$; $E$ wins iff it reaches $\Omega_{\mathrm{goal}}$ before capture; the pursuer team wins iff $E$ is captured in $\Omega_{\mathrm{play}}$ (p. 2). Since reaching $\Omega_{\mathrm{goal}}$ requires crossing $\mathcal T$, the entire analysis is a $t=0$ geometric construction.
- **Information structure**: non-anticipative, "as commonly adopted in the differential game literature (see for example, [10], [24])" — each team has complete up-to-date positions of all players and the control employed by the other team, but not future controls (p. 2). No formal Elliott–Kalton machinery is developed; the citation is definitional only.
- **Standing assumptions**: four players start from different positions; $E$ initially in $\Omega_{\mathrm{play}}$ ($K^T x_E^0 > b$); pursuers initially anywhere.

### 5.1 Efficient simplification (Sec. III-A, p. 2, eq. (3))

By a change of coordinates the plane description (1) is normalized to $\mathcal T=\{z\in\mathbb R^3\mid z=0\}$, $\Omega_{\mathrm{goal}}=\{z\mid z<0\}$, $\Omega_{\mathrm{play}}=\{z\mid z>0\}$ — i.e., $z$ doubles as the third coordinate and the signed distance variable. All subsequent formulas use this normalization.

### 5.2 Evasion space and BES — the core object (Sec. III-B, p. 3, eqs. (4)–(6))

- **Definition**: the *evasion space* (ES) is the set of points $E$ can reach before the pursuer(s) regardless of the pursuers' best effort; its boundary is the *BES*.
- **Eq. (4)**: $\mathscr E^i=\{z\in\mathbb R^3 \mid \|z-x_E^0\|_2 < \|z-x_{P_i}^0\|_2\}$, $\mathscr B^i=\{z\in\mathbb R^3 \mid \|z-x_E^0\|_2 = \|z-x_{P_i}^0\|_2\}$ — because speeds are equal, the ES is a **half-space** and the BES the **perpendicular bisector plane** of the segment $[x_E^0, x_{P_i}^0]$ (Fig. 2). This is the 3D degenerate case of the Apollonius circle (equal speeds ⟹ ratio 1 ⟹ bisector plane).
- **Eq. (5)**: two pursuers: $\mathscr E^{i,j}=\mathscr E^i\cap\mathscr E^j$, $\mathscr B^{i,j}=\partial\mathscr E^{i,j}$; **eq. (6)**: three pursuers: $\mathscr E=\mathscr E^1\cap\mathscr E^2\cap\mathscr E^3$, $\mathscr B=\partial\mathscr E$ — a polyhedral surface made of pieces of the bisector planes (Fig. 2).
- **Organizing trichotomy (used in every proof; implicit definition of the barrier)**: for a candidate initial evader position $x_E^0$, let $\mathscr B(x_E^0)$ be the team BES: $x_E^0$ is on the **barrier** iff $\mathscr B$ touches $\mathcal T$ but avoids $\Omega_{\mathrm{goal}}$ (capture exactly upon arrival); $x_E^0\in\mathcal W_{\mathrm P}$ iff $\mathscr B\cap(\mathcal T\cup\Omega_{\mathrm{goal}})=\varnothing$ (no safe entry point); $x_E^0\in\mathcal W_{\mathrm E}$ iff $\mathscr B$ intersects $\Omega_{\mathrm{goal}}$ (a reachable entry point exists). The reachable-point-first argument that makes the $\mathcal W_{\mathrm E}$ case airtight (pursuer cannot intercept a straight path to a point it reaches later, by the triangle inequality) is *not written out* — proof gap: the sufficiency of "BES intersects $\Omega_{\mathrm{goal}}$" for guaranteed arrival is used without the standard interception argument, pp. 3–5.
- **Notation caveat**: script $\mathscr E^i,\mathscr B^i$ denote ES/BES planes (dependent on $x_E^0$); italic $B^i, B^{i,j}, B$ denote **barriers** (sets of initial evader positions). The two are related by the trichotomy but the reuse of the letter $B$ across p. 3–5 requires constant disambiguation.

### 5.3 Lemma 1 (One pursuer, p. 3, eq. (7))

**Statement**: with only pursuer $P_i$, the barrier $B^i$ has two cases: if $z_{P_i}^0<0$ (pursuer in $\Omega_{\mathrm{goal}}$), $B^i=\{z\in\mathbb R^3 \mid x=x_{P_i}^0,\ y=y_{P_i}^0,\ z=-z_{P_i}^0\}$ — a **single point**, the mirror image of the pursuer across $\mathcal T$; otherwise $B^i=\varnothing$. Winning subspaces (eq. (7)):
$$\mathcal W_{\mathrm P}^i=\{z\in\mathbb R^3 \mid x=x_{P_i}^0,\ y=y_{P_i}^0,\ z>|z_{P_i}^0|\}\ (\text{a vertical ray}),\qquad \mathcal W_{\mathrm E}^i=\Omega_{\mathrm{play}}\setminus(B^i\cup\mathcal W_{\mathrm P}^i).$$
**Proof approach** (geometric case analysis, "See Fig. 3(a)"): if $x_E^0$ is on the barrier, $E$ is captured exactly upon reaching $\mathcal T$, so the bisector plane $\mathscr B^i$ must coincide with $\mathcal T$, forcing $x_E^0$ to be the reflection of $x_{P_i}^0$ (only possible when $z_{P_i}^0<0$); if $\mathscr B^i$ is parallel to $\mathcal T$ and lies in $\Omega_{\mathrm{play}}$ (which happens exactly for $x_{E}^0$ on the vertical line through $P_i$ with $z_E^0>|z_{P_i}^0|$), $E$ can never cross its own BES toward $\mathcal T$ — pursuer winning ray; otherwise $\mathscr B^i$ intersects $\Omega_{\mathrm{goal}}$ — evader winning. **Physical meaning**: a lone pursuer *below* the target plane guards it only against the single mirrored evader position; a lone pursuer *above* it guards only the vertical ray above itself. Analytic, exact, global. **Decisive intermediate fact**: $\mathscr B^i=\mathcal T \iff x_E^0=\hat x_{P_i}^0$ (reflection), p. 3.

### 5.4 Lemma 2 (Classification condition, two pursuers, pp. 3–4, eq. (8))

**Statement**: $B^{i,j}$ depends on **both** pursuers iff
$$(x_{P_i}^0-x_{P_j}^0)^2+(y_{P_i}^0-y_{P_j}^0)^2\neq 0 \tag{8}$$
(i.e., their projections into $\mathcal T$ differ). If (8) fails (pursuers on one vertical line), $B^{i,j}$ depends on one pursuer: $B^{i,j}=B^i$ if $|z_{P_i}^0|<|z_{P_j}^0|$, $B^{i,j}=B^j$ if $|z_{P_i}^0|>|z_{P_j}^0|$, and $B^{i,j}=\varnothing$ if $z_{P_i}^0=-z_{P_j}^0$ (mirror-symmetric pursuers; per Lemma 1 the mirror-point barrier degenerates because the reflection point is itself occupied by a pursuer). **Proof approach**: $P_i$'s dominance on $\mathcal T$ — "if $P_i$ can reach any point in $\mathcal T$ before $P_j$, then the barrier $B^{i,j}$ is determined by $P_i$ alone"; both contribute iff *each* has at least one point of $\mathcal T$ reachable before the other, which happens iff the projections differ (the perpendicular bisector plane of $[x_{P_i}^0,x_{P_j}^0]$ then cuts $\mathcal T$ into two nonempty half-planes). The sufficiency direction (projections differ ⟹ the joint barrier strictly exceeds each single-pursuer barrier) is argued through the arc part of Theorem 1 rather than proven independently — proof gap (minor): the "iff" is established jointly with Theorem 1, pp. 3–4.

### 5.5 Theorem 1 (Two active pursuers, p. 4, eqs. (9)–(10))

**Setting**: pursuers $P_i,P_j$ with (8) true. Notation (p. 4): $\bar x_{P_i}^0=(x_{P_i}^0,y_{P_i}^0,0)$ = projection into $\mathcal T$; $\mathcal L_{i,j}$ = straight line in $\mathcal T$ through $\bar x_{P_i}^0,\bar x_{P_j}^0$; $\mathcal P_{i,j}$ = vertical plane through $x_{P_i}^0,x_{P_j}^0$ and $\mathcal L_{i,j}$; $c_{i,j}$ = "the point in $\mathcal T$ that $P_i$ and $P_j$ can reach at the same time" (see proof gap (a) below).

**Statement**: the barrier is $B^{i,j}=\bigcup_{m=1}^3 B_{i,j}^m$ with
$$B_{i,j}^1=B^i,\qquad B_{i,j}^2=B^j,$$
$$B_{i,j}^3=\Big\{z\in\mathbb R^3 \,\Big|\, \|z-c_{i,j}\|_2=\|x_{P_i}^0-c_{i,j}\|_2,\quad x=\beta x_{P_i}^0+(1-\beta)x_{P_j}^0,\ y=\beta y_{P_i}^0+(1-\beta)y_{P_j}^0,\ \beta\in(0,1),\ z>0\Big\} \tag{9}$$
and the winning subspaces are
$$\mathcal W_{\mathrm P}^{i,j}=\Big\{z\in\mathbb R^3 \,\Big|\, \|z-c_{i,j}\|_2>\|x_{P_i}^0-c_{i,j}\|_2,\ x=\beta x_{P_i}^0+(1-\beta)x_{P_j}^0,\ y=\beta y_{P_i}^0+(1-\beta)y_{P_j}^0,\ \beta\in[0,1],\ z>0\Big\},$$
$$\mathcal W_{\mathrm E}^{i,j}=\Omega_{\mathrm{play}}\setminus(B^{i,j}\cup\mathcal W_{\mathrm P}^{i,j}). \tag{10}$$
Note $B^i$ or $B^j$ may be empty (Lemma 1) — e.g., for pursuers both in $\Omega_{\mathrm{play}}$ the barrier is the arc alone (Fig. 3(b)); the radius $\|x_{P_i}^0-c_{i,j}\|_2=\|x_{P_j}^0-c_{i,j}\|_2$ since $c_{i,j}$ is equidistant from both.

**Proof approach** (geometric, Fig. 3(b)): for $x_E^0\in B^{i,j}$, the BES $\mathscr B^{i,j}$ touches $\mathcal T$ without entering $\Omega_{\mathrm{goal}}$; the touching points lie either only in $\mathscr B^i$ (then $\mathscr B^i$ coincides with $\mathcal T$ ⟹ mirror-point case $B_{i,j}^1=B^i$ via Lemma 1), only in $\mathscr B^j$ ($B_{i,j}^2=B^j$), or at $\mathscr B^i\cap\mathscr B^j$; in the last case $x_E^0$ must lie in the vertical plane $\mathcal P_{i,j}$, on the circle of radius $\|x_{P_i}^0-c_{i,j}\|_2$ centered at $c_{i,j}$ *in that vertical plane*, with projection between $\bar x_{P_i}^0$ and $\bar x_{P_j}^0$ along $\mathcal L_{i,j}$ — giving (9). For $\mathcal W_{\mathrm P}^{i,j}$: $x_E^0$ must lie in $\mathcal P_{i,j}$ ("otherwise, $\mathscr B^{i,j}$ intersects with the set $\mathcal T\cup\Omega_{\mathrm{goal}}$"), outside the barrier circle, with projection between the two projections; the closing sentence "In addition, $x_E^0\in\mathcal W_{\mathrm P}^i$ or $x_E^0\in\mathcal W_{\mathrm P}^j$ is satisfactory" is elliptical — proof gap (b).

**Geometry / my consistency checks (verified)**: (i) $B_{i,j}^3$ is a **circular arc** in the vertical plane $\mathcal P_{i,j}$, centered at $c_{i,j}\in\mathcal T$; as $\beta\to1$ its endpoint height tends to $|z_{P_i}^0|$, i.e., to the pursuer's own position when $z_{P_i}^0>0$ and to the mirror point $B^i$ when $z_{P_i}^0<0$ — so points and arc join continuously into one curve; (ii) at $\beta=1$ the set $\mathcal W_{\mathrm P}^{i,j}$ reduces exactly to the one-pursuer ray $\mathcal W_{\mathrm P}^i$ of eq. (7) (outside-sphere condition $\|z-c\|_2>\|x_{P_i}^0-c\|_2$ on the vertical line $x=x_{P_i}^0,y=y_{P_i}^0$ is $z>|z_{P_i}^0|$) — the boundary conventions ($\beta\in(0,1)$ for the barrier, $[0,1]$ for $\mathcal W_{\mathrm P}$) are mutually consistent; (iii) dimensionally, $\mathcal W_{\mathrm P}^{i,j}$ is a planar region ("the part of a plane", Fig. 3(b)) — larger than the single-pursuer ray, as it must be for a stronger team.

**Proof gaps**: (a) *$c_{i,j}$ is not well-defined as printed*: in 3D the locus of points of $\mathcal T$ equidistant from two pursuers is generically a **line** (bisector plane ∩ $\mathcal T$), so "the point in $\mathcal T$ that $P_i$ and $P_j$ can reach at the same time" (p. 4) is not unique; the construction implicitly uses the unique such point lying on $\mathcal L_{i,j}$ (which makes the circle lie in $\mathcal P_{i,j}$, as the proof requires: "on the circle ... in this vertical plane"). The 2D analogue (P099) has a unique intersection point, so this is a 3D-specific carry-over imprecision. (b) The final step of the $\mathcal W_{\mathrm P}^{i,j}$ argument (p. 4) compresses the case analysis into one sentence.

### 5.6 Lemma 3 (Classification condition, three pursuers, p. 4, eqs. (11)–(13))

With index sets $I_i=\{1,2,3\}\setminus\{i\}$, $I_{i,j}=\{1,2,3\}\setminus\{i,j\}$ and $\hat x_{P_i}^0$ the reflection of $x_{P_i}^0$ across $\mathcal T$:

- $B=B^i$ (only $P_i$ contributes) **iff** for all $k\in I_i$:
$$x_{P_i}^0=x_{P_k}^0,\quad y_{P_i}^0=y_{P_k}^0,\quad |z_{P_i}^0|<|z_{P_k}^0| \tag{11}$$
(all three pursuers on one vertical line, $P_i$ closest to $\mathcal T$).
- $B=B^{i,j}$ (exactly two contribute) **iff** ($k\in I_{i,j}$):
$$(x_{P_i}^0-x_{P_j}^0)^2+(y_{P_i}^0-y_{P_j}^0)^2\neq0,\ \{x_{P_k}^0,\hat x_{P_k}^0\}\cap\mathcal W_{\mathrm P}^{i,j}\neq\varnothing \quad\text{or}\quad x_{P_i}^0=x_{P_k}^0,\ y_{P_i}^0=y_{P_k}^0,\ z_{P_i}^0=-z_{P_k}^0>0,\ x_{P_i}^0\notin\mathcal W_{\mathrm P}^j \tag{12}$$
i.e., the third pursuer $k$ is *neutralized* because it (or its mirror image) lies inside the pair's winning subspace, or the special case where $P_i,P_k$ are mirror-symmetric about $\mathcal T$ with $P_i$ in $\Omega_{\mathrm{play}}$ and outside $\mathcal W_{\mathrm P}^j$.
- $B$ depends on **all three** iff for all $i\neq j$ and $k\in I_{i,j}$:
$$(x_{P_i}^0-x_{P_j}^0)^2+(y_{P_i}^0-y_{P_j}^0)^2\neq0,\ \{x_{P_k}^0,\hat x_{P_k}^0\}\cap\mathcal W_{\mathrm P}^{i,j}=\varnothing \tag{13}$$

**Proof approach** (p. 4): "the barrier $B$ only depends on the pursuer $P_i$, if and only if $P_i$ can reach any point in $\mathcal T$ before the other two pursuers"; for the two-contributor case, Lemma 2 gives (8) and the third pursuer must reach no point of $\mathcal T$ before both, "meaning that $x_{P_k}^0\in\mathcal W_{\mathrm P}^{i,j}$ or $\hat x_{P_k}^0\in\mathcal W_{\mathrm P}^{i,j}$"; the symmetric-pair disjunct is argued in one sentence. **Proof gap (c)**: the equivalence between "$P_k$ dominates no point of $\mathcal T$ over the pair" and "$x_{P_k}^0$ or $\hat x_{P_k}^0\in\mathcal W_{\mathrm P}^{i,j}$" relies on the geometry of $\mathcal W_{\mathrm P}^{i,j}$ from Theorem 1 but is not verified step-by-step; the second disjunct of (12) (symmetric pair) is likewise asserted, not derived. All conditions are checkable closed-form predicates on the initial configuration — the classification is exact and complete (three cases exhaust the possibilities).

### 5.7 Theorem 2 (Three collinear-projection active pursuers, p. 5, eq. (14))

**Setting**: (13) true and the three projections $\bar x_{P_i}^0$ collinear, with $\bar x_{P_i}^0$ in the middle (index $i$ is the middle pursuer; $i=2$ in Fig. 4). **Statement**:
$$B=\bigcup_{k\in I_i}B^{i,k},\qquad \mathcal W_{\mathrm P}=\bigcup_{k\in I_i}\mathcal W_{\mathrm P}^{i,k},\qquad \mathcal W_{\mathrm E}=\bigcap_{k\in I_i}\mathcal W_{\mathrm E}^{i,k}. \tag{14}$$
**Proof approach** (Fig. 4): collinearity with the middle projection implies "there is no point in $\mathcal T$ that both two pursuers in $I_i$ can reach before $P_i$" — so no triple-contribution region exists and the barrier is exactly the union of the two pair barriers (two arcs, Fig. 4); the subspace formulas follow since $E$ must be stopped by both pairs. This is a pure set-decomposition theorem; its content is that for collinear projections the circumcenter construction of Theorem 3 degenerates (the pairwise equidistant loci in $\mathcal T$ are parallel lines).

### 5.8 Theorem 3 (Three noncollinear-projection active pursuers, p. 5, eqs. (15)–(16)) — main result

**Setting**: (13) true; projections not collinear. Notation: $S$ = closed triangular region in $\mathcal T$ with vertices $\bar x_{P_1}^0,\bar x_{P_2}^0,\bar x_{P_3}^0$; $c$ = "the unique point in $\mathcal T$ that has the equal distance to three pursuers" (well-defined here: for noncollinear projections the pairwise equidistant loci in $\mathcal T$ are two nonparallel lines; $c$ is their intersection — the "circumcenter in $\mathcal T$", equidistant from $x_{P_1}^0,x_{P_2}^0,x_{P_3}^0$, so $\|x_{P_1}^0-c\|_2=\|x_{P_2}^0-c\|_2=\|x_{P_3}^0-c\|_2$).

**Statement**: the barrier is the **spherical patch**
$$B=\Big\{z\in\mathbb R^3 \,\Big|\, \|z-c\|_2=\|x_{P_1}^0-c\|_2,\ (x,y)\in S,\ z>0\Big\} \tag{15}$$
and the winning subspaces are
$$\mathcal W_{\mathrm P}=\Big\{z\in\mathbb R^3 \,\Big|\, \|z-c\|_2>\|x_{P_1}^0-c\|_2,\ (x,y)\in S,\ z>0\Big\},\qquad \mathcal W_{\mathrm E}=\Omega_{\mathrm{play}}\setminus(B\cup\mathcal W_{\mathrm P}). \tag{16}$$
So $\mathcal W_{\mathrm P}$ is the region above the spherical patch inside the triangular prism erected on $S$ ("the region above B and bounded by a triangular prism", Fig. 5), and $\mathcal W_{\mathrm E}$ is everything else in $\Omega_{\mathrm{play}}$ — in particular all evader positions whose projection lies outside $S$.

**Proof approach** (p. 5, Fig. 5): $\mathscr B=\partial(\mathscr E^1\cap\mathscr E^2\cap\mathscr E^3)$ is made of parts of the three bisector planes $\mathscr B^i$. For $x_E^0\in B$, the BES touches $\mathcal T$ either within one plane ($x_E^0\in B^i$, mirror points), within two ($x_E^0\in B^{i,j}$, the arcs of Theorem 1 — the boundary edges of the patch), or at the triple point $\mathscr B^1\cap\mathscr B^2\cap\mathscr B^3$; "Since (13) is true and three pursuers' projections are not collinear, then $\mathscr B^1\cap\mathscr B^2\cap\mathscr B^3$ has the unique element, i.e., the point $c$." Hence $x_E^0$ lies on the sphere of radius $\|x_{P_1}^0-c\|_2$ centered at $c$, and $\mathscr E=\mathscr E^1\cap\mathscr E^2\cap\mathscr E^3$ forces $x_E^0$'s projection into $S$. For $\mathcal W_{\mathrm P}$: if $x_E^0$'s projection lay outside $S$, "on the opposite side of $\bar x_{P_k}^0$ ($k\in I_{i,j}$) with respect to $\mathcal L_{i,j}$, then there always exist points in $\mathcal T$ far away from $\mathcal L_{i,j}$ along the side of $x_E^0$'s projection, such that $E$ can reach before all three pursuers" — the *decisive escape argument* (far-away points of $\mathcal T$ slip through the seam between two adjacent guards); hence the projection must be in $S$, and then $x_E^0$ must be outside the sphere.

**Proof gap (d)**: the sentence "$\mathscr B^1\cap\mathscr B^2\cap\mathscr B^3$ has the unique element, i.e., the point $c$" conflates the $x_E^0$-dependent triple intersection of bisector planes (generically a single point of $\mathbb R^3$ equidistant from $x_E^0$ and all three pursuers) with the pursuer-determined point $c\in\mathcal T$; the intended argument is that on the barrier the triple point must be the BES–$\mathcal T$ contact point, hence lie in $\mathcal T$, hence coincide with the unique point of $\mathcal T$ equidistant from the three pursuers — from which $\|x_E^0-c\|_2=\|x_{P_i}^0-c\|_2$ follows. The step is recoverable but is not written; the same compression affects the projection-in-$S$ claim, which is stated to follow from "$\mathscr E=\mathscr E^1\cap\mathscr E^2\cap\mathscr E^3$" without derivation.

**Physical meaning and relation to Apollonius machinery**: because speeds are equal, no Apollonius *circles* appear — the dominance surface between $E$ and any pursuer is a bisector plane (eq. (4)), and the 3D barrier emerges from the *arrangement* of these planes: mirror points (one plane $\equiv\mathcal T$), circular arcs (two planes meeting on $\mathcal T$), and a spherical patch over the projection triangle (three planes meeting at $c\in\mathcal T$). The sphere (15) is the 3D lift of the 2D circular-arc barrier of the same authors (P099, their ref [18]); the guarded "footprint" grows from a segment (two pursuers) to the triangle $S$ (three pursuers). Global, exact, closed-form; no numerics anywhere.

### 5.9 Extension to $n$ pursuers — Algorithm 1 (p. 6) and Fig. 6 (p. 5)

**Algorithm 1** (inputs: $n$ pursuers' initial positions, $\mathcal T,\Omega_{\mathrm{play}},\Omega_{\mathrm{goal}}$; output: $B,\mathcal W_{\mathrm P},\mathcal W_{\mathrm E}$):
1. For each pursuer: **active** iff "there exists a point that it can reach before the other $n-1$ pursuers"; else inactive.
2. If fewer than three active pursuers: construct $B,\mathcal W_{\mathrm P},\mathcal W_{\mathrm E}$ by Lemma 1 or Theorem 1.
3. Else: find all **active triple-pursuer coalitions**, "in each of which three pursuers have a closer distance to the unique point in $\mathcal T$ that they can reach at the same time, before the other active pursuers"; for each coalition construct its barrier and winning subspaces by Theorem 2 or Theorem 3; finally $B=\bigcup$ (coalition barriers), $\mathcal W_{\mathrm P}=\bigcup$ (coalition $\mathcal W_{\mathrm P}$), $\mathcal W_{\mathrm E}=\bigcap$ (coalition $\mathcal W_{\mathrm E}$).

**Illustration (Fig. 6, p. 5)**: six active pursuers form four active triple coalitions $\{P_1,P_2,P_6\}$, $\{P_2,P_3,P_4\}$, $\{P_2,P_4,P_6\}$, $\{P_4,P_5,P_6\}$; the barrier "consists of the part of four spheres" and $\mathcal W_{\mathrm P}$ is "bounded by a hexagonal prism" over the hexagon $S$. Conclusion (p. 6): "multiple pursuers form a net to guarantee that the evader cannot reach the goal region", and the result "provides a potential way to design the pursuer placement such that the area of the net is maximized." **Proof gap (e)**: the coalition-union formulas (unions for $B,\mathcal W_{\mathrm P}$; intersection for $\mathcal W_{\mathrm E}$) are stated and illustrated but not proven — no lemma shows that the union of coalition barriers is exactly the team barrier for arbitrary $n$ (it is exact for the 6-pursuer configuration drawn, where the hexagonal footprint is covered by the four triangles, but overlapping/insufficient coverings are not analyzed); also "the unique point in $\mathcal T$ that they can reach at the same time" is unique only for noncollinear triples (collinear triples silently fall back to Theorem 2). Complexity is never analyzed (naively $O\!\binom{n}{3}$ coalitions).

**Count**: 3 Theorems + 3 Lemmas + 1 Algorithm; no propositions/corollaries. All statements above were checked word-by-word against 200-dpi renders of pp. 3–6; the arc/sphere endpoint consistency checks in §5.5 are my own verification (not in the paper).

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues

**Author-stated**: (1) the game is solved only *as a game of kind* — "Future work will focus on the quantitative problems, such as how to capture the evader within the minimal time" (p. 6); no payoff, no value function, no optimal trajectories. (2) Restrictive setup: equal unit speeds, simple motion, planar target plane, single evader. (3) Placement design is only suggested ("a potential way", p. 6), not formulated.

**Critical observations (with page/eq.)**:
1. **No simulation/experiments at all** (§6): the "complete, closed-form barrier" and "guaranteed winning for each team is a prior [sic]" (abstract) are never exercised numerically.
2. **No strategy construction**: the paper computes $B,\mathcal W_{\mathrm P},\mathcal W_{\mathrm E}$ but never writes the feedback strategies that *realize* the guarantee ("under $P_i$ and $E$'s optimal strategies", p. 3, is invoked, not constructed); the non-anticipative framework is cited ([10], [24]) but no admissibility/value argument ties the static geometry to guaranteed winning. The standard equal-speed interception argument (straight-line path dominance via the triangle inequality) that underwrites the $\mathcal W_{\mathrm E}$ side is nowhere written.
3. **$c_{i,j}$ ill-defined for pairs in 3D** (proof gap (a), §5.5): the equidistant locus in $\mathcal T$ is a line (p. 4); uniqueness holds only within the vertical plane $\mathcal P_{i,j}$, which the text does not say. Same looseness in Algorithm 1's "unique point in $\mathcal T$" for triples (unique only for noncollinear projections).
4. **Compressed proofs** (gaps (b)–(e)): Theorem 3's "$\mathscr B^1\cap\mathscr B^2\cap\mathscr B^3$ has the unique element, i.e., the point $c$" (p. 5) conflates the $x_E^0$-dependent triple BES point with the pursuer-determined circumcenter $c$; Theorem 1's final $\mathcal W_{\mathrm P}^{i,j}$ step and Lemma 3's second disjunct of (12) are one-liners; Algorithm 1's union/intersection formulas are unproven for general $n$.
5. **Notational overload**: script $\mathscr B^i$ (BES plane, eq. (4)) vs. italic $B^i$ (barrier, Lemma 1) and $B^{i,j}$ (BES in eq. (5) vs. barrier in Theorem 1); $z$ is simultaneously the generic point of $\mathbb R^3$ and its third coordinate after normalization (3); $x_{P_i}^0=x_{P_k}^0$ in (11)–(12) equates *scalar x-coordinates* although $x^0$ elsewhere denotes the full vector (p. 4).
6. **Measure-zero classifications, no robustness**: on $B$ capture and arrival are simultaneous; no treatment of perturbed initial positions, sensing noise, or which side of $B$ an $\varepsilon$-perturbed evader falls to; $\mathcal W_{\mathrm E}$ is characterized only as a complement (16), never positively.
7. **Static, $t=0$ analysis**: pursuers' positions are fixed inputs; no repositioning/online replanning despite the advertised "real-time updates" (abstract refers to computational cheapness, not a moving-horizon scheme).
8. **Coalition enumeration cost**: unanalyzed; for large $n$ the number of active triple coalitions can grow combinatorially, weakening "without computational burden" (abstract).
9. **As-printed errata** (record for citation accuracy): grant "1210012" (p. 1, 7-digit NSFC number); "Four players are assumed to have been equal unit speed" (p. 2); "Eculidean norm" (p. 2); "four player start" (p. 2); "puruser" (pp. 3–4); "ouside" (p. 4); "the guaranteed winning ... is a prior" (abstract, for *a priori*); "has been addressed from kind" (p. 6, for *as a game of kind*); "Hamilton-Jacob-Isaacs" (p. 1).

## 8. Citation Value

**Paragraph role**: P073 is the reference for the **3D equal-speed multiplayer reach-avoid barrier built from bisector-plane geometry** — the point in the literature where the barrier of a cooperating pursuer team stops being a 2D Apollonius/bisector arc (same authors' P099, IEEE TCYB 2019, their ref [18]) and becomes a **net of spherical patches**: mirror points for one pursuer, a circular arc in the vertical plane for two, a spherical patch over the pursuers' projection triangle for three (Theorem 3, eq. (15)), and a union of sphere patches over active triple coalitions for $n$ pursuers (Algorithm 1, Fig. 6). It is the natural citation for (i) complete game-of-kind solutions in 3D without HJI (closed-form winning subspaces enabling pre-game winner prediction), (ii) the active-pursuer / triple-coalition decomposition pattern for scaling multiplayer barriers, (iii) the "pursuers form a net" structure as a guard-coverage/placement primitive, and (iv) analytic real-time alternatives to HJI reachability for $\mathbb R^{12}$ simple-motion teams. Within this library it is the direct 3D precursor of P100/P074 (Yan et al., TRO 2020, task assignment *via* analytical barriers) and of P103 (Yan et al., Automatica 2022, 3D heterogeneous matching-based capture), and the 3D counterpart of P099; it contrasts with P035 (Garcia et al., RA-L 2020, 3D multiplayer reach-avoid by different dominance arguments) and is the methodological ancestor cited by later analytic-barrier 3D works (e.g., P075). Because the PDF is an unrefereed preprint with no printed venue (to verify), cite it alongside its published version once identified, or cite the journal descendants P099/P100 for the established results.
