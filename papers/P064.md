## 2. Research Background
Multiplayer reach-avoid games model two adversarial teams in a planar domain split into a play region and a target region, with applications to vehicle collision avoidance, aircraft conflict resolution, robot competitions and perimeter/target defense (p. 1, citing [1]–[5]). A large literature solves these games under complete information using Apollonius-circle/analytical barriers and pairwise outcomes, e.g., Yan–Shi–Zhong's analytical barriers (T-Ro [8]), Chen–Zhou–Tomlin's pairwise outcomes (TAC [9]), and 3D heterogeneous matching-based capture (Automatica [11]); another thread treats sensing-limited pursuit with open-loop strategies (Bopardikar–Bullo–Hespanha T-Ro [6]; Pachter–Garcia–Casbeer JGCD [7]). This paper moves to the realistic setting where pursuers have **heterogeneous maximum speeds and heterogeneous, finite sensing radii** while evaders are globally informed: pursuers see only what their sensing disks contain, so detection is asynchronous, assignments must be redone repeatedly over time, and closed-loop capture must additionally guarantee that the assigned evader never leaves the pursuer's sensing region. The work follows the "decompose-and-assign" paradigm: reduce the multiplayer game to a sequence of one-vs-one subgames, and solve deployment (where to wait) and assignment (who chases whom) separately.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
**Problem**: $N_p$ pursuers $\mathcal{P}=\{P_i\}$ with speeds $v_{P_i}$ and sensing radii $r_i$, versus $N_e$ evaders $\mathcal{E}=\{E_j\}$ with speeds $v_{E_j}$, simple motion in $\mathbb{R}^2$; the pursuit team must minimize the number of evaders reaching the target line $\mathcal{T}$ while each pursuer only detects evaders inside its own disk $S_i$ (pp. 1–3).

- [authors' claim] Open-loop capture strategies under partial information (as in [6], [7]) "depend on the accurate prediction of the evader states" and deployment optimality "was not further analyzed in [5]–[7], [13]" (pp. 1–2). — [Verified as a gap statement about those papers' scope; the cited works are indeed open-loop/vulnerable-area formulations, but this paper does not re-verify their contents.]
- [authors' claim] Matching methods in [8]–[10] "focused on the static assignment problem under complete information", and weights of pursuers in task assignment "were not fully studied in [8]–[11], [14]–[15]" (p. 2). — [Verified as authors' positioning; consistent with the cited titles, which assume pairwise outcomes/barriers under complete information.]
- [authors' claim] Equilibrium/saddle-point works [11], [12] and capture-strategy works [16]–[21] build on HJI-type reasoning "under complete information"; optimal capture under partial information was "not further investigated" (pp. 1–2). — [Authors' claim; the paper itself does not solve any HJI equation either — its optimality is geometric and conditional (see Lemma 1).]
- **Net gap addressed** (p. 2): a unified defense pipeline — closed-loop capture strategy valid under sensing constraints, *optimal* open-loop deployment of heterogeneous pursuers computed from a "conservative barrier" that accounts for the fastest *undetected* evader, and a *dynamic* (repeated) maximum-weight bipartite matching that reserves strong pursuers for future detections.

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **(Modeling/information)** A heterogeneous multiplayer reach-avoid differential game with an explicitly asymmetric information structure: pursuers know all evader speeds but only positions of *detected* evaders; evaders know all pursuer positions and speeds but not the sensing radii (p. 2, Sec. II-B). Non-anticipative patterns ([22]) are rejected because pre-detection evader states are inaccessible; a state-feedback partial-information pattern with a centralized coordinator is adopted instead.
2. **(Theory)** Closed-loop capture strategy (eq. (5), Lemma 1, p. 4) with a speed-ratio threshold $\alpha_{ij}\ge\sqrt{2}$ under which the pursuer can head straight to the optimal Apollonius point while provably keeping the evader inside its sensing disk; optimality "under exact velocity conditions" (authors' wording, p. 1).
3. **(Theory/modeling)** Conservative barrier and optimal deployment: Theorem 1 gives the minimal sensing radius needed to fully cover the conservative barrier, Lemma 2 solves the minimal-radius deployment, and Theorem 2 classifies heterogeneous pursuers into two categories $\mathcal{D}^1$, $\mathcal{D}^2$ with closed-form optimal deployment positions (pp. 5–6).
4. **(Algorithm)** Dynamic weighted task assignment as a maximum-cardinality-constrained maximum-weight bipartite matching, solved by Hopcroft–Karp + min-cost flow (eqs. (8)–(10), Algorithm 1), embedded in the unified Algorithm 2 (deployment → matching → capture loop) under asynchronous detections (pp. 6–7).
5. **(Experiment)** Physical validation on a NOKOV motion-capture platform with quadrotors (2 vs 2), plus software simulation in a 12 m × 5 m arena (pp. 7–8).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results (exhaustive)

### 5.1 Dynamics, information structure, payoff, termination (p. 2)
- **Dynamics (eq. (1))**: simple motion, $\dot{x}_{P_i}=v_{P_i}u_{P_i},\ x_{P_i}(0)=x^0_{P_i}$; $\dot{x}_{E_j}=v_{E_j}u_{E_j},\ x_{E_j}(0)=x^0_{E_j}$, $t\ge 0$, with headings $u_{P_i},u_{E_j}\in\mathcal{U}=\{u\in\mathbb{R}^2\,|\,\|u\|_2=1\ \text{or}\ u=0\}$ (players may stop). Geometry: play region $\Omega_{\text{play}}$ and target region $\Omega_{\text{tar}}$ separated by the affine hull of endpoints $\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n}$; the target line $\mathcal{T}$ is the segment $\overline{\mathfrak{mn}}$ of length $l$; origin fixed at $\mathfrak{m}$ (Figs. 1, 4, 5, 8: $\mathcal{T}$ horizontal, $\Omega_{\text{tar}}$ above, $\Omega_{\text{play}}$ below). Capture iff $\|x_{P_i}-x_{E_j}\|_2=0$ (point capture). Termination: all evaders captured or having reached $\mathcal{T}$.
- **Information structure (Sec. II-B)**: pursuer $P_i$'s partial information set $I_{P_i}(t)=\{x_{P_i},v_{P_i},r_i\}\cup\{v_{E_j}\}_{j=1}^{N_e}\cup\{x_{E_j}:x_{E_j}\in S_i\}$; evader $E_j$'s complete set $I_{E_j}(t)=\{x_{P_i},v_{P_i}\}_{i=1}^{N_p}\cup\{x_{E_j},v_{E_j}\}$. Controls $u_{P_i}=\Gamma_{P_i}(I_{P_i}(t))$, $u_{E_j}=\Gamma_{E_j}(I_{E_j}(t))$ (eq. (2)). The defense strategy splits as $\Gamma_{P_i}=\Gamma^d_{P_i}(\{x_{P_i},v_{P_i}\}\cup\{v_{E_j}\})$ (open-loop deployment) $+\ \Gamma^c_{P_i}(x_{P_i},x_{E_j},v_{P_i},v_{E_j})$ (closed-loop capture); matching map $\mathcal{M}(\cup_{i=1}^{N_p}I_{P_i})$ by a coordinator that aggregates local observations; no pursuer-to-pursuer communication. Evaders fully observe the pursuit team but not the radii $r_i$ — note the deliberate design that **no strategy below depends on $r_i$ on the evader side**, so radius-uncertainty is harmless to (4).
- **Assumptions** (pp. 2–3): **A1 (Assignment Constraint)** each pursuer captures at most one evader, each evader exclusively assigned to one pursuer (⇒ decomposition into 1-vs-1 subgames; no cooperative capture). **A2 (Speed Constraint)** $\max_j v_{E_j}<\min_i v_{P_i}$; speed ratio $\alpha_{ij}=v_{P_i}/v_{E_j}>1$. **A3 (Arrival Announcement)** each evader announces upon reaching the target region (termination detectability).
- **Payoff**: qualitative — evasion team maximizes / pursuit team minimizes the *number of evaders reaching $\mathcal{T}$*; no scalar integral cost or HJI value function is formally posed (Remark 1, p. 3, only gestures at a value function $V(x_{P_1},\dots,x_{P_{N_p}},x_{E_j})$).

### 5.2 Evasion region and Apollonius barrier (eq. (3), p. 3)
With $x_A(i,j)=\frac{\alpha_{ij}^2 x_{E_j}-x_{P_i}}{\alpha_{ij}^2-1}$ and $R_A(i,j)=\frac{\alpha_{ij}\|x_{P_i}-x_{E_j}\|_2}{\alpha_{ij}^2-1}$, the evasion region and its boundary are
$\mathcal{R}_{ij}=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^2:\|z-x_{A_{ij}}\|_2<R_{A_{ij}}\}$, $\mathcal{R}^{\partial}_{ij}=\{z:\|z-x_{A_{ij}}\|_2=R_{A_{ij}}\}$ — the classical Apollonius circle (interior = points the evader reaches strictly before $P_i$). "The barrier under complete information is shown in [8]" (no re-derivation). The multiplayer game is phased: **Phase 1** deployment (open-loop move to post, or random search); **Phase 2** matching (detection + coordinator assignment); **Phase 3** interaction (complete-information 1-vs-1 with the *additional constraint that the pursuer keeps the evader within its sensing range* throughout; start time $t_{p3}$) (Sec. III-B).

### 5.3 Breakout strategy of the evader (eq. (4), p. 3) — analytic result, no theorem number
$u_{E_j}=\dfrac{x^*-x_{E_j}}{\|x^*-x_{E_j}\|_2}$, $x^*=\arg\min_{x\in\partial(\cap_{i}^{N_p}\mathcal{R}_{ij})}\mathrm{dist}(x,\mathcal{T})$: head at full speed toward the point of the boundary of the intersection of all individual evasion regions that is closest to $\mathcal{T}$. **Remark 1** (p. 3): derived by minimizing the distance from the evader's terminal state to $\mathcal{T}$ (viewed as the value function); the reachable terminal point may be closer to $\mathcal{T}$ than the initial ER suggests; if the nearest point lies at an intersection of two BERs, capture "necessarily requires simultaneous engagement by two pursuers", which is excluded by A1 — the evader moves toward the intersection only because it does not know which pursuer was assigned. Proof approach: geometric, 3 steps (form terminal-distance objective → restrict to $\partial(\cap\mathcal{R}_{ij})$ → straight-line heading); no formal optimality proof against a knowing adversary is given (proof gap: the claim that (4) is the evader's best response under radius uncertainty is argued, not proven; it is optimal only within the stated uncertainty model).

### 5.4 Lemma 1 (p. 4, with eq. (5)) — mixed capture strategy and the $\sqrt{2}$ threshold
**Statement**: The strategy (5) guarantees capture if $\|\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathcal{T}}\,x_A(i,j)-x_A(i,j)\|_2>R_A(i,j)$ (i.e., the current Apollonius circle is strictly separated from $\mathcal{T}$), with the evader remaining inside the sensing region throughout the interaction phase.
**Strategy (eq. (5))**:
$$u_{P_i}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{x^\dagger-x_{P_i}}{\|x^\dagger-x_{P_i}\|_2}, & \alpha_{ij}\ge\sqrt{2}\\[4pt] \dfrac{\chi}{\|\chi\|_2}, & \alpha_{ij}<\sqrt{2}\end{cases}$$
$$\chi=\alpha_{ij}\big(R^{t_{p3}}_A(i,j)+\delta-R_A(i,j)\big)\cdot\frac{x_{E_j}-x_{P_i}}{\|x_{E_j}-x_{P_i}\|_2}+x^{t_{p3}}_A(i,j)-x_A(i,j),\qquad
x^\dagger=x_A(i,j)+R_A(i,j)\cdot\frac{\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathcal{T}}x_A(i,j)-x_A(i,j)}{\|\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathcal{T}}x_A(i,j)-x_A(i,j)\|_2}$$
with $\delta>0$ arbitrarily small and $R^{t_{p3}}_A,x^{t_{p3}}_A$ evaluated at $t_{p3}$. $x^\dagger$ is the point of the current Apollonius circle nearest $\mathcal{T}$; the $\alpha<\sqrt2$ branch is exactly the mixed strategy of Dorothy et al. (Automatica [17]) that trades radial closing ($\pm$ along the line of sight, coefficient $\alpha_{ij}(R^{t_{p3}}_A+\delta-R_A)$) against tracking the Apollonius-center drift ($x^{t_{p3}}_A-x_A$) so as to keep the evader on/near the BER.
**Proof approach (5 steps, geometric + one differentiation)**:
1. $\alpha_{ij}<\sqrt2$ branch: invoke **Corollary 2 of [17]** (strategy reduces to that work; no restatement of its hypotheses — minor proof gap).
2. For $\alpha_{ij}\ge\sqrt2$: differentiate the separation, $\frac{d}{dt}\|x_{E_j}-x_{P_i}\|_2=\frac{(x_{E_j}-x_{P_i})^T(v_{E_j}u_{E_j}-v_{P_i}u_{P_i})}{\|x_{E_j}-x_{P_i}\|_2}$.
3. Let $\theta$ be the angle between $u_{P_i}$ and the relative position $x_{E_j}-x_{P_i}$. **Decisive condition**: if $v_{P_i}\cos\theta\ge v_{E_j}$ holds for the worst case $\theta$, then $(x_{E_j}-x_{P_i})^T(v_{E_j}u_{E_j}-v_{P_i}u_{P_i})\le 0$, so the evader cannot exit the sensing region (non-increasing separation) and capture is guaranteed.
4. Worst-case angle: $\theta_{\max}$ is attained when the pursuer's velocity direction is tangent to the BER, giving $\sin\theta_{\max}=\frac{R_A(i,j)}{\|x_A(i,j)-x_{P_i}\|_2}=\alpha_{ij}^{-1}$ (uses $\|x_{P_i}-x_A\|=\alpha_{ij}R_A$ from the Apollonius geometry).
5. Substitute into the speed condition $\alpha_{ij}\ge 1/\cos\theta_{\max}$: $\sqrt{\alpha_{ij}^2-1}\ge 1\iff\alpha_{ij}\ge\sqrt{2}$ — the printed threshold. Local result (per assigned pair), analytic, exact (no numerics). **Physical meaning**: if the pursuer is more than $\approx41\%$ faster ($\alpha\ge\sqrt2$), guarding the evader inside the disk and capturing optimally are compatible — it can simply aim at the Apollonius point nearest $\mathcal{T}$; below $\sqrt2$ it must deliberately deviate (χ-branch). **Extensibility**: the $\sqrt2$ constant is specific to circular BER/point capture in $\mathbb{R}^2$; other capture radii or target geometries change the threshold. **Proof gap**: step 3 asserts "and the capture is guaranteed" — non-escape plus speed advantage is shown, but finite-time capture under the exact geometry (evader confined while the BER shrinks) is inherited from [17] without restatement; step 4's tangency characterization of $\theta_{\max}$ is asserted by a figure (Fig. 3), not derived.

### 5.5 Conservative barrier under partial information (p. 4–5, Sec. V-A)
Sensing region of the team $\mathcal{S}=\cup_i \mathcal{S}_i$; evaders split into undetected $\mathcal{E}_{\text{und}}$ (outside $\mathcal{S}$) and detected $\mathcal{E}_{\text{det}}$. Because the pursuer knows only evader *speeds* of undetected evaders, define the **conservative speed** $v^0_{\max}=\max\{v_{E_j}\,|\,E_j\in\mathcal{E}_{\text{und}}\}$ and **conservative speed ratio** $\alpha_i=v_{P_i}/v^0_{\max}=\min\{\alpha_{ij}\,|\,E_j\in\mathcal{E}_{\text{und}}\}$ — the worst (smallest) ratio the pursuer may face, updated as detections change $\mathcal{E}_{\text{und}}$. The **conservative barrier** $\mathcal{B}^i_{\text{con}}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^2\,|\,B(x;x_{P_i};\alpha_i;\mathcal{T})=0\}$ with barrier function
$$B(x;x_{P_i};\alpha_i;\mathcal{T}):=\|\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathcal{T}}(x_A)-x_A\|_2-R_A,\qquad x_A=\frac{\alpha_i^2 x-x_{P_i}}{\alpha_i^2-1},\quad R_A=\frac{\alpha_i\|x_{P_i}-x\|_2}{\alpha_i^2-1},$$
i.e., the locus of putative evader positions whose Apollonius circle (against $P_i$ at ratio $\alpha_i$) is *tangent* to $\mathcal{T}$. Winning regions (eq. (6)): $\mathcal{W}^{\mathcal{B}}_{P_i}=\{x:B>0\}$ (pursuit win), $\mathcal{W}^{\mathcal{B}}_{E_m}=\{x:B<0\}$ (fastest-evader win); the partition is fixed by the pursuer state alone ("independent of the state of evader", p. 5). Using $v^0_{\max}$ makes the barrier *conservative* (larger than any true barrier), guaranteeing deployment validity against the fastest undetected evader.

### 5.6 Theorem 1 (p. 5–6) — minimal sensing radius for full barrier coverage
**Statement**: For pursuer $P_i$ with conservative ratio $\alpha_i$, the minimal sensing radius covering $\mathcal{B}^i_{\text{con}}$ is $(1+1/\alpha_i)\max\mathrm{dist}\{P_i,\mathcal{T}\}$, where $\max\mathrm{dist}\{\cdot,\cdot\}$ is the maximum distance between elements of the two sets.
**Assumptions**: convex target region (Fig. 6: $\Omega_{\text{tar}}$ arbitrary convex, $\eta$ = projection of the Apollonius center onto it); conservative ratio $\alpha_i>1$; barrier as in 5.5. **Proof approach (4 steps, geometry + convexity/normal cone)**:
1. Any barrier point $\xi$ satisfies $\|\xi-x_{P_i}\|_2=\frac{(\alpha_i^2-1)R_A}{\alpha_i}$, so the max pursuer–barrier distance determines the max Apollonius radius $R_A$.
2. Apollonius geometry: $\|x_{P_i}-x_A\|_2=\frac{\alpha_i^2\|x_{P_i}-\xi\|_2}{\alpha_i^2-1}=\alpha_iR_A$; triangle inequality gives the **decisive intermediate inequality** $\alpha_iR_A-R_A\le\|x_{P_i}-\eta\|_2$, $\eta$ the projection point on the target line; equivalently $\|x_{P_i}-\xi\|_2\le(1+\alpha_i^{-1})\|x_{P_i}-\eta\|_2$.
3. Maximality forces normality: if $\eta$ maximizes $\|x_{P_i}-\eta\|$ over the convex target set but $x_{P_i}-\eta\notin N_{\mathcal{T}}(\eta)$ (outward normal cone), then some $\vartheta$ in the set satisfies $(\eta-x_{P_i})(\vartheta-\eta)>0$, contradicting maximality — hence $x_{P_i}-\eta\in N_{\mathcal{T}}(\eta)$.
4. $N_{\mathcal{T}}(\eta)$ contains the direction making $\eta,x_{P_i},\xi$ collinear, so $\max\{(1+\alpha_i^{-1})\|x_{P_i}-\eta\|_2\}$ is the supremum of the pursuer–barrier distance; since the sensing disk must cover all barrier points, this supremum is the required radius. ∎
**Scope**: analytic, global over the barrier, for a *fixed* pursuer position. **Physical meaning**: the sensing disk must reach the farthest barrier point; a pursuer standing near $\mathcal{T}$ needs radius $\approx(1+1/\alpha_i)\,l/2$ (farthest $\mathcal{T}$-point at an endpoint). **Proof gap**: step 4's collinearity/supremum argument is compressed into one sentence; attainment vs. supremum is glossed ("represents the supremum", p. 6).

### 5.7 Lemma 2 (p. 6) — optimal deployment for full coverage (Problem 2)
**Statement**: The optimization problem of Problem 1 [sic — the text body assigns this result to Problem 2: minimize over deployment positions the radius required to cover the barrier] is a convex optimization problem and the solution is $x^{\text{opt}}_{P_i}=(l/2,0)$ — the midpoint of $\mathcal{T}$.
**Proof (3 steps)**: formulate $\min\{\max\mathrm{dist}\{P_i,\mathcal{T}\}\}$; the maximum distance from points of a set to a variable point defines a convex function, so the problem is the **minimal enclosing circle** of $\mathcal{T}$; the center of the minimal enclosing circle of a segment is its midpoint $(l/2,0)$. ∎ Combined with Theorem 1, the covered-barrier radius at the optimum is $(1+\alpha_i^{-1})l/2$. Local analytic result; the convexity claim is standard (max of convex distance functions) though strict convexity/uniqueness is not discussed. (Notation slip: Lemma 2's statement says "of Problem 1" while p. 6 assigns Theorem 1 ↔ Problem 1, Lemma 2 ↔ Problem 2.)

### 5.8 Problem 3, payoff (eq. (7)) and Theorem 2 (p. 6) — two deployment categories
For pursuers whose radius cannot cover the barrier anywhere, the paper poses the constrained problem
$$\min_{\mathcal{S}_i} S_{\mathcal{W}^{\mathcal{B}}_{E_m}}\quad\text{s.t.}\quad \mathcal{S}_i\cap\mathcal{W}^{\mathcal{B}}_{E_m}=\varnothing \tag{7}$$
($S_{\mathcal{W}^{\mathcal{B}}_{E_m}}$ = area of the evasion winning region): place the pursuer so its sensing disk never wastes coverage inside the region where it cannot win anyway, while shrinking that region as much as possible — explicitly a "compromise optimality" for limited radii, solvable "by a geometry method".
**Theorem 2**: The pursuit team splits into two categories by conservative barrier/speed ratio, with optimal positions $\{\mathcal{D}^1: x^{\text{opt}}_{P_i}=(l/2,0)\}$ and $\{\mathcal{D}^2: x^{\text{opt}}_{P_i}=(l/2,\,r_i/(\alpha_i^{-1}-1))\}$.
**Proof approach (5 steps, monotonicity + KKT + tangency)**:
1. $\mathcal{D}^1$ position = Problem 2 solution; corresponding minimal radius $(1+\alpha_i^{-1})l/2$.
2. The solution of (7) corresponds to pursuers with $r_i\le(1+\alpha_i^{-1})l/2$ (the $\mathcal{D}^2$ class).
3. For $x_{P_i}\ge l,\ y_{P_i}\le 0$ the distance [to barrier point $\xi$, as printed] increases monotonically with $x_{P_i}$ and decreases with $y_{P_i}$; hence the area $S_{\mathcal{W}^{\mathcal{B}}_{E_m}}$ monotonically decreases as $x_{P_i}$ increases or $y_{P_i}$ decreases; by symmetry the same holds for $x_{P_i}\le0$.
4. Fixing $y_{P_i}=0$, $x_{P_i}\in(0,l)$: $S_{\mathcal{W}^{\mathcal{B}}_{E_m}}$ is convex in $x_{P_i}$; the KKT condition gives $x=l/2$ — the horizontal optimum.
5. Under the constraint $\mathcal{S}_i\cap\mathcal{W}^{\mathcal{B}}_{E_m}=\varnothing$ the optimum makes the sensing region **tangent to the barrier**: fixing $x_{P_i}=l/2$, tangency holds at $y_{P_i}=r_i/(\alpha_i^{-1}-1)$ (negative ⇒ stand-off distance $r_i\alpha_i/(\alpha_i-1)$ on the play-region side of $\mathcal{T}$). ∎
**Verification of step 5** (independent check against the printed experiment): pursuer at $(0,0.5)$, $\alpha=3$, $r=1$, $\mathcal{T}=\{(x,2):|x|\le1\}$ — the barrier branch with vertical projection satisfies $x^2=8((y-2)^2-0.25)$, apex $(0,1.5)$, which is exactly the top of the sensing disk: tangency confirmed, and the disk is disjoint from $\mathcal{W}^{\mathcal{B}}_{E_m}$. **Proof gap**: no closed-form area expression for $S_{\mathcal{W}^{\mathcal{B}}_{E_m}}$ is ever derived; monotonicity and convexity are asserted qualitatively, and KKT is applied to an unstated objective — the result is credible (and experimentally consistent for $\mathcal{D}^2$) but the optimization is not rigorously set up. Note also the two categories are *complementary*: a $\mathcal{D}^1$ pursuer standing on $\mathcal{T}$ leaves thin evader-winning wedges near the endpoints, while a $\mathcal{D}^2$ pursuer's tangent disk covers the region below its own barrier; team-level coverage of all wedges is not proven.

### 5.9 Task assignment: eqs. (8)–(10), Algorithms 1–2 (pp. 6–7) — no formal theorem
Feasibility of a perception pair $(P_i,E_j)$, $E_j\in\mathcal{E}_{\text{det}}$: capture is feasible iff $\mathcal{R}_{E_j}$ (the Apollonius circle/region of the pair) intersects the target line $\mathcal{T}$. Build the undirected weighted bipartite graph $\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{E}_{\text{det}},\mathcal{L},w)$, $\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathcal{P}\times\mathcal{E}_{\text{det}}$ feasible edges; the printed weight domain "$w:=\mathcal{L}\cup\mathcal{E}_{\text{und}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}$" is a notational slip (weights live on $\mathcal{L}$ only; $\mathcal{E}_{\text{und}}$ is not a vertex set). The matching restricted to maximum cardinality $N^*=\max_{\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{L}}|\mathcal{M}|$ and maximum total weight is
$$\mathcal{M}_{\max}=\arg\max_{\substack{\mathcal{M}\subset\mathcal{L}\\|\mathcal{M}|=N^*}}\sum_{(P_i,E_j)\in\mathcal{M}}w(\mathcal{L}\cup\mathcal{E}_{\text{und}})\tag{8}$$
($w(\mathcal{L}\cup\mathcal{E}_{\text{und}})$ in the summand is a typo for the edge weight $w_{ij}$), realized as the integer program
$$\max_{l_{ij}}\ \textstyle\sum_{(P_i,E_j)\in\mathcal{L}}w_{ij}l_{ij}\quad \text{s.t.}\ \sum_{j:(P_i,E_j)\in\mathcal{L}}l_{ij}\le1\ \forall P_i;\ \sum_{i:(P_i,E_j)\in\mathcal{L}}l_{ij}\le1\ \forall E_j\in\mathcal{E}_{\text{det}};\ \sum l_{ij}=N^*;\ l_{ij}\in\{0,1\}.\tag{9}$$
**Weight design (eq. (10))**: $w_{ij}=r_i^{-1}$ if $P_i\in\mathcal{D}^1$; $w_{ij}=(1+\alpha_i^{-1})r_i^{-1}$ if $P_i\in\mathcal{D}^2$, $(P_i,E_j)\in\mathcal{L}$ — weaker pursuers (small radius, small $\alpha_i$, both concentrated in $\mathcal{D}^2$) receive larger weights, so at fixed cardinality $N^*$ the max-weight matching spends *weak* pursuers on currently detected evaders and **reserves strong pursuers (large $r_i$, large $v_{P_i}$) for future detections** caused by asynchronous sensing.
**Algorithm 1** (task assignment): Step 1 build $\mathcal{L}$, $\mathcal{G}$; Step 2 cardinality $N^*$ by **Hopcroft–Karp** on the unweighted graph; Step 3 solve (9) as a **minimum-cost flow with total flow fixed to $N^*$** using (10); Step 4 remove matched pairs ($\mathcal{P},\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{\text{det}}$ updated). Exactness w.r.t. (9) holds by construction (polynomial bipartite b-matching/flow); the paper's assertion "Algorithm 1 ensures the optimality of the task allocation phase" (p. 7) means optimality *for the stated one-shot weight*, not global capture-count optimality — indeed p. 7 concedes: "The defense strategy follows a greedy principle and does not guarantee the maximization of the total number of captured evaders" (with unknown evader initial distribution, strong pursuers may be committed early).
**Algorithm 2** (unified defense): initialize $\mathcal{E}_{\text{und}},\mathcal{E}_{\text{det}},\alpha_i,\mathcal{L},\mathcal{D}^1,\mathcal{D}^2$ at $t=0$; run Algorithm 1; assigned pairs execute $\Gamma^c_{P_i}$ (eq. (5)); $\mathcal{D}^1$ pursuers move to $(l/2,0)$, $\mathcal{D}^2$ to $(l/2,r_i/(\alpha_i^{-1}-1))$; then a repeat-loop: remove evaders reaching $\mathcal{T}$; on sensing-region entry/exit update $\mathcal{E}_{\text{det}},\mathcal{L}$ and re-run Algorithm 1; refresh $\mathcal{E}_{\text{und}}$ and $v^0_{\max}$; if $v^0_{\max}$ changed, update $\alpha_i,\mathcal{D}^1,\mathcal{D}^2$ and redeploy. Termination when $\mathcal{E}=\varnothing$. No convergence/appropriateness proof of the event-triggered loop is given; each re-assignment is myopically optimal only.

## 6. Simulation and Experiments
- **Platform**: NOKOV optical motion-capture system, **24 cameras**, sub-millimeter position/velocity accuracy; each quadrotor carries a microcontroller (attitude stabilization) and a communication module; motion-capture data streamed to a central computer that computes strategies and issues commands; fixed-altitude flight; a captured quadrotor descends (p. 7, Fig. 7).
- **Scenario**: 2 pursuers vs 2 evaders, heterogeneous *sensing radii* only (speeds homogeneous within each team). Arena limited to **12 m × 5 m** in both software simulation and experiment; **target line length 2 m** (Fig. 8 shows $\mathcal{T}=\{(x,2)\,\mathrm{m}:|x|\le1\}$, $\Omega_{\text{tar}}$ above); "the evader aims to reach the target line in the shortest possible time". Boundary-critical cases (initial evader position on the barrier) deliberately excluded due to quadrotor control accuracy (p. 8).

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
**Author-stated**:
- Greedy, myopically-optimal assignment; total captured-evader count not guaranteed maximal because evader initial distribution is unknown and strong pursuers may be spent early (p. 7).
- Eq. (7) is only a "compromise optimality" for limited radii; incorporating probabilistic evader initial distributions or a bounded game space is deferred to future work (p. 6).
- Experiments exclude barrier-critical initial conditions due to quadrotor control accuracy; the 12 m × 5 m bounded arena contradicts the infinite-plane theory (p. 8).
- Cooperative capture among pursuers under partial information is out of scope (A1, p. 3) and left for future work (p. 8).
**Critical observations (with page/eq.)**:
1. **No game-theoretic solution concept is actually solved**: no HJI/Isaacs analysis, no saddle-point/equilibrium proof under the stated partial-information pattern; "optimality" is conditional geometry (Lemma 1's velocity condition; Theorem 2's constrained deployment). The asymmetric-information *value* of the game is never defined (Remark 1, p. 3, only names a value function).
2. **Proof gaps**: Lemma 1 inherits finite-time capture from [17, Corollary 2] without restated hypotheses and asserts the tangency characterization of $\theta_{\max}$ by figure only (p. 4); Theorem 1's normal-cone/collinearity supremum step is compressed (p. 6); Theorem 2 asserts monotonicity/convexity of an area that is never written down (p. 6).
3. **Detection-inevitability overclaim**: for a $\mathcal{D}^2$ pursuer the disk is *tangent* to its own conservative barrier (verified: tangent point $(0,1.5)$ for the experimental $P_2$), so most barrier points lie *outside* the disk (e.g., barrier point $\approx(0.5,1.47)$ is 1.09 m from $P_2$ vs $r_2=1$ m); an evader can therefore cross into $\mathcal{W}^{\mathcal{B}}_{E_m}$ undetected by that pursuer, contrary to the p. 5 claim that "the evader inevitably enters its sensing region in the process of reaching the evasion winning region". Team-level closure of these wedges (e.g., by the $\mathcal{D}^1$ pursuer) is used implicitly but never proven.
4. **Experimental inconsistency**: printed $x^{\text{opt}}_{P_1}=(0,2.5)$ m does not match the Theorem-2 $\mathcal{D}^1$ position $(l/2,0)\equiv(0,2)$ m in the experimental frame (Fig. 8: $\mathcal{T}$ from $(-1,2)$ to $(1,2)$); $x^{\text{opt}}_{P_2}$ matches exactly. Unexplained 0.5 m offset/likely typo (p. 8).
5. **Notational defects**: eq. (8) sums $w(\mathcal{L}\cup\mathcal{E}_{\text{und}})$ over edges; weight domain written $\mathcal{L}\cup\mathcal{E}_{\text{und}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}$ (p. 6); Lemma 2 says "Problem 1" but addresses Problem 2 (p. 6); eq. (4)'s $\mathrm{dist}(x,\mathcal{T})$ glosses $\mathcal{T}$ as "target region" (p. 3); "$\mathcal{R}_{E_j}$ intersects $\mathcal{T}$" feasibility is stated but its Apollonius-circle-vs-segment test is not formalized (p. 6).
6. **Structural assumptions**: centralized coordinator with full aggregation of local observations while "communication among pursuers is unavailable" (p. 2) — the architecture's failure modes and communication cost are not analyzed; A3 (arrival announcement by evaders) is an artificial cooperative assumption; deployment ignores the transient (pursuers travel to posts while evaders are already moving — no analysis of deployment-time detection).
7. **Scale of validation**: one 2-vs-2 scenario, homogeneous team speeds, no repeated trials, no baselines (e.g., vs. static assignment or [8], [9], [11]) — the dynamic-matching benefit (reservation of strong pursuers) is argued but not measured.

## 8. Citation Value
This paper occupies the intersection of three threads in the skill's literature: analytical-barrier multiplayer reach-avoid games (Yan et al. [8]; Yan et al. Automatica [11]; Chen–Zhou–Tomlin [9]), sensing-limited pursuit ([6], [7], [13]), and geometric target-guarding with one-Apollonius-circle strategies (Dorothy et al. [17]; Fu–Liu [16]; Lee–Bakolas [12], [18]). Its distinctive assets for citing are: (i) the **conservative barrier** construction — a radius-free, ratio-only barrier evaluated at the fastest *undetected* evader speed, which cleanly converts a partial-information deployment problem into a complete-information one; (ii) the **closed-form two-category deployment taxonomy** $\mathcal{D}^1/(\mathcal{D}^2$ with the tangency position $(l/2, r_i/(\alpha_i^{-1}-1))$) linking sensing radius to barrier coverage; (iii) the **$\alpha\ge\sqrt2$ speed-ratio threshold** at which optimal capture and sensing-region containment become compatible; and (iv) a **dynamic maximum-cardinality maximum-weight bipartite matching with reservation weights** $(1+\alpha_i^{-1})r_i^{-1}$ for weak-pursuer-first assignment, plus a rare hardware validation (NOKOV, quadrotors) of a partial-information defense pipeline. Cite it as: "Ma et al. decompose a heterogeneous multiplayer reach-avoid game under asymmetric sensing into deployment, dynamic bipartite-matching, and one-vs-one capture subproblems, deriving closed-form conservative-barrier deployment positions and a $\sqrt2$-speed-ratio capture strategy validated on a quadrotor testbed." Use it as the reference point for *deployment optimality under sensing constraints* and *repeated matching under asynchronous detection*; treat its optimality claims as conditional/geometric rather than game-theoretic (no HJI or equilibrium analysis), and note the greedy (non-globally-optimal) matching caveat when positioning globally-optimal or cooperative-capture alternatives.
