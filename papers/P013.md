## 2. Research Background
Cooperative capture in reach-avoid games has been studied extensively in 2-D, where differential-game and HJI-based methods dominate but scale poorly with the number of players (p. 1). In 3-D, most existing work assumes pursuers at least as fast as the evader (e.g., [8]–[10] in the paper's numbering: Garcia–Casbeer–Pacher RLAS 2020; Agasti–Reddy–Bhikkaji 2023; Yan et al. Automatica 2022), a regime where capture is always feasible (Remark 1, p. 3). The harder regime where the evader/intruder is *faster* but the pursuers/UAVs have numerical superiority (the "cops and robber" flavor, p. 1) has been treated mostly in 2-D via Apollonius circles (Isaacs [16]; Schwartz [17]; Rivera et al. TAC 2024 [18]; Davydov et al. L-CSS 2021 [19]). The few 3-D works on faster evaders — the 3-D fishing-game extension [21] (Fang–Cheng–Xie CCC 2020), the enclosing control of Chen–Duan JGCD 2024 [22], and the reachable-set-covering capture of Frommer et al. IFAC 2023 [23] — impose extra constraints (restricted movement area, escape required to pass between two pursuers, predetermined escape strategies, restricted initial distributions) that the authors argue deviate from practice (p. 2). This paper removes such constraints for a 3-D goal-protection scenario and reformulates the multiplayer game as a swarm-control problem.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
**Problem** (Section II, p. 2–3): N UAVs (set Ω) in the half-space S_game = {p ∈ R³ | [p]₃ > 0} must capture one intruder T before it enters a goal area S_goal ⊂ S_bdary = {p ∈ R³ | [p]₃ = 0}, both under single-integrator constant-speed dynamics (1): ṗ_i = v_i, v_i = V_i u_i with unit heading control u_i. **Assumption 1** (p. 3): V_i < V_T ∀i ∈ Ω, speed ratio λ_i = V_i/V_T < 1 — UAVs are strictly slower. **Definition 1**: capture when ∥p_i − p_T∥ ≤ r_i^c (predefined tolerance). **Assumption 2** (p. 3): (1) all UAVs start in S_game; (2) p_T(0) ∈ S_game and ∥p_T(0) − p_g∥ ≤ V_T t_f for all p_g ∈ S_goal (intruder can reach any goal point within the horizon t_f); (3) ∥p_i(0) − p_T(0)∥ > r_i^c and [p_i]₃ < [p_T]₃ (all UAVs start below the intruder); (4) UAV initial positions are distinct. **Winning conditions** (p. 3): the intruder wins if it reaches the goal area before capture; the UAV group wins if the intruder is captured by any UAV *or* fails to reach the goal area within the specified time t_f (both sides are fuel/time-limited).
**Gap** ([authors' claim], p. 2): engagement in 3-D space and UAVs slower than the intruder are "two key issues that have not yet been effectively solved"; prior 3-D faster-evader works need additional assumptions on escape routes or initial configurations. [Verified from cited-text framing]: the paper's own survey confirms 2-D coverage-control treatments of faster evaders ([18], [19]) and constrained 3-D treatments ([21]–[23]); the claim that the unconstrained 3-D case was open is consistent with the cited corpus, although the paper does not provide a formal impossibility or novelty proof — the gap statement is argumentative, not theorem-backed. The chosen remedy is to abandon game-theoretic (saddle-point/HJI) solution concepts entirely and recast the game as cooperative coverage + proximity + containment control with a CBF-based safety filter, i.e., it sacrifices optimality/guarantee for tractability (p. 2, p. 9 "Discussions").

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Geometry (theory)**: Extension of the 2-D Apollonius circle to the 3-D **Apollonius sphere** (Definition 2, eq. (2)–(5)) and a chain of geometric results: position-independent tangent cone angle (Lemma 1, eq. (6)); tangent-point locus centered on the UAV (Lemma 2, eq. (8)–(12)); closed-form implicit **and** parametric equations of the central projection of the sphere from the intruder onto the goal-plane, which is an **ellipse** defining the UAV's projected dominant area (Theorem 1, eq. (13)–(16)).
2. **Modeling (game → swarm)**: Reformulation of the N-vs-1 faster-intruder reach-avoid game into a swarm control problem: lower-altitude UAVs (z_i < z_T, capable of covering) cover the guarded area with their joint projected dominant ellipses via weighted Voronoi partitions (eq. (25)–(33)); higher-altitude UAVs cover a moving square around the intruder (eq. (39)–(40)); all UAVs approach (eq. (41)) and lower-altitude UAVs maintain an interception cone condition α_i ≤ θ_i (containment, eq. (42)), combined into a normalized nominal capture control law (eq. (43)).
3. **Algorithm (CBF-QCQP safety filter)**: A control barrier function h_i (eq. (47)) whose zero-superlevel set encodes *dominant-blind-zone prevention* (the invasion ray T→p_g pierces UAV i's Apollonius sphere, eq. (46)), integrated with the nominal law in a QCQP (eq. (49)) so that the optimized heading keeps the constraint forward invariant (by Lemma 3, quoted from [31]).
4. **Experiment (simulation)**: MATLAB simulations, 10 slower UAVs (22 m/s) vs one faster intruder (30 m/s) with a "smart" QCQP-driven intruder (eq. (50)); 100 random initial conditions yield 70% capture at N = 10 and ≈75% at N = 16 (Fig. 7).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results
**Dynamics and information structure.** All players: ṗ_i = v_i, v_i = V_i u_i, i ∈ Ω ∪ {T} (eq. (1)); u_i ∈ R³ is the control (unit heading enforced later by u_iᵀu_i = 1 in eq. (49)–(50)); speeds constant, V_i < V_T (Assumption 1). This is instantaneous-velocity control (single integrator); an author-stated limitation shared with [3], [4], [10], [21] (p. 9). Termination: capture (∃i: ∥p_i − p_T∥ ≤ r_i^c) or intruder arrival at S_goal, or timeout t_f. Information: each side's QCQP uses the opponent's current control input (u_T appears in eq. (49); u_i appears in eq. (50)) — an implicit full-information assumption, never listed as an assumption.

**Definition 2 (Apollonius sphere; p. 3, eq. (2)–(5)).** For UAV P at (x_P, y_P, z_P) and intruder T, λ = V_P/V_T < 1: any simultaneous-interception point Γ satisfies ∥Γ − P∥/∥Γ − T∥ = V_P·Δt/(V_T·Δt) = λ (eq. (2)). Squaring gives
(x_Γ − (x_P − λ²x_T)/(1 − λ²))² + (y_Γ − (y_P − λ²y_T)/(1 − λ²))² + (z_Γ − (z_P − λ²z_T)/(1 − λ²))² = λ²/(1 − λ²)² ∥P − T∥² (eq. (3), verified against rendered p. 3),
so Γ lies on the sphere {O, r} with
O = (1 − λ²)⁻¹(P − λ²T) (eq. (4)), r = (1 − λ²)⁻¹ λ ∥P − T∥ (eq. (5)).
For λ < 1 the UAV lies inside the sphere (∥P − O∥² = λ²r² < r²) and the intruder outside (∥T − O∥² = λ⁻²r² > r²) (p. 4). Physical meaning: the sphere is the boundary of points the UAV can contest; outside it the intruder arrives first. Attributed to [9], [22].

**Lemma 1 (p. 4, eq. (6)).** *Statement*: the angle θ between the line T→O (intruder to sphere center) and any tangent line from T to the Apollonius sphere satisfies sin θ = λ, hence θ depends only on the speed ratio, not on the players' positions. *Assumptions*: λ < 1, Definition 2 geometry. *Proof approach* (4 steps): (i) let F be a tangent point, ∥O − F∥ = r, OF ⊥ TF; (ii) compute ∥O − T∥ = ∥T − (P − λ²T)/(1 − λ²)∥ = ∥P − T∥/|1 − λ²| = r/λ (using eq. (4)–(5)); (iii) right triangle OTF gives sin θ = ∥O − F∥/∥O − T∥ = r/(r/λ); (iv) conclude position-independence. *Decisive equation*: sin θ = λ (eq. (6)). Local/analytic. Physical meaning: the UAV's **dominant area** is a circular cone with apex at the intruder, axis TO, half-angle θ = arcsin λ — a fixed-angular-width "capture shadow"; any intruder straight path entering this cone crosses the Apollonius sphere at a point the UAV can reach simultaneously, so capture is feasible if the UAV heads to that crossing point (p. 4, geometric assertion illustrated in Fig. 2; the sufficiency claim is argued from the Definition-2 geometry, not given as a formal proof).

**Definition 3 (Reachable set of the intruder; p. 4, eq. (7)).** S_r := ∪_{p_g ∈ S_goal} {p ∈ (S_game ∪ S_bdary) : ∥p − p_T(t₀)∥ + ∥p − p_g∥ ≤ (t_f − t₀) V_T} — all points from which the intruder can still visit and then reach some goal point within the horizon (an ellipsoid-like set bounded by dynamics and t_f). Used in simulation to delimit the guarded area S_lcov on the plane (p. 7).

**Definition 4 (Evasion space; p. 4).** With f_PT(p) = ∥p − P∥² − λ²∥p − T∥², S_T := {p ∈ S_game : f_PT(p) > 0}. Since (∥p − P∥/V_P)² > (∥p − T∥/V_T)² ⟺ f_PT(p) > 0, S_T is where the intruder arrives strictly earlier. ∂S_T = {f_PT = 0} is the Apollonius sphere; S_r ∩ S_T is where the intruder evades capture *and* still reaches the goal in time (p. 4). Analytic, exact for straight-line interception.

**Lemma 2 (p. 4–5, eq. (8)–(12)).** *Statement*: the locus of tangent points between the cone and the sphere is a circle centered on the UAV P (equivalently, the sphere's intersection with the plane through P perpendicular to OT); there is always a tangent point F with y_F = y_O and coordinates given in closed form:
x_F = x_P − [λϑ(z_T − z_P) + λ²(y_T − y_P)²(x_T − x_P)] / [(λ² − 1)((x_T − x_P)² + (z_T − z_P)²)],
y_F = (1 − λ²)⁻¹(y_P − λ² y_T),

**Theorem 1 (p. 5, eq. (13)–(16)).** *Statement*: the central projection of the Apollonius sphere from the intruder T onto the boundary plane S_bdary (z = 0) is an ellipse
Â x² + B̂ xy + Ĉ y² + D̂ x + Ê y + F̂ = 0 (eq. (13)),
with Â = (x_P − x_T)² + D, B̂ = 2(x_P − x_T)(y_P − y_T), Ĉ = (y_P − y_T)² + D, D̂ = 2(C(x_P − x_T) − D x_T), Ê = 2(C(y_P − y_T) − D y_T), F̂ = C² + D(x_T² + y_T² + z_T²), where C = x_T² + y_T² + z_T² − x_P x_T − y_P y_T − z_P z_T and D = (λ² − 1)∥P − T∥². The ellipse's interior is the **projected dominant area** of the UAV on the goal plane; its exterior is where the intruder reaches the plane uncaught. Parametric form (eq. (14)): ((x − x₀)cos χ + (y − y₀)sin χ)²/a² + (−(x − x₀)sin χ + (y − y₀)cos χ)²/b² = 1 with center (eq. (15)) x₀ = (B̂Ê − 2ĈD̂)/(4ÂĈ − B̂²), y₀ = (B̂D̂ − 2ÂÊ)/(4ÂĈ − B̂²); semi-axes (eq. (16)) a² = 2(Âx₀² + Ĉy₀² + B̂x₀y₀ − F̂)/(Â + Ĉ + √((Â − Ĉ)² + B̂²)), b² with the minus sign of the root; inclination χ = ½atan2(B̂, Â − Ĉ) if |Â| < |Ĉ|, else ½atan2(B̂, Â − Ĉ) + π/2. *Assumptions*: z_P < z_T (UAV below intruder; otherwise S_i^cp = ∅, p. 7). *Proof approach* (5 steps, p. 6): (i) take the representative tangent point F from eq. (8); (ii) parametrize the generatrix TF: M on TF satisfies x_M, z_M affine in y_M (eq. (17), (20)); (iii) impose TO ⊥ MG for G on the latitude circle of M (eq. (18)) and ∥M − T∥ = ∥G − T∥ (eq. (19)) to eliminate M and get the conical surface equation ((x − x_T)² + (y − y_T)² + (z − z_T)²)·D + (x(x_P − x_T) + y(y_P − y_T) + z(z_P − z_T) + C)² = 0 (eq. (22)); (iv) set z = 0 → D((x − x_T)² + (y − y_T)² + z_T²) + (x(x_P − x_T) + y(y_P − y_T) + C)² = 0 (eq. (23)); (v) expand eq. (23) and collect terms to obtain eq. (13) — the expansion is algebraically consistent term-by-term (checked: x²-coefficient D + (x_P − x_T)² = Â, x-coefficient −2D x_T + 2C(x_P − x_T) = D̂, constant D(x_T² + y_T² + z_T²) + C² = F̂, etc.); eq. (14)–(16) follow by the standard rotated-ellipse coordinate transformation. *Decisive equation*: eq. (22)–(23). Global/analytic. Physical meaning: the 3-D capture geometry collapses to a 2-D "footprint" ellipse on the protected plane, giving each UAV a computable guarding region; only the semi-minor axis b is reused as the Voronoi weight (p. 7). *Proof gap*: none fatal; but the printed eq. (12) typo (above) and the fact that D < 0 for λ < 1 (so the "ellipse" is real only when the cone actually meets the plane, i.e., z_P < z_T and the cone axis points downward enough) are handled only implicitly through the S_i^cp = ∅ condition.

**Eq. (24) (p. 7).** The projected center p_i^cp = [x_i⁰, y_i⁰]ᵀ is a function (ϑ, ζ) of (p_i, p_T) via eq. (13) and (15): p_i ↦ p_i^cp is the controlled map from UAV position to its footprint center on the plane.

**Eq. (25)–(26) (p. 7).** Guarded domain S_lcov (goal area extended to the intruder's reachable boundary, from Definition 3) is partitioned among lower-altitude covering UAVs Ω_low = {i : S_i^cp ≠ ∅, S_i^cp ∩ S_lcov ≠ ∅} by the weighted Voronoi diagram Π_i(p, t) = {p ∈ S_lcov : ∥p_i^cp − p∥/b_i^cp ≤ ∥p_j^cp − p∥/b_j^cp, ∀j ≠ i} (eq. (25)), weights b_i^cp being the semi-minor axes of the projected ellipses (guarding ability decays from the footprint center toward the cone surface, p. 7). Neighbors Ω_i = {j : Π_i ∩ Π_j ≠ ∅} (eq. (26)).

**Eq. (27)–(33) (p. 7–8).** Coverage cost H_c(p, t) = Σ_{i∈Ω_low} ∫_{Π_i(p,t)} ∥q − p_i^cp∥² φ(q, t) dq with density φ ≡ 1 (eq. (27)); optimal location satisfies ∂H_c/∂p_i^cp = 0 (eq. (28)); via the Leibniz integral rule (eq. (29)–(30)) and cancellation over shared Voronoi boundaries (n_i = −n_j on ∂Π_ij; static outer boundary kills ∂q/∂p_i^cp; ∥q − p_i^cp∥² = 0 on the remaining internal boundary — p. 8, three-case argument), ∂H_c/∂p_i^cp = 2∫_{Π_i} φ dq (p_i^cp − c_i) (eq. (31)) with mass centroid c_i = ∫_{Π_i} q φ dq / ∫_{Π_i} φ dq (eq. (32)); hence the centroid tracking law ṗ_i^cp = −β_i^cov (p_i^cp − c_i) (eq. (33)) minimizes H_c. Standard centroidal-Voronoi coverage machinery ([24]–[28]).

(25); the guarded-domain boundary treated as static. *Proof approach* (4 steps): (i) Lyapunov candidate H(p) = H_c(p, t) (eq. (36)); (ii) chain rule Ḣ = Σ_i (∂H_c/∂p_i^cp)ᵀ ṗ_i^cp (eq. (37)); (iii) combine eq. (33)–(35) and substitute eq. (30) to get Ḣ(p) = −2 Σ_{i∈Ω_low} (β_i^cov ∥p_i^cp − c_i∥² × ∫_{Π_i(p,t)} φ(q, t) dq) ≤ 0 (eq. (39)–(40) (p. 9).** For higher-altitude UAVs Ω_high = {i ∈ Ω : z_i ≥ z_T}, the guarded domain is a square S_hcov centered on the moving intruder with a predestinated side length (5 m in simulation, p. 12), partitioned by plain Voronoi on orthogonal projections p_i^op (eq. **Eq. (41)–(43) (p. 9).** Approaching component u_i^appr = −β_i^appr (p_i − p_T)/∥p_i − p_T∥ (eq. (41)) (drives the UAV toward the intruder). Containment component u_i^con = β_i^con [(p_i − p_T) × v_T]/∥p_i − p_T∥ × (p_i − p_T)/∥p_i − p_T∥ (eq. (42), both products are cross products, verified on rendered p. 9): using ((r × v) × r)/∥r∥² = (v∥r∥² − r(r·v))/∥r∥², i.e., the component of v_T perpendicular to r scaled — this rotates the relative vector r = p_i − p_T so that the included angle α_i between (p_i − p_T) and v_T decreases toward α_i ≤ θ_i, keeping the intruder's invasion direction inside UAV i's dominant cone. Nominal capture control law (eq. (43), verified against rendered p. 9): u_i^nom = (u_i^cov + u_i^appr + u_i^con)/∥u_i^cov + u_i^appr + u_i^con∥ for i ∈ Ω_low; (u_i^cov + u_i^appr)/∥·∥ for i ∈ Ω_high; (u_i^appr + u_i^con)/∥·∥ for i ∈ Ω\Ω_low\Ω_high (below intruder but without covering capability). The normalization to unit heading matches the speed-scaled dynamics (1). *Author-stated*: no rigorous proof of the combined law exists ("providing rigorous mathematical proof for the effectiveness of the combined control law is challenging", p. 9) because of the dynamic confrontation and nonlinear interactions from the linear combination.

**CBF preliminaries (p. 9–10, eq. (44)–(48)).** Definition 5 (valid CBF, eq. (45): sup_{u∈U} [L_f h + L_g h u] ≥ −κ(h(x)) ∀x ∈ D, κ locally Lipschitz extended class-K, per [30]); Definition 6 and Lemma 3 (quoted from [31]): if h is a valid CBF on D, any continuous u with L_f h + L_g h u ≥ −κ(h) renders C = {h ≥ 0} forward invariant.
**Dominant-blind-zone condition (eq. (46), p. 10)**: UAV i (z_i < z_T) has no dominant blind zone iff
r_i > ∥(p_T − O_i) × (p_T − p_g)∥/∥p_T − p_g∥, ∀i ∈ Ω, z_i < z_T,
i.e., the distance from the sphere center O_i to the intruder→goal ray is less than the sphere radius r_i, meaning the invasion ray pierces UAV i's Apollonius sphere and the intrusion direction stays capturable. **CBF (eq. (47))**: h_i(p_i, t) = r_i² − ∥(p_T − O_i) × (p_T − p_g)∥²/∥p_T − p_g∥², with zero-superlevel set C_i = {(p_i, t) ∈ R³ × R_{≥t₀} : h_i(p_i, t) ≥ 0} (eq. (48)). Note h_i depends on p_i through O_i = (1 − λ_i²)⁻¹(p_i − λ_i² p_T) and on p_T — it is a moving, state-coupled constraint. The "if and only if" status of eq. (46) is asserted geometrically (Fig. 4) without a formal lemma.

**QCQP safety filter (eq. (49), p. 10).** Rewriting (1) in control-affine form eq. (44), the paper states f = 0₃ₓ₁ and g = 1₃ₓ₁ (p. 10 — imprecise: with ṗ_i = V_i u_i the input matrix is V_i I₃; the speed factors are dropped in the constraint below). Then
u_i* = argmin_{u_i} (u_i − u_i^nom)ᵀ(u_i − u_i^nom) s.t. (∂h_i/∂p_i)ᵀ u_i + (∂h_i/∂p_T)ᵀ u_T ≥ −κ(h_i); u_iᵀu_i = 1 (eq. (49), verified against rendered p. 10).
Validity argument ("Valid of the proposed CBF", p. 10): from Lemma 3, if (49) always generates a u_i then (47) is a valid CBF; if ∂h_i/∂p_i ≠ 0₃ₓ₁ there exists u_i satisfying the constraint; ∂h_i/∂p_i = 0 and ∂h_i/∂p_T = 0 never happen except p_T = p_g; hence h_i is a valid CBF. **Proof gaps**: (i) feasibility of eq. (49) with the *unit-norm* constraint u_iᵀu_i = 1 is not proven — the attainable left-hand side is bounded by ∥∂h_i/∂p_i∥ + ∥∂h_i/∂p_T∥ (at best), so −κ(h_i) may be unreachable for large violations; (ii) the speeds V_i, V_T are missing from the Lie-derivative terms (dimensional inconsistency with dynamics (1)); (iii) u_T (the opponent's current control) is required; (iv) κ(·) is never specified; (v) the set D on which h_i must be a valid CBF is never specified, and forward invariance of C_i is claimed, not proven, because the argument is a feasibility assertion rather than a CBF-verification.

**Intruder model for simulation (eq. (50), p. 11, verified against render).** A "smart intruder" solves
u_T* = argmin_{u_T} (u_T − (p_T − p_g)/∥p_T − p_g∥)ᵀ(u_T − (p_T − p_g)/∥p_T − p_g∥)
s.t. (∂h_{i,T}/∂p_i)ᵀ u_i + (∂h_{i,T}/∂p_T)ᵀ u_T ≥ −κ(h_{i,T}) ∀i ∈ Ω; (∂h_{t,T}/∂p_T)ᵀ u_T ≥ −κ(h_{t,T}); u_Tᵀu_T = 1,
with h_{i,T} = ∥p_i − p_T∥ − 1.3 r_i^c (evade every UAV by a 1.3× capture-radius margin) and h_{t,T} = (t_f − t)²V_T² − ∥p_g − p_T∥² (guarantee arrival at p_g within t_f). This is a heuristic worst-case adversary, not a value-optimal escape policy; its first constraint also consumes the UAVs' controls u_i.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
**Author-stated** (p. 9, 12): (i) no rigorous mathematical proof for the combined nominal control law (43) — only components are individually justified (Theorem 2 covers the covering component alone); (ii) instantaneous-velocity-control (single-integrator) assumption, shared with [3], [4], [10], [21]; releasing it is "beyond the scope"; isochrone-based approaches [29] remain unsolved for faster intruders; (iii) the nominal law "does not guarantee the maintenance of covering the invasion path throughout the engagement", motivating the CBF layer, which itself only *tries* to prevent blind zones ("each UAV must restrict its control input to try to ensure...", p. 10).
**Critical observations** (with page/eq. anchors):
1. **No capture guarantee**: even with the CBF filter, capture probability is never characterized analytically; the winning condition "intruder fails to reach the goal within t_f" (p. 3) is what carries the 25–30% non-capture outcomes in Fig. 7 — the game outcome in those cases is UNREPORTED (timeout vs intrusion).
2. **CBF layer technical gaps** (p. 10, eq. (49)): speed factors V_i, V_T absent from the Lie-derivative constraint (inconsistent with (1) given f = 0, g = 1₃ₓ₁); QCQP feasibility under u_iᵀu_i = 1 unproven (the "exists u_i" argument ignores the unit-norm restriction and the magnitude of −κ(h_i)); κ unspecified; the opponent's control u_T must be known; set D never defined; forward invariance of C_i asserted via Lemma 3 without verifying Definition 5 on a specified D.
3. **Theorem 2 proof gaps** (p. 8, eq. (36)–(38)): LaSalle applied to a time-varying coverage functional (moving partitions, moving centroid, moving guarded domain); singularity of the inverse kinematics (35) (divisions by ϑ_{x_i} and (ϑ_{y_i}ζ_{x_i} − ϑ_{x_i}ζ_{y_i})) unanalyzed; ż_i = 0 means the covering component cannot manage altitude, so the Ω_low/Ω_high split can change discontinuously with no switching/chattering analysis.
4. **Information assumptions**: both QCQPs (49), (50) embed the opponent's current control input, and eq. (35) needs the intruder's velocity — full/persistent information is used throughout without being stated as an assumption; distributed/limited-sensing implementation is unaddressed (the weighted Voronoi partition eq. (25) also presumes each UAV knows all projected centers in Ω_low).
5. **Typographical defects**: eq. (12) prints (y − y_O)² twice instead of (z − z_O)² (p. 5, verified on render); "UVA" for "UAV" (p. 9, before eq. (42)); header placeholder "AUTHOR ET AL.: SHORT ARTICLE TITLE" and "VOL. XX, No. XX XXXXX 2022" remain from the early-access template.
6. **Scaling evidence is thin**: capture improves only from 70% to ~75% when N grows 10 → 16 (p. 12, Fig. 7); the claim "easily extended to larger-scale games" (p. 12) is not substantiated beyond this, and the QCQP (49) couples all UAVs through the shared p_T-dependent constraint set with no complexity analysis.
7. **Single-point goal area** in all simulations (p. 10); the multi-point/area goal case (the actual Definition-3 machinery with S_lcov) is exercised only through the partition, not through a genuinely extended goal set.
8. **Adversary model**: h_{i,T} = ∥p_i − p_T∥ − 1.3 r_i^c forces margin from *all* UAVs simultaneously; when UAVs encircle, (50) may be infeasible, and the paper does not specify the fallback behavior — so the reported 70–75% capture rates are conditional on this specific evasion parametrization.

## 8. Citation Value
This is the flagship "geometry + swarm-control" reference for the speed-disadvantaged 3-D goal-defense problem: it converts a multiplayer reach-avoid game with a faster intruder into centroidal-Voronoi coverage over intruder-referenced footprints, with the 3-D Apollonius-sphere → tangent-cone → central-projection-ellipse chain (Lemma 1–2, Theorem 1) as its load-bearing geometric contribution and a CBF-QCQP filter (eq. (46)–(49)) as its safety layer. In a paper's related-work section it plays the role of (a) the 3-D, faster-intruder, coverage-control-based cooperative capture baseline alongside 2-D coverage approaches [18], [19] and constrained 3-D fishing-game/reachable-set approaches [21], [23]; and (b) an example of game-to-swarm reformulation that trades saddle-point guarantees for scalability — best cited together with its admitted lack of a combined-law proof and capture guarantee. Suggested citation sentence: "Chen and Xia [P013] identify the dominant region of a speed-disadvantaged UAV in 3-D via the Apollonius sphere and its elliptical central projection onto the protected plane, and coordinate a UAV group through coverage, containment, and proximity components filtered by a dominant-blind-zone CBF-QCQP, capturing a 30%-faster intruder in 70–75% of simulated engagements; however, the combined strategy carries no formal capture guarantee and assumes instantaneous velocity control with full information."
