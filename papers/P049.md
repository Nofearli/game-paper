## 2. Research Background

Pursuit-evasion and target-defense games (Isaacs [1]) model adversarial motion planning in aerospace, military, and robotics settings, and target-guarding variants are now classified by target geometry: point [3], [4], line [5]–[7], circle [8], arbitrary convex set [9]–[12], perimeter-defense games with boundary-constrained defenders [13], [14], turret-defense [15], and active target defense with mobile/maneuverable targets [16]–[19] (all labels as printed, p. 1). The authors point out that comparatively little work treats *hybrid* problems carrying both pursuit-evasion and target-defense aspects — aerial reconnaissance and coast guarding — where the intruder's goal is not to attack the target but to reconnoiter its vicinity and then escape to a safe zone before being neutralized (p. 1). The reconnaissance game captures this dual goal: the intruder minimizes its distance to the target region *and* must enter a safe zone before capture, which "makes the game difficult to analyze with the classical differential game approach" (p. 1) because the payoff is a non-integral running-minimum and termination is a hard constraint. The lineage is the game-decomposition method introduced by Plante and Levis [20] (open-loop, point target, IEEE TAC 1972), later reused for multi-stage problems such as fixed-course target observation [21], engage-or-retreat [22], and capture-the-flag [23], [24].

## 3. Research Problem and Gap

**Problem.** In $\mathbb R^2$, an Intruder $I$ (speed 1) and a faster Defender $D$ (speed $\alpha>1$) play a zero-sum differential game (Section II). $I$ must reconnoiter the target region $\mathcal T=\{[x,y]^\top: y\ge l\}$, $l>0$ (eq. (6)) and then return to the retreat region $\mathcal R=\{[x,y]^\top: y\le0\}$ (eq. (7)) — two disjoint closed half-planes — while $D$ tries to capture it (zero capture radius). The paper treats the scenario in which **the Defender is not guaranteed to capture the Intruder before the latter reaches the retreat region**, i.e., the game state ends on the terminal manifold $\mathcal T_r=\{x\in\mathbb R^4: y_I\le0\}$ (eq. (8)); Defender capture is $\mathcal T_c=\{x\in\mathbb R^4\setminus\mathcal T_r: (x_I-x_D)^2+(y_I-y_D)^2=0\}$ (eq. (9)), so capture *exactly on* $\partial\mathcal R$ counts as an Intruder win (p. 2). Goals: (i) partition the state space into the agents' winning regions and characterize the barriers; (ii) find the Value function $V$ and state-feedback saddle strategies $\phi^\star,\psi^\star$ satisfying $J(\phi^\star,\psi)\le J(\phi^\star,\psi^\star)\le J(\phi,\psi^\star)$ (eq. (12)).

## 4. Core Contributions

1. **Theory**: (a) *Theorem 1* (p. 3, eqs. (14)–(19)): closed-form Value $V_{II}$ and feedback equilibrium of the Phase-II retreat game via a max-over-half-plane of the $\Delta$ function (adopted from [5], [10], [11]); (b) *Theorem 2* (p. 5, eqs. (53)–(56)): closed-form Value $V_I$ and state-feedback equilibrium strategies of the Phase-I approach game, **verified against the HJI equation** (eqs. (57)–(59)) — a step "absent in [20]" (p. 1).
2. **Modeling**: Formulation of the reconnaissance game with disjoint half-planar $\mathcal T$ and $\mathcal R$ (eqs. (1)–(12)); decomposition into Phase I (approach, payoff $J_I=l-y_I^s$, eq. (23)) and Phase II (retreat, terminal-separation payoff $J_{II}$, eq. (13)) based on the Intruder's myopic goal, with the coupling encoded in a parameterized terminal constraint $\Psi_I(x^s)=\Delta(x^s,p)-\delta=0$ (eq. (24)).
3. **Algorithm (analytic)**: Optimal selection of the two coupling parameters — the retreat point $p^\star=\left[\frac{\alpha^2x_I^0-x_D^0}{\alpha^2-1},\,0\right]^\top$ (orthogonal projection of the Apollonius-disk center onto $\mathcal R$, eq. (52), Remark 4) and $\delta^\star=0$ — obtained by convexity of the scalarized Value $F(p_x)$ (eqs. (49)–(51)); this makes the state-feedback strategies (55)–(56) computable in closed form (the $\arg\max_{p\in\mathcal R}$ in (19) has an analytic solution precisely because $\mathcal R$ is a half-plane).
4. **Experiment (numerical)**: Three scenarios S1–S3 (both optimal / nonoptimal Defender with pure pursuit / nonoptimal Intruder) whose Values order exactly as the saddle-point condition (12) requires (0.4311 < 0.4682 < 0.5120, Fig. 6), plus a level-set/barrier visualization (Fig. 2). ## 5. Key Mathematical Results

### 5.0 Model, information structure, cost, termination (Section II, pp. 2, eqs. (1)–(12))

- **Dynamics** (eqs. (1)–(5)): simple motion; $\dot x_I=\cos\phi,\ \dot y_I=\sin\phi$, $\phi\in[-\pi,\pi]$; $\dot x_D=\alpha\cos\psi,\ \dot y_D=\alpha\sin\psi$, $\psi\in[-\pi,\pi]$; $\alpha>1$ is the speed ratio (Defender faster). Game state $x=[x_I,y_I,x_D,y_D]^\top\in\mathbb R^4$, $f\in C^1$. Initial conditions: $[x_I^0,y_I^0]\in\mathbb R^2\setminus(\mathcal T\cup\mathcal R)$, $[x_D^0,y_D^0]\in\mathbb R^2\setminus\mathcal R$.
- **Regions**: $\mathcal T=\{y\ge l\}$ (6), $\mathcal R=\{y\le0\}$ (7), disjoint closed half-planes.
- **Termination / winning** (eqs. (8)–(9)): Intruder wins on $\mathcal T_r=\{y_I\le0\}$; Defender wins on $\mathcal T_c=\{x\in\mathbb R^4\setminus\mathcal T_r:\|x_I-x_D\|^2=0\}$ (zero capture radius); capture on $\partial\mathcal R$ = Intruder win (p. 2).
- **Payoff** (eq. (10)): $J=\min_{t\in[0,t_f]}\mathrm{dist}([x_I(t),y_I(t)]^\top,\mathcal T)$ with $t_f=\inf\{t\ge0: x\in\mathcal T_r\}$ — a non-integral (terminal-value-like) running minimum; **Value** (11): $V(x_0)=\min_{\phi(\cdot)}\max_{\psi(\cdot)}J$; saddle condition (12).
- **Information structure**: perfect state feedback throughout (strategies in (17)–(18) and (55)–(56) are functions of the current state $x$); the Phase-I derivation is open-loop (PMP) and then lifted to feedback. No noise/delay/partial observability.

### 5.1 Phase II: retreat game — Theorem 1 (p. 3, eqs. (13)–(22))

- **Theorem 1 (p. 3, eqs. (14)–(19))**. For the game with kinematics (1)–(4), terminal payoff $J_{II}=\sqrt{(x_I^{f}-x_D^{f})^2+(y_I^{f}-y_D^{f})^2}$ (13) and terminal manifold (8), define $\Delta:\mathbb R^4\times\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R$,
$$\Delta(\mathbf x,\boldsymbol{x})=\sqrt{(x-x_D)^2+(y-y_D)^2}-\alpha\sqrt{(x-x_I)^2+(y-y_I)^2},\quad \boldsymbol{x}=[x,y]^\top\in\mathbb R^2 \ (14)$$
(first argument = 4-D game state, second = candidate retreat point; the notation $\Delta(\mathbf x,\boldsymbol x)$ reuses the symbol $x$ for both). Then $V_{II}(\mathbf x)=\max_{p\in\mathcal R}\Delta(\mathbf x,p)$ (15) is the Value over the domain $\mathcal X_{II}=\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^4: V_{II}(\mathbf x)\ge0\}$ (16), with state-feedback equilibrium strategies
$$\phi^\star_{II}(\mathbf x)=\mathrm{atan2}(p_y^\star-y_I,\,p_x^\star-x_I)\ (17),\qquad \psi^\star_{II}(\mathbf x)=\mathrm{atan2}(p_y^\star-y_D,\,p_x^\star-x_D)\ (18),$$
where $p^\star=\arg\max_{p\in\mathcal R}\Delta(\mathbf x,p)$ (19) is the **optimal retreat point**. *Assumptions*: $\alpha>1$; zero capture radius; $p$ restricted to the retreat half-plane $\mathcal R$. *Proof approach*: "See [11]" (Lee & Bakolas, "Guarding a convex target set from an attacker in Euclidean spaces," *IEEE Contr. Syst. Lett.*, vol. 6, pp. 1706–1711, 2021) — **no proof in this paper**; declared a slight modification of [5], [10] (p. 3). *Physical meaning* (Remark 1, p. 3): $\Delta(\mathbf x,p)$ is the separation between the agents when $I$ reaches retreat point $p$, **assuming both fly straight lines** ($I$ at speed 1, $D$ at speed $\alpha$); $\Delta\ge0$ ⟺ $I$ arrives no later than $D$; $\mathcal X_{II}$ is exactly the set of states from which the Phase-II game is winnable by $I$ — if $x_0\in\mathbb R^4\setminus\mathcal X_{II}$, $D$ captures before $I$ reaches $\mathcal R$. Because $\mathcal R$ is a half-plane, $\partial\mathcal X_{II}$ has an analytic expression, deferred to [5] (Remark 1; expression **not reproduced in this paper**). *Extensibility*: the max-over-$p$ form works for any $\mathcal R$; half-planarity is what makes (19) analytic.
- **Apollonius disk** (p. 3, eqs. (20)–(22)): the Intruder's safe-reachable set $\mathcal A=\{\boldsymbol x\in\mathbb R^2:\Delta(\mathbf x,\boldsymbol x)\ge0\}$ (20) is a closed disk with
$$c=\left[\tfrac{\alpha^2x_I-x_D}{\alpha^2-1},\ \tfrac{\alpha^2y_I-y_D}{\alpha^2-1}\right]^\top\ (21),\qquad r=\tfrac{\alpha}{\alpha^2-1}\sqrt{(x_I-x_D)^2+(y_I-y_D)^2}\ (22).$$
(Independently re-derived and confirmed: expanding $\|p-x_D\|^2=\alpha^2\|p-x_I\|^2$ gives exactly (21)–(22).) This disk is the geometric engine of the whole paper.

### 5.2 Phase I: approach game — open-loop necessary conditions (Section IV-A–B, pp. 3–5, eqs. (23)–(52))

- **Phase-I payoff and terminal constraint** (pp. 3, eqs. (23)–(24)): since $\mathcal T$ is a half-plane, $J_I=\Phi_I(x^s)=l-y_I^s$ (23) with $x^s\in\mathcal X_{II}$ the state at the switch time $t_s$ (negative $J_I$ allowed "without loss of generality" = Intruder penetrates into $\mathcal T$); safe-retreat terminal constraint
$$\Psi_I(x^s)=\Delta(x^s,p)-\delta=0\ (24),$$
with $p=[p_x,p_y]^\top\in\mathcal R$ a retreat point and $\delta\ge0$ the agent separation at the end of Phase II.
- **Remark 2 (p. 3) — the key relaxation**: to embed Phase II's solution, $p$ and $\delta$ *should* be functions of $x$, but "this approach is intractable as these two variables admit no closed-form expressions"; hence they are treated as **parameters independent of $x$**, an open-loop solution is developed for arbitrary $(p,\delta)$, and the pair consistent with Phase II's equilibrium is then selected. This is the paper's central methodological device (and its main theoretical soft spot; see Section 7).
- **PMP conditions** (p. 3–4): Hamiltonian $H=\lambda_{x_I}\cos\phi+\lambda_{y_I}\sin\phi+\alpha(\lambda_{x_D}\cos\psi+\lambda_{y_D}\sin\psi)$ (25). Minimization over $\phi$ / maximization over $\psi$ (eqs. (26)–(27)): $\cos\phi_I^\ast=-\lambda_{x_I}/\|\lambda_I\|$, $\sin\phi_I^\ast=-\lambda_{y_I}/\|\lambda_I\|$; $\cos\psi_I^\ast=\lambda_{x_D}/\|\lambda_D\|$, $\sin\psi_I^\ast=\lambda_{y_D}/\|\lambda_D\|$. Costates are **constant** because $\partial H/\partial x\equiv0$ (eqs. (28)–(29)). Transversality with multiplier $\nu\in\mathbb R$ (eqs. (30)–(33)): $\lambda_{x_I}(t_s)=\alpha\nu\cos\theta_I^s$, $\lambda_{y_I}(t_s)=-1+\alpha\nu\sin\theta_I^s$, $\lambda_{x_D}(t_s)=-\nu\cos\theta_D^s$, $\lambda_{y_D}(t_s)=-\nu\sin\theta_D^s$, where $\theta_I^s=\mathrm{atan2}(p_y-y_I^s,\,p_x-x_I^s)$ (34), $\theta_D^s=\mathrm{atan2}(p_y-y_D^s,\,p_x-x_D^s)$ (35) — the bearings from each agent's switch position to the retreat point.
- **Decisive step — free-terminal-time condition** (eq. (36)): $H^\ast(t_s)=-\sqrt{\alpha^2\nu^2-2\alpha\nu\sin\theta_I^s+1}+\alpha|\nu|=0\ \Rightarrow\ \nu=\frac{\csc\theta_I^s}{2\alpha}$. (Verified: $\|\lambda_I(t_s)\|^2=\alpha^2\nu^2-2\alpha\nu\sin\theta_I^s+1$, and $H^\ast=0$ forces $\|\lambda_I(t_s)\|=\alpha|\nu|=\|\lambda_D(t_s)\|$·$\alpha$, giving $\nu=1/(2\alpha\sin\theta_I^s)$; requires $\sin\theta_I^s\neq0$.) Substituting back (eq. (37)): $\lambda_{x_I}=\tfrac12\cot\theta_I^s$, $\lambda_{y_I}=-\tfrac12$, whence the equilibrium headings in Phase I are **constant** (eqs. (38)–(39)): $\cos\phi_I^\ast=\cos\theta_I^s,\ \sin\phi_I^\ast=-\sin\theta_I^s$; $\cos\psi_I^\ast=\cos\theta_D^s,\ \sin\psi_I^\ast=\sin\theta_D^s$. So the Intruder flies a straight line *upward* at mirror angle $\theta_I^s$ and the Defender a straight line *toward the retreat point*.
- **Remark 3 (p. 4)**: if $p=p^\star$ from (19), the Defender's Phase-I and Phase-II equilibrium headings coincide (eqs. (18) and (39)) — **the Defender's entire trajectory is one straight line** from initial to terminal position. This is the property exploited in simulation S1.
- **Optimal parameter selection** (Section IV-B, p. 4, eqs. (40)–(52)): with $X_I^0=p_x-x_I^0$, $Y_I^0=p_y-y_I^0$ (40) and $\eta_D^0=\sqrt{(p_x-x_D^0)^2+(p_y-y_D^0)^2}$ (41), and $p$ satisfying (19) at $t_s$, the final time is $t_f=\frac{\eta_D^0-\delta}{\alpha}$ (42) with $\delta\in[0,\eta_D^0]$. Matching displacements (both agents' headings constant per phase, $I$'s $y$-velocity sign flipping at $t_s$): $X_I^0=t_f\cos\theta_I^s$ (43); $Y_I^0=-t_s\sin\theta_I^s+(t_f-t_s)\sin\theta_I^s\Rightarrow\sin\theta_I^s=\frac{Y_I^0}{t_f-2t_s}$ (44); $\sin^2+\cos^2=1$ gives $t_s=\frac{t_f}{2}\Big(1\pm\frac{Y_I^0}{\sqrt{t_f^2-(X_I^0)^2}}\Big)$ (45); since $y_I>0$ during play forces $\sin\theta_I^s<0$, the plus branch is taken (46) and $\sin\theta_I^s=-\sqrt{1-\big(\tfrac{X_I^0}{t_f}\big)^2}$ (47). The Value (48):
$$V_I(x_0)=l-y_I^0-\tfrac12\Big(\sqrt{t_f^2-(X_I^0)^2}+Y_I^0\Big).$$
*Optimal pair*: $p_y^\star=0$ ($p^\star$ on $\partial\mathcal R$ by (7)); maximizing $t_f$ forces $\delta^\star=0$ — both agents arrive at $p^\star$ **simultaneously**, which is still an Intruder win by the $\partial\mathcal R$-capture convention of Section II (p. 4). Scalarizing in $p_x$ (eq. (49)): $F(p_x)=l-\tfrac12\big(y_I^0+\Gamma^{1/2}\big)$ with $\Gamma=\frac{1}{\alpha^2}(p_x-x_D^0)^2-(p_x-x_I^0)^2+\frac{1}{\alpha^2}(y_D^0)^2$ (a concave quadratic in $p_x$, so $\{\Gamma\ge0\}$ is a compact interval — asserted "turns out to be compact", p. 4), with derivatives
$$\frac{\partial F}{\partial p_x}=-\frac{1}{2\Gamma^{1/2}}\Big(\tfrac{1-\alpha^2}{\alpha^2}p_x+x_I^0-\tfrac{1}{\alpha^2}x_D^0\Big)\ (50),\qquad \frac{\partial^2F}{\partial p_x^2}=\frac{1}{2\Gamma^{3/2}}\Big(\tfrac{1-\alpha^2}{\alpha^2}p_x+x_I^0-\tfrac{1}{\alpha^2}x_D^0\Big)^2+\frac{\alpha^2-1}{2\alpha^2\Gamma^{1/2}}\ (51).$$
Since (51) $\ge0$ (both terms nonnegative for $\alpha>1$, $\Gamma>0$), **$F$ is convex** and the unique minimizer is $p_x^\star=\frac{\alpha^2x_I^0-x_D^0}{\alpha^2-1}$. Summary (52): $p^\star=\left[\frac{\alpha^2x_I^0-x_D^0}{\alpha^2-1},\,0\right]^\top$, $\delta^\star=0$.
- **Remark 4 (p. 5)**: $p^\star$ (52) is the **orthogonal projection of the Apollonius-disk center** $c$ (21) of $\mathcal A^0=\{\boldsymbol x:\Delta(x_0,\boldsymbol x)\ge0\}$ onto $\mathcal R$ — i.e., the optimal retreat point is the foot of the vertical from the Apollonius center to the line $y=0$. Geometrically clean and immediately generalizable in spirit (projection of $c$ onto any convex $\mathcal R$), though only the half-plane is proven here.
- **Proof gap (author-stated, p. 5)**: "Due to space limitations, the rigorous proof for showing that $p^\star$ satisfies (19) is left for future work. The sketch of this proof is that the boundary of $\mathcal R$ is tangent to the disk $\mathcal A^s=\{\boldsymbol x:\Delta(x^s,\boldsymbol x)\ge0\}$ at $p^\star$, that is, $\Delta(x^s,p^\star)=0$ and $\Delta(x^s,p)<0$ for all $p\in\mathcal R\setminus\{p^\star\}$." So the claim $\arg\max_{p\in\mathcal R}\Delta=p^\star$ (tangency of the switch-time Apollonius disk with $\partial\mathcal R$ at $p^\star$) is **sketched, not proven**.

### 5.3 Phase I: state-feedback solution — Theorem 2 (p. 5, eqs. (53)–(59))

- **Theorem 2 (p. 5, eqs. (53)–(56))**. Consider $V_I:\mathcal X_{II}\to\mathbb R$,
$$V_I(\mathbf x)=l-\frac12\left(y_I+\sqrt{\frac{(x_I-x_D)^2}{\alpha^2-1}+\frac{y_D^2}{\alpha^2}}\right)\ (53).$$
Then $V_I$ is the Value of the game with kinematics (1)–(4), payoff (23), terminal manifold (8), over the domain $\mathcal X_I=\{\mathbf x\in\mathcal X_{II}: V_I(\mathbf x)\ge0\}$ (54), with state-feedback equilibrium strategies
$$\phi_I^\star(\mathbf x)=\mathrm{atan2}\big(\sqrt{1-\Lambda^2},\,\Lambda\big)\ (55),\qquad \psi_I^\star(\mathbf x)=\mathrm{atan2}\big(-\sqrt{1-\alpha^2\Lambda^2},\,\alpha\Lambda\big)\ (56),\qquad \Lambda=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha^2+\frac{(\alpha^2-1)^2}{\alpha^2}\frac{y_D^2}{(x_I-x_D)^2}}}.$$
*Assumptions*: $x\in\mathcal X_I\subset\mathcal X_{II}$; $\alpha>1$; $x_I\neq x_D$ (else $\Lambda$ undefined — collocation is capture anyway); $1-\alpha^2\Lambda^2\ge0$ always holds since $\alpha^2\Lambda^2=\frac{\alpha^4(x_I-x_D)^2}{\alpha^4(x_I-x_D)^2+(\alpha^2-1)^2y_D^2}\le1$. *Proof approach*: (53) is (49) with (52) substituted and the current state read as the initial condition; (55)–(56) are (38)–(39) with (43), (47) substituted (the paper's wording, p. 5). **Verification**: the gradient (57)
$$\frac{\partial V_I}{\partial x}=-\frac12\Big[\frac{\Lambda}{\sqrt{1-\Lambda^2}},\ 1,\ -\frac{\Lambda}{\sqrt{1-\Lambda^2}},\ \frac1\alpha\sqrt{\frac{1-\alpha^2\Lambda^2}{1-\Lambda^2}}\Big]^\top$$
is inserted into the stationary HJI equation $0=\frac{\partial V_I}{\partial t}+\min_\phi\max_\psi\{\frac{\partial V_I}{\partial x}\cdot f(x,\phi,\psi)\}$ (58, citing Isaacs [1]); at the equilibrium controls the velocity vector is $(\Lambda,\sqrt{1-\Lambda^2},\alpha^2\Lambda,-\alpha\sqrt{1-\alpha^2\Lambda^2})$ and the inner product (59) **vanishes identically** (the $\Lambda^2$, $1-\Lambda^2$ and $1-\alpha^2\Lambda^2$ terms cancel exactly). Since $V_I$ is $C^1$ and satisfies the HJI equation over $\mathcal X_I$, it is the Value (p. 5). *Analytic, global on $\mathcal X_I$*; no viscosity/boundary treatment at $\mathcal B_I$.
- **Independent verification (this archive)**: with $\Lambda^2=\frac{\alpha^2(x_I-x_D)^2}{\alpha^4(x_I-x_D)^2+(\alpha^2-1)^2y_D^2}$ one gets $\Lambda=\cos\theta_I^s$ and $\alpha\Lambda=\cos\theta_D^s$ at feedback level, so (55)–(56) are exactly (38)–(39) evaluated at the optimal retreat point (52) — the open-loop-to-feedback lifting is internally consistent; and the derivative chain (53)→(57) reproduces (57) exactly.
- **Physical meaning**: the Intruder climbs at constant bearing-mirrored heading until the switch, then dives on $p^\star$; the Defender flies one straight line to $p^\star$; the guaranteed closest-approach distance to $\mathcal T$ is $l-\frac12\big(y_I+\sqrt{\frac{(x_I-x_D)^2}{\alpha^2-1}+\frac{y_D^2}{\alpha^2}}\big)$ — note it **degrades with $y_D$** (a Defender lurking just below $\mathcal T$ hurts most) and improves with horizontal offset $|x_I-x_D|$.

### 5.4 Level sets, barriers, and winning-region partition (Section IV-D, p. 5, eqs. (60)–(63))

For a fixed Defender position $[x_D^0,y_D^0]=[0,0.6]^\top$, $\alpha=1.1$, $l=1$ (p. 5), the planar partition of the Intruder's position space is
$$\bar{\mathcal X}_I=\{[x,y]\in\bar{\mathcal X}_{II}: V_I([x,y,x_D^0,y_D^0])\ge0\}\ (60),\quad \bar{\mathcal X}_{II}=\{[x,y]\in\mathbb R^2: V_{II}([x,y,x_D^0,y_D^0])\ge0\}\ (61),$$
$$\bar{\mathcal B}_I=\{[x,y]\in\bar{\mathcal X}_{II}: V_I([x,y,x_D^0,y_D^0])=0\}\ (62),\quad \bar{\mathcal B}_{II}=\{[x,y]\in\mathbb R^2: V_{II}([x,y,x_D^0,y_D^0])=0\}\ (63).$$
$\bar{\mathcal B}_I,\bar{\mathcal B}_{II}$ are the **barriers** demarcating the winning regions (citing Isaacs [1]). Reading of Fig. 2 (p. 5): gray region $\bar{\mathcal X}_{II}^c=\mathbb R^2\setminus\bar{\mathcal X}_{II}$ above $\bar{\mathcal B}_{II}$ = Defender's winning region (it captures $I$ before $\mathcal R$ using, e.g., the strategies of [10]); $\bar{\mathcal X}_I$ (inside $\bar{\mathcal B}_I$, colored level sets of $V_I$, colorbar 0–0.7) = Intruder's winning region — **guaranteed safe reconnaissance of $\mathcal T$ and return to $\mathcal R$**; the region $\bar{\mathcal X}_{II}\setminus\bar{\mathcal X}_I$ (wings beyond $\bar{\mathcal B}_I$) is where $V_I<0$: the Intruder returns safely but cannot reach $\mathcal T$, and "the game may have non-unique solutions" there (p. 5). Geometrically, $\bar{\mathcal B}_I=0$ is the conic $(x_I-x_D)^2=(\alpha^2-1)\big[(2l-y_I)^2-y_D^2/\alpha^2\big]$ (from (53)), appearing as near-straight rays over the plotted window, while $\bar{\mathcal B}_{II}$ is the half-plane retreat barrier of [5] with a cusp under the Defender marker.

**Summary of the game-theoretic structure**: this is a *penetrate–observe–return* game solved as a Game of Kind (barriers (62)–(63)) plus two Games of Degree (Theorems 1 and 2); the two phases are coupled one-way — Phase I's terminal constraint (24) imports Phase II's value landscape through $(p,\delta)$, and Phase II never looks back.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues

**Author-stated:**
1. **Rigorous optimality of $p^\star$ left open** (p. 5): "the rigorous proof for showing that $p^\star$ satisfies (19) is left for future work"; only the tangency sketch ($\Delta(x^s,p^\star)=0$, $\Delta(x^s,p)<0$ on $\mathcal R\setminus\{p^\star\}$) is given — the linchpin connecting Phase I's convex optimization to Phase II's $\arg\max$ is unproven.
2. **The $(p,\delta)$ relaxation** (Remark 2, p. 3): $p,\delta$ should be state-dependent but "admit no closed-form expressions"; treating them as free parameters and selecting them afterward is a heuristic closure of the two-phase coupling, albeit one that happens to be HJI-consistent (Theorem 2).
3. **Chattering under nonoptimal play** (p. 5, S2): the phase-switch rule is myopic; under a pure-pursuing Defender the trajectory alternates between phases. No switching-surface regularization, no analysis of the chattering cost.
4. Domain restriction: the analysis covers only $\mathcal X_{II}$ (the "Defender not guaranteed to capture" scenario, Abstract/p. 2); the complementary case is outsourced to [10], [5].

**Critical observations (this archive):**
5. Theorem 1 is **wholly imported** ("Proof. See [11]", p. 3): the paper's proof burden for the retreat phase rests on [11]'s convex-target result, and the analytic expression of $\partial\mathcal X_{II}$ is deferred to [5] and never displayed; $V_{II}$ is kept "in general form" (Remark 1), so the barrier $\bar{\mathcal B}_{II}$ of Fig. 2 cannot be reproduced from this paper alone.
6. The HJI verification (58)–(59) is **interior-only**: $V_I$ is $C^1$ on $\mathcal X_I$ but no viscosity-solution argument is given at the barrier $\bar{\mathcal B}_I$ (where $V_I=0$, the domain boundary), at $\partial\mathcal X_{II}$, or on the non-unique-solution region $\bar{\mathcal X}_{II}\setminus\bar{\mathcal X}_I$ (admitted, p. 5); "satisfies the HJI equation over $\mathcal X_I$, therefore it is the Value" is a verification-style argument, not a uniqueness proof.
7. **$\delta^\star=0$ knife-edge** (p. 4): the optimal plan drives both agents to $p^\star$ *simultaneously*; the Intruder's win then hinges entirely on the convention that capture on $\partial\mathcal R$ is an Intruder win (p. 2, below eq. (9)). With any positive capture radius or a strict-interior terminal set, the optimal $\delta$ trade-off would change discontinuously.
8. Degenerate cases are not handled: eq. (36) requires $\sin\theta_I^s\neq0$ (retreat point due east/west of the switch position); $\Lambda$ (Theorem 2) is undefined at $x_I=x_D$; $\{\Gamma\ge0\}$ compactness is asserted, not shown (p. 4) — it follows because $\Gamma$ is concave in $p_x$, but the empty/nonempty boundary cases are not discussed.
9. Model idealizations: zero capture radius, perfect information, constant speed ratio, static half-planes; the Intruder's "observe" task is proxyed purely by distance to $\mathcal T$ (no sensing/dwell-time modeling, payoff (10)).
10. Simulation weaknesses: single scenario, no Monte-Carlo, no platform/solver disclosure, and no numerical cross-check against [20].

## 8. Citation Value

Role in a future paper: this is the **canonical modern reference for the reconnaissance game as a decomposed two-phase reach-observe-return differential game with geometric (half-planar) barriers** — the natural citation when a paper needs (i) the penetrate–observe–return problem statement with disjoint target/retreat half-planes, (ii) the "retreat point = projection of the Apollonius center" device, or (iii) an example of closed-form Value + HJI verification for a multi-stage target-guarding game, positioned between Plante–Levis' 1972 open-loop point-target original and the capture-the-flag / engage-or-retreat family. Ready-to-adapt citation sentences: *"Lee and Bakolas [P049] solved the two-player reconnaissance game with disjoint half-planar target and retreat regions in closed form by decomposing it into an approach phase and a retreat phase, deriving state-feedback equilibrium strategies and verifying them via the Hamilton–Jacobi–Isaacs equation, and characterizing the barriers that partition the state space into the agents' winning regions."* Alternatively, for the geometric contribution: *"…showed that the optimal retreat point is the orthogonal projection of the Apollonius-circle center onto the retreat half-plane, yielding an analytic Game of Degree for the intruder's guaranteed closest approach to the target."* Caveat when citing: the optimality proof of the retreat point is acknowledged as future work by the authors themselves, and the retreat-phase Value is imported from [5], [10], [11] rather than re-derived.
