## 2. Research Background

Active target defense concerns a three-agent engagement: an Attacker missile (A) pursues a Target aircraft (T), while a Defender missile (D) escorts T by intercepting A before A reaches T. Prior treatments (Garcia et al. [7], [8], [18]) assumed A flies a *known* guidance law (pure pursuit or proportional navigation), reducing the problem to a one-sided optimal-control problem for the T–D team; Li and Cruz [12] used an LQ game formulation for asset defense; Fisac and Sastry [13] decomposed the pursuit–evasion–defense game into smaller two-player games; Chen, Zhou and Tomlin [14] solved multiplayer reach-avoid via pairwise outcomes. The active target defense differential game (ATDDG) as a genuine zero-sum *differential* game with an adversarial, unconstrained Attacker was introduced by Pachter, Garcia and Casbeer at Allerton 2014 [17] and analyzed in [18], both in a reduced three-dimensional state space. This paper is the journal-length, rigorous treatment: the full solution is carried out in the original six-dimensional fixed Cartesian frame, with closed-loop state-feedback saddle-point strategies, a closed-form Value function, and a proof that the solution is the *complete* solution of the game (regular solution + Game of Kind). The authors position the work within Isaacs' program [23] and note that very few pursuit-evasion differential games with state dimension greater than two have ever been solved in closed form.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap

**Problem.** Solve the zero-sum differential game in which A chooses its heading χ to minimize, and the cooperating T–D team chooses headings (φ, ψ) to maximize, the Target–Attacker separation at the instant D intercepts A (Mayer payoff), over the region of the state space where T survives (the T/D "winning region" R_e).

**Gap, as stated by the authors:**
- [Authors' claim, p. 554]: the ATDDG of [17], [18] was studied in a reduced state space of dimension three, "however, formal proof was not provided in order to conclude that the proposed solution is in fact the complete solution of the differential game." (Claim about their own prior conference/journal work; consistent with the works cited but not independently verified from this PDF.)
- [Authors' claim, p. 554]: possible nondifferentiability of the HJI PDE solution is a central concern in differential games (viscosity solutions [19], [20] handle equivocal surfaces); here, by contrast, "it is shown that no singular surfaces exist, and the Value function is C1", which [14] calls the ideal situation "if it is attainable." **Verified within this paper**: the C1 property is proved constructively via the envelope argument (64)–(67) and the continuity/gradient-matching argument on p. 564; no singular surfaces (equivocal/switch/envelope) are exhibited because a single regular solution covers the entire winning region.
- [Authors' claim, p. 554]: working in the original 6-D frame avoids the change of coordinates into the reduced 3-D space and the inverse transformation needed to implement strategies in the fixed frame; "more importantly, we offer a formal synthesis of the saddle point strategies" and the Game of Kind solution. (The paper notes "reduced state space" means an exactly equivalent, fully nonlinear 3-D representation, not an approximation.)
- New results w.r.t. [17] listed on p. 554: (i) formal synthesis of the regular solution and proof that it covers the whole Target-survival region; (ii) optimal Target heading for the degenerate case where T lies on the orthogonal bisector of AD (DT = AT), where the generic formula degenerates; (iii) complete solution of the Game of Kind (escape-set characterization).

**Gap in the literature context** (verified from the reference list contents): [12] is a linear-quadratic formulation (bounded-role asset defense), [13] splits the game into two smaller games for computational tractability, [14] uses single-attacker–single-defender pairwise outcomes for a multi-attacker/multi-defender game in a bounded domain; none gives a closed-form, verified saddle-point solution of the three-agent ATDDG with a free (adversarial) Attacker strategy in the original frame.

## 4. Core Contributions

1. **Theory — complete regular solution of the ATDDG Game of Degree in the 6-D Cartesian state space** (Theorem 1 for DT ≤ AT, Theorem 2 for DT > AT, x ∈ R_e): closed-form state-feedback saddle-point headings for all three agents, determined by a single scalar — the optimal interception point I* = (x*, y*) on the orthogonal bisector of AD, whose abscissa x* is the (real) root of an explicit quartic equation (15) parameterized by the speed ratio α.
2. **Theory — closed-form Value function** (17)/(74) (piecewise V_s/V_g, continuous and C1 over the escape region), proved to be the classical (C1) solution of the stationary HJI equation everywhere in the T/D winning region (67); explicit gradient (66). No singular surfaces.
3. **Theory — degenerate-case Target heading** (Corollary 1 + Definition 2 "dual problem"): when DT = AT the Target sits on the bisector and the generic heading rule (11)/(73) is undefined (x* = x_T, y* = y_T); the optimal heading is selected between the two feasible roots (60)–(61)/(68) as the one steering T into D's reachable side, decided by κ = argmin Δ_i (70).
4. **Theory — Game of Kind / barrier** (Theorem 3): the escape set R_e in the region DT > AT is characterized exactly by the Attacker's-side branch of a hyperbola (82)–(83) centered at the midpoint of AD, with principal axes parallel/perpendicular to AD; derived via a tangency (critical Apollonius circle) construction (84)–(92).
5. **Existence/well-posedness**: proof that the design quartic (15) always has at least two real roots in the relevant configuration (p. 564, using (80)), plus a computable alternative quartic in y (71)–(72) to cover degenerate slopes (m = 0 or m′ = 0). Closed-form solvability of the quartic makes the feedback law real-time implementable.
6. **Modeling**: precise two-target formulation of the ATDDG — termination set C = C1 ∪ C2 (2), Type-P order of preference (Definition 1), perfect-information pattern with no discriminatory/stroboscopic strategies needed (p. 555).

No hardware or numerical-simulation contribution exists (see Section 6).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results (exhaustive)

### 5.0 Dynamics, information structure, cost, termination

- **Dynamics (p. 554, eq. (1))**: Isaacs' "simple motion" in the Euclidean plane, six-dimensional state x = (x_T, y_T, x_A, y_A, x_D, y_D) ∈ R^6, game set = entire R^6:
  - ẋ_T = α cos φ, ẏ_T = α sin φ; ẋ_A = cos χ, ẏ_A = sin χ; ẋ_D = cos ψ, ẏ_D = sin ψ, with initial conditions.
  - Speeds: V_A = V_D (equal Attacker/Defender capability), normalized V_A = 1; speed ratio α = V_T/V_A < 1 (Target slower); controls are headings χ, φ, ψ ∈ [−π, π]; state and controls unconstrained. Fixed Cartesian frame deliberately retained (no nonlinear reduced dynamics).
- **Termination set (p. 554–555, eq. (2))**: C = C1 ∪ C2, C1 = {x : (x_A−x_D)² + (y_A−y_D)² = 0} (D intercepts A; T escapes), C2 = {x : (x_A−x_T)² + (y_A−y_T)² = 0} (A captures T). Point capture. Conceptually a two-target differential game [24]–[26].
- **Order of preference (Definition 1, p. 555)**: Type P: m_t ≻_P n_t ≻_P Υ and Υ ≻_E n_t ≻_E m_t — nontermination Υ is worst for A (it cannot win by running: D follows A while T increases the A–T separation) and best for the T/D team; the game terminates only on penetrating contact. This rules out nonterminating play and justifies no discriminatory/stroboscopic strategies.
- **Game of Degree terminal manifold (p. 555, eq. (3))**: x_A = x_D, y_A = y_D — a 4-D hyperplane in R^6 (matrix form (7)). Free terminal time t_f.
- **Payoff (eqs. (4)–(5))**: Mayer/terminal-cost only: J = Φ(x_f) = √((x_Af − x_Tf)² + (y_Af − y_Tf)²); Value V(x_0) = min_{u_A(·)} max_{u_B(·)} J (eq. (6)) subject to (1), (3). Perfect information, causal state-feedback strategies.
- If the initial state lies where optimal play ends in A capturing T (region R_c), the Game of Degree analyzed here "is not played"; a different Game of Degree (A capturing T unmolested) exists there but is explicitly excluded (p. 555).

### 5.1 Hamiltonian and Isaacs' condition (p. 556, eq. (8))

H = λ_xA cos χ + λ_yA sin χ + λ_xD cos ψ + λ_yD sin ψ + αλ_xT cos φ + αλ_yT sin φ.
The Hamiltonian and dynamics are separable (decoupled) in the controls, hence min_χ max_{φ,ψ} H = max_{φ,ψ} min_χ H — **Isaacs' condition holds** (saddle-point in controls, no ambiguity). Costate order used: λ = (λ_xA, λ_yA, λ_xD, λ_yD, λ_xT, λ_yT).

### 5.2 Theorem 1 (pp. 556–561; case DT ≤ AT)

**Statement** (p. 556). Consider the ATDDG (1)–(7) with state such that DT ≤ AT (Target no farther from Defender than from Attacker; then "the Target is guaranteed to be able to escape for any speed ratio α ≥ 0", p. 556). Problem parameter 0 ≤ α < 1. Then the optimal state-feedback strategies are:
- **Target (eq. (11))**: heads directly away from the interception point: cos φ* = (x_T − x)/√((x_T−x)² + (y_T−y)²), sin φ* = (y_T − y)/√((x_T−x)² + (y_T−y)²).
- **Defender (eq. (12))**: heads to the interception point: cos ψ* = (x − x_D)/√((x−x_D)² + (y−y_D)²), sin ψ* = (y − y_D)/√(...) — same point.
- **Attacker (eq. (13))**: heads to the same interception point: cos χ* = (x − x_A)/√(...), sin χ* = (y − y_A)/√(...).
- The interception point I = (x, y) lies on the **orthogonal bisector of AD** (eq. (14)): y = mx + n, with (eq. (9)–(10)) midpoint x_0 = ½(x_A + x_D), y_0 = ½(y_A + y_D), slope m = −(x_A − x_D)/(y_A − y_D), n = y_0 − m x_0. (I on the bisector because V_A = V_D: A and D arrive at I simultaneously.)
- The abscissa x is a **real solution of the quartic equation (15)**:
  (1−α²)(m²+1)³x⁴ + (1−α²)(m²+1)²(k₁+2k₂)x³ + [(m²+1)²(k₃−α²k₄) + 2(1−α²)(m²+1)k₁k₂ + (m²+1)(k₂² − (α²/4)k₁²)]x² + [(m²+1)(2k₂k₃ − α²k₁k₄) + k₁k₂(k₂ − (α²/2)k₁)]x + k₂²k₃ − (α²/4)k₁²k₄ = 0,
  parameterized by α, with state-function coefficients (eq. (16)): k₁ = 2mn − [x_A + x_D + m(y_A + y_D)], k₂ = mn − x_T − m y_T, k₃ = ½(x_A² + x_D² + y_A² + y_D²) + n² − n(y_A + y_D), k₄ = x_T² + (y_T − n)².
- **Value function (eq. (17))**: V(x) = α·√[ ¼((x_A−x_D)² + (y_A−y_D)²) + (x − ½(x_A+x_D))² + (y − ½(y_A+y_D))² ] + √((x_T−x)² + (y_T−y)²) = α·AI + TI, i.e., α × (distance Attacker–interception point) + (distance Target–interception point). V is C1 and satisfies the HJI PDE.

**Proof approach** (steps, with tools):
1. **Isaacs' Main Equation 1 (eq. (18))** min_χ max_{φ,ψ} H = 0, with separable H ⇒ optimal controls characterized by the costates (eqs. (19)–(21)): (cos χ*, sin χ*) = −λ_A/‖λ_A‖, (cos ψ*, sin ψ*) = λ_D/‖λ_D‖, (cos φ*, sin φ*) = λ_T/‖λ_T‖.
2. **Costate dynamics** λ̇ = −∂H/∂x ⇒ all six costates constant (p. 557) ⇒ all optimal headings constant ⇒ straight-line optimal play.
3. **TPBVP in R^12**: augmented terminal payoff Φ_a (eq. (22)) with Lagrange multipliers ν₁, ν₂; transversality λ(t_f) = ∂Φ_a/∂x (eq. (23)) gives (24)–(29); eliminating ν₁, ν₂ from (24)–(27) yields (30)–(31); together with terminal manifold (3), exactly the six needed terminal conditions; t_f fixed by H(t_f) ≡ 0, i.e., ME 1 (eq. (32)). (Method: two-sided/two-person PMP [28], [27].)
4. **Backward propagation** with constant headings (eqs. (33)–(38)); define the interception point x ≡ x_Af = x_Df, y ≡ y_Af = y_Df (eq. (39)) ⇒ I on bisector (14). Terminal costate relations recast as (40)–(43); Target heading in terms of its terminal position (44), equivalently of its current state (46); A and D headings (47)–(48). Terminal time t_f(x; x) = AI = √(AD²/4 + OI²) (eq. (45)), since t_f − t′ = AI = DI.
5. **Costate resolution**: solving the four equations (42), (43), (49), (50) in the four unknowns λ_xA, λ_yA, λ_xD, λ_yD gives closed forms (51) (rational expressions in the interception point and terminal Target position); substituting the Target's propagated terminal position via (52) yields costates (53) purely in terms of the current state and I.
6. **ME 1 as the scalar design equation**: (32) ⇔ (54): ‖λ_A‖ − ‖λ_D‖ − α‖λ_T‖ = 0. Using (46)–(48) and (53), and canceling the common factor 1/TI, this reduces to eq. (55): √(AD²/4 + OI²)·[x_T − x + m(y_T − y)] = α·√((x_T−x)² + (y_T−y)²)·[x + my − ½(x_A + x_D + m(y_A + y_D))].
7. **Decisive step — squaring to the quartic**: substituting y = mx + n into (55) and squaring both sides gives eq. (56): [(m²+1)x² + k₁x + k₃]·[(m²+1)x + k₂]² = α²[(m²+1)x² + 2k₂x + k₄]·[(m²+1)x + k₁/2]²; expanding and rearranging yields the quartic (15). x* = a real root, y* = m x* + n.
8. **Degenerate case DT = AT**: then x* = x_T, y* = y_T (A and D aim at the Target's current position — from (55)) and (46) is 0/0, so the heading is instead obtained by writing the costates (57) in terms of (cos φ, sin φ); ME 1 reduces to (58): t_f[(y_A−y_D)cos φ − (x_A−x_D)sin φ] = α[(x−x_A)(y−y_D) − (x−x_D)(y−y_A)], and after division by (y_A−y_D) and use of m = (y−y_0)/(x−x_0) to eq. (59): m sin φ + cos φ = α(m²+1)(x − x_0)/t_f. Solving the resulting quadratic in sin φ (after cos²φ = 1 − sin²φ):
   - **eq. (60)**: sin φ* = αm(x* − x_0)/t_f ± √(AD²/4 + (1−α²)(m²+1)(x*−x_0)²)/(t_f√(m²+1)),
   - **eq. (61)**: cos φ* = α(x* − x_0)/t_f ∓ m√(AD²/4 + (1−α²)(m²+1)(x*−x_0)²)/(t_f√(m²+1)).
   For DT = AT substitute x* = x_T, giving (62)–(63) with t_f(x, x_T) = √(AD²/4 + (x_T−x_0)² + (y_T−y_0)²) evaluated on the bisector (y_T = m x_T + n).
9. **Value function is C1 and solves HJI**: with y* = m x* + n, dV/dx* is computed as eq. (64); writing (55) in the equivalent form (65) shows dV/dx* equals the left-hand side of (55)-form (65) evaluated at x* — which vanishes because x* solves (15) ⇔ (65). Hence dV/dx* = 0 (envelope theorem), and the partial derivatives are (eq. (66)): ∂V/∂x_A = α(x_A−x*)/(2AI) = −(α/2)cos χ*, ∂V/∂y_A = −(α/2)sin χ*, ∂V/∂x_D = −(α/2)cos ψ*, ∂V/∂y_D = −(α/2)sin ψ*, ∂V/∂x_T = (x_T−x*)/TI = cos φ*, ∂V/∂y_T = sin φ*. Then the HJI equation −∂V/∂t = ∂V/∂x·f(x, χ*, ψ*, φ*) + g reduces (with ∂V/∂t = 0, g = 0, running cost absent) to eq. (67): ∂V/∂x·f = −(α/2)cos²χ* − (α/2)sin²χ* − (α/2)cos²ψ* − (α/2)sin²ψ* + αcos²φ* + αsin²φ* = −α/2 − α/2 + α = 0. ∎

**Physical meaning**: all three agents fly straight lines to a common interception point on the perpendicular bisector of AD; the T/D team's payoff is α·AI + TI — the Attacker is forced to fly to a point as far as possible from T (weighted by the Target's slowness α) while T maximizes its own distance from that point. Locality/globality: the result holds globally on the sub-region {DT ≤ AT} ⊂ R^6 (in fact on all of R^6 restricted by DT ≤ AT), analytically, with the only "numerical" step being rooting the quartic (solvable in closed form, p. 560). Extensibility: the same two-sided-PMP recipe is what Theorem 2 reuses for DT > AT.

**Critical remarks on Theorem 1**: (i) step 7 squares (55); squaring can introduce extraneous roots (sign mismatch with the unsquared (55)), and the paper does not state a sign/feasibility filter for roots of (15) — a **proof gap (minor, practical)**: the admissible-root selection rule is left implicit ("The coordinate x is a real solution", p. 556). (ii) The paper later proves ≥ 2 real roots exist (p. 564) but never says which root is the game-theoretic one; presumably geometry (bisector intersection feasibility) disambiguates. (iii) The C1 claim relies on x*(x) being C1, which follows from the implicit function theorem applied to (65) only where the relevant derivative does not vanish — not discussed.

### 5.3 Corollary 1 (p. 561; heading selection when DT = AT)

**Statement.** When DT = AT, the optimal Target heading is cos φ* = cos φ_κ, sin φ* = sin φ_κ (eq. (69)) with κ = argmin_i {Δ_i}, i = 1, 2 (eq. (70)), where the two feasible pairs are (eq. (68)) (sin φ₁, cos φ₁) = (mp + q, p − mq), (sin φ₂, cos φ₂) = (mp − q, p + mq), with p = α(x_T − x_0)/t_f(x, x_T), q = √(AD²/4 + (1−α²)(m²+1)(x_T−x_0)²)/(t_f(x,x_T)√(m²+1)), and Δ_i = √((x_D − x_Ti)² + (y_D − y_Ti)²), x_Ti = x_T + α t_f(x,x_T) cos φ_i, y_Ti = y_T + α t_f(x,x_T) sin φ_i (distance from D to the Target's projected terminal position under heading i).

**Proof approach**: (1) (62)–(63) offer four sign combinations; (2) direct computation of sin²φ₁ + cos²φ₁ = (m²+1)(x_T−x_0)² + AD²/4)/t_f² = 1 shows the "matched-sign" pairs are unit vectors (decisive identity: t_f² = (m²+1)(x_T−x_0)² + AD²/4), whereas mismatched pairs give sin² + cos² = 1 + 4αm(x_T−x_0)√(AD²/4 + (1−α²)(m²+1)(x_T−x_0)²)/(t_f²√(m²+1)) ≠ 1 in general — not valid headings (equivalently: only (68) satisfies (59)); (3) to pick between the two, **Definition 2 (Dual Problem, p. 562)** swaps A ↔ D keeping α and T fixed; when DT = AT all quantities entering (62)–(63) (AD, midpoint, slope m, t_f) are invariant under the swap, so the two feasible headings are the solutions of the original and the dual problem; (4) the correct heading is the one steering T into the reachable side of D (the other steers T into A's reachable side = D's side of the dual); operationally κ = argmin Δ_i. ∎ (The tie-break argument is geometric/constructive rather than a variational proof — the min-Δ_i criterion is asserted, justified via the dual problem, not derived from the payoff.)

### 5.4 Theorem 2 (pp. 562–563; case DT > AT, x ∈ R_e)

**Statement.** Consider the ATDDG (1)–(7) with DT > AT and x ∈ R_e (escape region — Game of Kind assumed solved, Section V). Parameter 0 < α < 1. Then A and D use the same state-feedback laws (13), (12) (head to the interception point on the bisector (14), x a real root of the same quartic (15)), and the Target heading is (eq. (73)): cos φ* = (x − x_T)/√((x−x_T)² + (y−y_T)²), sin φ* = (y − y_T)/√(...) — i.e., T heads **toward** the interception point (sign flipped w.r.t. (11)): T must break through toward D's side. The Value function is (eq. (74)): V(x) = α·√[¼((x_A−x_D)²+(y_A−y_D)²) + (x−½(x_A+x_D))² + (y−½(y_A+y_D))²] − √((x−x_T)² + (y−y_T)²) = α·AI − TI; V is C1 and satisfies the HJI PDE on R_e.

**Proof approach**: identical machinery — costates constant, (19)–(21); headings (73); Target terminal position (75); costates (76) in terms of state and I; ME 1 gives (77), which reduces to the same quartic (15); alternatively costates in the (cos φ, sin φ) form (57) give (59) and hence (60)–(61), so **(60)–(61) hold for any x ∈ R_e regardless of DT ≶ AT**; dV/dx* (eq. (78)) equals the left-hand side of (79) (the (55)-analogue for this case), which vanishes at the root, so dV/dx* = 0; gradient (66) again; HJI verified exactly as in (67). ∎

**Global assembly (p. 564)**: the Value on the whole escape region is the piecewise function (unnumbered displayed equation, p. 564): V(x) = V_s (eq. (17)) if DT ≤ AT; V_g (eq. (74)) if DT > AT and x ∈ R_e. At DT = AT, x* = x_T, y* = y_T ⇒ V_s = V_g ⇒ V continuous; the gradient (66) with (60)–(61) is the same expression in all three regimes ⇒ ∂V/∂x continuous (no discontinuity in the Target's feedback strategy, which (11)/(73) alone would not deliver); hence **V is C1 and there are no singular surfaces in the ATDDG**, and V satisfies HJI for every x ∈ R_e.

**Existence of real roots (p. 564, unnumbered result)**: let F(x) be the left-hand side of (15). F(±∞) > 0. Let T_p be the closest point of the bisector to T (slope −1/m through T); then (m²+1)x_Tp + k₂ = 0 (eq. (80), a short chain of equalities), and evaluating F at x_Tp through the pre-squared form (56): F(x_Tp) = −(α·T_Tp·[(m²+1)x_Tp + k₁/2])² < 0. By the intermediate value theorem the quartic has **at least two real roots**. (Caveat: the negativity requires α > 0, T_Tp > 0, and (m²+1)x_Tp + k₁/2 ≠ 0; α = 0 and T-on-bisector cases are excluded/degenerate — consistent with 0 < α < 1 in this section.)

### 5.5 Theorem 3 (pp. 564–566; Game of Kind — the Barrier)

**Statement.** For a given speed ratio 0 < α < 1 and fixed A, D coordinates, the Barrier-surface cross-section separating the plane into the escape region R_e and capture region R_c is the **Attacker's-side branch of the hyperbola** (eq. (82)): h_xx x² + h_yy y² + 2h_xy xy + 2h_x x + 2h_y y + h = 0, with coefficients (eq. (83)):
- h_xx = cos²σ − α², h_yy = sin²σ − α², h_xy = sin σ cos σ;
- h_x = α²x_A sin²σ − (1−α²)x_0 cos²σ − [α²y_A + (1−α²)y_0] sin σ cos σ;
- h_y = α²y_A cos²σ − (1−α²)y_0 sin²σ − [α²x_A + (1−α²)x_0] sin σ cos σ;
- h = [α²y_A + (1−α²)y_0]² sin²σ + [α²x_A + (1−α²)x_0]² cos²σ − α²(x_A² + y_A²) + 2[α²y_A + (1−α²)y_0][α²x_A + (1−α²)x_0] sin σ cos σ;
- cos σ = (x_A − x_D)/√((x_A−x_D)² + (y_A−y_D)²), sin σ = (y_A − y_D)/√(...), (x_0, y_0) the AD midpoint (9).
Additionally the hyperbola center is (x_oh, y_oh) = (x_0, y_0) (the AD midpoint) and the principal-axis angle is θ = σ = arctan((y_A−y_D)/(x_A−x_D)) — the axes are parallel/perpendicular to AD. The escape-set definition in R^6 is **eq. (81): R_e := {x | H(x; α) < 0}**, whose cross sections (fixing A, D, α) are given by the hyperbola. When DT ≤ AT, T always escapes for any 0 < α < 1: the entire D-side of the bisector is in R_e, so the boundary lies on A's side of the plane (only A's-side branch matters; the D-side branch is irrelevant, p. 567).

**Proof approach** (pp. 565–566):
1. **Apollonius circle setup**: for the pair (A, T) and ratio α = TP/AP, the Apollonius circle has radius r_A = (α/(1−α²))·d (d = AT distance) and center C at distance (α²/(1−α²))·d from T on line AT, with coordinates x_c = (1/(1−α²))x_T − (α²/(1−α²))x_A, y_c = (1/(1−α²))y_T − (α²/(1−α²))y_A (eqs. (85)–(86), derived from (84), the line through A and T). A intercepts T on this circle's circumference.
2. **Qualitative criterion**: T can escape (D has a role) **iff the Apollonius circle of segment AT intersects the orthogonal bisector (14)** of AD (if the circle misses the bisector, A reaches T before any interception can occur on the bisector).
3. **Critical case = tangency** (Fig. 4): at the boundary the circle is tangent to (14). With B the tangency point, BC ∥ AD; the line BC has slope (y_A−y_D)/(x_A−x_D) (eq. (87)); intersecting (87) with (14) gives x_B = (y_0 − y_c) sin σ cos σ + x_0 cos²σ + x_c sin²σ, y_B = m x_B + n.
4. **Decisive inequality/equation (eq. (88))**: escape condition (y_c − y_B)² + (x_c − x_B)² < (α/(1−α²) d)² (circle radius exceeds distance BC). Replacing "<" by "=" and simplifying: using m sin σ cos σ = −cos²σ and sin²+cos² = 1, eq. (89) collapses to (90): [y_c − y_0 − m(x_c − x_0)]² sin²σ = (α/(1−α²) d)².
5. Substituting (85)–(86) into (90) and clearing (1−α²)² gives (91): ([y_T − α²y_A − (1−α²)y_0] sin σ + [x_T − α²x_A − (1−α²)x_0] cos σ)² = α²[(x_A−x_T)² + (y_A−y_T)²].
6. **Change of variables** x_T → x, y_T → y and expansion (eq. (92)); grouping terms yields the conic (82)–(83).
7. **Conic identification**: discriminant D = det[[cos²σ−α², sin σ cos σ],[sin σ cos σ, cos²σ−α²]] = −α²(1−α²) < 0 (p. 567) ⇒ hyperbola. Center from determinants x_oh = −(1/D)|h_x h_xy; h_y h_yy|, y_oh = −(1/D)|h_xx h_x; h_xy h_y|; expansion and cancellation gives x_oh = x_0, y_oh = y_0 (the midpoint of AD). Principal axes: tan 2θ = 2h_xy/(h_xx − h_yy) = 2 tan σ/(1 − tan²σ) (double-angle identity) ⇒ θ = σ. ∎

**Physical meaning**: the barrier is the locus of Target positions for which the Apollonius circle (A–T reachability at speed ratio α) is exactly tangent to D's interception bisector; inside R_e (hyperbola's A-side "exterior", plus the whole D-side half-plane) the T/D team wins; on the other side A captures T regardless of D. Analytic, global (in the fixed-A,D cross-section), exact.

## 6. Simulation and Experiments

**There is no simulation, numerical example, or experiment in this paper.** No parameter table is printed (UNREPORTED — no scenario parameters such as α values, coordinates, or initial conditions are ever instantiated numerically); no baselines, metrics, platform, or runtime data exist. All verification is analytic: (a) the HJI PDE check (67) (and its Theorem 2 counterpart), (b) the C1/continuity argument on p. 564, (c) the real-root existence proof (80), and (d) the PMP-based necessary-conditions synthesis, which together constitute the "verification" of the saddle-point solution. The five figures are analytic/geometric illustrations, not computational outputs:
- **Fig. 1 (p. 555)**: the T–A–D engagement geometry (validates the model setup, headings, and distance definitions).
- **Fig. 2 (p. 558)**: optimal play when DT ≤ AT — A, D, T converge to the interception point I on the orthogonal bisector of AD through midpoint O (validates Theorem 1's strategy structure).
- **Fig. 3 (p. 563)**: optimal play when initially DT > AT (validates Theorem 2, including the Target breaking toward D's side).
- **Fig. 4 (p. 565)**: critical case — Apollonius circle of AT tangent to the orthogonal bisector of AD (validates the boundary condition underpinning Theorem 3).
- **Fig. 5 (p. 567)**: cross-section of the Barrier surface showing the hyperbola branch on the Attacker's side separating R_e and R_c, with D inside the escape side (validates Theorem 3's geometric claims: center at midpoint, axes along AD, A-side branch relevant).

Consequently, claims such as implementability ("a quartic equation can be solved in closed form", p. 560; real-time rooting needed for x*(x)) are asserted but not demonstrated by timing or robustness experiments; behavior of the feedback laws under root-branch ambiguity (see Section 7) is untested here.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues

**Author-stated:**
- Simple motion only: holonomic, constant-speed, unconstrained point-mass players; no turn-rate/inertia/nonholonomic constraints; point capture (p. 554, p. 567: extension to nonholonomic dynamics and to multiple Attackers/Defenders is "the cornerstone for future and more challenging" work).
- V_A = V_D assumed (equal Attacker/Defender speeds) and α < 1 (slower Target); the case V_D > V_A or defender speed advantage is not treated.
- The Game of Degree in the capture region R_c (Attacker-wins region) is explicitly "not discussed in this paper" (p. 555) — the game solved there (A capturing T unmolested) is left open.
- Isaacs' method "does not scale well as the dimension of the state increases" (p. 554); the closed form here is an exception, not a template.
- Perfect information; causal strategies; no measurement noise, delays, or information asymmetry (p. 555).

**Critical observations (with loci):**
- **Root selection ambiguity**: the feedback law "hinges on the rooting in real time of the quartic equation (15)" (p. 560), and at least two real roots always exist (p. 564); the paper never states which root defines I* (nor how to discard roots introduced by squaring (55) into (56)). This is a practical gap for implementation — an incorrect root choice would be a valid-looking but non-optimal (possibly infeasible-sign) strategy. proof gap (minor): sign-feasibility filter after (56) absent.
- The envelope argument dV/dx* = 0 via (64)–(65)/(78)–(79) presumes x*(x) is differentiable; the implicit-function-theorem conditions on (65) (nonvanishing denominator) are not examined, so C1-ness of V is argued constructively but not fully rigorously at points where the quartic has multiple nearby roots (possible near-tangency of the design equation). No singular surfaces are found, but a local analysis at the boundary of R_e (where the Value → 0 continuously? V = αAI − TI → barrier) is not carried out; behavior of V on ∂R_e (the hyperbola) is not analyzed.
- The Corollary 1 tie-break (κ = argmin Δ_i, eq. (70)) is justified geometrically through the dual problem (Definition 2) rather than by a variational argument; the min-Δ_i criterion is plausible (steer into D's reachable side) but is not derived from the payoff functional.
- Theorem 1's statement admits α = 0 ("0 ≤ α < 1", p. 556), while the escape-guarantee sentence says "for any speed ratio α ≥ 0" and the Game-of-Kind section uses 0 < α < 1; the α = 0 edge (static Target) is harmless but inconsistent across sections.
- Degenerate geometries are handled by case-splitting (m = 0 ⇒ use (15); m′ = 0 ⇒ use (71); DT = AT ⇒ (60)–(63) with x* = x_T), but the y_A = y_D vertical-bisector case combined with the Corollary 1 selection is not illustrated.
- No numerical validation whatsoever (Section 6 above), so numerical conditioning of the quartic (e.g., near-double roots close to the barrier) is unknown.

## 8. Citation Value

This is the canonical, complete closed-form solution of the three-player active target defense differential game and a flagship example of a differential game with a six-dimensional state solved in the classical sense — saddle-point state-feedback strategies, an explicit C1 Value (α·AI ± TI), HJI verification, and an exact Apollonius/hyperbola barrier. Role in a paper's argument: (i) as the foundational ATDDG reference when formulating target-defense games with an adversarial Attacker (as opposed to prescribed PN/pursuit guidance), (ii) as the benchmark solution against which reduced-order, numerical (HJ PDE), differential-theoretic, or learning-based active-defense schemes are compared, and (iii) as the rare instance where no singular surfaces exist and the classical Value is obtainable — useful when discussing viscosity vs. classical solutions or the limits of Isaacs' method. Suggested citation sentence: "The active target defense differential game admits a complete closed-form solution in the original Cartesian frame: state-feedback saddle-point strategies parameterized by the real root of a quartic equation, a C1 Value function satisfying the HJI equation everywhere in the Target's escape set, and a barrier given by the Attacker-side branch of a hyperbola centered at the Attacker–Defender midpoint [Garcia et al., IEEE TAC 64(2), 2019]." Natural companion citations: the conference precursor [17] (Allerton 2014) and the fixed-Attacker-strategy predecessors [7], [8], [18].
