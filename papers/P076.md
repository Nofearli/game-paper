## 2. Research Background
This paper belongs to the classical Isaacs line of differential games initiated by the Homicidal Chauffeur problem and its surveillance variant [Lewin & Breakwell 1975; Lewin & Olsder 1979, the paper's Refs. [9], [15], [6]]. The specific setting — a pursuer that must keep a faster, more agile evader inside a *limited field of view* rather than merely within a circular detection radius — is motivated by robotic applications: a camera-equipped robot monitoring a potentially malicious agent, or a robot assisting a person (p. 292). The author (CICESE) has built a series of OA-vs-DDR games: capture of an OA by a DDR [3, T-RO 2013], role-reversed capture [4, IJC 2016], circular-detection-region surveillance with a DDR pursuer [5, IJC 2022], and constant-distance tracking [16, Auton. Robots 2011]. The present paper changes the detection region from a circle to a semi-infinite cone rigidly attached to the DDR heading, which — together with the DDR's nonholonomic, rotation/translation trade-off — produces a richer singular-surface structure than all predecessors (five singular surfaces vs. none/fewer in [3,4,5]). The solution method is analytical (Isaacs + Bernhard [7] + Lewin [8] junction-condition theory), with numerics used only for integrating the equivocal-surface ODE and for plotting (p. 293).

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
- **Problem**: a DDR (disc of radius $b\ge 1$, FoV = semi-infinite cone of half-angle $\phi_d\in(0,\pi/2]$ fixed to the DDR center and aligned with its heading) wants to keep a point-like omnidirectional agent inside the cone as long as possible; the OA wants to escape the cone in minimum time. Planar, obstacle-free, purely kinematic, zero-sum; payoff is escape time $J=\int_{t_i}^{t_f}dt=t_f-t_i$ (eq. (7), p. 295).
- **[authors' claim]** "To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work addressing the problem described above and presents a solution to it based on differential game theory" (p. 292). **[verified from the related-work comparison, pp. 293–294]** The nearest works are indeed distinct: [3,4] (same players, but capture games, no FoV), [5] (same players but circular detection region), [6] (Lewin–Olsder conic surveillance evasion: same cone sensor but car-like pursuer that cannot move backwards, no DDR area constraint), [15] (surveillance-evasion of degree: car-like pursuer, circular region), [16] (same players, constant-distance tracking, non-time-optimal monotonicity-based strategies). The gap claim is consistent with the printed titles/venues of these references; full-text verification of [6], [15] was not performed here.
- **[authors' claim]** The FoV restriction "makes the problem harder to solve compared to existing works [3-5] dealing with a DDR pursuer" and the DDR's finite area "impos[es] a boundary constraint" absent in [6] (pp. 292, 294). **[verified within this paper]** The area constraint generates the evader's state constraint surface (ESCS, Section 6.4) and the escape-time threshold depends on $b$ (eq. (15)), both absent from [3,4]; the cone vs. circle change requires new tributary constructions vs. [5,15].
- **Gap filled**: first closed-form (Isaacs-type) solution of the minimum-time visibility-breaking game between an OA and a DDR with a cone FoV, including the winner criterion (a speed-ratio test), five singular surfaces, and a four-region partition of the playing space with admissible strategies everywhere off the singular surfaces.

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Game-of-kind criterion (theory)**: escape is decided by the speed ratio alone — if $V_a^{\max}\le V_r^{\max}/b$ the DDR keeps the OA under surveillance forever (rotate in place at max speed); if $V_a^{\max}>V_r^{\max}/b$ the OA escapes from any valid initial configuration; there is no barrier splitting the state space into two outcome regions (pp. 293, 297, 300).
2. **Singular-surface taxonomy (theory)**: five singular surfaces are exhibited — an evader's dispersal surface (EDS), an evader's state constraint surface (ESCS), a pursuer's equivocal surface (PES), a pursuer's dispersal surface (PDS), and a pursuer's state constraint surface (PSCS) (pp. 294, 297–298, 300–302; Figs. 2–3).
3. **Partition of the playing space (theory/modeling)**: four regions I–IV, each with explicit time-optimal motion strategies for both players (Section 5 overview pp. 297–298; constructions in Section 6, eqs. (21)–(49)). The value is computable at any state by numerically solving one scalar implicit equation ((34) or (43)).
4. **Simulations (experiment, numerical)**: three runs showing optimal play in reduced (DDR-body) and global frames, including a case visiting the ESCS and a comparison of the optimal OA strategy against a plausible non-optimal one (escape 0.45 s vs. 0.4760 s), validating strict time-optimality (pp. 302–304, Figs. 4–10).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results (MOST IMPORTANT)
The paper contains **no numbered theorems/lemmas**; the results are an Isaacs-style constructive solution expressed as equation blocks. Below, every analytic result is recorded with its equation numbers, assumptions, proof mechanism, and status. Page numbers are the printed journal pages 292–305.

**Game setup, dynamics, information structure (pp. 294–295, eqs. (1)–(5)).** Realistic space: DDR pose $(x_r,y_r,\theta_r)$, OA position $(x_a,y_a)$,
$$\dot x_r=\tfrac{u_1+u_2}{2}\cos\theta_r,\quad \dot y_r=\tfrac{u_1+u_2}{2}\sin\theta_r,\quad \dot\theta_r=\tfrac{u_2-u_1}{2b},\qquad u_1,u_2\in[-V_r^{\max},V_r^{\max}]\quad(1)$$
(wheel controls, unit wheel radius, $b$ = center-to-wheel distance; kinematic model cited from [17] Balkcom–Mason). Wheel-speed trade-off, eq. (2): $|\dot\theta_r|=\tfrac{1}{b}\big|V_r^{\max}-\tfrac{u_1+u_2}{2}\big|$ — an *inverse* relation between rotational and translational speed (see Limitations: (2) is inconsistent with (1) for backward-rolling wheels). OA: $\dot x_a=v_a\cos\psi_a,\ \dot y_a=v_a\sin\psi_a$, $v_a\in[0,V_a^{\max}]$, $\psi_a\in[0,2\pi)$ (eq. (3)) — simple motion, $360^\circ$ agility. Reduced space (DDR body frame, origin at DDR center, $y$-axis along heading; angles measured clockwise from the $y$-axis), eq. (4):
$$\dot x=\tfrac{u_2-u_1}{2b}y+v_1\sin v_2,\qquad \dot y=-\tfrac{u_2-u_1}{2b}x-\tfrac{u_1+u_2}{2}+v_1\cos v_2,$$
with $v_1\in[0,V_a^{\max}]$, $v_2\in[0,2\pi)$. Polar reduced coordinates $x_r=(r,\phi)$ ($x=r\sin\phi$, $y=r\cos\phi$), eq. (5):
$$\dot r=v_1\cos(v_2-\phi)-\tfrac{u_1+u_2}{2}\cos\phi,\qquad \dot\phi=\tfrac{u_2-u_1}{2b}+\tfrac{v_1}{r}\sin(v_2-\phi)+\tfrac{u_1+u_2}{2}\tfrac{\sin\phi}{r}.$$
*Verification (mine)*: both (4) and (5) follow exactly from relative-motion kinematics $\dot r_{rel}=v_a-R(\theta_r)^{-1}v_p-\dot\theta_r\hat z\times r$; signs and terms are correct. Payoff and value, eqs. (6)–(8): $J=\int L\,dt+G$, here $L=1$, $G=0$, $V(x(t_i))=\min_v\max_u J$ — the OA minimizes, DDR maximizes escape time; note (8) is written $\min_v\max_u$, i.e., the OA is the minimizer (p. 295). HJB (9) and PMP Hamiltonian (10)–(11) with $H=\nabla V\cdot f+L$; saddle point $\min_v\max_u H=0$ (20). Termination: OA escapes when $|\phi|$ exceeds $\phi_d$; playing space excludes the DDR disc ($r\ge b$; the evader never enters the disc, p. 294). Information: state-feedback (admissible) strategies off the singular surfaces; on singular surfaces one player uses a **non-admissible** strategy (a function of the opponent's controls) — explicitly flagged for ESCS, PES, PSCS.

**Result 1 — Usable part (UP) and winner criterion (pp. 296–297, eqs. (12)–(16)).** *Statement*: the UP (configurations from which the OA escapes regardless of the DDR) is $\{ \phi=\phi_d,\ r<bV_a^{\max}/V_r^{\max}\}$ (15); its boundary BUP is $r=bV_a^{\max}/V_r^{\max}$ on $\phi=\phi_d$ (16); left side by symmetry. *Derivation*: UP $\Leftrightarrow \min_{u}\max_v(-\dot\phi)<0$ at $\phi=\phi_d$ (13)–(14). Decisive step: at $r\ge b>b\sin\phi_d$ the DDR's best is rotation in place ($u_1=V_r^{\max}$, $u_2=-V_r^{\max}$, so $(u_1+u_2)/2=0$, $(u_2-u_1)/2b=-V_r^{\max}/b$) while the OA's best is max speed perpendicular to the FoV boundary ($v_1=V_a^{\max}$, $\sin(v_2-\phi)=1$), giving $\max\dot\phi=-V_r^{\max}/b+V_a^{\max}/r$; hence UP $\Leftrightarrow r<bV_a^{\max}/V_r^{\max}$ (my re-derivation confirms, including the min over $(u_1,u_2)$ via the diamond $|s|+|d|\le V_r^{\max}$ in $(s,d)=((u_1+u_2)/2,(u_2-u_1)/2)$). *Consequence (game of kind, p. 297)*: if $V_a^{\max}\le V_r^{\max}/b$ the UP lies inside the invalid disc ($r<b$), so the DDR wins everywhere by spinning in place; if $V_a^{\max}>V_r^{\max}/b$ the OA escapes (from any valid state) using more elaborate strategies — heading straight at far boundary points is *not* optimal because the DDR counteracts ("That behavior hints more elaborated strategies are needed", p. 297). *Local/global*: global, analytic. *Physical meaning*: the DDR's max angular rate $V_r^{\max}/b$ vs. the OA's angular rate $V_a^{\max}/r$ about the DDR center decide the immediate-escape set; the ratio threshold $1/b$ is the DDR's area effect.

**Result 2 — Optimal controls from the PMP (pp. 298–299, eqs. (17)–(24)).** Hamiltonian (18) (Cartesian) and (19) (polar) from substituting (4)/(5) into (17) ($H=\lambda^T f+1$). DDR controls, eq. (21):
$$u_1^*=\mathrm{sgn}\Big(-\tfrac{y\lambda_x}{b}+\tfrac{x\lambda_y}{b}-\lambda_y\Big)V_r^{\max},\qquad u_2^*=\mathrm{sgn}\Big(\tfrac{y\lambda_x}{b}-\tfrac{x\lambda_y}{b}-\lambda_y\Big)V_r^{\max},$$
OA controls, eq. (22): $v_1^*=V_a^{\max}$, $\sin v_2^*=-\lambda_x/\gamma$, $\cos v_2^*=-\lambda_y/\gamma$, $\gamma=\sqrt{\lambda_x^2+\lambda_y^2}$ (bang-bang in each wheel; OA always at max speed heading opposite the costate). Polar forms (23)–(24): $u_{1,2}^*=\mathrm{sgn}\big(-\tfrac{\cos\phi}{2}\lambda_r\mp\tfrac{1}{2b}\lambda_\phi+\tfrac{\sin\phi}{2r}\lambda_\phi\big)V_r^{\max}$ and $[\cos(v_2^*-\phi),\sin(v_2^*-\phi)]\ \|\ [-\lambda_r,-\lambda_\phi/r]$ ("$\|$" = parallel). *Verification (mine)*: (21)–(24) are exactly the argmax/argmin of the affine-in-$(u_1,u_2)$, affine-in-$(v_1,v_2)$ Hamiltonian over the control boxes; correct. Retro costate equations, eqs. (25)–(26): $\mathring\lambda_x=-\tfrac{u_2^*-u_1^*}{2b}\lambda_y,\ \mathring\lambda_y=\tfrac{u_2^*-u_1^*}{2b}\lambda_x$ ($\mathring{\ }=d/d\tau$, $\tau=t_f-t$); verified by differentiating (18). *Physical meaning*: only the wheel-sign combination matters — the DDR's optimal play is always one of {rotate in place ±, translate ±max} or a face of the control diamond; the OA's heading is anti-parallel to the costate gradient of the value.

**Result 3 — Primary solution (pp. 298–300, eqs. (27)–(34)).** Transversality at the UP, eq. (27): $\lambda_x=-\cos\phi_d,\ \lambda_y=\sin\phi_d$ (unit normal to the FoV boundary pointing into the cone). Retro costate, eqs. (28)–(29): with $u_1^*=V_r^{\max},u_2^*=-V_r^{\max}$ (rotation in place), $\lambda_x=-\cos(\phi_d+\tfrac{V_r^{\max}}{b}\tau)$, $\lambda_y=\sin(\phi_d+\tfrac{V_r^{\max}}{b}\tau)$. Retro motion, eqs. (30)–(31), solved in closed form, eq. (32):
$$x(\tau)=r_f\sin\Big(\phi_d+\tau\tfrac{V_r^{\max}}{b}\Big)-\tau V_a^{\max}\cos\Big(\phi_d+\tau\tfrac{V_r^{\max}}{b}\Big),\qquad y(\tau)=r_f\cos\Big(\phi_d+\tau\tfrac{V_r^{\max}}{b}\Big)+\tau V_a^{\max}\sin\Big(\phi_d+\tau\tfrac{V_r^{\max}}{b}\Big),$$
where $x_f=r_f\sin\phi_d$, $y_f=r_f\cos\phi_d$, $r_f$ satisfying (15) (escape point on the UP). *Verification (mine)*: differentiating (32) reproduces (31) exactly. *Invariants satisfied by all primary trajectories*: eq. (33) $\sin(\phi_d+\tau\tfrac{V_r^{\max}}{b})\,x(\tau)+\cos(\phi_d+\tau\tfrac{V_r^{\max}}{b})\,y(\tau)=r_f$ and eq. (34) $r\sin\big(\phi_d+\tau\tfrac{V_r^{\max}}{b}-\phi\big)-\tau V_a^{\max}=0$. *Verification (mine)*: both verified by direct substitution of (32); note the paper's prose attribution of the multiplier combinations contains sign slips ("x(τ) by λy and y(τ) by λx" for (33); "x(τ) by −λx" for (34)) — the printed equations (33)/(34) themselves are correct, the correct combinations being $\lambda_y x-\lambda_x y$ and $\lambda_x x+\lambda_y y$ respectively. *Decisive use of (34)*: it is a scalar implicit equation whose numerical root gives the retro-time $\tau$ (hence the value $V=r_f$-trajectory time-to-escape) at any point $(r,\phi)$ of a primary trajectory. *No-switching lemma (p. 300)*: substituting (29) and (32) into (21), $u_1$ switches iff $r_f-\sin(\phi_d+\tfrac{V_r^{\max}\tau}{b})=0$ and $u_2$ iff $-r_f-\sin(\cdot)=0$ (as printed; my computation gives $(r_f-b\sin(\cdot))/b=0$, i.e., a missing factor $b$ in the paper — a typo not affecting the conclusion); since $r_f>b\ge1$ and $|\sin|\le1$, neither can occur — the DDR never switches control on the primary solution. *Barrier discussion (p. 300)*: starting the retro-integration at BUP ($r_f=bV_a^{\max}/V_r^{\max}$) yields a trajectory outside the FoV — invalid; hence no barrier exists and the playing space is a single outcome region. *Geometry (my derivation, not stated in the paper)*: (32) is the involute of the circle of radius $r_f$ centered at the DDR; in the realistic space the OA's heading is constant (world-frame straight-line motion, $\dot\alpha=\dot\theta_r-\dot v_2=-V_r^{\max}/b+V_a^{\max}\text{-term}\cdot0=0$ via $v_2^*=\pi/2+\phi_d+\omega\tau$, $\omega=V_r^{\max}/b$), i.e., the DDR spins in place while the OA runs straight and the FoV ray sweeps like a lighthouse beam. *Analytic, global on its domain; validity limited to configurations actually reached by (32).*

**Result 4 — Evader's state constraint surface ESCS and its tributaries (pp. 300–301, eqs. (35)–(43)).** *Setup*: the DDR disc border is part of the playing-space boundary (state constraint in the sense of [7,8]); ESCS $=\{r=b,\ \phi=\phi_{sc}\in[0,\phi_d]\}$ with coordinates $x=b\sin\phi_{sc}$, $y=b\cos\phi_{sc}$. *Constraint*: the OA keeps $\dot r=0$ on the ESCS using a non-admissible strategy $v_1(u_1,u_2),v_2(u_1,u_2)$; eq. (35): $v_1\cos(v_2-\phi)=\tfrac{u_1+u_2}{2}\cos\phi$. *Min–max at the surface*, eqs. (36)–(38): $\min_u\max_v\dot\phi>0$; conclusion: the OA's best is tangential motion along the disc at max speed toward the FoV boundary, and the DDR's counter-best is rotation in place ($u_1^*=V_r^{\max}$, $u_2^*=-V_r^{\max}$) — because if the DDR translated at max speed, (2) would force $\dot\theta_r=0$ and the OA would exploit its agility (p. 300). *Assumption to flag*: the text justifies the ESCS by "the evader's rotational and translational speeds are greater than the ones of the DDR" at $r=b$ — the translational part requires $V_a^{\max}>V_r^{\max}$, stronger than the escape threshold $V_a^{\max}>V_r^{\max}/b$ (see Limitations). *Tributaries*: transversality on the ESCS, eq. (39): $\lambda_x=-\cos\phi_{sc},\ \lambda_y=\sin\phi_{sc}$ (tangential vector to the disc); retro costate (40); retro motion, eq. (41):
$$x(\tau)=b\sin\Big(\phi_{sc}+\tau\tfrac{V_r^{\max}}{b}\Big)-\tau V_a^{\max}\cos\Big(\phi_{sc}+\tau\tfrac{V_r^{\max}}{b}\Big),\quad y(\tau)=b\cos\Big(\phi_{sc}+\tau\tfrac{V_r^{\max}}{b}\Big)+\tau V_a^{\max}\sin\Big(\phi_{sc}+\tau\tfrac{V_r^{\max}}{b}\Big).$$
*Smooth-junction property (p. 301)*: (32) with $r_f=b$ coincides with (41) with $\phi_{sc}=\phi_d$ — ESCS tributaries join the primary solution smoothly. *Invariants*: eq. (42) $\sin(\phi_{sc}+\tau\tfrac{V_r^{\max}}{b})x+\cos(\phi_{sc}+\tau\tfrac{V_r^{\max}}{b})y=b$ and eq. (43) $r\sin(\phi_{sc}+\tau\tfrac{V_r^{\max}}{b}-\phi)-\tau V_a^{\max}=0$ (value via numerical root of (43)). *Analytic; valid for $\phi_{sc}\in[0,\phi_d]$, $V_a^{\max}>V_r^{\max}$ implicitly.*

**Result 5 — Pursuer's equivocal surface PES (pp. 301–302, eqs. (44)–(48)).** *Motivation*: primary and/or ESCS-tributary trajectories reach the $y$-axis tangentially; the contact point $Q$ ends the EDS (p. 301). Since the Hamiltonian is linear in $u_1,u_2$, junction-condition theory [8] makes $Q$ the retro-time starting point of an equivocal surface; it is a *PES* because the DDR (non-dominant player) must play a non-admissible strategy there, while the OA (dominant — superior angular capability, as on the EDS) may stay or cross. *Notation*: $S^+$/$S^-$ = left/right side of the PES in retro-time; $\lambda^{S+},\lambda^{S-}$ the corresponding costates; on the PES the OA plays $v^{ES}=v^{S+}$ (strategy continuity across the junction from $S^+$, per [8]). *Junction condition*, eq. (44): $H(\boldsymbol x,\lambda^{S-},u_1^{ES}(v^{ES}),u_2^{ES}(v^{ES}),v_1^{S+},v_2^{S+})=0$ along the PES. *Side $S^+$ construction*: non-tangential arrival + linearity of $H$ in $u_1,u_2$ imply one sign function vanishes at the junction — $u_1$ switches, so on $S^+$ the DDR's retro strategy is backward translation at max speed, $u_1^{S+}=u_2^{S+}=-V_r^{\max}$ (forward: the DDR moves away from the OA at max speed); vanishing of the $u_1$-coefficient gives eqs. (45)–(46): $-y\lambda_x^{S+}+(x-b)\lambda_y^{S+}=0$, satisfied by eq. (47): $\lambda_x^{S+}=x-b,\ \lambda_y^{S+}=y$ (magnitude $\rho=\sqrt{(x-b)^2+y^2}$ unnormalized — irrelevant for (22)-type ratios), whence $v_1^{S+}=V_a^{\max}$, $(\sin v_2^{S+},\cos v_2^{S+})=(-\tfrac{x-b}{\rho},-\tfrac{y}{\rho})$: the OA heads toward the fixed body-frame point $(b,0)$ (my geometric reading). *Side $S^-$ costate*: $\lambda^{S-}$ equals (29) or (40) depending on whether the tangential trajectory at $Q$ is primary or an ESCS tributary; the authors "were not able" to invert (34)/(43) analytically, so $\lambda^{S-}(x,y)$ is computed **numerically**: solve (34)/(43) for $\tau$ at $(x,y)$, substitute into (29)/(40) (p. 302). *PES integration*: $u_1^{ES},u_2^{ES}$ obtained at each step by solving (44) with the current $v^{S+},\lambda^{S-}$; then (30) is integrated numerically from $Q$ — on the PES (forward time) the DDR performs a combined rotation+translation motion (p. 298, Region III). *$S^+$ tributaries*, eq. (48): with $u_1^*=u_2^*=-V_r^{\max}$, costates constant in $S^+$ (from (26) with $u_2^*-u_1^*=0$),
$$x(\tau)=x_{PES}+V_a^{\max}\tfrac{(x-b)}{\rho}\tau,\qquad y(\tau)=y_{PES}+(-V_r^{\max}+V_a^{\max}\tfrac{y}{\rho})\tau.$$
*Semi-analytic* (closed-form tributaries; PES curve itself numerical). **Proof gap (under-determination)**: (44) is a single scalar linear equation for the *two* DDR controls $u_1^{ES},u_2^{ES}$; the text does not state the additional selection rule (e.g., the semi-permeability/stay-on-surface condition or which point of the solution segment inside the control box is taken) that pins the pair down; the implementation detail lives only in the numerics (p. 302).

**Result 6 — Pursuer's state constraint surface PSCS (p. 302, eq. (49)).** *Statement*: part of the FoV boundary ($\phi=\pm\phi_d$, $r>bV_a^{\max}/V_r^{\max}$, where the DDR's max angular rate $V_r^{\max}/b$ exceeds the OA's $V_a^{\max}/r$ — my equivalence, not printed as an inequality chain) is a PSCS: the DDR can guarantee $\dot\phi=0$ with a non-admissible strategy $u_1(v_1,v_2),u_2(v_1,v_2)$, eq. (49):
$$\dot\theta_r=\tfrac{u_2-u_1}{2b}=-\tfrac{v_1}{r}\sin(v_2-\phi)-\tfrac{u_1+u_2}{2}\tfrac{\sin\phi}{r}.$$
On the PSCS the OA minimizes $\dot r$; the DDR rotates to cancel the OA's angular drift while "translat[ing] backward as much as possible" (rotation consumes translational capability via (2)). PSCS tributaries (Region IV): straight-line trajectories with the DDR moving backward at max translational speed; the tributary at the PES–PSCS intersection point is shared by both surfaces (continuity check, p. 302). *No explicit closed-form equations are printed for the PSCS anchor curve or its tributary field beyond (49)* — the thinnest part of the construction.

**Result 7 — Global partition and strategies per region (pp. 297–298, Figs. 2–3).** Four regions bounded by five singular surfaces: **Region I** = primary solution (blue; DDR rotates in place; trajectories reach the UP; in case 2 of Fig. 3 some primary trajectories reach the $y$-axis and define part of the EDS). **Region II** = ESCS + its tributaries (yellow; DDR rotates in place; in case 1 (Fig. 2) part defines an EDS at the $y$-axis, in case 2 (Fig. 3) all of them do). **Region III** = PES + its tributaries (red/green; DDR translates away at max speed on tributaries, combined rotation+translation on the PES). **Region IV** = PSCS + its tributaries (pink; DDR translates backward at max speed, then combined motion keeping the OA inside the FoV). *Singular-surface semantics*: on the EDS the OA is dominant (its angular speed exceeds the DDR's there, so it can push the system to either side; the DDR must play non-admissibly); the EDS ends at $Q$ where the PES begins (retro-time); on the PES the OA decides to stay or cross; on the PDS (defined by PES tributaries reaching the $y$-axis in retro-time) "the DDR moves away from the OA at maximum translational speed" and the OA chooses right/left (p. 298). *Two cases* (Figs. 2 vs. 3, both $V_r^{\max}=1$, $b=1$, $\phi_d=\pi/6$; $V_a^{\max}=2$ vs. $4$): whether the ESCS tributaries alone (case 1) or primary trajectories plus ESCS tributaries (case 2) reach the $y$-axis and define the EDS; **no analytic criterion separating the two cases is given** — the split is established numerically for the tested parameters. *Coverage claim*: the union of the four regions fills the playing space inside the cone (asserted constructively via Figs. 2–3; no formal measure-theoretic completeness proof).

**Summary of the time-optimal escape solution.** Value: at any state, $V(x)=\tau$ obtained by (i) identifying the region (by the geometry of Figs. 2–3), (ii) solving the corresponding implicit equation (34), (43) (or closed-form (48) time) for the retro-time to the terminal surface (UP, ESCS or PES), and (iii) adding the numerically integrated PES traversal where applicable. Winner: DDR iff $V_a^{\max}\le V_r^{\max}/b$; otherwise OA escapes in finite time from every valid state. Termination: $|\phi|>\phi_d$ (escape) with state constraint $r\ge b$; payoff (7); information: feedback + non-admissible play only on singular surfaces.

## 6. Simulation and Experiments
All experiments are numerical simulations of the constructed solution (Section 7, pp. 302–304); no hardware experiments, no external baselines, no metric tables.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
**Author-stated**: future work is to construct strategies for *several cooperating DDRs* maintaining surveillance (p. 304). Numerics are admittedly needed for the equivocal surface and for evaluating the value via (34)/(43) (pp. 293, 302).

**Critical observations (with page/eq. references)**:
1. **Intermediate speed regime not analyzed**: the winner criterion is $V_a^{\max}$ vs. $V_r^{\max}/b$ (p. 297, eq. (15)), but the ESCS construction is justified by "the evader's rotational and translational speeds are greater than the ones of the DDR" (p. 300) — the translational clause requires $V_a^{\max}>V_r^{\max}$. For $b>1$ and $V_r^{\max}/b<V_a^{\max}\le V_r^{\max}$ the UP lies inside the disc yet the paper asserts escape; both simulations use $b=1$ where the two thresholds coincide. Whether the constructed tributaries still cover the space in that regime is open.
2. **PES control under-determined**: eq. (44) is one scalar linear equation for the two DDR controls on the PES; the selection rule producing a unique $(u_1^{ES},u_2^{ES})$ is not stated, and $\lambda^{S-}(x,y)$ is only available numerically (p. 302) — the PES construction is not reproducible from the text alone (proof gap).
3. **Modeling inconsistency in eq. (2)**: $|\dot\theta_r|=\tfrac1b|V_r^{\max}-\tfrac{u_1+u_2}{2}|$ contradicts (1) for backward-rolling wheels (e.g., $u_1=u_2=-V_r^{\max}$ gives LHS 0, RHS $2V_r^{\max}/b$); it holds only on the forward-rolling boundary of the control set. Conclusions drawn from it (max translation ⇒ $\dot\theta_r=0$) remain correct via (1), but the stated relation is not generally valid.
4. **Typographical slips (verified against the rendered PDF)**: the multiplier descriptions of (33)/(34) on p. 300 have sign errors (equations themselves correct — my substitution check); the switching conditions on p. 300 omit a factor $b$ ($r_f-b\sin(\cdot)=0$); neither affects results.
5. **No formal global-optimality proof**: the solution is the classical Isaacs/PMP construction (necessary conditions + junction conditions from [7,8]); no HJI/viscosity verification that the piecewise value is the game value, and completeness of the four-region cover is argued constructively (Figs. 2–3), not proved. The barrier non-existence argument (p. 300, "the resulting trajectory is outside the DDR's FoV and, therefore, is invalid") is stated in two sentences.
6. **Case-1/case-2 split**: no analytic parameter criterion distinguishing Figs. 2 and 3 topologies is provided (p. 297); the boundary in parameter space between them is unknown.
7. **Naming ambiguity for dispersal surfaces**: on both the EDS and the PDS the OA is stated to be the choosing (dominant) player (pp. 297–298), so the E/P prefix convention (which player's trajectories define the surface vs. which player dominates) is not applied consistently in the text.
8. **Idealizations**: point evader, disc pursuer with unit wheel radius, perfect state information, no obstacles/noise/latency/sensor errors, cone half-angle limited to $(0,\pi/2]$, purely kinematic; the OA "avoids being located inside the disc" is assumed, not enforced by the DDR (p. 294). The PSCS analysis (Section 6.6) gives no explicit tributary equations beyond (49) and no value expression on the PSCS itself.

## 8. Citation Value
This paper is the knowledge base's reference for the **DDR-vs-OA surveillance-evasion game with a cone field of view**, completing Ruiz's OA/DDR tetralogy ([P3-adjacent T-RO 2013 capture; IJC 2016 role reversal; IJC 2022 circular surveillance; this paper conical FoV]). Its citable assets are: the speed-ratio winner criterion ($V_a^{\max}\lessgtr V_r^{\max}/b$) with the "no barrier — single outcome region" finding; the closed-form primary/ESCS-tributary trajectories (32)/(41) with their implicit value equations (34)/(43) and the involute/lighthouse geometry (DDR spins in place, OA runs straight); and the rare five-singular-surface taxonomy (EDS, ESCS, PES, PDS, PSCS) demonstrating state-constraint and equivocal surfaces absent from the predecessor games — useful as a comparative benchmark whenever FoV-limited, nonholonomic pursuers are modeled. Cite it when constructing visibility-constrained pursuit-evasion or target-surveillance formulations with kinematic pursuers (camera-equipped UGVs, patrolling robots), when illustrating Isaacs' method with state constraints and equivocal surfaces, or as the DDR-kinematics counterpart of Lewin–Olsder's conic surveillance evasion [6]; pair it with [5] (same players, circular region) and [3,4] (capture versions) when the sensor geometry is the variable of interest. Suggested citation sentences: "Ruiz solved the minimum-time visibility-breaking game between an omnidirectional evader and a differential-drive pursuer with a conical field of view, showing that the winner is decided solely by the speed ratio $V_a^{\max}/(V_r^{\max}/b)$ and that the solution features five singular surfaces, including pursuer's equivocal and both players' state-constraint surfaces [P076]"; or "For a DDR guarding a semi-infinite-cone FoV, escape is time-optimal along involute-type primary trajectories generated by rotation-in-place pursuit, while naive boundary-perpendicular evasion is measurably slower (0.4760 s vs. 0.45 s in the printed example) [P076]."
