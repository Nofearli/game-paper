## 2. Research Background

Isaacs' classical lifeline game (Section 9.5 of Isaacs 1967, as cited by the authors) places two simple-motion players in a half-plane: the evader E wants to reach the bounding line before capture, the pursuer P wants to prevent exactly that. The authors motivate a time-critical variant from applications in which the evader must reach its goal within a prescribed time window because of finite energy, mission requirements, or time-saving goals; they call the deadline the **lifetime** $T_l$. The lifeline game abstracts oil-pipeline protection, guarding a target area, border/coastline protection, and worst-case safe motion planning. Methodologically, the paper positions itself against the Hamilton–Jacobi–Isaacs (HJI) PDE approach to reach-avoid games (Mitchell et al. 2005; Margellos & Lygeros 2011; Zhou et al. 2012), which suffers the curse of dimensionality, and against geometric approaches (Apollonius circles, Voronoi diagrams, paths of defense) that mostly assume point capture (zero capture radius). The paper supplies a closed-form geometric solution — evasion region, barrier, and optimal strategies — for the single pursuer–single evader lifeline game with a non-zero capture radius $l$ and a finite lifetime, plus a multiplayer extension scheme.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap

**Problem.** In the plane split by the lifeline $L=\{y=0\}$ into play region $\Omega_{\mathrm{play}}=\{y>0\}$ and goal region $\Omega_{\mathrm{goal}}=\{y\le 0\}$ (Fig. 1), a slower evader ($E$, speed $\alpha v$, $0<\alpha<1$) starting in $\Omega_{\mathrm{play}}$ must penetrate $L$ within lifetime $T_l$ before getting within capture radius $l>0$ of the pursuer ($P$, speed $v$), who may start anywhere in the plane and wins by capture or by delay. Three problems are posed (p. 4–5): Problem 1 (game of kind: which player wins from $x_0$), Problem 2 (game of degree in the pursuer winning region with payoff "evader-goal terminal distance" $\Phi(\bar x)=\bar y_E$, eq. (6)–(8)), Problem 3 (game of degree in the evader winning region with payoff "evader-pursuer terminal distance" $\Psi(\bar x)=\|\bar x_P-\bar x_E\|_2$, the "safe distance on arrival", eq. (9)–(11)).

**Gap, as claimed by the authors** (to be marked against verification within this PDF):
- [authors' claim, p. 1] "Determining the game winner and finding the strategies for two players are challenging due to non-unique terminal conditions and various initial configurations, which is beyond the scope of the classical Hamilton-Jacobi-Isaacs (HJI) approach." — This is a methodological claim; the paper itself only demonstrates that its geometric route yields closed forms; no HJI computation is run for comparison (see §6–7).
- [authors' claim, p. 3] Isaacs' classical method constructs the lifeline barrier only when P lies in the play region $\Omega_{\mathrm{play}}$; this paper extends to $P\in\Omega_{\mathrm{play}}\cup\Omega_{\mathrm{goal}}$ (stated at §5.1, p. 11; the extension is real in the construction: the admissible tangency-point set $\mathcal P$ in eq. (46) handles $|y_P|<l$).
- [authors' claim, p. 3] "Most of current literature on pursuit-evasion games focuses on point capture (i.e., zero capture radius), when aiming at finding analytical results" — literature-positioning claim consistent with the cited set (Oyler et al. 2016; Yan et al. 2019; Zhou et al. 2016), not independently verifiable from this PDF.
- [authors' claim, p. 19, Conclusion] "This is the first attempt to add the concept of lifetime into lifeline differential games." — priority claim, not verifiable from the PDF.
- [verified from cited text within the PDF] The reduction to the classical Apollonius circle at $l=0$ is genuine: eq. (19)–(21) with $l=0$ reproduces Isaacs' Apollonius construction (the paper cites Isaacs 1967, Section 6.7, p. 7). The non-anticipative information structure adopted as a proof device is standard (cites Mitchell et al. 2005; Elliott & Kalton 1972; Basar & Olsder 1999, p. 11).

## 4. Core Contributions

1. **(Modeling)** Formulation of the lifeline game with limited lifetime: state $\mathbf x=(\mathbf x_P,\mathbf x_E,t)\in\mathbb R^4\times\mathbb R_{\ge0}$, simple-motion dynamics (1); capture set $\mathcal C$ (2); two target sets from lifeline and lifetime, $\mathcal T_1=\{\mathbf x\,|\,y_E=0\}$, $\mathcal T_2=\{\mathbf x\,|\,t=T_l\}$ (3); player target sets $\mathcal T_P=\mathcal C\cup\mathcal T_2$, $\mathcal T_E=\mathcal T_1$ (4); game space $\mathbb E$ and terminal surface $\mathcal C:=\mathcal T_P\cup\mathcal T_E$ (5); two games of degree with payoffs (6)–(11). The winner is decided by which target set the state hits first (p. 4).
2. **(Theory)** Introduction of the **evasion region** $\mathcal R_e$ and its boundary $\mathcal A^l$, the **l-Apollonius circle** (claimed as new; degenerates to Isaacs' Apollonius circle at $l=0$): set characterization (19), explicit polar-coordinate formula (22)–(24), potential function $F(\mathbf z)$ with sign characterization (25)–(27); strict convexity (Lemma 4) and capture-time monotonicity (Lemma 6).
3. **(Theory/strategy)** A pursuit feedback strategy (36) that guarantees the closest-to-goal point $H$ of $\mathcal A^l$ never approaches $L$ ($\dot y_H\ge 0$, Lemma 5), so the eventual capture point keeps a guaranteed distance from the lifeline; equality characterization of E's best response (37).
4. **(Theory, game of kind)** Analytic construction of the barrier without lifetime (Lemma 7, eq. (44)–(46), parametrized by the admissible tangency point $x^*\in\mathcal P$) and with limited lifetime (Theorem 1, eq. (58)–(59)): a four-part barrier $\mathcal B_1$–$\mathcal B_4$ whose pieces are respectively mapped to terminal intersections $\mathcal C\cap\mathcal T_1$, $\mathcal C\cap\mathcal T_1\cap\mathcal T_2$, $\mathcal T_1\cap\mathcal T_2$; identification of the **new pursuer winning region created by the lifetime** (Remark 2) and the global "hat-like" barrier surface in $(x_E,y_E,y_P)$-space with a discontinuity (hole at the hat tip) when $|y_P|<l$ (Remark 3, Fig. 6).
5. **(Theory, games of degree)** Optimal strategies of both players expressed through a single scalar — the **optimal target point (OTP)** $\mathbf z^*$ (62): partition of $\mathcal W_P$ by boundary curves $\Gamma_1$ (70) and $\Gamma_2$ (73) into $\mathcal R_1,\mathcal R_2,\mathcal R_3$ with three distinct OTP regimes (Theorem 2, (74)–(76)), including a dispersal surface at $\mathbf x_P=\mathbf x_E$; partition of $\mathcal W_E$ by $\Gamma_3$ (81) into $\mathcal R_4,\mathcal R_5$ (Theorem 3, (77), (82)).
6. **(Algorithm)** Multiplayer extension by winning-region-based assignment: build a bipartite graph (pursuer $P_i$ → evader $E_j$ iff $x_{E_j}\in\mathcal W_P(\mathbf x_{P_i})$) and run maximum bipartite matching in real time (Section 7, five-step algorithm; Fig. 8). Semi-analytic/approximate; no performance guarantees for unmatched players.
7. **(Experiment)** None — the paper is purely analytical (see §6).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results (exhaustive)

**Setup (pp. 3–5).** Dynamics (1): $\dot{\mathbf x}_P=v\mathbf u_P$, $\dot{\mathbf x}_E=\alpha v\mathbf u_E$, $\mathbf u_P,\mathbf u_E\in U=\{\mathbf u\in\mathbb R^2\,|\,\|\mathbf u\|_2=1\}$, speeds $v>0$ and $\alpha v$ with $0<\alpha<1$ (E strictly slower). Capture set (2) $\mathcal C:=\{\mathbf x\,|\,\|\mathbf x_P-\mathbf x_E\|_2\le l\}$, $l>0$. Targets (3)–(4): $\mathcal T_1=\{\mathbf x|y_E=0\}$ (lifeline), $\mathcal T_2=\{\mathbf x|t=T_l\}$ (lifetime), $\mathcal T_P=\mathcal C\cup\mathcal T_2$, $\mathcal T_E=\mathcal T_1$. Game space and terminal surface (5): $\mathbb E=\{\mathbf x|\mathbf x\notin\mathcal C,\ \mathbf x_E\in\Omega_{\mathrm{play}},\ 0\le t<T_l\}$, terminal surface $\mathcal C:=\mathcal T_P\cup\mathcal T_E$. **Winning conditions** (p. 4): P wins iff the state hits $\mathcal T_P=\mathcal C\cup\mathcal T_2$ first (capture in $\Omega_{\mathrm{play}}$ at $t<T_l$, or delay past $T_l$); E wins iff it hits $\mathcal T_E=\mathcal T_1$ first (reaches $L$ uncaptured within $T_l$). All four terminal intersections appear later as barrier/regime labels: $\mathcal C\cap\mathcal T_1$ (captured exactly upon reaching $L$), $\mathcal C\cap\mathcal T_2$ (captured exactly at lifetime end), $\mathcal T_1\cap\mathcal T_2$ (reaches $L$ exactly at lifetime end), $\mathcal C\cap\mathcal T_1\cap\mathcal T_2$. **Information structure**: perfect information declared (p. 4); a **non-anticipative Stackelberg structure** (P knows E's current input before choosing $u_P$) is introduced in §5.1 as a proof device (p. 11) and later eliminated via uniqueness (eq. (52), p. 13). **Payoffs**: Problem 2 in $\mathcal W_P$: $V_G(x_0):=\max_{\mathbf u_P}\min_{\mathbf u_E}J_G$, $J_G=\Phi(\bar x)=\bar y_E$ (evader-goal terminal distance, eq. (6)–(8)); Problem 3 in $\mathcal W_E$: $V_P(x_0):=\min_{\mathbf u_P}\max_{\mathbf u_E}J_P$, $J_P=\Psi(\bar x)=\|\bar{\mathbf x}_P-\bar{\mathbf x}_E\|_2$ (evader-pursuer terminal distance / safe distance on arrival, eq. (9)–(11)).

- **Lemma 1 (Strictly Decreasing Function) (p. 6, eq. (12)–(16)).** For $k_1\ge0$, $k_2>0$, $k_3\ge0$ with $k_1+k_3>0$, define $G(\beta)=1+\dfrac{k_1\beta^2-k_2(\beta-1)^2}{(\beta-1)^2(k_3\beta^2+k_2)}$ (12) on the interval $\beta<0,\ (\sqrt{k_2}-\sqrt{k_1})\beta<\sqrt{k_2}$ (13). Then $G$ is strictly decreasing there with values in $(0,1)$. *Proof*: $G'(\beta)=2\beta\,\dfrac{G_1(\beta)-k_1k_2}{(\beta-1)^3(k_3\beta^2+k_2)^2}$ (14) with $G_1(\beta)=k_2k_3(\beta-1)^3-k_1k_3\beta^3$ (15). Case $k_3=0$: $G'(\beta)=2\beta\frac{-k_1}{(\beta-1)^3k_2}<0$ (16). Case $k_3>0$: factorization $G_1'(\beta)=3k_3\big(\sqrt{k_2}(\beta-1)+\sqrt{k_1}\beta\big)\big((\sqrt{k_2}-\sqrt{k_1})\beta-\sqrt{k_2}\big)$ (17) is positive on (13), hence $G_1(\beta)<G_1(0)=-k_2k_3<0\Rightarrow G'(\beta)<0$ (18); limits $G\to0$ as $\beta\to0$ and $G\to1$ at the far end of (13). *Decisive step*: the sign factorization (17) together with the domain constraint (13). Analytic, global on (13); used only to prove uniqueness in Lemma 4. *Minor imprecision (verified by recomputation)*: when $k_3=0$ and $k_2>k_1$, $G(\beta)=\frac{k_1}{k_2}\frac{\beta^2}{(\beta-1)^2}\to k_1/k_2<1$ as $\beta\to-\infty$, so the range is a proper subset of $(0,1)$ rather than onto it; strict monotonicity and the uniqueness application are unaffected.

- **Lemma 2 (Optimal Trajectories) (p. 6–7, no eq.).** For the games (1)+(6)–(8) or (1)+(9)–(11), the optimal trajectories of P and E are both straight lines. *Proof (entire)*: "follows from the fact that two players have simple motion and the cost is of Meyer type" (p. 7). *Proof gap*: no formal derivation (no PMP/Isaacs path-equation argument, no verification that the straight-line candidate is globally optimal under each player's information structure); the lemma underpins the OTP construction (62) and Lemma 7's eq. (47)–(48), so this is the paper's most consequential informal step.

- **Lemma 3 (Evasion Region) (p. 7, eq. (19)–(24) and (25)–(27)).** Assumption $\|\mathbf x_P-\mathbf x_E\|_2>l$. E's evasion region and its boundary: $\mathcal R_e=\{\mathbf z\,|\,\|\mathbf z-\mathbf x_E\|_2<\alpha(\|\mathbf z-\mathbf x_P\|_2-l)\}$, $\mathcal A^l=\{\mathbf z\,|\,\|\mathbf z-\mathbf x_E\|_2=\alpha(\|\mathbf z-\mathbf x_P\|_2-l)\}$ (19). *Proof approach*: travel-time comparison — E reaches $\mathbf z$ before P can be within $l$ of it iff $\|\mathbf z-\mathbf x_E\|_2<\alpha(\|\mathbf z-\mathbf x_P\|_2-l)$ (20). Polar coordinates about $\mathbf x_E$ ($\mathbf z=\mathbf x_E+\rho\mathbf e$): the boundary condition becomes $(1-\alpha^2)\rho^2-2(\alpha^2(\mathbf x_E-\mathbf x_P)^\mathsf T\mathbf e-\alpha l)\rho+\alpha^2(l^2-\|\mathbf x_E-\mathbf x_P\|_2^2)=0$ (22), whose unique admissible root is $\rho=\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha^2}\Big(\alpha(\mathbf x_E-\mathbf x_P)^\mathsf T\mathbf e-l+\sqrt{(\alpha(\mathbf x_E-\mathbf x_P)^\mathsf T\mathbf e-l)^2+(1-\alpha^2)(\|\mathbf x_E-\mathbf x_P\|_2^2-l^2)}\Big)$ (23) — the explicit **l-Apollonius circle** (24); at $l=0$ it degenerates to Isaacs' Apollonius circle (Isaacs 1967 §6.7). Companion potential function $F(\mathbf z)=\|\mathbf z-\mathbf x_P\|_2-\frac{\|\mathbf z-\mathbf x_E\|_2}{\alpha}-l$ (25) with $F(\mathbf z)>0\Leftrightarrow \mathbf z\in\mathcal R_e$, $F(\mathbf z)=0\Leftrightarrow\mathbf z\in\mathcal A^l$ (26) and gradient (27); this potential is the analytic backbone of Lemmas 4–5 and Theorems 2–3. Global, analytic, real-time computable. *Proof gap (minor)*: the sufficiency direction ("$\mathbf z$ satisfying (20) ⇒ E reaches it before capture") is argued only for P moving directly towards $\mathbf z$; the arbitrary-P case needed by the definition "regardless of P's best effort" holds by a standard triangle-inequality argument but is not written out.

- **Lemma 4 (Convex Property) (p. 8–9, eq. (28)–(35)).** $\mathcal R_e$ is strictly convex (for $\|\mathbf x_P-\mathbf x_E\|_2>l$, $\mathbf x_E\in\Omega_{\mathrm{play}}$). *Proof approach*: approach $\mathcal A^l$ with a line $S$ of fixed slope until first tangency at $I$; fix the origin at $I$ with $S$ as x-axis; tangency ⟺ the system $F(\mathbf z)=0,\ y=0,\ F_x(\mathbf z)=0$ (28) has a unique solution. Substituting $\mathbf x=\beta\mathbf x_P+(1-\beta)\mathbf x_E$ reduces (28) to $G(\beta)=\alpha^2$ with $k_1=y_P^2,\ k_2=y_E^2,\ k_3=(x_E-x_P)^2$ (32)–(33), with $\beta\in(-\infty,0)$ from (29)–(31); uniqueness follows from Lemma 1 (sign analysis (34)). Degenerate case $\mathbf x_E=\mathbf x_P$ solved directly. *Corollary built into the proof*: the closest point $H$ of $\mathcal A^l$ to $\Omega_{\mathrm{goal}}$ is the **unique** solution of $F(\mathbf x_H)=0,\ F_x(\mathbf x_H)=0,\ F_y(\mathbf x_H)>0$ (35); $y_H$ is the minimum distance from $\mathcal A^l$ to the goal region. This uniqueness is what makes $\dot{\mathbf x}_H$ well defined (implicit-function argument $x_H=g(x_P,x_E)$, stated explicitly on p. 10) and is reused to prove the barrier independent of the information structure (52). Decisive step: reduction of a geometric tangency to the monotonicity of $G$. Analytic, global.

- **Lemma 5 (Pursuit and Evasion Strategies) (p. 9–10, eq. (36)–(40)).** Assumption $\mathcal A^l\cap\Omega_{\mathrm{goal}}=\emptyset$. If P plays the feedback $u_P=\frac{x_H-x_P}{\|x_H-x_P\|_2}$ (36), then $\dot y_H\ge0$ (the l-Apollonius circle never approaches $L$), with $\dot y_H=0$ **iff** E plays $u_E=\frac{x_H-x_E}{\|x_H-x_E\|_2}$ (37). *Proof*: differentiate $F(x_H)=0$ along trajectories (38) to get $\Big(\frac{x_H-x_P}{\|x_H-x_P\|_2}-\frac{x_H-x_E}{\alpha\|x_H-x_E\|_2}\Big)^{\!\mathsf T}\dot x_H=v-\frac{v(x_H-x_E)^{\mathsf T}u_E}{\|x_H-x_E\|_2}\ge v-v=0$ (39) (Cauchy–Schwarz; equality iff (37)); the left side equals $F_x(x_H)\dot x_H+F_y(x_H)\dot y_H=F_y(x_H)\dot y_H$ (40) since $F_x(x_H)=0$ by (35), and $F_y(x_H)>0$ gives $\dot y_H\ge0$. *Physical meaning*: by always aiming at the closest-to-goal point of the l-Apollonius circle, P guarantees the eventual capture point stays at least its current distance from the lifeline — "capture with a guaranteed distance to $\Omega_{\mathrm{goal}}$" (Remark 1 adds that the same strategy drives an evader away from any straight line). Requires uniqueness of $H$ (Lemma 4). Analytic, feedback (global in state along the play).

- **Lemma 6 (Capture Time) (p. 10–11, eq. (41)–(43)).** The line through P and E divides $\mathcal A^l$ into two curves symmetric about it, and the capture time is strictly monotone on each. *Proof*: Lagrangian $\mathcal H(z,\lambda)=\|z-x_E\|_2+\lambda\big(\|z-x_E\|_2-\alpha(\|z-x_P\|_2-l)\big)$ (41); stationarity $(1+\lambda)\frac{z^*-x_E}{\|z^*-x_E\|_2}=\alpha\lambda\frac{z^*-x_P}{\|z^*-x_P\|_2}$ (42) forces collinearity, giving $(x^*-x_E)(y_E-y_P)=0$ (43), hence the only extreme points are the two intersections $Q,R$ with the line $PE$. Global, analytic; used in Theorem 2(II) to assert at most two points of $\mathcal A^l$ have capture time $T_l$.

- **Lemma 7 (Barrier without Lifetime Limitation) (p. 11–13, eq. (44)–(52)).** Assumptions: $0<\alpha<1$, $l>0$, $T_l=\infty$ (so $\mathcal T_P=\mathcal C$), E in $\Omega_{\mathrm{play}}$ ($y_E>0$), P at $x_P=(x_P,y_P)$ arbitrary. The barrier $\mathcal B^\infty$ is the curve, parametrized by the tangency point $z^*=(x^*,0)\in L$ with $x^*\in\mathcal P$, $$\mathcal B^\infty:\ x=x^*-\alpha^2ab,\quad y=\alpha a\sqrt{1-\alpha^2b^2}\ (44)$$ where $a=\|z^*-x_P\|_2-l$ (minimum distance from P's capture disk to the OTP), $b=\frac{x^*-x_P}{\|z^*-x_P\|_2}$ (45), and the admissible tangency set $\mathcal P=\mathbb R$ if $|y_P|\ge l$, $\{x^*\,|\,|x^*-x_P|\ge\sqrt{l^2-y_P^2}\}$ if $|y_P|<l$ (46). *Proof approach*: under the non-anticipative structure and Lemma 2, if E exits at $z=(x,0)$, P's optimal reply is to run straight at $z$, so $J_P(x)=\|z-x_P\|_2-\frac{\|z-x_E\|_2}{\alpha}$ (47) = the P–E separation when E reaches $z$; note $J_P(x)=F(z)+l$. The optimal target point (OTP) satisfies $\frac{\mathrm dJ_P(x^*)}{\mathrm dx}=0\Rightarrow\frac{x^*-x_P}{\|z^*-x_P\|_2}=\frac{x^*-x_E}{\alpha\|z^*-x_E\|_2}$ (48); on the barrier E is captured exactly upon reaching $z^*$: $J_P(x^*)=l\Leftrightarrow\|z^*-x_E\|_2=\alpha(\|z^*-x_P\|_2-l)$ (49), which forces $x^*\in\mathcal P$; (48)–(49) reduce to $\alpha b\|z^*-x_E\|_2=x^*-x_E$, $\|z^*-x_E\|_2=\alpha a$ (50), whence $x_E=x^*-\alpha^2ab$, $y_E=\alpha a\sqrt{1-\alpha^2b^2}$ (51). *Perfect information*: (48)–(49) ⟺ $F_x(z^*)=0,\ F(z^*)=0$ (52), which by the uniqueness argument of Lemma 4 has a unique solution depending only on current positions — E's current input is redundant, so the barrier is information-structure independent. *Geometric meaning*: $\mathcal B^\infty$ is the locus of E positions for which $\mathcal A^l$ is **tangent to $L$** (its closest point to $\Omega_{\mathrm{goal}}$ lies on $L$); E on the barrier is captured exactly at the tangency point. The set $\mathcal P$ handles P starting in the goal region, including $|y_P|<l$ where the barrier is discontinuous. *Proof gap (minor)*: that the stationary point (48) is the relevant constrained maximum of $J_P$ over E's feasible exit interval on $L$ (i.e., the global-max step) is not spelled out — it follows from strict convexity of $\mathcal R_e$ but only Fig. 5 is cited.

- **Theorem 1 (Barrier with Limited Lifetime) (p. 14–15, eq. (53)–(61); statement (58)–(59)).** Assumptions: $0<\alpha<1$, $l>0$, $0<T_l<\infty$, P at $x_P=(x_P,y_P)$, E in $\Omega_{\mathrm{play}}$. **Case $|y_P|>vT_l+l$**: P is too far from $L$ to matter, and the barrier is the pure lifetime line $\mathcal B:\ x\in\mathbb R,\ y=\alpha vT_l$ (58). **Case $|y_P|\le vT_l+l$**: $\mathcal B$ is symmetric about $x=x_P$ and consists of four parts (59): $$\mathcal B_1: x=x^*-\alpha^2ab,\ y=\alpha a\sqrt{1-\alpha^2b^2}\quad(x_A\le x\le x_D)$$ (the middle piece of $\mathcal B^\infty$, with $x^*\in\mathcal P$); $$\mathcal B_2: (x-x_B)^2+y^2=\alpha^2v^2T_l^2\quad(x_C<x<x_A,\ y>0),\qquad \mathcal B_3: (x-x_M)^2+y^2=\alpha^2v^2T_l^2\quad(x_D<x<x_N,\ y>0)$$ (arcs of the circles of radius $\alpha vT_l$ about the tangency points $B,M$); $$\mathcal B_4: y=\alpha vT_l\quad(x\le x_C\ \text{or}\ x\ge x_N).$$ Key points (construction, eq. (53), (55), (57)): from $a=vT_l$ and $b=\frac{x_B-x_P}{vT_l+l}$, $\|x_B-x_P\|_2=vT_l+l$ and $x_B-x_A=\alpha^2vT_l\frac{x_B-x_P}{vT_l+l}$, giving $x_B=x_P-\sqrt{(vT_l+l)^2-y_P^2}$, $y_B=0$, $x_A=x_B+\frac{\alpha^2vT_l\sqrt{(vT_l+l)^2-y_P^2}}{vT_l+l}$; $x_C=x_B,\ y_C=\alpha vT_l$ (55); mirrored $x_M=x_P+\sqrt{(vT_l+l)^2-y_P^2}$, $x_D=x_M-\frac{\alpha^2vT_l\sqrt{(vT_l+l)^2-y_P^2}}{vT_l+l}$, $x_N=x_M,\ y_N=\alpha vT_l$ (57). *Proof approach*: case analysis on which terminal intersection the state hits first from the barrier — for $x_E\in[x_A,x_D]$: $\mathcal C\cap\mathcal T_1$, recovering (51) as (60) ($\mathcal B_1$); for $x_E\in(x_C,x_A)$ (resp. $(x_D,x_N)$): $\mathcal C\cap\mathcal T_1\cap\mathcal T_2$ with OTP $x_B$ (resp. $x_M$), so $\|x_E-x_B\|_2=\alpha vT_l$ (54) and $\|x_E-x_M\|_2=\alpha vT_l$ (61) ($\mathcal B_2,\mathcal B_3$); for $x_E\in(-\infty,x_C]\cup[x_N,+\infty)$: $\mathcal T_1\cap\mathcal T_2$ with E running straight to $L$, $y_E=\alpha vT_l$ (56) ($\mathcal B_4$). *Decisive intermediate relations*: (53)–(54). Local (fixed $x_P$), analytic, global over the play region. *Physical meaning*: the lifetime **expands the pursuer winning region** $\mathcal W_P$ and shrinks $\mathcal W_E$ (Remark 2): the region between $\mathcal B^\infty$ and $\mathcal B$ (dot-dash-shaded in Fig. 5(b)) is now P-winning because E cannot arrive in time. Remark 3 + Fig. 6: for $x_P=0$ and all $y_P$, $\mathcal B$ is a "hat-like" surface in $(x_E,y_E,y_P)$-space with a hole at the tip caused by the discontinuity of the admissible set $\mathcal P$ when $|y_P|<l$; for $|y_P|>vT_l+l$ the hat flattens to the horizontal line $y_E=\alpha vT_l$.

- **Theorem 2 (Optimal Strategies in Pursuer Winning Region) (p. 16–18, eq. (62)–(76)).** Assumptions: $x_E\in\mathcal W_P$; game (1)+(6)–(8) (P maximizes / E minimizes the evader-goal terminal distance). Both optimal trajectories are straight lines ending at the OTP $z^*$, so the optimal strategies are $u_P=\frac{z^*-x_P}{\|z^*-x_P\|_2}$, $u_E=\frac{z^*-x_E}{\|z^*-x_E\|_2}$ (62); the entire game of degree reduces to locating $z^*$. $\mathcal W_P$ is divided by the boundary curves $\Gamma_1\subset\mathcal W_P$ (70) and $\Gamma_2\subset\mathcal W_P$ (73) into three regions $\mathcal R_1,\mathcal R_2,\mathcal R_3$:
  - **(I) $\mathcal R_1$**: OTP unique, $F(z^*)=0,\ F_x(z^*)=0,\ F_y(z^*)>0$ (74) — i.e., $z^*=H$, the closest point of $\mathcal A^l$ to $L$; E is captured within the lifetime ($\mathbf x$ hits $\mathcal C$ before $\mathcal T_1,\mathcal T_2$).
  - **(II) $\mathcal R_2$**: OTP satisfies $F(z^*)=0,\ \|z^*-x_E\|_2=\alpha vT_l$ (75) — $z^*$ lies on $\mathcal A^l$ at exactly lifetime travel distance; E is captured exactly when the lifetime ends ($\mathbf x$ hits $\mathcal C\cap\mathcal T_2$ before $\mathcal T_1$). If $x_P\ne x_E$, the solution closer to $L$ is the unique OTP; if $x_P=x_E$, there is a **dispersal surface** (Isaacs 1967 Ch. 6.2; Von Moll et al. 2019) with two alternative OTPs.
  - **(III) $\mathcal R_3$**: OTP unique, $x^*=x_E,\ y^*=y_E-\alpha vT_l$ (76) — E runs straight to $L$ uncaptured and the lifetime runs out ($\mathbf x$ hits $\mathcal T_2$ first).
  *Boundary-curve construction* (pp. 15–16): with $k_E:=\alpha\|z^*-x_E\|_2=\alpha^2vT_l$ and $k_P:=\|z^*-x_P\|_2=vT_l+l$, the stationary condition gives $x^*=\frac{k_Px_E-k_Ex_P}{k_P-k_E}$ (65); parametrizing $x^*=x_P+k_P\cos\theta,\ y^*=y_P+k_P\sin\theta$ (67) and using the squared $F_x(z^*)=0$ relation (66) with $y_E>y^*$ yields $\Gamma_1: x=x_P+(k_P-k_E)\cos\theta,\ y=y_P+k_P\sin\theta+k_E\sqrt{1/\alpha^2-\cos^2\theta}$, $\theta\in[0,2\pi[$ (68)–(70) (locus where capture time = lifetime with OTP $=H$, condition (64)); and $\Gamma_2: x=x_P+k_P\cos\theta,\ y=y_P+k_E/\alpha+k_P\sin\theta$, $\theta\in[0,2\pi[$ (71)–(73) (locus where the straight-to-$L$ strategy ends in capture exactly at arrival, condition (72)). *Proof approach*: geometric ("see Fig. 7") — in $\mathcal R_1$ E can still reach $H$ within $T_l$; in $\mathcal R_3$ E outruns capture; in $\mathcal R_2$ neither holds, so capture and lifetime expiry coincide and $z^*$ is pinned by (75) plus Lemma 6's monotonicity (at most two candidates; take the one closer to $L$). *Proof gaps*: no formal saddle-point verification (no unilateral-deviation inequalities); the "closer to $L$" selection is argued by monotonicity, not by an explicit payoff comparison; P's optimality in $\mathcal R_2,\mathcal R_3$ rests on the non-anticipative reasoning of §5.1 and Lemma 2 rather than a written-out verification; the dispersal surface is identified but no tie-breaking rule is given.

- **Theorem 3 (Optimal Strategies in Evader Winning Region) (p. 18–19, eq. (77)–(83)).** Assumptions: $x_E\in\mathcal W_E$; game (1)+(9)–(11) (E maximizes / P minimizes the safe distance on arrival). Strategies again of the form (62); $\mathcal W_E$ is divided by $\Gamma_3\subset\mathcal W_E$ (81) into $\mathcal R_4,\mathcal R_5$:
  - **(I) $\mathcal R_4$**: OTP $z^*=(x^*,0)$ unique, given by $F_x(z^*)=0$ (77) — the stationary safe-exit point on $L$; E reaches $L$ within the lifetime ($\mathbf x$ hits $\mathcal T_1$ before $\mathcal C,\mathcal T_2$).
  - **(II) $\mathcal R_5$**: lifetime is the binding constraint; E reaches $L$ exactly when it expires ($\mathbf x$ hits $\mathcal T_1\cap\mathcal T_2$ before $\mathcal C$) and the OTP is $x^*=\begin{cases}x_E-\sqrt{\alpha^2v^2T_l^2-y_E^2} & (x_E\le x_P)\\ x_E+\sqrt{\alpha^2v^2T_l^2-y_E^2} & (x_E>x_P)\end{cases}$ (82) — of the two intersections of the radius-$\alpha vT_l$ circle around $x_E$ with $L$, E takes the one **farther from P**.
  *Boundary-curve construction* (p. 18): the critical case (capture time $=T_l$ with only $\mathcal T_1$ hit) satisfies $F_x(z^*)=0,\ z^*=(x^*,0),\ \|z^*-x_E\|_2=\alpha vT_l$ (78); parametrizing $x_E=x^*+\alpha vT_l\cos\theta,\ y_E=\alpha vT_l\sin\theta$, $\theta\in[0,\pi[$ (79) and substituting into $F_x(z^*)=0$ gives $\frac{-\cos\theta}{\alpha}=\frac{x_E-x_P-\alpha vT_l\cos\theta}{\sqrt{(x_E-x_P-\alpha vT_l\cos\theta)^2+y_P^2}}$, hence $\sqrt{\alpha^2-\cos^2\theta}\,(x_E-x_P-\alpha vT_l\cos\theta)=-|y_P|\cos\theta$ (80) with $\theta\in[\arccos\alpha,\ \pi-\arccos\alpha]$; therefore $\Gamma_3: \sqrt{\alpha^2-\cos^2\theta}\,x=\sqrt{\alpha^2-\cos^2\theta}\,(x_P+\alpha vT_l\cos\theta)-|y_P|\cos\theta,\ y=\alpha vT_l\sin\theta$ (81). *Proof approach*: geometric; uniqueness of the $\mathcal R_4$ OTP "by following the proof of Lemma 4" (strict convexity of $\mathcal R_e$ restricted to $L$); $\mathcal R_5$ by direct geometry of (83). *Proof gaps*: as in Theorem 2 — no formal minmax verification; the sign choice in (82) is justified verbally ("E naturally should select the one of two solutions farther from P", p. 19).

- **Multiplayer extension (Section 7, p. 19; no equation numbers).** For each pursuer compute $\mathcal W_P(x_{P_i})$; evader $E_j$ is assignable to $P_i$ iff $x_{E_j}\in\mathcal W_P(x_{P_i})$; build the bipartite graph and run **maximum bipartite matching** (citing Chen, Zhou & Tomlin 2017 and Shishika & Kumar 2019); matched pairs play the Theorem 2 strategies; unmatched players play arbitrarily; the matching is recomputed in real time as more evaders become winnable. Five-step algorithm printed verbatim (pp. 19); Fig. 8 illustrates 5 pursuers vs 4 evaders. This is an assignment heuristic — no guarantee is claimed for unmatched evaders, and inter-team cooperation/coordination beyond matching is deferred to future work.

**Summary of what is new analytically**: the limited-lifetime lifeline setup reduces the game of kind to a four-piece barrier (Theorem 1) assembled from (i) the lifetime-free tangency barrier $\mathcal B^\infty$ (Lemma 7), (ii) two circles of radius $\alpha vT_l$ about the tangency points $B$ and $M$, and (iii) the horizontal lifetime line $y=\alpha vT_l$; and the games of degree reduce, in all five regions $\mathcal R_1$–$\mathcal R_5$, to computing a single scalar OTP $z^*$ from (74), (75), (76), (77), or (82) and applying (62).

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues

**Author-stated** (p. 19, Conclusion; Section 7): future work on "lifetime games with closer cooperation between multiple players, complex dynamics and general game domains"; the multiplayer treatment is an assignment scheme, not a solved multiplayer game; dynamics restricted to simple motion; domain restricted to a half-plane lifeline.

**Critical observations (with page/eq. anchors)**:
1. **Lemma 2 is unproven in substance** (p. 7): one sentence invoking "simple motion" and "Meyer type" cost. Straight-line optimality of *both* players is the foundation of eq. (47)–(48), (62) and of Theorems 2–3; a formal derivation (e.g., via PMP or Isaacs' path equations under the stated information structures) is absent — the main proof gap of the paper.
2. **Lemma 3's sufficiency direction is informal** (p. 7): "E can reach $\mathbf z$ prior to the capture" is argued only against P heading to $\mathbf z$; the definition of $\mathcal R_e$ ("regardless of P's best effort") requires all P strategies. The gap is closeable by a triangle-inequality argument, but it is not given.
3. **Lemma 1's value-domain claim is loose for $k_3=0,k_2>k_1$** (p. 6, eq. (12),(16)): $G(\beta)=\frac{k_1}{k_2}\frac{\beta^2}{(\beta-1)^2}\to k_1/k_2<1$ as $\beta\to-\infty$, so the range is a proper subset of $(0,1)$, not onto; only the monotonicity/uniqueness (used in Lemma 4) is needed, so the flaw is benign.
4. **Games of degree lack saddle-point verification** (Theorems 2–3, pp. 16–19): the proofs are geometric case analyses ("see Fig. 7"); no inequality shows that the announced OTP strategies are mutually best responses against arbitrary deviations; Lemma 2's gaps propagate here. The dispersal surface at $x_P=x_E$ (Theorem 2(II), p. 17, after (75)) is identified but not resolved (no strategy selection or value statement on it).
5. **Global-optimality step in the barrier construction is implicit** (Lemma 7, eq. (48)): that the stationary point of $J_P$ over $L$ is the constrained maximum relevant to E's feasible exit set is argued through Fig. 5 rather than analytically; likewise barrier symmetry about $x=x_P$ (p. 13) is asserted, and continuity at the junction points $A,C,D,M,N$ is only illustrated.
6. **Model restrictions**: E strictly slower ($\alpha<1$, eq. (1) context, p. 3); straight-line lifeline and half-plane split (Fig. 1); P's winning-region analysis fixes $x_P$ and treats $\mathcal W_P,\mathcal W_E$ as functions of $x_P$ (p. 11), so moving configurations rely on the real-time recomputability claim; barrier analysis assumes $y_E>0$ ($x_E\in\Omega_{\mathrm{play}}$, used in eq. (50)–(51) derivation, p. 12).
7. **No empirical validation whatsoever** (§6): no numeric example, no HJI comparison on a low-dimensional instance, no robustness study; the "real-time" claim is unsupported.
8. **Multiplayer scheme is heuristic** (p. 19): maximum matching on pairwise winning regions ignores payoff coupling between simultaneous pursuits and leaves unmatched players' guarantees undefined.

## 8. Citation Value

This is the canonical reference for the **lifeline differential game under a hard deadline**: it is the first (by the authors' claim, p. 19) to inject a limited lifetime into Isaacs' lifeline game, and it delivers a complete closed-form solution — l-Apollonius evasion region (Lemma 3), tangency barrier with and without lifetime (Lemma 7, Theorem 1), and region-wise optimal strategies via the OTP construction (Theorems 2–3) — all for a non-zero capture radius, a setting where most analytical reach-avoid results still assume point capture. In a paper's related-work section it serves as (i) the geometric alternative to HJI reach-avoid computation for line-goal problems, citable when a barrier/winning-region characterization with closed-form strategies is needed; (ii) the source of the l-Apollonius circle when handling finite capture radii in Apollonius-based multiplayer pursuit; and (iii) the natural baseline for time-critical (deadline-constrained) pursuit-evasion formulations. Suggested citation sentence: "Yan, Shi and Zhong [P102] solve the lifeline differential game with a limited lifetime in closed form, introducing the l-Apollonius circle to characterize the evasion region for non-zero capture radius, constructing the four-part barrier that enlarges the pursuer's winning region, and deriving region-wise saddle-point strategies through an optimal-target-point construction, with a maximum-bipartite-matching extension to multiplayer cases."
