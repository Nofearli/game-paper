## 2. Research Background
Differential games founded by Isaacs [1 of the paper] are the standard tool for adversarial agent conflicts; within them, the reach-avoid game (attacker tries to reach a target set, defender tries to capture it or delay it indefinitely) is a canonical "game of kind" whose solution is a semi-permeable barrier partitioning the joint state space into an attack dominance region (ADR) and a defense dominance region (DDR). The classical solution engine is the Hamilton–Jacobi–Isaacs (HJI) PDE, which is computationally heavy and rarely admits closed forms beyond low-dimensional, simple-motion settings. The authors observe that in real engagements (missiles, UAVs) agents have finite energy/mission time, yet almost the entire reach-avoid literature lets the confrontation last indefinitely; the only prior exception they identify is the limited-lifetime lifeline game of Yan et al. [18], where only the evader carries a time limit. This paper builds a complete, closed-form barrier theory for the planar reach-avoid game in which **both** players have maximum operating times $\bar t_A$, $\bar t_D$, so that the game naturally decomposes into a two-sided confrontation phase before $t_{\min}=\min\{\bar t_A,\bar t_D\}$ and a one-sided endgame after it.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
**Problem.** Two simple-motion players on $\mathbb R^2$: an attacker $A$ (speed $v_A$) and a faster defender $D$ (speed $v_D>v_A$, $\alpha=v_A/v_D<1$). The line $\mathcal T=\{y=c_T\}$ splits the plane into play region $\Omega_P=\{y>c_T\}$ and target region $\Omega_T=\{y\le c_T\}$; $D$ starts at the origin, x-axis parallel to $\mathcal T$ (p. 194, Fig. 1). $A$ wins by entering $\Omega_T$ within its own time limit $\bar t_A$ without being captured; $D$ wins by capturing $A$ within $\bar t_D$ or forcing $A$ to terminate (at $\bar t_A$) inside $\Omega_P$. Point capture only; full state information; simultaneous, instantly switchable heading controls (p. 194). The game of kind question: given $(x_A^0,x_D^0,v_A,v_D,\bar t_A,\bar t_D)$, who wins — answered by constructing the barrier $\mathcal B$ in $\mathbb R^4$.

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Modeling**: first formulation of a planar reach-avoid game with time limits on **both** players (p. 193–195), classified by the pair $(\Delta t=\bar t_A-\bar t_D,\; m=\hat r_D/l^0)$ into two stages and three circle-relation subgames (inclusion / intersection / separation of $\Omega_A,\Omega_D$).
2. **Concepts/tools** (theory): the *reaching region* (RR, from Yan et al. [31]) $\Omega_{ARR}=\Omega_A\cap\Omega_{AD}$ with the AD-based Apollonius circle (Properties 1–2, Remarks 1–2, p. 196–197), and the *optimal target point* (OTP) — the feasible point of smallest y-coordinate in $\Omega_{ARR}$ whose location relative to $\mathcal T$ decides the game; on the barrier the OTP lies exactly on $\mathcal T$ (neutral/equilibrium outcome).
3. **Analytic barriers** (theory): closed-form polar-coordinate expressions of the barrier for all three subgames (Theorems 1, 2, 6) and the **complete composite barrier** $\mathcal B_\alpha$ with both time limits (Theorems 7, 8, 9, eqs. (53), (66), (67), plus degenerate cases (54), (55), (68), (69)), including the discovery that for $\Delta t>0$ an **additional connecting arc** $\mathcal B^{ADD}_\alpha$ and even a **disconnected DDR** ($\Omega_{DDR1}\cup\Omega_{DDR2}$, eq. (69), Fig. 7) can occur.
4. **By-products** (theory): barriers with a time limit of one player only, $\mathcal B^{rA}$ (attacker only — identical to the $\Delta t\le0$ case of Theorem 7) and $\mathcal B^{rD}$ (defender only, eqs. (70)–(71)), with the monotonicity statement that adding the defender's time limit can only shrink the DDR (p. 205).
5. **Extension recipe** (algorithm): multiplayer $N_A$-vs-$N_D$ generalization by pairwise DDR evaluation → bipartite graph → maximum matching (Hopcroft–Karp or LP, per [23]) → defender-to-attacker assignment, re-run in real time (p. 205–206, Algorithm 1, Figs. 8–9).
6. **Zero online computation** (claim, validated qualitatively by simulation): the outcome for any initial state is obtained by substituting into closed-form expressions; simulations (Figs. 10–11) confirm DDR/ADR membership predicts capture vs. successful penetration.

### 5.0 Setup, dynamics, information, termination (p. 194–196)
- Dynamics, eq. (1): $\dot x_A=v_A\cos\phi_A,\ \dot y_A=v_A\sin\phi_A;\ \dot x_D=v_D\cos\phi_D,\ \dot y_D=v_D\sin\phi_D$, $\phi_A,\phi_D\in[-\pi,\pi)$, constant speeds, $\alpha=v_A/v_D<1$. State $\mathbf x=(\mathbf x_A,\mathbf x_D)\in\mathbb R^4$; origin at $x_D^0$; polar system (eq. (3)) with pole at $x_D^0$, polar axis = Cartesian x-axis, $\mathbf x_A=(\rho_A,\theta_A)$; $l=\|\mathbf x_A-\mathbf x_D\|_2$ (eq. (2)), $l^0=\rho_A^0$, $m=\hat r_D/\rho_A^0$.
- Termination/winning: $A$ reaches $\Omega_T$ ($\mathcal T$: $y=c_T$) within $\bar t_A$ uncaptured → $A$ wins; $D$ captures within $\bar t_D$ or $A$ still in $\Omega_P$ at $\bar t_A$ → $D$ wins; simultaneous achievement at the OTP on $\mathcal T$ = neutral/equilibrium outcome. Assumption 1: $y_A^0>c_T$; Assumption 2: $\|x_A^0-x_D^0\|_2>0$ (p. 194).
- Game-of-kind conversion, eqs. (5)–(9) (p. 196): Mayer payoff $J=Q(\mathbf x(t_f))\in\{+1,0,-1\}$ ($A$ wins / equilibrium / $D$ wins); Hamiltonian eq. (6) $\mathcal H=\lambda_1v_A\cos\phi_A+\lambda_2v_A\sin\phi_A+\lambda_3v_D\cos\phi_D+\lambda_4v_D\sin\phi_D$; saddle condition eq. (8) $\mathcal H(\mathbf x,\boldsymbol\lambda,\phi_A^*,\phi_D^*)=\min_{\phi_D}\max_{\phi_A}\mathcal H=\max_{\phi_A}\min_{\phi_D}\mathcal H$; optimality eq. (9): $\cos\phi_A^*=\lambda_1/\rho_1,\ \sin\phi_A^*=\lambda_2/\rho_1,\ \rho_1=\sqrt{\lambda_1^2+\lambda_2^2}$; $\cos\phi_D^*=-\lambda_3/\rho_2,\ \sin\phi_D^*=-\lambda_4/\rho_2$; co-state $\dot{\boldsymbol\lambda}=-\partial\mathcal H/\partial\mathbf x=0$ ⇒ constant co-states ⇒ **optimal trajectories are straight lines within $t_{\min}$**. This is the only optimal-control argument in the paper; everything after is geometry.
- Apollonius circle (Property 1, p. 197): center $O=\big((x_A^0-\alpha^2x_D^0)/(1-\alpha^2),\,(y_A^0-\alpha^2y_D^0)/(1-\alpha^2)\big)$, radius $r_O=\alpha l^0/(1-\alpha^2)$, $l_{OD}=l^0/(1-\alpha^2)$. Property 2: $A$ lies inside the AD-Apollonius circle; $\Omega_{ARR}=\Omega_A\cap\Omega_{AD}$. Remark 1: $A$ wins iff $\Omega_{ARR}\cap\Omega_T\ne\emptyset$.

### 5.1 Theorem 1 (p. 197, eq. (10)) — barrier when RAA inside RAD ($\Omega_A\subset\Omega_D$, $m\ge1/(1-\alpha)$)
**Statement.** For $\rho_A^0\le\hat r_D-\hat r_A=(1-\alpha)\hat r_D$ and $c_T\in[-\hat r_D,\ (1-\alpha)\hat r_D/(1+\alpha)]$,
$$\mathcal B^1_\alpha=\Big\{\mathbf x_B\ \big|\ \sin\theta_B=(1-\alpha^2)\frac{c_T}{\rho_B}+\alpha,\ \rho_B\le\hat r_D-\hat r_A\Big\}.$$
**Proof approach.** With $x_D^0=0$, the Apollonius-circle lowest point (OTP) is $\mathbf x^*=\big(\rho_A^0\cos\theta_A^0/(1-\alpha^2),\ \rho_A^0(\sin\theta_A^0-\alpha)/(1-\alpha^2)\big)$ (eq. (11)). Neutral state = OTP on $\mathcal T$: $\rho_A^0(\sin\theta_A^0-\alpha)/(1-\alpha^2)=c_T$ (eq. (12)), i.e. the Apollonius circle is tangent to $\mathcal T$; solving, $\sin\theta_A^0=(1-\alpha^2)c_T/\rho_A^0+\alpha$ (eq. (13)). Decisive intermediate inequality, eq. (14): solvability of (13) needs $-1\le(1-\alpha^2)c_T/\rho_A^0+\alpha\le1\iff -\rho_A^0/(1-\alpha)\le c_T\le\rho_A^0/(1+\alpha)$; requiring this for some $\rho_A^0\le(1-\alpha)\hat r_D$ forces exactly $c_T\in[-\hat r_D,(1-\alpha)\hat r_D/(1+\alpha)]$. **Side classification** (p. 197): $\sin\theta_A^0<(1-\alpha^2)c_T/\rho_A^0+\alpha$ ⇒ OTP below $\mathcal T$ ⇒ ADR;反之 DDR. Local/global: global over the inclusion zone $\rho_B\le\hat r_D-\hat r_A$; analytic/exact. Meaning: the barrier is the locus of attacker positions whose Apollonius-circle lowest point is tangent to the target line. Extensibility: reused as the innermost piece of all composite barriers (Thms. 7–9). *Verified: the $c_T$-range derivation is correct.*

### 5.2 Theorem 2 (p. 197–198, eq. (16)) — barrier when RAA outside RAD ($\Omega_A\cap\Omega_D=\emptyset$, $m\le1/(1+\alpha)$)
**Statement.** For $\hat r_D+\hat r_A\le\rho_A^0$,
$$\mathcal B^2_\alpha=\Big\{\mathbf x_B\ \big|\ \sin\theta_B=\frac{c_T+\hat r_A}{\rho_B},\ \hat r_D+\hat r_A\le\rho_B\Big\}.$$
**Proof approach.** $D$ cannot interfere (except the degenerate tangent case $\rho_A^0=\hat r_D+\hat r_A$, where the unique capture point on segment $AD$ occurs only when $A$ is directly above $D$ and "does not affect the expression of the barrier"); $A$ flies straight down, OTP $\mathbf x^*=(x_A^0, y_A^0-\hat r_A)$; neutral state eq. (17): $y_A^0-\hat r_A=c_T$, i.e. eq. (18) $\sin\theta_A^0=(c_T+\hat r_A)/\rho_A^0$ — a straight barrier **line parallel to the x-axis** at height $y=c_T+\hat r_A$. For any $c_T$ some point of the line satisfies the domain condition. Analytic, global in the outer zone; boundary case handled by remark. Meaning: without interception threat the game reduces to a single-player minimum-time reachability test.

### 5.3 Theorem 3 (p. 199, eqs. (20)–(24)) — candidate OTP ordering when the circles intersect ($m\in(1/(1+\alpha),1/(1-\alpha))$, eq. (19))
Setting: $P_1$ = lowest point of $\partial\Omega_{AD}$ (as in Thm. 1), $P_2$ = lowest point of $\Omega_A$ (as in Thm. 2), $P_3$ = lower intersection of $\partial\Omega_A$ and $\partial\Omega_{AD}$ (which lies on $\partial\Omega_D$); left half plane only, $x_A^0\le0$, $\theta_A^0\in[\pi/2,3\pi/2]$; $P_1$ feasible iff $P_1\in\Omega_D$, $P_2$ feasible iff $P_2\notin\Omega_D$, $P_3$ always feasible (p. 198–199, Fig. 4).
**Statement.** (i) $y_3\ge y_1$ and $y_3\ge y_2$ (eq. (22)) — $P_3$ is never the best candidate. (ii) If $P_1$ coincides with $P_3$ then $\sin\theta_A^0=\dfrac{(1+\alpha^2)-(1-\alpha^2)^2m^2}{2\alpha}$ (eq. (23)); (iii) if $P_2$ coincides with $P_3$ then $\sin\theta_A^0=\dfrac{1-(1-\alpha^2)m^2}{2\alpha m}$ (eq. (24)).
**Key objects.** $P_3$ from the circle system eq. (20) ($x_3^2+y_3^2=\hat r_D^2$, $(x_3-x_A^0)^2+(y_3-y_A^0)^2=(\alpha\hat r_D)^2$):
$$y_3=\frac{n\,y_A^0-\sqrt{(x_A^0)^2(4m^2-n^2)}}{2},\qquad n=(1-\alpha^2)m^2+1 \quad (\text{eq. (21)}),$$
with $4m^2-n^2>0$ guaranteed by (19) (I re-derived (21) via the radical line — correct). Proof of (ii): set $y_3-y_1=0$ (eq. (25)), define $h=\frac{ny_A^0}{2}-\frac{y_A^0-\alpha\rho_A^0}{1-\alpha^2}=\frac{\alpha\rho_A^0}{1-\alpha^2}\big(\zeta_1(m)\sin\theta_A^0+1\big)$, $\zeta_1(m)=((1-\alpha^2)^2m^2-\alpha^2-1)/(2\alpha)$, and $g=\frac12\sqrt{\cdot\,(4m^2-n^2)}$ (eq. (26); **the printed argument is $(\rho_A^0\sin\theta_A^0)^2$ but algebra forces $(\rho_A^0\cos\theta_A^0)^2=(x_A^0)^2$ — see proof gap below**). Then $h^2-g^2=\frac{(\rho_A^0)^2}{4(1-\alpha^2)^2}\big(a_1\sin^2\theta_A^0+b_1\sin\theta_A^0+c_1\big)=0$ (eq. (27)) with $a_1=4\alpha^2$, $b_1=4\alpha((1-\alpha^2)^2m^2-(1+\alpha^2))=8\alpha^2\zeta_1(m)$, $c_1=((1-\alpha^2)^2m^2-(1+\alpha^2))^2=(2\alpha\zeta_1(m))^2$ (eq. (28)); root $\sin\theta_A^0=-\zeta_1(m)$ (eq. (29)), valid since $\min_m\zeta_1=-1$, $\max_m\zeta_1=1$ over $m\in[1/(1+\alpha),1/(1-\alpha)]$ (eq. (30)). Part (iii) symmetric with $\zeta_2(m)=((1-\alpha^2)m^2-1)/(2\alpha m)$ (eqs. (33)–(36)). **Geometric by-products**: $\psi=\arccos\frac{\hat r_D^2+(\rho_A^0)^2-\hat r_A^2}{2\hat r_D\rho_A^0}=\arccos\frac{1+(1-\alpha^2)m^2}{2m}$ (eq. (31)); $\theta_{I1}=\arcsin\frac{(1+\alpha^2)-(1-\alpha^2)^2m^2}{2\alpha}+\psi$ (eq. (32)); $\theta_{I2}=\arcsin\frac{1-(1-\alpha^2)m^2}{2\alpha m}+\psi$ (eq. (37)), where $I_1,I_2$ are the left-half-plane intersections of the $P_1$/P$_2$ trajectories with $\partial\Omega_D$ (feasibility thresholds: $P_1$ feasible iff $\theta(P_1)\in[\pi/2,\theta_{I1})$, $P_2$ feasible iff $\theta(P_2)\in[\pi/2,\theta_{I2})$).
**Proof gap (typo) in eq. (26), p. 199.** As printed, $g=\tfrac12\sqrt{(\rho_A^0\sin\theta_A^0)^2(4m^2-n^2)}$. Substituting into eq. (27) does **not** yield $a_1=4\alpha^2$: expanding $h^2-g^2$ with $g\propto|\sin\theta|$ produces an $m$-dependent $\sin^2$ coefficient, contradicting (28). With $g=\tfrac12\sqrt{(x_A^0)^2(4m^2-n^2)}=\tfrac{\rho_A^0|\cos\theta_A^0|}{2}\sqrt{4m^2-n^2}$ (the term actually appearing in eqs. (21)/(25)) one gets exactly $a_1=4\alpha^2$, $b_1=8\alpha^2\zeta_1$, $c_1=(2\alpha\zeta_1)^2$ (I verified all three expansions). Consistency is triple-confirmed by eq. (57), where $\partial g/\partial(\sin\theta_A^0)=\sqrt{4m^2-n^2}\sin\theta_A^0/(2\sqrt{1-\sin^2\theta_A^0})$ is precisely the derivative of $g\propto\sqrt{1-\sin^2\theta}$. **Conclusion: "sin" in (26) is a typo for "cos"; implementations from the printed text alone would misplace the middle barrier branches $\mathcal B^{3,2}_\alpha/\mathcal B^{3,5}_\alpha$.**

### 5.4 Theorem 4 (p. 200) — OTP partition of the left half plane
**Statement.** $I_1$ lies above $I_2$, i.e. $\theta_{I1}<\theta_{I2}$. For fixed $\rho_A^0$: $\theta_A^0\in[\pi/2,\ \theta_{I1}-\psi]\Rightarrow$ OTP $=P_1$; $\theta_A^0\in(\theta_{I1}-\psi,\ \theta_{I2}-\psi]\Rightarrow$ OTP $=P_3$; $\theta_A^0\in(\theta_{I2}-\psi,\ 3\pi/2]\Rightarrow$ OTP $=P_2$.
**Proof approach.** Define $f_1(m)=\sin(\theta_{I1}-\psi)-\sin(\theta_{I2}-\psi)=\frac{(1+\alpha^2)-(1-\alpha^2)^2m^2}{2\alpha}-\frac{1-(1-\alpha^2)m^2}{2\alpha m}$ (eq. (38)); $\partial f_1/\partial m$ (eq. (39)) is decreasing since $\partial^2f_1/\partial m^2=-\frac{(1-\alpha^2)^2}{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2\alpha m^3}<0$ (eq. (40)); boundary slopes $\partial f_1/\partial m|_{1/(1-\alpha)}=-2(1-\alpha)(2+\alpha)/2<0$, $\partial f_1/\partial m|_{1/(1+\alpha)}=-2(1+\alpha)(\alpha-2)/2>0$ (eq. (41)); with $f_1(1/(1-\alpha))=f_1(1/(1+\alpha))=0$ this gives $f_1(m)>0$ inside, hence $\theta_{I1}<\theta_{I2}$. Feasibility + Theorem 3's $y_3\ge y_1,y_2$ then assign the OTP per arc. Analytic, exact.

### 5.5 Theorem 5 (p. 200) — monotonicity, existence of the partition for all $\rho_A^0$
**Statement.** $\sin\theta_{I1}$ and $\sin\theta_{I2}$ vary monotonically from $1$ to $-1$ as $\rho_A^0$ increases over $(\hat r_D-\hat r_A,\ \hat r_D+\hat r_A)$; the three-way division of Theorem 4 always exists.
**Proof approach.** $\psi|_{m=1/(1-\alpha)}=\psi|_{m=1/(1+\alpha)}=0$ (eq. (31)) and (30)/(35) give the endpoint values $\pm1$; monotonicity via the coincident-case $y_3$: for $y_3=y_1$, $y_3=\frac{\hat r_D}{m}\frac{1}{1-\alpha^2}\Big(\frac{(1+\alpha^2)-(1-\alpha^2)^2m^2}{2\alpha}-\alpha\Big)$ (eq. (42)) with $\partial y_3/\partial m=\frac{\hat r_D}{1-\alpha^2}\Big(-\frac{1-\alpha^2}{2\alpha m^2}-\frac{(1-\alpha^2)^2}{2\alpha}\Big)<0$ (eq. (43), decisive inequality); for $y_3=y_2$, $y_3=\hat r_D\big(\frac{1}{2\alpha m}-\frac{1-\alpha^2}{2\alpha}\big)-\alpha\hat r_D$ (eq. (44)), decreasing in $m$ (equivalently increasing in $\rho_A^0=\hat r_D/m$... the paper states the decrease in $m$ maps to the stated increase in $\rho_A^0$). Global, analytic.

### 5.6 Theorem 6 (p. 201, eqs. (45)–(47)) — barrier when RAA intersects RAD (stage 1)
**Statement.** For $\hat r_D-\hat r_A<\rho_A^0<\hat r_A+\hat r_D$, $\mathcal B^3_\alpha=\mathcal B^{3,1}_\alpha\cup\mathcal B^{3,2}_\alpha\cup\mathcal B^{3,3}_\alpha$:

### 5.7 Theorem 7 (p. 202, eqs. (53)–(55)) — complete barrier, $\Delta t=\bar t_A-\bar t_D\le0$
**Statement.** With $\Delta t\le0$, $\rho_1=\hat r_D-\hat r_A$, $\rho_2=\hat r_D+\hat r_A$:
- If $c_T\in(-\hat r_D,\ (1-\alpha)\hat r_D/(1+\alpha))$: $\mathcal B_\alpha=\mathcal B^1_\alpha\cup\mathcal B^{3,1}_\alpha\cup\mathcal B^{3,2}_\alpha\cup\mathcal B^{3,3}_\alpha\cup\mathcal B^2_\alpha$, i.e. eq. (53):
$\{\rho_B=\tfrac{(1-\alpha^2)c_T}{\sin\theta_B-\alpha},\ \rho_B\in(0,\rho_1]\}\cup\{\rho_B=\tfrac{(1-\alpha^2)c_T}{\sin\theta_B-\alpha},\ \rho_B\in(\rho_1,\rho_2),\ \tfrac{c_T}{\hat r_D}\ge\sin\theta_{I1}\}\cup\{\rho_B=\tfrac{c_T}{S(\theta_B)},\ \rho_B\in(\rho_1,\rho_2),\ \tfrac{c_T}{\hat r_D}\in(\sin\theta_{I2},\sin\theta_{I1})\}\cup\{\rho_B=\tfrac{c_T+\hat r_A}{\sin\theta_B},\ \rho_B\in(\rho_1,\rho_2),\ \tfrac{c_T}{\hat r_D}\le\sin\theta_{I2}\}\cup\{\rho_B=\tfrac{c_T+\hat r_A}{\sin\theta_B},\ \rho_B\in[\rho_2,+\infty)\}$.
- If $c_T\in[(1-\alpha)\hat r_D/(1+\alpha),\ \hat r_D)$: $\mathcal B_\alpha=\mathcal B^2_\alpha\cup\mathcal B^{3,1}_\alpha\cup\mathcal B^{3,2}_\alpha\cup\mathcal B^{3,3}_\alpha$ (eq. (54); $\mathcal B^1_\alpha$ does not exist).
- If $c_T\in(-\infty,-\hat r_D]\cup[\hat r_D,+\infty)$: $\mathcal B_\alpha=\mathcal B^2_\alpha$ only (eq. (55)) — a straight line parallel to the x-axis.
**Proof approach.** For $c_T<-\hat r_D$: $c_T\le\hat r_D\sin\theta_{I2}$ holds for every admissible $m$ (since $c_T/\hat r_D<-1\le\sin\theta_{I2}$ — verified), so only the $P_2$-branch survives in the annulus, and no $\mathcal B^1$ exists; I additionally verified that for $\rho_A^0<\rho_1$ and $c_T<-\hat r_D$ every state is in DDR because $y_1=\rho_A^0(\sin\theta_A^0-\alpha)/(1-\alpha^2)\ge-\rho_A^0/(1-\alpha)>-\hat r_D>c_T$. For $c_T>\hat r_D$: $\Omega_D\subset\Omega_T$, capture inside $\Omega_P$ is impossible, $A$ flies straight down, and the restriction $\rho_B\ge\rho_2$ on $\mathcal B^2$ is automatically satisfied because $c_T+\hat r_A\ge\rho_2\iff c_T\ge\hat r_D$ (verified). **Remark 3 (p. 202):** this $\Delta t\le0$ barrier is a special case of Yan et al. [18]'s barrier at zero capture radius — an independent cross-check of correctness — while the $\Delta t>0$ case below is beyond [18]. *Note: Remark 3 says "Theorem 5" where Theorem 7 is clearly meant (typo).*

### 5.8 Δt > 0: new OTP candidates M2, M3 and the comparison function F (p. 202–203, eqs. (56)–(65))
When $\bar t_D<\bar t_A$ and $m\in(1/(1+\alpha),1/(1-\alpha))$, part of $\partial\Omega_A$ lies outside $\Omega_D$; surviving to $\partial\Omega_A$ guarantees survival after $t_{\min}$, after which $A$ flies straight down. Candidates become $P_1,\ M_2,\ M_3$ with $M_2,M_3$ the points below $P_2,P_3$ at distance $v_A\Delta t$ (segments $P_iM_i\perp$ x-axis, Fig. 5). Since $y_3\ge y_2$, $M_2$ is never above $M_3$; $M_3$ always reachable; $M_2$ reachable iff $P_2$ is. OTP: $M_2$ on $(\theta_{I2}-\psi,3\pi/2]$; $M_3$ on $(\theta_{I1}-\psi,\theta_{I2}-\psi]$; on $[\pi/2,\theta_{I1}-\psi]$ compare $M_3$ with $P_1$ through
$$\mathcal F(\theta_A^0)=y_3-v_A\Delta t-y_1=h-v_A\Delta t-g \quad(\text{eq. (56)}),$$
$\partial\mathcal F/\partial(\sin\theta_A^0)=\frac n2-\frac{1}{1-\alpha^2}+\frac{\sqrt{4m^2-n^2}\sin\theta_A^0}{2\sqrt{1-\sin^2\theta_A^0}}$ (eq. (57)), $\partial^2\mathcal F/\partial(\sin\theta_A^0)^2=\frac{2\sqrt{4m^2-n^2}}{4(1-\sin^2\theta_A^0)\sqrt{1-\sin^2\theta_A^0}}>0$ (eq. (58)); using $h(\theta_{I1}-\psi)=g(\theta_{I1}-\psi)=k_1>0$ and $h^2-g^2$ being an upward parabola minimized at $\theta_{I1}-\psi$ (eq. (27)) one gets $\partial\mathcal F/\partial(\sin\theta_A^0)|_{\theta_{I1}-\psi}=0$ (eqs. (59)–(60)), hence $\mathcal F$ decreases monotonically on $[\pi/2,\theta_{I1}-\psi]$. If $\mathcal F(\pi/2)<0$: OTP $=M_3$ on the whole first arc. If $\mathcal F(\pi/2)\ge0$: a unique crossing angle $\theta_{I3}$ exists with $\mathcal F(\theta_{I3}-\psi)=0$ (eq. (61)), reducing to
$$a_3\sin^2(\theta_{I3}-\psi)+b_3\sin(\theta_{I3}-\psi)+c_3=0\ (\text{eq. (62)}),$$
$a_3=4\alpha^2$, $b_3=8\alpha\big(\alpha-(1-\alpha^2)k_2\big)\zeta_1(m)$, $c_3=(2\alpha\zeta_1(m))^2-8\alpha(1-\alpha^2)k_2+4(1-\alpha^2)^2k_2^2$, $k_2=v_A\Delta t/\rho_A^0$ (eq. (63)). Discriminant $\Delta=64\alpha^2(1-\alpha^2)^2\big(\zeta_1(m)^2-1\big)\Big[k_2^2-\frac{2\alpha k_2}{1-\alpha^2}\Big]$ (eq. (64)); $\zeta_1^2-1<0$ makes $\Delta$ a downward parabola in $k_2$, vanishing at $k_2=0$ and $k_2=2\alpha/(1-\alpha^2)$; from $\mathcal F(\pi/2)\ge0$, $k_2\in\Big[0,\frac{(1-\alpha^2)m^2+1}{2}-\frac{1}{1+\alpha}\Big]$ (eq. (65)), and $\max k_2\le k_2|_{m=1/(1-\alpha)}=2\alpha/(1-\alpha^2)$, so $\Delta\ge0$ and real roots always exist; the applicable root satisfies $\sin\theta_{I3}>\sin\theta_{I1}$ (selected by monotonicity + geometry, not algebraically).

### 5.9 Theorem 8 (p. 204, eq. (66)) — complete barrier, Δt > 0, smooth case
**Statement.** If $\mathcal F(\theta_A^0)\ge0$, $c_T^N:=c_T+v_A\Delta t\in(-\hat r_D,\hat r_D)$, and some $m\in(1/(1+\alpha),1/(1-\alpha))$ meets $c_T^N=\hat r_D\sin\theta_{I3}$, then
$$\mathcal B_\alpha=\mathcal B^1_\alpha\cup\mathcal B^{3,4}_\alpha\cup\mathcal B^{3,5}_\alpha\cup\mathcal B^{3,6}_\alpha\cup\mathcal B^{2,N}_\alpha$$
with (eq. (66)): $\{\rho_B=\tfrac{(1-\alpha^2)c_T}{\sin\theta_B-\alpha},\ \rho_B\in(0,\rho_1]\}$ ($\mathcal B^1$, note: still $c_T$); $\{\rho_B=\tfrac{(1-\alpha^2)c_T}{\sin\theta_B-\alpha},\ \rho_B\in(\rho_1,\rho_2),\ \tfrac{c_T^N}{\hat r_D}\ge\sin\theta_{I3}\}$ ($\mathcal B^{3,4}$, $P_1$-branch); $\{\rho_B=\tfrac{c_T^N}{S(\theta_B)},\ \rho_B\in(\rho_1,\rho_2),\ \tfrac{c_T^N}{\hat r_D}\in(\sin\theta_{I2},\sin\theta_{I3})\}$ ($\mathcal B^{3,5}$, $M_3$-branch); $\{\rho_B=\tfrac{c_T+\bar r_A}{\sin\theta_B},\ \rho_B\in(\rho_1,\rho_2),\ \tfrac{c_T^N}{\hat r_D}\le\sin\theta_{I2}\}$ ($\mathcal B^{3,6}$, $M_2$-branch, note $\bar r_A$, because $M_2$'s height is $\rho_A^0\sin\theta_A^0-\bar r_A$); $\{\rho_B=\tfrac{c_T+\bar r_A}{\sin\theta_B},\ \rho_B\in[\rho_2,+\infty)\}$ ($\mathcal B^{2,N}$). If $c_T\notin(-\hat r_D,(1-\alpha)\hat r_D/(1+\alpha))$, drop $\mathcal B^1_\alpha$. Proof mirrors Theorem 6 with $c_T\to c_T^N$ on the $M_3$ branch; continuity holds exactly when some $m$ meets $c_T^N=\hat r_D\sin\theta_{I3}$.

### 5.10 Theorem 9 (p. 204, eq. (67)) — complete barrier, Δt > 0, discontinuous case with additional arc
**Statement.** If $\mathcal F(\pi/2)<0$, or $c_T^N<\hat r_D\sin\theta_{I3}$ for all possible $\theta_{I3}$ (so $\mathcal T$ never crosses $P_1$ and $M_3$ simultaneously), the barrier needs an **additional arc** connecting the $P_1$-type and $M_3$-type branches at the switch radius $\rho_1=\hat r_D-\hat r_A$:
$$\mathcal B_\alpha=\mathcal B^1_\alpha\cup\mathcal B^{ADD}_\alpha\cup\mathcal B^{3,5}_\alpha\cup\mathcal B^{3,6}_\alpha\cup\mathcal B^{2,N}_\alpha,\qquad
\mathcal B^{ADD}_\alpha=\{\mathbf x_B|\ \rho_B=\rho_1,\ \sin\theta_B\in[\min\{s_{\varepsilon1},s_{\varepsilon3}\},\max\{s_{\varepsilon1},s_{\varepsilon3}\}]\},$$
where $\sin\varepsilon_1=(1-\alpha^2)c_T/\rho_1+\alpha$ and $S(\varepsilon_3)=(c_T+v_A\Delta t)/\rho_1$, $s_{\varepsilon i}=\sin\varepsilon_i$; the $\mathcal B^{3,5}/\mathcal B^{3,6}$ switch condition is now $c_T^N\gtrless\hat r_D\sin\theta_{I2}$ (eq. (67)). **Reasoning (p. 204):** for $\rho_A^0=\rho_1-\Delta\rho$ the boundary angle is $\varepsilon_1$ (Thm.-1 expression), for $\rho_A^0=\rho_1+\Delta\rho$ it is $\varepsilon_3$ ($S$-expression); since the two expressions disagree at $m=1/(1-\alpha)$, all states at radius exactly $\rho_1$ with $\sin\theta_B$ between $\varepsilon_1$ and $\varepsilon_3$ lie on the ADR/DDR separatrix — an origin-centered arc. **The proof is omitted ("similar to the proofs above")** — the only unproven theorem statement in the paper. The authors note eq. (67) is essentially inexpressible in Cartesian coordinates, crediting the polar formulation for tractability (Fig. 6(b)).

### 5.11 Degenerate cases (p. 204–205, eqs. (68)–(69)) and single-sided limits (eqs. (70)–(71))
- $c_T^N<-\hat r_D$ (with $\Delta t>0$): only the straight-line branch survives, $\mathcal B_\alpha=\{\mathbf x_B|\ \rho_B=\tfrac{y_T+\bar r_A}{\sin\theta_B},\ c_T^N<-\hat r_D\}$ (eq. (68)) — **printed symbol "$y_T$" is evidently $c_T$** (the derivation two lines above uses $c_T<-\hat r_D$; typo).
- $c_T^N>\hat r_D$ with $c_T\in(-\hat r_D,(1-\alpha)\hat r_D/(1+\alpha))$: the DDR **splits into two disconnected parts** — the bounded closed pocket $\Omega_{DDR1}$ (enclosed by $\rho_B=\tfrac{(1-\alpha^2)c_T}{\sin\theta_B-\alpha}$ and the arc $\rho_B=\hat r_D-\hat r_A,\ \sin\theta_B>\sin\varepsilon_1$) and the unbounded $\Omega_{DDR2}$ (cut off by the $M_2$ line), with $\Omega_{DDR1}\not\subseteq\Omega_{DDR2}$, $\mathrm{DDR}=\Omega_{DDR1}\cup\Omega_{DDR2}$, and
$$\mathcal B_\alpha=\{\rho_B=\tfrac{(1-\alpha^2)c_T}{\sin\theta_B-\alpha},\ \rho_B\in(0,\hat r_D-\hat r_A]\}\cup\{\rho_B=\hat r_D-\hat r_A,\ \sin\theta_B>\sin\varepsilon_1\}\cup\{\rho_B=\tfrac{c_T+\bar r_A}{\sin\theta_B},\ \rho_B\in[\hat r_D-\hat r_A,+\infty)\}\quad(\text{eq. (69), Fig. 7}).$$
- Attacker-only time limit $\mathcal B^{rA}$: identical to Theorem 7's $\Delta t\le0$ barrier ($\bar t_D=\infty$); the ADR under the both-limits barrier $\mathcal B_\alpha$ is never smaller than under $\mathcal B^{rA}$ — adding a defender time limit only shrinks the DDR (p. 205).
- Defender-only time limit $\mathcal B^{rD}$ (p. 205): for $m\ge1/(1-\alpha)$, $\mathcal B^{rD,1}=\{\rho,\theta|\ \sin\theta=(1-\alpha^2)\tfrac{c_T}{\rho}+\alpha,\ \bar r_D/\rho\ge\tfrac{1}{1-\alpha}\}$ (eq. (70)); for $m\le1/(1-\alpha)$ part of $\Omega_A$ is outside $\Omega_D$ but inside $\Omega_{AD}$, $A$ can then always win, the upper ADR boundary is the circle $\rho=(1-\alpha)\bar r_D$, and
$$\mathcal B^{rD}=\{\sin\theta=(1-\alpha^2)\tfrac{c_T}{\rho}+\alpha,\ \bar r_D/\rho\ge\tfrac{1}{1-\alpha}\}\cup\{\rho=(1-\alpha)\bar r_D,\ \sin\theta>\tfrac{(1+\alpha)c_T}{\bar r_D}+\alpha\}\quad(\text{eq. (71)}),$$
i.e. the defender's time limit "dramatically" enlarges the ADR and makes the DDR a bounded domain.

### 5.12 Multiplayer extension (p. 205–206)
No new theorem. Pairwise outcomes via $\Omega_{DDR}(\mathbf x_{A,i},\mathbf x_{D,j})$; edge $D_j$–$A_i$ iff $D_j$ wins the pair; maximum matching (LP or Hopcroft–Karp, citing [23]) yields the assignment maximizing captured attackers; re-run online. Stated as a recipe, with a 5v5 illustration (Figs. 8–9); no optimality proof of the resulting team strategy is given.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
**Author-stated** (p. 207–208): simple motion only; unbounded planar domain; game of kind only — "control directives... cannot be directly acquired using game of kind theory", game-of-degree fusion left to future work; richer dynamics and obstacles postponed; multiplayer integration (ANN / task assignment) only sketched; point capture; full information; funding/data statements nil.
**Critical observations (with page/eq.):**
1. **Typo in eq. (26)** (p. 199): $g$ defined with $(\rho_A^0\sin\theta_A^0)^2$; algebra (eqs. (21), (25), (27), (57)) requires $(\rho_A^0\cos\theta_A^0)^2=(x_A^0)^2$. See Section 5.3 — the single most implementation-dangerous erratum.
2. **Remark 3 (p. 202) cites "Theorem 5"**; the $\Delta t\le0$ complete barrier is Theorem 7 — cross-reference error in the paper's only external-validation claim.
3. **Eq. (68) (p. 204)** uses "$y_T$" where $c_T$ is meant.
4. **Simulation cross-reference slip (p. 206)**: outcome "A1 captured / A2 reaches $\mathcal T$" attributed to Fig. 9 (the matching graph) instead of Fig. 10; **Fig. 8's caption duplicates Fig. 5's** (p. 206 vs p. 203).
5. **Section 5.2 notation** (p. 206): $\Omega_{DDR}(\mathbf x_{A,i},\mathbf x_{D,j})$ is introduced as "the ADR related to the pair" — the description contradicts the subscript (it is the DDR).
6. **No formal semipermeability/equilibrium theorem**: the barrier is assembled from neutral-state loci (OTP on $\mathcal T$); optimality rests on the heuristic game-of-kind conversion eqs. (5)–(9) with a discontinuous payoff $Q\in\{+1,0,-1\}$ and on Remark 1. There is no verification argument (e.g., Isaacs' main theorem 4 / viscosity-solution certificate) that the *composite* $\mathcal B_\alpha$ is semipermeable, nor that the piecewise optimal strategies form non-anticipative equilibria across the switching radii $\rho_1,\rho_2$ and the arc $\mathcal B^{ADD}_\alpha$; continuity at the branch junctions is argued, not proven, and **Theorem 9's proof is omitted entirely** (p. 204).
7. **Boundary-bracket inconsistencies**: Theorem 1 states $c_T\in[-\hat r_D,(1-\alpha)\hat r_D/(1+\alpha)]$ (closed) while Theorem 7's first case uses the open interval; endpoint behavior at $c_T=-\hat r_D$, $c_T=(1-\alpha)\hat r_D/(1+\alpha)$, $m=1/(1\pm\alpha)$ and $\rho_A^0\in\{\rho_1,\rho_2\}$ is never treated uniformly.
8. **Root selection for $\theta_{I3}$** (eqs. (62)–(65)) is justified by monotonicity and "geometric relations", not by an explicit closed-form root — the $\Delta t>0$ machinery is therefore semi-analytic (one quadratic per $\rho_A^0$, still closed-form).
9. **Model restrictions**: $v_D>v_A$ only (no slower-defender analysis); infinite straight target line (no finite/convex targets); $D$'s initial position fixed at the coordinate origin (WLOG by translation/rotation, but the $c_T$ meaning then couples to $D$); capture exactly at $\bar t_D$ boundary cases handled by informal "arbitrarily small manoeuvre" arguments (p. 195); stage-2 optimality of "straight down" holds only for the binary objective — acknowledged by the authors (p. 207).
10. **No robustness study**: zero-computation outcome prediction presumes exact speeds/positions and time limits; no sensitivity analysis despite the stated real-time conflict-assessment application.
11. Claimed novelty ("first attempt", p. 207) is relative to the surveyed literature; the library also holds the same group's 2022 companion "Reach-Avoid Games with a Time Limit and Detection Range: A Geometric Approach" (title-level check only), indicating the time-limited line was already public in conference form — the journal delta is the both-player limits and the $\Delta t>0$ theory.

## 8. Citation Value
**Role in the literature**: the definitive closed-form treatment of *time-limited* planar reach-avoid games of kind. It converts a terminal-surface complication that defeats the HJI machinery into pure geometry: reachable-area pairings $(\Omega_A,\Omega_D)$ → Apollonius-based reaching regions → three candidate optimal target points → piecewise polar barriers, with the $\Delta t>0$ case supplying genuinely new phenomena (the additional connecting arc $\mathcal B^{ADD}_\alpha$, the discontinuous barrier of Theorem 9, and the disconnected DDR of eq. (69)). It is the natural pairwise-outcome oracle inside maximum-matching pipelines for multiplayer reach-avoid / perimeter-style assignment, sitting between Yan et al.'s two-defender analytical barriers [31,32] and limited-lifetime lifeline games [18], and it is methodologically adjacent to the Apollonius-circle works (Dorothy, Oyler, Garcia) in this library. Use it whenever agents' endurance/endurance-vs-mission-time is modeled, when a zero online-computation win-region oracle is needed, or when a paper needs the canonical citation for "time limits make the DDR disconnected".
**Suggested citation sentences**: "Chen et al. derived the complete analytical barrier for planar reach-avoid games in which both the attacker and the defender have finite operating times, showing via a two-stage decomposition and Apollonius-circle reaching regions that a defender time limit can split the defense dominance region into two disconnected components [P012]." — or, in a methods section: "Pairwise win/loss is evaluated in closed form by the time-limited reach-avoid barrier of [P012], whose polar-coordinate expressions incur no online computational burden and therefore support real-time defender-to-attacker assignment."
**Caveat when citing**: verify statements against the corrected eq. (26) ($\cos$ not $\sin$) and re-route the paper's internal cross-references (Remark 3 → Theorem 7; eq. (68) $y_T$ → $c_T$; p. 206 Fig. 9 → Fig. 10), since the printed text contains these slips.
