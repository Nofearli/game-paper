## 2. Research Background

Perimeter defense asks a mobile defender (here a single UAV-like vehicle with a finite capture radius) to intercept mobile intruders before they enter a protected region; the scenario arises when a UAV must tag or relay information to intruders before they reach a region of interest (p. 1). Prior perimeter-defense literature either determined optimal strategies for a small number of agents or assumed stochastic intruder arrival processes [1]–[3], and hence "do not address the worst-case performance where the intruders might coordinate their actions" [4] (p. 1). The dominant game-theoretic treatment formulates multiplayer perimeter defense as a reach-avoid game [10]–[12] solved via Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman-Isaacs (HJBI) equations, "which is generally only suitable for low dimensional state spaces and in simple environments" [13], [14] (p. 1); recent variants include receding-horizon maximum matching [19], perimeter-constrained defenders [16], [17], and $n$-dimensional extensions [18]. A related area is dynamic vehicle routing (DVR), where demands become available over time but are static [6], [7]; here the demands (intruders) move toward the perimeter, making the problem harder. The authors' own prior work introduced perimeter defense in circular and rectangular environments with stochastic inputs [3], [8] and, crucially, competitive analysis for **linear (line-segment) environments** [20] (archived as P005). This paper transports that worst-case competitive-analysis methodology to a genuinely two-dimensional **conical environment**, where the geometry introduces new phenomena absent on a line: an optimal parking radius $\rho/\cos\theta$, the role of the capture radius $r$ in the necessary conditions, and a sector-based algorithm whose competitiveness depends on the number of sectors (p. 1–2).

## 3. Research Problem and Gap

**Environment and model** (Section II, p. 2): the conical environment is $\mathcal{E}(\theta) = \{(y,\alpha) : 0 < y \le 1,\ -\theta \le \alpha \le \theta\}$, written in polar coordinates where $y$ is the radial coordinate and $\alpha$ is measured with respect to the $y$-axis; the protected **perimeter** is the conical region $\mathcal{R}(\rho,\theta) = \{(z,\alpha) : 0 < z \le \rho < 1,\ -\theta \le \alpha \le \theta\}$, i.e., a concentric sector of radius $\rho$ and aperture $2\theta$, with $0 < \theta \le \pi$ ($\theta = \pi$ recovers the full disk). Intruders are released at arbitrary time instants on the circumference $y = 1$ (any angle $\alpha_l \in [-\theta,\theta]$) and then move **radially inward at fixed speed** $v < 1$ toward the origin, aiming to breach the perimeter. The defense is a single vehicle modeled as a first-order integrator with maximum speed $1$ and a **finite capture radius** $r < \rho$; a capture circle of radius $r$ is centered at the vehicle's location; an intruder inside or on it is captured and removed; an intruder reaching the perimeter uncaptured is **lost**. A problem instance is $\mathcal{P}(\theta,\rho,r,v)$; an input instance is $\mathcal{I} = \{t, N(t), \{(1,\alpha_1),\dots,(1,\alpha_{N(t)})\}\}_{t=0}^{T}$ (release times, multiplicities, arrival angles). An online algorithm $\mathcal{A}$ assigns a velocity of magnitude $\le 1$ as a function of the revealed input $\mathcal{I}(t) \subseteq \mathcal{I}$, i.e., $\dot{x}(t) = \mathcal{A}(\mathcal{I}(t))$ with $x$ the vehicle's polar coordinates — so release times, counts and locations of already-released intruders are fully observed, but future releases are unknown. An optimal offline algorithm $\mathcal{O}$ is non-causal and knows the entire $\mathcal{I}$ in advance. Footnote 1 (p. 2): "The techniques and analysis used in this work can be extended to other models such as double integrator, and would be addressed in a future work."

**Metric** (Definition 1, p. 2): $c_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{I}) = \mathcal{O}(\mathcal{I})/\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{I}) \ge 1$ where $\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{I})$ ($\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{I})$) counts intruders captured by the online (optimal offline) algorithm; $c_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{P}) = \sup_{\mathcal{I}} c_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{I})$; $c(\mathcal{P}) = \inf_{\mathcal{A}} c_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{P})$; $\mathcal{A}$ is $c$-competitive if $c_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{P}) \le c$. **Problem statement**: "establish fundamental guarantees and to design $c$-competitive algorithms for the vehicle with minimum $c$" (p. 2). By Lemma 1 of [20], it suffices to restrict to **extreme speed** algorithms (move at unit speed or stay stationary).

**Gap**: [authors' claim] prior perimeter-defense work is either small-team game-theoretic or stochastic-arrival based [1]–[3] and "does not address the worst-case performance where the intruders might coordinate their actions" [4] (p. 1) — verified: refs [1]–[3] are a border-defense differential game, adaptive partitioning (IROS 2020), and the authors' CDC 2019 radial-targets paper, all average-case or small-$n$; ref [4] is the turret-runner-penetrator game. [authors' claim] HJBI-based reach-avoid solution methods are limited to low-dimensional state spaces and simple environments [13], [14] (p. 1) — verified as stated against the cited Margellos–Lygeros (TAC 2011) and Chen–Zhou–Tomlin (CDC 2014) line of work, a standard and fair characterization. The precise gap w.r.t. the authors' own [20] (line environments, P005): "The key distinction of this work from [20] is the geometry of the environment which yields novel results in terms of optimally placing the vehicle, role of capture radius and additional conditions to ensure competitiveness of the algorithms" (p. 1) — verified: on a line the defender patrols a 1-D segment and $r$ plays no role in [20]'s thresholds, whereas here Lemma III.1 (static capture-all placement at $(\rho/\cos\theta, 0)$), the crossing-time Lemma III.2, and the sector count $n_s$ of SNP are all genuinely 2-D/conical effects. Note the intruders are **non-adaptive by construction** (fixed radial motion, no evasion), so the worst case is adversarial scheduling of release times/angles, not adversarial closed-loop evasion.

## 4. Core Contributions

1. **Theory (impossibility)**: Theorem III.3 (p. 2) — a necessary condition on $(\theta,\rho,r,v)$ under which **no** $c$-competitive online algorithm exists for any finite $c$; in fact no algorithm, online or offline, can capture all intruders.
2. **Theory (lower bound)**: Theorem III.4 (p. 3) — a necessary condition under which $c(\mathcal{P}) \ge 2$ for every algorithm: no online algorithm can capture both intruders of five crafted two-intruder instances $\{\mathcal{I}_1,\dots,\mathcal{I}_5\}$ while an offline algorithm captures both.
3. **Algorithm (open-loop, optimal)**: Angular Sweep (Section IV-A) — the vehicle sweeps a circular arc at radius $x_S$; provably **1-competitive** (captures every intruder, matching the offline optimum) iff $x_S$ lies in a non-empty interval guaranteed by condition (1) (Theorem IV.1), and **not** $c$-competitive for any $c$ otherwise.
4. **Algorithm (event-driven)**: Conical Compare-and-Capture (ConCaC, Section IV-B, Algorithm 1) — per-epoch comparison of intruder counts on the left/right arc sets; provably **2-competitive** under condition (2), which strictly enlarges the feasible parameter regime beyond Angular Sweep (Theorem IV.3, Lemma IV.2).
5. **Algorithm (sector-based)**: Stay Near Perimeter (SNP, Section IV-C, Algorithm 2) — partitions the cone into $n_s$ sectors with resting points near the perimeter, works in time intervals of length $D$, uses two comparisons C1/C2 to pick and possibly switch sectors; provably $\frac{3n_s-1}{2}$-competitive under $3D \le \frac{1-\rho}{v}$ and $\frac{\rho}{\cos\theta_s} \le 2D$ (Theorem IV.7), proved by a charging/accounting argument (Lemmas IV.4–IV.6). SNP trades a larger competitive ratio for a **larger feasible parameter regime** (smaller $r$).
6. **Numerical insight (experiment)**: Section V, Fig. 4 — parameter-regime plot in $(\rho, v)$ space for $r = 0.05$, $\theta = \pi/3$, overlaying the two necessary-condition curves and the three algorithms' sufficient-condition curves; additional plots deferred to [21] (arXiv:2110.04667v2, which contains all detailed proofs).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results

**Geometry conventions**: polar coordinates $(y,\alpha)$, $y$ = radius, $\alpha$ measured from the $y$-axis (cone bisector). All results below verified against the rendered page images (200–400 dpi crops).

**Lemma III.1 (static capture-all placement; p. 2).** *For a problem instance $\mathcal{P}(\theta,\rho,r,v)$ with $\theta < \frac{\pi}{4}$, all intruders can be captured if $r \ge \rho\tan(\theta)$ by positioning the vehicle at $\left(\frac{\rho}{\cos(\theta)}, 0\right)$.*
- Assumptions: $\theta < \pi/4$; $r \ge \rho \tan\theta$. Proof approach: omitted ("follows directly from the geometry", cf. [21]). Decisive equation: the distance from $(\rho/\cos\theta, 0)$ to the perimeter endpoint $(\rho, \theta)$ equals $\sqrt{\rho^2/\cos^2\theta + \rho^2 - 2\rho^2} = \rho\tan\theta \le r$ (equivalence $r \ge \rho\tan\theta \iff \theta \le \arctan(r/\rho)$ is exactly the sector-half-angle relation used later in SNP). Local/global: global — every radially moving intruder must cross the arc $z = \rho$, which lies entirely inside the capture circle, so capture occurs before breach. Physical meaning: if the capture circle is wide enough to cover the whole perimeter arc at once, the defense is trivially perfect by parking at the arc's "focus-like" point on the bisector. Extensibility: this placement is the $n_s = 1$ case of SNP's resting points (eq. (3) discussion, p. 5–6).

**Lemma III.2 (endpoint crossing time; p. 2).** *The minimum time required by the vehicle to move from a location such that the capture circle contains one end of the perimeter, $(\rho, \theta)$, to a location such that the capture circle contains the opposite end, $(\rho, -\theta)$, is $2(\rho\sin(\theta) - r)$ if $\theta < \frac{\pi}{2}$ and $2(\rho - r)$, otherwise.*
- Proof: omitted (geometry, cf. [21]). Decisive equation: chord length between symmetric perimeter endpoints is $2\rho\sin\theta$; cutting the corner by $r$ at each end gives $2(\rho\sin\theta - r)$; for $\theta \ge \pi/2$ the shortest admissible path through the cone passes within $r$ of the origin, giving $2(\rho - r)$. Physical meaning: the "re-arm time" to swing coverage from one end of the perimeter to the other — it is the bottleneck quantity in every impossibility theorem below. Extensibility: reused verbatim in the SNP sector bound with $\theta \to \theta_s$.

**Theorem III.3 (necessary condition for finite $c(\mathcal{P})$; p. 2).** *For any problem instance $\mathcal{P}(\theta, r, \rho, v)$ with parameters satisfying*
$$2(\rho\sin(\theta) - r) > \frac{1-\rho}{v} \ \text{ if } \theta < \tfrac{\pi}{2}, \qquad 2(\rho - r) > \frac{1-\rho}{v} \ \text{ if } \theta \ge \tfrac{\pi}{2},$$
*there does not exist a $c$-competitive algorithm for any constant $c$, and no algorithm, either online or offline, can capture all intruders.*
- Assumptions: the displayed inequality (LHS = endpoint crossing time from Lemma III.2; RHS = intruder travel time from the circumference to the perimeter).
- Proof approach: adversarial input construction. A **stream** of intruders is released at $(1,\theta)$ every $\frac{1-\rho}{v}$ time units starting at time 1 (so each reaches the perimeter exactly as the next is released). If $\mathcal{A}$ never captures a stream intruder, it is not $c$-competitive for any $c$ (the stream never ends) while $\mathcal{O}$ parks at $(\rho,\theta)$ and captures all — first claim. Otherwise $\mathcal{A}$ captures the $i$-th stream intruder at some time $t$; at that instant the input ends with a **burst** of $c+1$ intruders released simultaneously at $(1,-\theta)$. $\mathcal{A}$ cannot capture any stream intruders $1,\dots,i-1$ (already lost by stream spacing) and, since at capture time the vehicle is within $r$ of the $i$-th intruder near the $(+)$ end and has only $\frac{1-\rho}{v}$ time to cross, Lemma III.2's inequality guarantees the entire burst is lost (decisive intermediate inequality: $2(\rho\sin\theta - r) > \frac{1-\rho}{v}$, resp. $2(\rho-r) > \frac{1-\rho}{v}$). $\mathcal{O}$ instead waits at $(x,\alpha)$ (the Lemma III.2 location) through the first $i-1$ intruders, then crosses to $(x,-\alpha)$ capturing the burst, losing only the $i$-th intruder. Hence $\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{I}) \ge i + c > c \ge c\cdot\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{I})$ with $\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{I}) = 1$.
- Local/global: global impossibility over the whole parameter half-space defined by the inequality. Analytic. Physical meaning: if swinging coverage between cone ends takes longer than an intruder's free travel time, coordinated releases at both ends defeat any defense — the capture radius $r$ enters the threshold, which was impossible on a line where $r = 0$ effectively.
- Extensibility: the stream-then-burst template is the standard adversarial device of this line of work; reused in Theorem IV.1's converse.

**Theorem III.4 (necessary condition for $c(\mathcal{P}) \ge 2$; p. 3).** *For any problem instance $\mathcal{P}(\theta, r, \rho, v)$, $c(\mathcal{P}) \ge 2$ if*
$$\frac{1-\rho}{v} \le \sqrt{1+\rho^2 - 2\rho\cos(2\theta)} - 2r, \quad \text{if } \theta \le \tfrac{\pi}{2}; \qquad \frac{1-\rho}{v} \le 1 + \rho - 2r, \quad \text{if } \theta > \tfrac{\pi}{2}.$$
- Meaning: whenever the intruder travel time $\frac{1-\rho}{v}$ is at most the (geometry-dependent) time to ferry the capture circle between the two arrival angles' capture zones, every online algorithm loses at least half of some instance.
- Proof approach: five two-intruder instances $\mathcal{I}_1,\dots,\mathcal{I}_5$ with intruders $a,b$ released at $(1,\theta)$ and $(1,-\theta)$, vehicle starting at the origin. Key quantities (p. 3, printed exactly):
$$t_1 = \sqrt{1 + r^2 - \tfrac{2r(1-\rho\cos(2\theta))}{\sqrt{1+\rho^2-2\rho\cos(2\theta)}}},\qquad
\alpha_1 = \tan^{-1}\!\left(\tfrac{\sin(\theta)\sqrt{1+\rho^2-2\rho\cos(2\theta)}\,-\,r(1+\rho)\sin(\theta)}{\cos(\theta)\sqrt{1+\rho^2-2\rho\cos(2\theta)}\,-\,r(1-\rho)\cos(\theta)}\right),$$
$$t_2 = \sqrt{\rho^2 + r^2 + \tfrac{2r\rho(\cos(2\theta)-\rho)}{\sqrt{1+\rho^2-2\rho\cos(2\theta)}}},\qquad
\alpha_2 = \tan^{-1}\!\left(\tfrac{-\rho\sin(\theta)\sqrt{1+\rho^2-2\rho\cos(2\theta)}\,+\,r(1+\rho)\sin(\theta)}{\rho\cos(\theta)\sqrt{1+\rho^2-2\rho\cos(2\theta)}\,+\,r(1-\rho)\cos(\theta)}\right).$$
  These are the vehicle locations whose capture circles **tangentially** contain $(1,\theta)$ (resp. $(\rho,-\theta)$) — placing the vehicle at $(t_1,\alpha_1)$ at time $t_1$ captures $a$ exactly upon arrival (Fig. 1, p. 3). Locations "determined analogously to the proof of Lemma III.2", proof omitted (→ [21]).
- Case (i) $\theta \le \pi/2$, equality case $\frac{1-\rho}{v} = \sqrt{1+\rho^2-2\rho\cos(2\theta)} - 2r$: in $\mathcal{I}_1$ both intruders are released at time $t_1$. The total time for any algorithm to capture both, as a function of the first capture's radial coordinate $\rho \le x_i \le 1$, is $\frac{1-x_i}{v} + \sqrt{x_i^2 + \rho^2 - 2x_i\rho\cos(2\theta)} - 2r$ (printed without proof: "determined through geometry and is omitted", → [21]); the paper asserts it is **monotonically decreasing in $x_i$**, so its minimum is at $x_i = 1$ — i.e., the best any algorithm can do is capture one intruder exactly at $t_1$ and reach the second after $\sqrt{1+\rho^2-2\rho\cos(2\theta)} - 2r$ more. The offline algorithm attains this bound and captures both, so any online algorithm loses one of two → ratio $\ge 2$. Input instances $\mathcal{I}_2, \mathcal{I}_3$ (staggered releases: $b$ at $t_1 + \epsilon$, resp. $a$ at $t_1 + \epsilon$, with $\epsilon < L$ where
$$L = 2\sin(\theta)\left(1 - \tfrac{r(1+\rho)}{\sqrt{1+\rho^2-2\rho\cos(2\theta)}}\right)$$
  is the minimum time to move between $(t_2,\alpha_2)$ and $(t_2,-\alpha_2)$) punish the algorithm's choice of side: an algorithm arriving at $(t_1,-\alpha_1)$ (resp. $(t_1,\alpha_1)$) at $t_1$ captures only one of $\mathcal{I}_2$ (resp. $\mathcal{I}_3$), since the second intruder arrives within $\epsilon < L$ and the vehicle cannot cross. Strict inequality case $\frac{1-\rho}{v} < \sqrt{1+\rho^2-2\rho\cos(2\theta)} - 2r$: instances $\mathcal{I}_4, \mathcal{I}_5$ with $\epsilon = \sqrt{1+\rho^2-2\rho\cos(2\theta)} - 2r - \frac{1-\rho}{v}$ play the same role.
- Case (ii) $\theta > \pi/2$: "Except for when $\theta = \pi$, the vehicle must move first to the origin and then to the next intercept point" — the special locations become $(t_1,\alpha_1) = (1-r,\theta)$ and $(t_2,\alpha_2) = (\rho-r,-\theta)$; instances $\mathcal{I}_1,\dots,\mathcal{I}_5$ are constructed analogously (omitted for brevity).
- Local/global: global lower bound over the stated parameter half-space. Analytic, constructive. Physical meaning: the angular separation $2\theta$ of the two extreme arrival angles creates an unavoidable "which end do I guard?" dilemma for fast intruders — the conical analogue of [P005]'s 2-competitiveness threshold $v \ge \frac{1-\rho}{1+\rho}$, with $\sqrt{1+\rho^2-2\rho\cos(2\theta)}$ being the distance between the two perimeter ends (chord of the unit circle subtending $2\theta$ at radius 1 down to radius $\rho$; note at $\rho = 1$ it equals $2\sin\theta$, consistent with $L$).
- Extensibility: the chord-distance term $\sqrt{1+\rho^2-2\rho\cos(2\theta)}$ replaces the line-environment term $2\rho$; the same construction extends to any convex perimeter split into two capture zones.
- **Proof gap**: (a) the monotonicity of $\frac{1-x_i}{v} + \sqrt{x_i^2+\rho^2-2x_i\rho\cos(2\theta)} - 2r$ is asserted, not proven in-paper (it does hold for $v < 1$: the derivative of the square-root term is bounded by 1 while $\frac{1-x_i}{v}$ decreases at rate $1/v > 1$); (b) the $t_1,\alpha_1,t_2,\alpha_2$ derivations and the case (ii) instances are entirely deferred to [21]; (c) Theorem III.4 uses $\theta \le \pi/2$ while Theorem III.3 splits at $\theta < \pi/2$ — boundary $\theta = \pi/2$ treated inconsistently between the two theorems (as printed, p. 2–3).

**Angular path (definition, Section IV, p. 4).** For the vehicle at $(x,\alpha) \in \mathcal{E}(\theta)$, an angular path is a circular arc centered at the origin $\mathcal{T}(x,\bar\beta,\underline\beta) := \{(x,\beta) : \underline\beta \le \beta \le \bar\beta\}$ for $\bar\beta, \underline\beta \in [-\theta,\theta]$ with $\underline\beta \le \alpha \le \bar\beta$ and $\underline\beta \ne \bar\beta$. The vehicle "completes its motion on the angular path" when it returns to its start after traversing all points of $\mathcal{T}$ **twice** (once to an endpoint, once across to the other endpoint and back).

**Theorem IV.1 (Angular Sweep competitiveness; p. 4, eq. (1)).** *For any problem instance $\mathcal{P}(r,\rho,\theta,v)$ such that*
$$v \le \min\left\{\frac{2r}{(\rho+r)\,a\theta},\ \frac{1-\rho}{(1-r)\,a\theta}\right\}, \tag{1}$$
*where $a = 2$ (if $\theta = \pi$) or $a = 4$ (if $\theta \ne \pi$), with the choice of any $x_S \in \left[\frac{\rho-r}{1-a\theta v},\ \min\{1-r, \rho+r\}\right]$, Angular Sweep is 1-competitive. Otherwise, Angular Sweep is not $c$-competitive for any constant $c$.*
- Algorithm (open loop): the vehicle starts at $(x_S, 0)$, $x_S$ in the stated interval, and sweeps the angular path $\mathcal{T}(x_S, -\theta, \theta)$: toward $(x_S,\theta)$ at time 0, then back to $(x_S,-\theta)$, thereafter reversing only at endpoints (for $\theta = \pi$: continuous circling on the radius-$x_S$ circle, one direction, forever).
- Proof approach: if (1) holds, the interval $\left[\frac{\rho-r}{1-a\theta v}, \min\{1-r, \rho+r\}\right]$ is non-empty (decisive algebra: $\frac{\rho-r}{1-a\theta v} \le 1-r \iff a\theta v \le \frac{1-\rho}{1-r}$ and $\frac{\rho-r}{1-a\theta v} \le \rho+r \iff a\theta v \le \frac{2r}{\rho+r}$ — exactly (1)). Worst case: at time $t$ the vehicle has just left $(x_S,\theta)$ and intruder $i$ sits at $(x_S + r, \theta)$. The vehicle needs $a\theta x_S$ to return to $(x_S,\theta)$ (factor $a = 4$ for $\theta \ne \pi$ because it must traverse down to $(x_S,-\theta)$ and back: $2\theta x_S + 2\theta x_S$; factor $a = 2$ for $\theta = \pi$ because circling means the worst case is one full lap $2\pi x_S = 2\theta x_S$); the intruder needs $\frac{x_S + r - \rho}{v}$ to reach the perimeter. Capture is guaranteed iff $a\theta x_S \le \frac{x_S + r - \rho}{v}$ and $x_S \le 1 - r$ (decisive inequality pair, both implied by $x_S \in [\frac{\rho-r}{1-a\theta v}, \min\{1-r,\rho+r\}]$). Every released intruder is therefore captured — 1-competitive since the offline optimum cannot exceed capturing all. Conversely, for any $x_S \notin [\frac{\rho-r}{1-a\theta v}, \min\{1-r,\rho+r\}]$ a stream always arriving at $(1,\theta)$ timed so that an intruder sits at $(x_S+r,\theta)$ whenever the vehicle leaves $(x_S,\theta)$ loses all intruders.
- Local/global: global; **iff** characterization for this algorithm class. Analytic. Physical meaning: 1-competitiveness (offline-equivalent performance) is achievable iff the intruder is slow enough relative to the sweep period — the sweep period $a\theta x_S$ must fit inside the intruder's margin $\frac{x_S+r-\rho}{v}$; larger capture radius $r$ and smaller cone aperture $\theta$ help. Note the two regimes in eq. (1): the $2r$ term dies as $\rho \to 1$ region grows and the sweep term $\frac{1-\rho}{a\theta}$ dies as $\rho \to 1$; the binding constraint switches with $r/\rho$.
- Extensibility: the "period vs. margin" argument is the conical analogue of [P005]'s 1-competitive sweep on a segment ($v \le \frac{1-\rho}{1+\rho}$ regime there); here the aperture $\theta$ and capture radius $r$ enter explicitly.

**Lemma IV.2 (no inter-epoch loss for ConCaC; p. 5).** *Any intruder that lies beyond the location $\left(x_c + r + (2\theta - \beta)v x_C,\ \beta\right)$, $\forall \beta \in [-\theta,\theta]$, in epoch $k$ will either be contained in the set $S^{k+1}_{\text{left}}$ or in $S^{k+1}_{\text{right}}$ in epoch $k+1$ and is not lost at the start of epoch $k+1$ if $v \le \frac{x_C + r - \rho}{2\theta x_C}$.*
- Footnote 2 defines "beyond" = radial coordinate more than $x_c + r + (2\theta-\beta)v x_C$. Proof (sketch, p. 5): the region beyond the sets $\mathcal{S}^k_{\text{left}}, \mathcal{S}^k_{\text{right}}$ is the annular band between them and the environment circumference; comparing the vehicle's epoch duration with the worst-case intruder time-to-perimeter gives the condition — decisive inequality: vehicle epoch time $\le$ intruder remaining time, i.e., $2\theta v x_C \le x_C + r - \rho$ (equivalent to $v \le \frac{x_C+r-\rho}{2\theta x_C}$, which is exactly the non-emptiness requirement $x_C \ge \frac{\rho-r}{1-2\theta v}$ of the epoch radius interval).
- Physical meaning: intruders too far out to be captured this epoch are still alive and re-classified next epoch — ConCaC never loses an intruder "for free"; losses only occur through the majority-side choice.

**Theorem IV.3 (ConCaC competitiveness; p. 5, eq. (2)).** *For any problem instance $\mathcal{P}(\theta, r, \rho, v)$ such that*
$$v \le \min\left\{\frac{r}{\theta(\rho+r)},\ \frac{1-\rho}{\theta(2 - 3r + \rho)}\right\}, \tag{2}$$
*with the choice of any $x_C \in \left[\frac{\rho-r}{1-2\theta v},\ \min\left\{\rho+r,\ \frac{1-r}{1+v\theta}\right\}\right]$, the ConCaC algorithm is 2-competitive.*
- Algorithm (Algorithm 1, p. 5): select $x_C$ in the interval; wait until time $1 - \min\{1, x_C + r + 2\theta v x_C\}$ (time 0 = first intruder's arrival; vehicle starts at $(x_C,0)$); then in each epoch $k$, compare $|S^k_{\text{left}}|$ vs $|S^k_{\text{right}}|$ and sweep the majority side: if $|S^k_{\text{left}}| < |S^k_{\text{right}}|$ sweep $\mathcal{T}(x_C, 0, \theta)$ out to $(x_C,\theta)$ and back to $(x_C,0)$; else sweep $\mathcal{T}(x_C, -\theta, 0)$. The sets (p. 4, printed exactly):
$$S^k_{\text{right}}(\rho,v) := \{(y,\beta) : \rho + \beta x_C v < y \le \min\{1,\ x_C + r + (2\theta - \beta)v x_C\}\ \forall \beta \in [0,\theta]\},$$
$$S^k_{\text{left}}(\rho,v) := \{(y,\beta) : \rho - \beta x_C v < y \le \min\{1,\ x_C + r + (2\theta + \beta)v x_C\}\ \forall \beta \in (0,-\theta]\}.$$
  The lower edge $\rho \pm \beta x_C v$ is the radial position at epoch end of an intruder at $\rho$ when the sweep passes its angle; the upper edge is the farthest intruder still capturable within the epoch's sweep. Fig. 2 (p. 5) illustrates for $x_C = r + \rho$.
- Proof approach: (2) is exactly the non-emptiness of the $x_C$ interval (decisive algebra: $\frac{\rho-r}{1-2\theta v} \le \rho+r \iff 2\theta v(\rho+r) \le 2r \iff v \le \frac{r}{\theta(\rho+r)}$; $\frac{\rho-r}{1-2\theta v} \le \frac{1-r}{1+v\theta} \iff v\theta(\rho - r + 2(1-r)) \le 1-\rho \iff v \le \frac{1-\rho}{\theta(2-3r+\rho)}$). Lemma IV.2 then guarantees every intruder is in one of the two sets every epoch; the vehicle always sweeps the side with more intruders, so "the vehicle will capture at least half of the total number of intruders that arrive in the environment, assuming that an optimal offline algorithm captures all intruders" (p. 5) — ratio $\le 2$.
- Local/global: global within the regime (2). Analytic (halving/charging argument). Physical meaning: ConCaC sacrifices the perfect-play regime of Angular Sweep for a twice-optimal guarantee on a strictly larger parameter set — compare (2) with (1): the $a\theta$ factor drops to $\theta$ (no $a = 4$ sweep-period penalty and no $2r$ numerator constraint), so ConCaC dominates Angular Sweep's regime wherever 1-competitiveness is unattainable; on Fig. 4 the ConCaC curve strictly dominates the Angular Sweep curve.
- **Proof gap**: the halving step is stated in one sentence; the charging showing per-epoch majority capture implies $\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{I}) \ge \mathcal{O}(\mathcal{I})/2$ over the whole instance (including intruders deferred across multiple epochs and the initial waiting phase) is not carried out in-paper — details in [21]. Also the epoch duration is implicit (arc out-and-back of length $2\theta x_C$ plus zero dwell) and must be inferred from Lemma IV.2's proof sketch.

---

**SNP machinery (Section IV-C, p. 5–6).**
- Partition $\mathcal{E}(\theta)$ into $n_s = \lceil \theta/\theta_s \rceil$ sectors, each of aperture $2\theta_s = 2\arctan(r/\rho)$; since $r < \rho$, $\theta_s < \pi/4$. Sector $N_1$ = leftmost; resting point of sector $N_l$:
$$(x_l, \alpha_l) = \left(\frac{\rho}{\cos(\theta)},\ \left(l - \tfrac{n_s+1}{2}\right)2\theta_s\right) \quad \text{(as printed; the } \cos(\theta) \text{ argument is presumably } \theta_s \text{ — see limitations)},$$
  defined so that the portion of the perimeter within that sector is completely contained in the capture circle (consistency check: distance from $\left(\frac{\rho}{\cos\theta_s}, \alpha_l\right)$ to the sector's perimeter edges $(\rho, \alpha_l \pm \theta_s)$ is $\rho\tan\theta_s = r$ exactly, since $\theta_s = \arctan(r/\rho)$).
- Distance between the two farthest resting points, eq. (3) (p. 6, printed exactly):
$$D = \begin{cases} 2\frac{\rho}{\cos(\theta_s)}\sin\left((n_s-1)\theta_s\right), & \text{if } (n_s-1)\theta_s < \frac{\pi}{2} \\[4pt] 2\frac{\rho}{\cos(\theta_s)}, & \text{otherwise.}\end{cases} \tag{3}$$
  ($n_s = 1 \Rightarrow D = 0$: the single resting point captures everything, cf. Lemma III.1.) Note the chord-vs-diameter switch at $(n_s-1)\theta_s = \pi/2$ mirrors Lemma III.2's split.
- Time is divided into intervals of length $D$ (equivalently, three annuli of width $Dv$); the $j$-th interval is $[(j-1)D, jD]$; $S^j_l$ = intruders arriving in sector $N_l$ during interval $j$. Finite competitiveness requires $\frac{1-\rho}{v} \ge 3D$ (intruders take at least $3D$ to reach the perimeter) and, for the initial phase (wait $D$ at the origin after the first intruder arrives, move to the max-count sector, wait until $3D$), $\frac{\rho}{\cos(\theta_s)} \le 2D$ (p. 6).
- Comparison **C1** (Line 6 of Algorithm 2): with the vehicle at sector $N_i$'s resting point at iteration $j$, define $\eta^l_i = |S^{j+2}_l| + |S^{j+3}_l|$ if $l \ne i$ and $\eta^i_i = |S^{j+1}_i| + |S^{j+2}_i| + |S^{j+3}_i|$; choose $k^* = \arg\max_{k}\{\eta^1_i,\dots,\eta^{n_s}_i\}$ (ties: prefer $N_i$, then the sector with the most intruders in interval $j+2$, then least index). Output $N_o$. The head-start given to the current sector ($j+1$ term) and to others ($j+2, j+3$ terms) accounts for the $D$-time switching cost.
- Comparison **C2** (Line 7): if $N_o \ne N_i$ and $|S^{j+2}_o| \ge |S^{j+1}_i|$, move to $(x_o, \alpha_o)$ (arriving within $D$) and capture $S^{j+2}_o$; else stay at $(x_i,\alpha_i)$ and capture $S^{j+1}_i$, re-evaluating after $D$.

**Lemma IV.4 (per-iteration capture guarantee; p. 6).** *Let the vehicle be located at a resting point $(x_i,\alpha_i)$ of sector $N_i$. Then, for any $j \ge 1$, the vehicle always captures intruders in either $S^{j+1}_i$ or $S^{j+2}_o$, where $N_o$ denotes the sector selected by SNP after C1.*
- Proof (in-paper, short): if $N_o = N_i$ the vehicle stays and captures $S^{j+1}_i$; if $N_o \ne N_i$, case (i) $|S^{j+1}_i| > |S^{j+2}_o|$: stays, captures $S^{j+1}_i$; case (ii) $|S^{j+1}_i| \le |S^{j+2}_o|$: spends at most $D$ moving to $N_o$'s resting point and captures $S^{j+2}_o$ (feasible because the interval length $D$ equals the maximum inter-resting-point travel time and the $j+2$ intruders arrive after the move).

**Lemma IV.5 (charging; p. 6–7).** *In algorithm SNP, any two consecutive captured intervals pay for a total of $3(n_s - 1)$ lost intervals.*
- Proof (in-paper, dense): by Lemma IV.4 the vehicle always captures an interval, so consecutive captured intervals fall into four types (illustrative images only in [21]): (a) stay–stay on the same side; (b) stay then move; (c) move then stay at the new sector; (d) move then move elsewhere. For each type the paper enumerates which lost intervals $S^j_l, S^{j+1}_l, \dots$ ($\forall l \ne i$ etc.) are charged to the two captured intervals via C1/C2, always totalling $(n_s-1) + (n_s-1) + (n_s-1) = 3(n_s-1)$ charges. Example (type (a), p. 6–7): capturing $S^{j+1}_i, S^{j+2}_i$ while losing $S^{j+2}_l, S^{j+3}_l$ for all $l \ne i$ ($2n_s - 2$ charges) plus, because capturing $S^{j+1}_i$ required C1 to have selected $N_i$ one step earlier, another $n_s - 1$ charges for $S^j_l, S^{j+1}_l$ combined.
- Physical meaning: each dwell epoch of length $D$ leaves $n_s - 1$ other sectors unguarded; over the two-interval (and transition) span, three such windows of $n_s-1$ unguarded sector-intervals accrue.

**Lemma IV.6 (completeness of accounting; p. 7).** *Each lost interval is accounted for by the captured intervals of the SNP algorithm.*
- Proof (in-paper, case analysis): any SNP realization decomposes into **traces** $\{k_1,\dots,k_{n_s}\}$ ($k_l$ = number of intervals captured while dwelling at $N_l$'s resting point) of three cases: (i) $k_i = 3$, $k_l = 0\ \forall l \ne i$; (ii) $0 \le k_i < 3$ and $k_o = 2$; (iii) $k_i = 0$, $k_o = 1$, $k_{o'} = 1$. Each case verifies via C1/C2 inequalities (e.g., in case (i) sub-case (b), $\eta^l_i \le \eta^i_i$ accounts for $S^{j+2}_l, S^{j+3}_l$, and a sector $N_o$ losing $|S^{j+2}_o| < |S^{j+1}_i|$ cannot be re-selected at $(j+1)D$, so $\eta^o_i < \eta^i_i$ then accounts for $S^{j+3}_o$) that every lost interval is charged to some captured interval. Boundary intervals handled with dummy zero-cardinality intervals $S^0_i$ and $S^{Y+1}_i$ ($Y$ = last non-empty interval), "assumed captured" by the vehicle.

**Theorem IV.7 (SNP competitiveness; p. 8).** *For any problem instance $\mathcal{P}(\theta,\rho,v,r)$ that satisfies $3D \le \frac{1-\rho}{v}$ and $\frac{\rho}{\cos(\theta_s)} \le 2D$, SNP is $\frac{3n_s - 1}{2}$-competitive, where $n_s = \lceil\theta/\theta_s\rceil$, $\theta_s = \arctan(r/\rho)$ and $D$ is defined in (3).*
- Proof: from Lemmas IV.5 and IV.6, "every two consecutively captured intervals pay for $3n_s - 3$ lost intervals and every lost interval is accounted by two consecutive captured intervals. Assuming that the optimal offline algorithm captures all intruder intervals, i.e., $3n_s - 1$, the claim follows" (p. 8). Decisive ratio: per 2 captured intervals there are at most $3(n_s-1)$ lost, so $\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{I})/\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{I}) \le \frac{2 + 3(n_s-1)}{2} = \frac{3n_s-1}{2}$.
- Local/global: global within the stated regime; analytic (accounting/amortized analysis). Physical meaning: the price of guarding with a **small capture radius** ($r$ small ⇒ $n_s$ large) is a linearly growing competitive ratio $\approx \frac{3}{2} n_s \approx \frac{3\theta}{2\arctan(r/\rho)}$ — a clean, explicit r-to-c tradeoff unavailable in the two earlier algorithms (whose regimes simply become empty as $r \to 0$). Example: $n_s = 2$ gives 2.5-competitiveness, matching the paper's "$r = 0.05$, SNP is 2.5-competitive for $\rho < 0.2$" (p. 8).
- Extensibility: the sector/interval/charging template applies to any star-shaped perimeter with locally covering resting points; the trace decomposition (Lemma IV.6) is the paper's main combinatorial device and would need re-derivation for other geometries.

**Information / termination structure across all results**: the online algorithm observes each intruder only upon release (position and time), then tracks radial motion deterministically; "capture" (win per intruder) occurs when the intruder enters the capture circle, "loss" when it reaches $z = \rho$; the offline adversary is the input instance itself (sup over $\mathcal{I}$), so the model is a worst-case scheduling game rather than a differential game with evading control — intruders have no control input after release.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues

**Author-stated**:
- Conference version omits proofs: "For brevity, we only provide an outline for some of our intermediate results. The detailed proofs of all results are available in [21]" (p. 2); Lemma III.1/III.2 proofs "omitted for brevity" (p. 2); Theorem III.4's time expression "determined through geometry and is omitted" (p. 3); case (ii) instances "omitted for brevity" (p. 4); Lemma IV.5's four type illustrations only "see [21] for images" (p. 6).
- Model restriction: intruders move radially at fixed speed (no evasion control); single vehicle; footnote 1 defers double-integrator and other dynamics to future work (p. 2); "Key future directions include a cooperative multi-vehicle scenario with communication and energy constraints" (p. 8) — realized in the follow-up Bajaj et al. 2024 "Multivehicle Perimeter Defense in Conical Environments" (present in this library).
- The choice of $x_S$ and $x_C$ intervals is "justified in [21]" (p. 4, p. 4–5), not in the conference paper.

**Critical observations** (with page/eq.):
- **No exact characterization of $c(\mathcal{P})$**: only two necessary conditions (Thms III.3, III.4) and three algorithm-specific sufficient conditions are given; the gap between them is visible in Fig. 4 (e.g., the region between the III.4 curve and the ConCaC curve where $c(\mathcal{P})$ is between 2 and ConCaC's 2 is claimed but optimality of 2 is unproven; whether some algorithm is better than $\frac{3n_s-1}{2}$ in SNP's regime is open).
- **Resting-point typo**: the sector resting point is printed as $\left(\frac{\rho}{\cos(\theta)}, (l - \frac{n_s+1}{2})2\theta_s\right)$ (p. 5–6), but the containment property (sector perimeter arc within capture circle) requires $\frac{\rho}{\cos(\theta_s)}$ with the sector half-angle $\theta_s = \arctan(r/\rho)$ — the correct form is used in eq. (3), in the requirement $\frac{\rho}{\cos(\theta_s)} \le 2D$ (p. 6, 8), and in the Fig. 3 annotation $\frac{\rho}{\cos(\theta_s)}$; the printed $\cos(\theta)$ is inconsistent with $\theta_s < \pi/4 < \theta$ in general.
- **Boundary inconsistency**: Theorem III.3 splits cases at $\theta < \pi/2$ / $\theta \ge \pi/2$; Theorem III.4 at $\theta \le \pi/2$ / $\theta > \pi/2$ (p. 2–3). The measure-zero boundary $\theta = \pi/2$ is thus covered by different formulas in the two theorems (harmless but should be reconciled).
- **Theorem IV.1 converse subtlety**: the "otherwise not $c$-competitive" claim holds for the open-loop sweep with fixed $x_S$, and the adversarial stream is timed to the sweep phase — for $x_S$ outside the interval the vehicle loses *all* intruders of that stream, a strong statement that relies on the stream never ending; fine, but it means Angular Sweep's 1-competitiveness is knife-edge in $x_S$.
- **ConCaC's halving argument is terse** (p. 5, end of Theorem IV.3 proof): "assuming that an optimal offline algorithm captures all intruders" quietly restricts to the regime where offline captures everything — which by Theorem III.3 is exactly the complement of the finite-$c$ impossibility region, so consistency holds, but the multi-epoch deferral bookkeeping is not shown in-paper.
- **SNP initial-phase feasibility**: the requirement $\frac{\rho}{\cos\theta_s} \le 2D$ (p. 6) can fail when $n_s$ is small and $D$ is small; SNP's regime then vanishes (consistent with "SNP is ineffective for large $\rho$" and "for high values of $r$", p. 8).
- **Accounting bound is loose by construction**: Lemma IV.5's $3(n_s-1)$ charges double-count overlapping windows of lost intervals; the true ratio of SNP could be smaller — no instance is shown to achieve $\frac{3n_s-1}{2}$ (no matching lower bound for the algorithm).
- **Extreme-speed restriction** is imported from [20] (Lemma 1 there) without restating its proof; it presumes the intruder positions are continuously observable once released (consistent with the information structure, p. 2).
- Minor editorial: "SNR" appears once for SNP (p. 6); abstract phrasing "provably 1, and 2-competitive" reads oddly but correctly means 1- and 2-competitive (p. 1).

## 8. Citation Value

This paper is the **second pillar of the worst-case (competitive-analysis) perimeter-defense thread** archived in this library (after the line-environment P005 = ref [20], before the multivehicle conical extension, Bajaj et al. 2024), and it is the canonical citation for **worst-case guarantees in 2-D sector/conical guard zones with a finite capture radius**. Its role in a related-work section is (a) as the citation establishing that the conical geometry — the natural model for a sensor/weapon cone or a UAV approach wedge — yields qualitatively new thresholds (capture-radius-dependent impossibility $2(\rho\sin\theta - r) > \frac{1-\rho}{v}$, chord-based $c(\mathcal{P}) \ge 2$ bound, sector-count-dependent ratio $\frac{3n_s-1}{2}$), and (b) as the deterministic worst-case counterpoint to HJBI/reach-avoid game formulations whose applicability is limited to low dimensions [13], [14]. Suggested citation sentences: "Bajaj et al. [P006] extended competitive perimeter defense from linear to conical environments, proving that no finite-competitive algorithm exists when $2(\rho\sin\theta - r) > \frac{1-\rho}{v}$, that $c(\mathcal{P}) \ge 2$ when $\frac{1-\rho}{v} \le \sqrt{1+\rho^2-2\rho\cos 2\theta} - 2r$, and designing algorithms that are 1-competitive (angular sweep, $v \le \min\{\frac{2r}{(\rho+r)a\theta}, \frac{1-\rho}{(1-r)a\theta}\}$), 2-competitive (ConCaC, $v \le \min\{\frac{r}{\theta(\rho+r)}, \frac{1-\rho}{\theta(2-3r+\rho)}\}$), and $\frac{3n_s-1}{2}$-competitive (sector-based SNP, $n_s = \lceil\theta/\arctan(r/\rho)\rceil$), respectively." Or, for gap-setting in a modeling section: "Finite capture radius and two-dimensional angular geometry were first accounted for in worst-case perimeter defense by [P006], which showed the achievable competitive ratio to grow linearly in the number of capture-radius-limited sectors, $\frac{3}{2}\lceil\theta/\arctan(r/\rho)\rceil$, leaving the exact optimal ratio $c(\mathcal{P})$ open."
