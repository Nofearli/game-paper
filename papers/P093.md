## 2. Research Background
The paper studies multiplayer pursuit-evasion on a plane where two parallel "lifelines" (goal lines) separate a play area from two goal areas — a water-crossing metaphor in which several slower swimmers try to reach a riverbank while a faster opponent tries to intercept them. Motivated by cooperative anti-predation behavior in biology (deer vs. cheetah), robot competitions, coastal patrol against multiple intruders, and multi-vehicle collision avoidance, the authors formalize a scenario in which **one superior (faster) pursuer P chases n slower evaders E_1..E_n** that must reach one of two goal areas. Classical Isaacs/HJI retrograde-path analysis becomes intractable with multiple participants and multiple terminal manifolds, so the authors follow the explicit-strategy + decomposition line of the lifeline-game literature (Zhang & Zha 2018; Zha et al. 2016; Yan, Shi & Zhong 2021b; their own TAC companion paper on two pursuers vs. one evader). The contribution direction is reversed relative to that companion paper: here the pursuer is superior and the many weak agents must cooperate.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
- Problem (Sec. 2): simple-motion players, point capture (radius 0), speed ratios a_i = v_p/v_i ≥ 1 (if a_i < 1, "the pursuer will never capture the evader E_i", p.3). Two questions: **Q1** (game of kind) — given initial positions and optimal play, how many evaders does P capture before the rest reach a goal area; **Q2** — what are the cooperative evasion strategies of the multiple evaders (p.3). Termination: P captures all; all evaders reach goal area; a subset captured and the remainder escapes (p.3).
- Gap [authors' claim]: "The majority of current research has concentrated on scenarios involving cooperative interactions between two agents" (p.2); existing lifeline/reach-avoid work handles one-vs-two (Yan et al. 2021a; Deng, Shi & Zhong 2024; Garcia et al. 2019) or two-vs-one (their own TAC paper), but not **one superior pursuer vs. many evaders with two lifelines and evader-side teamwork**.
- Verified against cited text: Zhang & Zha (2018, ref. [62], = P-archive "Evasion strategies of a three-player lifeline game") indeed studies a three-player lifeline game by decomposition, and Yan, Shi & Zhong (2021b, ref. [58]) study the lifeline game with limited lifetime — both are 1v1/3-player, not n-evader teaming; Chen, Zhou & Tomlin (2017) aggregate multiplayer reach-avoid into pairwise 1v1 games via matching, which does not model sacrifice-type cooperation. The claimed gap is genuine, with the caveat that the "cooperation" analyzed here is one-sided: only the evader side is optimized, and P's sequence is assumed (then enumerated), not game-theoretically optimized against the evader team.
- Method stance [authors' claim]: since multiple terminal conditions preclude direct use of Isaacs' method, they use "explicit policy and decomposition methods" — build per-lifeline barriers, merge them, and give explicit strategies (p.5). Verified: the construction is geometric/analytic (Apollonius circles, payoff stationarity), no HJI PDE is solved.

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Theory (barriers)**: closed-form barrier curves for each lifeline **without time delay** (Thm 4.1, eq. (13)) and **with time delay** Δt = Σ t_j accumulated while P dispatches previously pursued evaders (Thm 4.2 eq. (19), Cor. 4.1 eq. (26)); merging B_i^1 and B_i^2 yields per-evader pursuer dominance region (PDR, 𝒲_{P_i}) and evader dominance region (EDR, 𝒲_{E_i} = Ω_play ∖ 𝒲_{P_i}); Δ-PDR shrinks monotonically with Δt and can vanish (Fig. 5).
2. **Modeling (game of kind)**: terminal payoff functions J_2 (28) / J_1 (31) equal to the topmost/bottommost y-coordinate reachable on the evader's updated Apollonius circle; cooperative vs. non-cooperative value functions (29)-(30); lifeline-selection rule min{J_1, h−J_2} (Cor. 5.1) and escape criterion min{J_1, h−J_2} ≤ 0 (Cor. 5.2); necessary-and-sufficient dominance characterization under delays (Thm 5.1, eq. (33)); capture-count bookkeeping: with m+z evaders in PDR, z escape via delays and the remaining m form s teams giving m−s captures and n−m+s escapes; three two-evader scenarios (n=0, n=1, n≥1).
3. **Algorithm (cooperative strategies)**: cooperative sacrifice of E_1..E_{k−1} to maximize the escape height of the team's designated (last-pursued) escaper E_k, cast as a constrained optimization over heading angles ξ_1, φ_2..φ_k — problems (38)/(45) for two evaders, (50)/(51) for team 1 of k evaders, (54)/(55) for later teams — solved by **CMA-ES** (Hansen & Ostermeier 2001); Cartesian oval used for the time-optimal target once escape is guaranteed (Lemma 6.2). Unknown pursuit order handled by enumerating sequences, maximizing captures, tie-broken by shortest total pursuit time (p.11).
4. **Experiment**: 2-evader validations of the three scenarios including a maneuvering (direction-changing) first evader, plus 3-, 4-, and 5-evader (two-team) cooperative escapes (Figs. 10-16); no quantitative benchmark table.

## 5. Key Mathematical Results

**Model and information structure (pp. 2-3, eq. (1)-(3)).** Simple motion, all players: ẋ_i = v_i cos φ_i, ẏ_i = v_i sin φ_i, ẋ_P = v_p cos φ, ẏ_P = v_p sin φ (eq. (1)), with a_i = v_p/v_i ≥ 1. Lifelines L_1 = {(x,y): y = 0}, L_2 = {y = h}; Ω_goal = {y ≤ 0} ∪ {y ≥ h}; Ω_play = {0 < y < h}. Evader E_i's winning set (eq. (2)): 𝓔_i = {x_i, y_i, x_P, y_P | ‖x_P − x_i‖₂ ≥ 0 ∧ y_i ∈ {0, h}} (as printed; the ≥ 0 is trivially true — evidently meant "> 0", i.e., not yet captured); pursuer's winning set (eq. (3)): 𝓟 = {‖x_P − x_i‖₂ = 0 ∧ 0 < y_i < h}. Information: non-anticipative; P "can acquire the control inputs and current states of the evaders" — labeled a perfect-information game; evaders know all current states and all know (1) (p.3).

- **Lemma 3.1 (p. 3, eq. (6))** — *Parallel pursuit strategy.* If E_i uses control φ_i, then P using **φ = arcsin((1/a_i) sin φ_i)** (eq. (6)) captures E_i on or within the Apollonius circle 𝓒_i. Proof: sine theorem in ▵PCE_i, sin φ_i / sin φ = |PC|/|E_iC| = a_i (eq. (7)) ⇒ (8). If E_i keeps changing direction, the reachable region shrinks and P, mirroring via (6), still captures inside 𝓒_i (Fig. 3(b)). Global, analytic; the workhorse pursuit policy used throughout all simulations.
- **Lemma 3.2 (p. 3, eq. (9))** — *Capture time along straight lines.* T_i = ‖x_i − x_P‖₂ sin φ / (v_i sin(φ_i + φ)) (from sine law eq. (10)), bounded by ‖x_i−x_P‖₂/(v_i+v_p) ≤ T_i ≤ ‖x_i−x_P‖₂/(v_p−v_i); minimum at the near intersection of line P E_i with 𝓒_i, maximum at the far one.
- **Lemma 3.3 (p. 4, eq. (11))** — *Capture angle.* Θ = arccos(1 − 2/a_i²) = 2 arcsin(1/a_i), from sin∠BPO_i = R_i/‖x_P − x_{O_i}‖₂ = 1/a_i (eq. (12)) with tangents PA, PB from P to 𝓒_i. Physical: the cone of directions from P inside which interception on 𝓒_i occurs.
- **Reachable region (eq. (4)-(5), p. 3).** 𝓡_i = {x : ‖x−x_i‖₂ ≤ (1/a_i)‖x−x_P‖₂}, boundary 𝓒_i the Apollonius circle with center O_i = ((a_i²x_i − x_P)/(a_i²−1), (a_i²y_i − y_P)/(a_i²−1)) and radius R_i = (a_i/(a_i²−1))‖x_P − x_i‖₂; for a_i = 1, 𝓒_i degenerates to the perpendicular bisector of P E_i.

- **Theorem 4.1 (p. 5, eq. (13)-(18))** — *Barriers without time delay.* For any P position x_P^0 = (x_P^0, y_P^0) in the play area, the barrier w.r.t. L_2 is B_i^2(x_P^0, a_i) = {(x,y) ∈ ℝ² : (x − x_P^0)² − (a_i²−1)(y − h)² + (1 − 1/a_i²)(y_P^0 − h)² = 0, x ∈ [k₁,k₂], y ∈ [0,h]}, and w.r.t. L_1 is B_i^1(x_P^0, a_i) = {(x,y): (a_i²−1)y² − (x − x_P^0)² − (1 − 1/a_i²)(y_P^0)² = 0, x ∈ [k₃,k₄], y ∈ [0,h]}, with k₁,k₂ = x_P^0 ∓/± √((a_i²−1)h² − (1−1/a_i²)(y_P^0−h)²) and k₃,k₄ = x_P^0 ∓/± √((a_i²−1)h² − (1−1/a_i²)(y_P^0)²). *Assumptions:* a_i > 1, 0 < y_P^0 < h, point capture, straight-line optimal trajectories. *Proof:* payoff J(τ) = ‖x_T − x_P^0‖₂ − a_i‖x_T − x_i‖₂ (eq. (14)) with target x_T = (τ, h) on L_2; stationarity dJ(τ*)/dτ = 0 gives (τ*−x_P)/‖x_T*−x_P^0‖₂ = a_i(τ*−x_i)/‖x_T*−x_i‖₂ (eq. (15)); on the barrier J(τ*) = 0 gives ‖x_T*−x_P^0‖₂ = a_i‖x_T*−x_i‖₂ (eq. (16)); eliminating τ* yields the evader-locus equation (17): (x_i − x_P^0)² − (a_i²−1)(y_i−h)² + (1−1/a_i²)(y_P^0−h)² = 0; evaluating at y_i = 0 gives (18) hence k₁,k₂; symmetric argument for L_1. *Decisive step:* combining (15)-(16) ⇒ τ* − x_P^0 = a_i²(τ* − x_i), i.e., the barrier is the locus of evader positions whose *optimal* (tangency) Apollonius circle exactly touches the lifeline. Analytic, global; geometrically each B is a hyperbola branch whose vertex sits at distance (h−y_P^0)/a_i below L_2 (equivalently above L_1), so the branch exists only for y ≤ h − (h−y_P^0)/a_i (my check; consistent with Fig. 4 and with k-values taken at the *opposite* lifeline). *Meaning:* B_i^1/B_i^2 partition Ω_play into PDR 𝒲_{P_i} (lens between the two branches, containing x_P in Fig. 4) and EDR 𝒲_{E_i} = Ω_play ∖ 𝒲_{P_i}: inside PDR capture is guaranteed before the lifeline; inside EDR escape is guaranteed.
- **Theorem 4.2 (pp. 5-6, eq. (19)-(25))** — *Barrier with time delay (w.r.t. L_2).* Setup: P pursues m ≤ n evaders in sequence E_1..E_m; after capturing E_{k−1} at position x_P, the accumulated delay is Δt = Σ_{j=1}^{k−1} t_j. The delayed barrier is the parameterized curve **x(τ*) = τ* − mn, y(τ*) = −m√(1−n²) + h** (eq. (19)), where m = ‖x_T* − x_P‖₂/a_k + l, n = (τ* − x_P)/(a_k‖x_T* − x_P‖₂), l = v_k Δt, x_T* = (τ*, h); constraint 0 ≤ −m√(1−n²) + h ≤ h. Admissible target set: if Δt ≤ (a_k²−1)|h−y_P|/(a_k v_k) then 𝕌 = ℝ; else 𝕌 = {τ* : |τ* − x_P| ≥ √((a_k l/(a_k²−1))² − (h−y_P)²)}. *Proof:* delayed payoff J(τ) = ‖x_T − x_P‖₂ − a_k‖x_T − x_k‖₂ + v_p Δt (eq. (20)) — the evader position on the barrier is its position *before* the delay, so J = 0 ⇔ a_k‖x_T*−x_k‖ = ‖x_T*−x_P‖ + v_pΔt ⇔ travel times satisfy D_E/v_k = Δt + D_P/v_p (simultaneous arrival at the lifeline when P is busy for Δt); stationarity (21) + J(τ*) = 0 (22) give the circle parameterization (23); feasibility of τ* as optimal target: for x_k ≥ x_P, (24) (τ*−x_P)(1 − (‖x_T*−x_P‖₂ + a_k v_kΔt)/(a_k²‖x_T*−x_P‖₂)) ≥ 0, whence (25) (a_k²−1)‖x_T*−x_P‖₂ ≥ a_k v_kΔt, which holds for all τ* exactly under the stated Δt threshold (proof pp. 5-6). *Decisive inequality:* (25). Analytic, global. *Meaning:* the barrier point lies at distance m = D_P/a_k + v_kΔt from the target point — the evader's head start is converted into an enlarged "simultaneity circle"; as Δt grows the enclosed Δ-PDR 𝒲^Δ_{P_k} shrinks and can become empty (Fig. 5(a) vs. 5(b)).
- **Corollary 4.1 (p. 6, eq. (26))** — *Delayed barrier w.r.t. L_1.* x(τ*) = τ* − mn, y(τ*) = m√(1−n²), same m, n with x_T* = (τ*, 0), l = v_kΔt; constraint 0 ≤ m√(1−n²) ≤ h; 𝕌: if Δt ≤ **(a_k²−1)|y_P|/a_k** then 𝕌 = ℝ (as printed — **typo: v_k missing**, compare the second threshold and Fig. 5's caption which both use (a_k²−1)|y_P|/(a_k v_k)); if Δt > (a_k²−1)|y_P|/(a_k v_k) then 𝕌 = {τ*: |τ*−x_P| ≥ √((a_k l/(a_k²−1))² − y_P²)}. Proof omitted ("similar to Theorem 4.2").

- **Payoffs and value functions (pp. 6-7, eq. (27)-(31)).** After P captures E_{k−1} at x*_{k−1}, E_k sits at x*_k; the updated Apollonius circle (27) has center ((a_k²x*_k − x*_{k−1})/(a_k²−1), (a_k²y*_k − y*_{k−1})/(a_k²−1)) and radius R_k = a_k‖x*_{k−1} − x*_k‖₂/(a_k²−1). Hence **J₂ (28) = (a_k²y*_k − y*_{k−1})/(a_k²−1) + (a_k/(a_k²−1))‖x*_{k−1} − x*_k‖₂** is exactly the *maximum y-coordinate E_k can still reach* (top of the circle) and **J₁ (31) = same first term − (a_k/(a_k²−1))‖x*_{k−1}−x*_k‖₂** the minimum. Value functions: non-cooperative V_{n2} = min_{u_P} max_{u_{E_k}} J₂ (29), cooperative V_{c2} = min_{u_P} max_{u_{E_1},…,u_{E_k}} J₂ (30); for L_1, V_{n1} = u_P max_{u_{E_k}} min J₁ and V_{c1} = u_P max_{u_{E_1},…,u_{E_k}} min J₁ (as printed, the min over u_P is missing from the V_{n1}/V_{c1} expressions — typographical slip, p.7).
- **Corollary 5.1 (p. 7, eq. (32))** — *Lifeline choice.* E_k selects L_2 iff J₁ > h − J₂, L_1 iff J₁ < h − J₂, either iff equal; i.e., choose the lifeline attaining min{J₁, h−J₂}.
- **Corollary 5.2 (p. 7)** — *Escape criterion.* Under optimal cooperative or non-cooperative evasion by k evaders, E_k reaches a goal area iff **min{J₁, h − J₂} ≤ 0**; if min{J₁, h−J₂} > 0, P captures E_k first. (Because J₂ ≥ h ⇔ the circle touches L₂ and J₁ ≤ 0 ⇔ it touches L₁.)
- **Lemma 5.1 (p. 7)** — For the game (1) with terminal payoff J₂ (28) or J₁ (31), optimal trajectories of all players are straight lines. Proof: one-line appeal to classical Isaacs' method for simple motion with terminal cost (*proof gap (minor): no verification argument, e.g., via the HJI equation or a nonanticipativity argument, is provided*).
- **Theorem 5.1 (pp. 7-8, eq. (33)-(36))** — *Necessary and sufficient dominance conditions under delay.* E_k escapes iff its initial position x_k ∈ 𝓡_{E_k}; P captures iff x_k ∈ 𝓡_{P_k} = 𝓡²_{P_k} ∩ 𝓡¹_{P_k}, where (33): 𝓡²_{P_k} = {x ∈ ℝ : x = x*_k** − nm, 0 < y < −m√(1−n²) + h}, 𝓡¹_{P_k} = {x = x*_k** − nm, m√(1−n²) < y < h}, 𝓡_{E_k} = Ω_play ∖ 𝓡_{P_k}, with **m = ‖x*_k** − x*_{k−1}‖₂/a_k + v_{k−1}Δt** (as printed; by the proof's own reduction to Thm 4.2 and by the 𝕌 conditions below, v_k·Δt is expected — **probable typo**), n = (x*_k** − x*_{k−1})/(a_k‖x*_k** − x*_{k−1}‖₂), Δt = (‖x_P^0 − x*_1‖₂ + Σ_{j=2}^{k−1}‖x*_j − x*_{j−1}‖₂)/v_p. Parameter sets: for 𝓡²_{P_k} (x*_k** = (x*_k**, h)): if Δt ≤ (a_k²−1)|h − y*_{k−1}|/(a_k v_k), 𝕌₂ = ℝ; else 𝕌₂ = {x*_k** : |x*_k** − x*_{k−1}| ≥ √((a_k v_kΔt/(a_k²−1))² − (h−y*_{k−1})²)}. For 𝓡¹_{P_k} (x*_k** = (x*_k**, 0)): if Δt ≤ **(1−a_k²)|y*_{k−1}|/(a_k v_k)**, 𝕌₁ = ℝ (**as printed; negative for a_k > 1 — typo for (a_k²−1)**); else 𝕌₁ = {x*_k** : |x*_k** − x*_{k−1}| ≥ √((a_k v_kΔt/(a_k²−1))² − (y*_{k−1})²)}. *Proof:* two-phase decomposition — [t₀, Δt]: E_k moves from x_k to x*_k on the circle (x*_k − x_k)² + (y*_k − y_k)² = (v_kΔt)² (34); [Δt, Δt+t_k]: Apollonius circle 𝓒_k(Δt+t_k) = {x : ‖x − x*_k‖₂ = (1/a_k)‖x − x*_{k−1}‖₂} (35); escape iff 𝓒_k(Δt+t_k) intersects or is tangent to the lifeline; straight-path optimality of E_k via triangle inequality ‖x*_k** − x_k‖₂ < ‖x'_k − x_k‖₂ + ‖x*_k** − x'_k‖₂ (36); then apply Thm 4.2 with initial points x*_{k−1} (P) and x_k (E_k) and delay Δt. *Meaning:* the two time-delay barriers enclose exactly the states from which the delayed pursuer still wins; Remark 5.1: 𝓡_{P_k} ≡ 𝒲^Δ_{P_k}, 𝓡_{E_k} ≡ 𝒲^Δ_{E_k}. Global, analytic, necessary-and-sufficient.
- **Capture-count structure (p. 8).** With m + z evaders inside their no-delay PDRs: P's first capture is guaranteed; z of the rest escape purely by exploiting the delays; the remaining m (non-cooperating) are all captured; organized into s ≤ m cooperative teams, each team guarantees exactly one escaper (the last-pursued member), so captures = m − s and escapes = n − m + s. Two-evader scenarios: **Scenario 1** (n = 0): both E_1, E_2 in their EDRs; **Scenario 2** (n = 1): one in PDR, one in EDR; **Scenario 3** (n ≥ 1): both in PDR — the cooperative case of interest. P's policy: parallel strategy (6) with sequence choice per scenario (pursue the PDR evader first in Scenario 2; arbitrary then switch in Scenarios 1/3).

- **Lemma 6.1 (pp. 8-9, eq. (37)-(40))** — *Two-evader cooperative strategies.* With P pursuing E_1 first, E_2 targeting L_2, Δt = ‖x*_1 − x_P‖₂/v_p: feedback controls u_{E_1} = (x*_1 − x₁)/‖x*_1 − x₁‖₂, u_{E_2} = (x*_2 − x₂)/‖x*_2 − x₂‖₂ (37), where x*_1 = (a₁²x₁ − x_P)/(a₁²−1) + (a₁‖x₁ − x_P‖₂/(a₁²−1))(cos ξ₁, sin ξ₁)^T is any point of 𝓒₁ (38a) — E_1's *sacrifice direction* ξ₁ ∈ [0, 2π) — and x*_2 = x₂ + v₂Δt(cos φ₂, sin φ₂)^T, φ₂ ∈ (0, π) (38b). The pair maximize the J₂-type objective (38) = (a₂²y*_2 − y*_1)/(a₂²−1) + (a₂/(a₂²−1))‖x*_1 − x*_2‖₂ ≡ escape height h_{c2} of E_2 (top of the Apollonius circle formed at time Δt by P at x*_1 and E_2 at x*_2); proof via (39) (center/radius of 𝓒₁) and (40).
- **Corollary 6.1 (p. 9)** — If **h > h_{n2} > 0, E_2 is captured; if h ≤ h_{n2}, E_2 reaches L₂** (printed with h_{n2} although h_{c2} is defined — notation slip; should read h_{c2}). If h_{c2} > h, the maximum-height target x*_2** is no longer time-optimal (Fig. 7(a): reaching m on 𝓒₂ ∩ L₂ beats aiming at the top point T), motivating Lemma 6.2.
- **Lemma 6.2 (p. 9, eq. (41)-(43))** — *Time-optimal target via Cartesian oval.* If h_{c2} > h, choose x*_2** minimizing ‖x*_2** − x₂‖₂ over x*_2** ∈ 𝓡*₂ ∩ {(x,y): y ≥ h} (41), where the reachable region bounded by the **Cartesian oval** 𝓒*₂(Δt) = {x : ‖x − x₂‖₂/v₂ − ‖x − x*_1‖₂/v_p = Δt} = {x : a₂‖x − x₂‖₂ − ‖x − x*_1‖₂ = v_pΔt} (42) is 𝓡*₂(Δt) = {x : a₂‖x−x₂‖₂ − ‖x−x*_1‖₂ ≤ v_pΔt} (43) — points E₂ reaches no later than P (which is busy until Δt and then travels from x*_1). Analytic; used to finalize every team's escaper target in (50)/(54).

- **Corollary 6.2 (p. 10)** — Non-cooperative escape height h_{n2} satisfies the same capture/escape dichotomy; the **cooperation gain is the escape height H = h_{c2} − h_{n2}** (eq. (46)).
- **Remark 6.1 (p. 10)** — L₁ variants: cooperative objective min_{ξ₁,φ₂} (a₂²y*_2 − y*_1)/(a₂²−1) − (a₂/(a₂²−1))‖x*_1 − x*_2‖₂ with φ₂ ∈ (π, 2π); non-cooperative analog min_{φ₂}; lifeline choice per Cor. 5.1.
- **Multi-evader cooperative optimization (pp. 10-11, eq. (47)-(55)).** Teams g₁,…,g_s (s < m); in each team exactly one evader — the *last* in P's sequence — may escape. One-vs-three building block (Fig. 8): x*_2** (capture point of E₂) must satisfy (47): (i) on Apollonius circle 𝓒₂ of (x*_2, x*_1): (x − (a₂²x*_2 − x*_1)/(a₂²−1))² + (y − (a₂²y*_2 − y*_1)/(a₂²−1))² = (a₂‖x*_1 − x*_2‖₂/(a₂²−1))²; (ii) on the line through x₂ and x*_2: y = y₂ − ((y*_2 − y₂)/(x*_2 − x₂))(x₂ − x); (iii) in the forward direction: (x*_2 − x₂)(x − x₂) ≥ 0 ∧ (y*_2 − y₂)(y − y₂) ≥ 0. Then x*_3 = x₃ + v₃(t₁+t₂)(cos φ₃, sin φ₃)^T (48) and the team payoff J_{c2} = (a₃²y*_3 − y*_2**)/(a₃²−1) + (a₃‖x*_3 − x*_2**‖₂)/(a₃²−1) (49). General team-1 problem (50): max_{ξ₁,φ₂,…,φ_k} (a_k²y*_k − y*_{k−1}**)/(a_k²−1) + (a_k‖x*_k − x*_{k−1}**‖₂)/(a_k²−1) s.t. (50a) x*_1** on 𝓒₁ via ξ₁ (with x*_1** ≔ x*_1, Remark 6.2); (50b) x*_i = x_i + v_i(Σ_{n=1}^{i−1} t_n)(cos φ_i, sin φ_i)^T, i = 2..k; (50c) x*_i** on the Apollonius circle of (x*_i, x*_{i−1}**) for i = 2..k−1; (50d) collinearity y*_i** = y_i − ((y*_i − y_i)/(x*_i − x_i))(x_i − x*_i**); (50e) forward-direction inequalities; (50f) t₁ = ‖x*_1** − x_P‖₂/v_p; (50g) t_i = ‖x*_i** − x*_{i−1}**‖₂/v_p, i = 2..k−1; with ξ₁, φ_i ∈ [0,2π) (i = 2..k−1), φ_k ∈ (0,π). Then Lemma 6.2 fixes x*_k**. L₁ version (51): same constraints, min-objective with −a_k‖·‖₂ term and φ_k ∈ (π,2π). Second and later teams (54): min_{φ_{k+1},…,φ_j} (a_j²y*_j − y*_{j−1}**)/(a_j²−1) − (a_j‖x*_j − x*_{j−1}**‖₂)/(a_j²−1), s.t. (54a) x*_i = x_i + v_i(Σ_{n=1}^{i−1} t_n)(cos φ_i, sin φ_i)^T, i = k+1..j; (54b)-(54d) circle/collinearity/direction conditions, i = k+1..j−1; (54e) t_i = ‖x*_i** − x*_{i−1}**‖₂/v_p; φ_j ∈ (π,2π); L₂ version (55) with max and φ_j ∈ (0,π). In the Fig. 9 example the team-2 delay is the full chain Δt = (‖x*_1** − x_P‖₂ + ‖x*_2** − x*_1**‖₂ + ‖x*_3** − x*_2**‖₂)/v_p. Remark 6.2: the first team's E₁ is captured on its *initial* Apollonius circle (4) (no delay); later teams via (54)/(55); Thm 5.1 decides each team's escaper; **all problems solved by CMA-ES**. If P's order is unknown, enumerate all sequences, pick maximum captures, tie-break by shortest total pursuit time — "evaders and pursuer reach an equilibrium" (p.11; *informal — no equilibrium concept is formalized*).

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
Author-stated:
- Future work: "investigating multiple pursuers and evaders in more complex environments" (p.15). All results restricted to simple motion, constant speeds, unrestricted heading, point capture (radius 0), and a planar two-lifeline arena (pp. 2-3).
- The pursuit order is assumed given for the main analysis; the unknown-order case is handled by exhaustive enumeration over sequences (p.11), which is factorial in the number of evaders.

Critical observations (with page/eq.):
- Asymmetric "equilibrium": only evader-side cooperation is optimized; P's sequence and parallel strategy (6) are heuristic w.r.t. the team objective; the final "equilibrium" claim (p.11) is not formalized (no Nash/SPE definition, no proof).
- The information structure is called "perfect information" because P observes evader controls/states (p.3), but the cooperative controls ξ₁, φ₂..φ_k are **open-loop** angle plans computed before the run; feedback implementation under perturbations is not analyzed (Fig. 14 only shows P robust to E₁'s direction changes under (6)).
- Printing/notation defects (verified on page images, Section 5 items 1-6 above): vacuous ≥ 0 in (2); v_k missing in Cor. 4.1's first threshold; v_{k−1}Δt vs. v_kΔt in Thm 5.1's m; (1−a_k²) instead of (a_k²−1) in Thm 5.1's 𝕌₁; h_{c2}/h_{n2} mix-up in Cor. 6.1; missing min_{u_P} in V_{n1}, V_{c1}. None break the geometry, but they require care when reimplementing.
- Lemma 5.1 (straight-line optimality) and the necessity half of Thm 5.1 lean on Remark 5.1's equivalence 𝓡_{P_k} ≡ 𝒲^Δ_{P_k} and on eq. (36)'s triangle-inequality sketch; optimality of the switching point x*_k on 𝓒_k(Δt) is asserted, not proven — a proof gap for strict game-theoretic use.
- Barrier existence requires (a_i²−1)h² ≥ (1−1/a_i²)(y_P^0−h)² (k₁,k₂ real, eq. (18)); the paper notes positivity via a_i > 1 and h > y_P^0 but does not treat P initially very close to a lifeline (small margin) or a_i = 1 degeneracy inside Thm 4.1.
- No statistical/quantitative validation of CMA-ES solutions (global optimality unverified), no comparison with HJI numerics or with the decomposition of Chen et al. 2017, and no extension to capture radius > 0, delayed information, or curved lifelines.

## 8. Citation Value
Role: this is the archival reference for **barrier-based dominance analysis and cooperative "sacrifice" strategies in a one-superior-pursuer vs. many-evader two-lifeline (reach-two-goals) differential game** — the multi-evader sequel to the lifeline-game line (Isaacs' lifeline problem, Zha's fishing game, Yan et al.'s limited-lifetime lifeline game, Zhang & Zha's three-player game) and the mirror image of the authors' TAC two-pursuer/one-evader paper. Use it when a paper needs: (i) explicit no-delay and time-delay barrier formulas for straight lifelines, (ii) PDR/EDR and Δ-PDR shrinkage arguments, (iii) Apollonius-circle/escape-height payoff criteria (J₁, J₂, min{J₁, h−J₂}), (iv) Cartesian-oval time-optimal target selection, or (v) team-structured evader cooperation solved as a CMA-ES optimization. Ready-to-adapt citation sentences: "Xu et al. [P093] constructed no-delay and time-delay barriers for a two-lifeline pursuit-evasion game with one superior pursuer and multiple evaders, partitioning the play area into per-evader dominance regions and deriving necessary and sufficient escape conditions for sequentially pursued evaders." and "To rescue evaders trapped in the pursuer's dominance region, [P093] organizes them into cooperative teams that maximize the escape height of a designated last-pursued member via a CMA-ES-solved heading optimization, guaranteeing exactly one escapee per team."
