## 2. Research Background
Point defense of a fixed asset against a mobile threat is a canonical protection task [1], made operationally pressing by recent large-scale attacks (more than 300 airborne threats against ground targets [2]); the Mitchell Forum report [3] argues that success requires coordination between heterogeneous defensive assets, both ground-based (e.g., gun/missile turrets) and airborne (escort vehicles). Prior differential-game work treated these asset types mostly in isolation: turret-only defense with singular surfaces [7], mobile-defender active target defense (Boyell line [11]), perimeter defense with patrollers-plus-defenders [12], and turret-vs-two-attackers role selection [13]. Cooperation among homogeneous pursuers is classical (Hagedorn–Breakwell two-pursuer game [8]; min-max capture-time multi-pursuer games [9], [10]), but equilibrium analysis of a team whose members have **completely different kinematic capabilities** (a stationary, turn-rate-limited Turret plus a holonomic constant-speed Defender) had not been carried out. This letter fills that hole with a geometric differential-game solution of a Turret+Defender vs. Attacker target-defense game, demonstrating that Isaacs-type analysis can handle heterogeneous-team cooperation without resorting to numeric HJI computations.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
**Problem.** A stationary Turret $T$ (turn rate bounded by $\omega$, initial look angle along $+x$) and a mobile Defender $D$ (speed $\mu$, simple motion) protect a circular target of radius $1$ centered at $T$ against an Attacker $A$ (speed $\nu$, simple motion) with $\nu < \mu$ and $\nu < \omega$ (p. 2). $A$ wins by reaching $r_A = 1$; the D–T team wins by capturing $A$ — via $D$ colliding with $A$, or $T$ slewing its line of sight onto $A$ — while $r_A > 1$. The objective is the terminal distance $\min_{u_A} \max_{u_D, u_T} J$, $J = r_{A_f} - 1$ (eq. (5)): capture $A$ as far from $T$ as possible. This is a two-player (team-vs-agent) zero-sum terminal-cost differential game solved by the geometric method.

**Gap, as claimed by the authors (p. 2, contributions list):** (1) the Attacker's dominance region w.r.t. the Turret is characterized "for the first time"; (2) equilibrium strategies and the Value function of the min-max terminal-distance game are obtained; (3) closed-form analytic conditions determine how the game ends (solo capture by D, solo capture by T, or simultaneous capture); and, as the headline message, differential game theory is shown to address cooperation in a team with completely different capabilities.

**Verification against cited sources.** The building blocks are largely imported: the D-solo subgame is admitted by the authors to be "a particular case of Guarding a Target from [4]" (Isaacs, p. 3) and "a special case of the more general result from [16]" (Lee & Bakolas, TAC 2024, which handles arbitrary convex targets, multiple pursuers, higher dimensions — p. 3, after Lemma 2). The T-vs-A barrier and the Value of the A-vs-T subgame are imported from [17] (Von Moll et al., "Circular target defense differential games," TAC 2023) — Lemma 4's proof literally lifts the barrier $\theta_B(r) = g(r) - g(\nu/\omega)$ from [17]. What is genuinely new in P086 is the **composition**: the interplay of the two dominance regions $\mathcal{R}_{A/D}$ (Apollonius circle) and $\mathcal{R}_{A/T}$ (turn-rate-limited slew region), the three-way termination classification with explicit checkable conditions (Lemmas 7–8, Theorem 1), and the turning-direction resolution $V = \max\{V^+, V^-\}$ with the dispersal-surface argument (Theorem 2). The claim that the A-vs-T dominance region is characterized "for the first time" should be read as "for this turret-alignment capture model in this combined game"; the region itself derives from the [17] machinery.

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Modeling (theory):** a hybrid-capability defense team — stationary turn-constrained Turret (control = normalized turn rate $u_T \in [-1,1]$) plus holonomic Defender — against a simple-motion Attacker, formulated as a zero-sum terminal-cost differential game with capture sets defined as a union of two geometric predicates (eqs. (1)–(7)).
2. **Dominance-region geometry (theory):** $\mathcal{R}_{A/D}$ = interior of the Apollonius circle (eqs. (13)–(15)); $\mathcal{R}_{A/T}$ = set of points $A$ reaches before $T$ can slew into alignment, bounded by a closed curve with an angular domain $[\underline{\theta}, \bar{\theta}]$ determined by the transcendental equation $\nu\theta/\omega = r_A|\sin(\theta - \theta_A)|$ (Lemmas 3, 5; eqs. (19)–(26)).
3. **Equilibrium characterization (theory):** all equilibrium strategies are constant — straight lines for $A$ and $D$, max-rate one-direction turn for $T$ (Lemma 1); per-mode Values and capture points: D-solo $V = \|c\| - \rho - 1$ (Lemma 2, closed form), T-solo $V = g^{-1}(g(r_A) - \theta_A) - 1$ (Lemma 6, one-dimensional numerical inversion), simultaneous capture $V = d(\theta^*_{A_f}) - 1$ via a scalar root-find on $t(\theta) = d(\theta)$ (eqs. (30)–(35)).
4. **Termination determination (algorithm/theory):** closed-form sufficient conditions (time-to-go comparisons at geometrically defined aim points) selecting among the three capture modes (Lemmas 7–8, Theorem 1), and the full solution $V = \max\{V^+, V^-\}$ over the Turret's two turning directions, including the dispersal-surface case and the infeasible-geometry case $\theta_A > \theta_B(r_A)$ solved by a closest-feasible-point computation (Theorem 2, eqs. (40)–(42)).
5. **Robustness at the singularity (theory, deferred to [15, Appendix]):** once $T$ commits to a turning direction, the corresponding $\mathcal{R}_{A/T}$ only shrinks relative to its initial-time extent, so $A$ cannot exploit the dispersal singularity (p. 5, citing [18] for singular-surface methodology).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results
### 5.0 Game model, information, cost, termination (p. 2, eqs. (1)–(7))
- **Dynamics (eq. (1))**: state $x = (x_D, y_D, x_A, y_A, \theta_T)$;
$$\dot x = (\mu\cos u_D,\ \mu\sin u_D,\ \nu\cos u_A,\ \nu\sin u_A,\ \omega u_T)^\top,\quad u_D, u_A \in [-\pi, \pi],\ u_T \in [-1,1].$$
$T$ is at the origin w.l.o.g., initial look angle $\theta_T$ along $+x$. Polar coordinates w.r.t. the look angle: $r_P = \|P\|$, $\theta_P = \mathrm{atan2}(y_P, x_P) - \theta_T$ (eq. (2)). **Assumptions**: $\nu < \mu$ and $\nu < \omega$ ("Attacker slower than the Defender and Turret"; note $\nu$ is a linear speed while $\omega$ is an angular rate — the comparison is meaningful only after nondimensionalization by the unit target radius).
- **Capture/winning conditions (eq. (3))**: $C_D = \{x \mid r_D = r_A > 1,\ \theta_D = \theta_A\}$ (Defender collides with Attacker at equal bearing); $C_T = \{x \mid \theta_A = 0,\ r_A > 1\}$ (Turret's LOS aligned with $A$ while outside the target disk); $C_{D,T} = C_D \cap C_T$ simultaneous capture. Termination time $t_f = \inf\{t \mid x \in C_D \cup C_T\}$ (eq. (4)). $A$'s alternative win: reach $r_A = 1$ (target circle).
- **Cost (eq. (5))**: $J = r_{A_f} - 1$, $A$ minimizing, D–T team maximizing; note $J$ presupposes team capture (an $A$-win is not represented — see §7).
- **Information structure**: perfect state feedback / open-loop Isaacs equilibrium; Value satisfies Isaacs' condition (eq. (6)) and the Nash-saddle property (eq. (7)) over admissible control sets $U_D, U_A, U_T$.

### 5.1 Lemma 1 (p. 3): equilibrium strategies are constant
**Statement.** For the game (1)–(6), $A$'s and $D$'s equilibrium strategies are straight-line trajectories and $T$'s equilibrium strategy is to turn in one direction at max turn rate.
**Proof approach.** (1) Terminal-cost game ⇒ Hamiltonian $H = \lambda_{x_A}\nu\cos u_A + \lambda_{y_A}\nu\sin u_A + \lambda_{x_D}\mu\cos u_D + \lambda_{y_D}\mu\sin u_D + \lambda_{\theta_T}\omega u_T$ (eq. (8)) with costates ordered $[\lambda_{x_A}, \lambda_{y_A}, \lambda_{x_D}, \lambda_{y_D}, \lambda_{\theta_T}]$; (2) costate dynamics $\dot\lambda = -\partial H/\partial x$ [14]; $H$ has no explicit state dependence ⇒ $\dot\lambda = 0$ (eq. (9)) — the decisive equation; (3) stationary minimization/maximization of $H$ gives $\cos u_A^* = -\lambda_{x_A}/\sqrt{\lambda_{x_A}^2 + \lambda_{y_A}^2}$, $\sin u_A^* = -\lambda_{y_A}/\sqrt{\lambda_{x_A}^2+\lambda_{y_A}^2}$ (eq. (10)), the analogous maximization for $D$ (eq. (11)), and $u_T^* = \mathrm{sign}(\lambda_{\theta_T})$ (eq. (12)) — bang-bang; (4) all controls are functions of the **constant** costates, hence constant in time.
**Meaning/extensibility.** Physical: each agent heads to a fixed aim point; the Turret commits to one slew direction at full rate. This constancy is the hinge of the whole geometric solution (fixed intercept points). Standard tool (Pontryagin necessary conditions for terminal-cost simple-motion games); applies to any terminal-cost game with state-free kinematics.

### 5.2 D-solo capture geometry (p. 3, eqs. (13)–(18))
- **Dominance region (eq. (13))**: $\mathcal{R}_{A/D} = \{y \in \mathbb{R}^2 \mid \|y - A\|/\nu < \|y - D\|/\mu\}$; boundary = Apollonius circle with center and radius (eq. (14))
$$c = (x_c, y_c) = (1+\alpha)A - \alpha D,\qquad \rho = \frac{\mu\alpha}{\nu}\|D - A\|,\qquad \alpha = \frac{\nu^2}{\mu^2 - \nu^2},$$
equivalently $\mathcal{R}_{A/D} = \{y \mid \|y - c\| < \rho\}$ (eq. (15)). (Consistent with the standard form $c = (\mu^2 A - \nu^2 D)/(\mu^2-\nu^2)$, $\rho = \mu\nu\|D-A\|/(\mu^2-\nu^2)$.) **Assumption**: the origin ($T$'s position) is not contained in $\mathcal{R}_{A/D}$.

### 5.3 Lemma 2 (p. 3, eqs. (16)–(18)): D-solo Value and capture point
**Statement.** When $T$ has no effect on the outcome under equilibrium play, the equilibrium capture point is the closest point of the Apollonius circle $\partial\mathcal{R}_{A/D}$ to $T$, and
$$V(x) = \|c\| - \rho - 1 \quad (16).$$
Equilibrium strategies (eq. (17)): both $A$ and $D$ head straight to the intercept point
$$p_D^* = (x_{p_D}^*, y_{p_D}^*) = \frac{c}{\|c\|}(\|c\| - \rho) \quad (18),$$
i.e., $u_A^*$ aligns with $p_D^* - A$ and $u_D^*$ with $p_D^* - D$ (typo in print: the $\sin u_A^*$ numerator is written $y_{p_A}^*$; it should read $y_{p_D}^*$).
**Proof.** Omitted for space, see [15]; a special case of Isaacs' Guarding a Target [4] and of [16] (arbitrary convex targets, multiple pursuers, higher dimensions). **Analytic/closed form; global.** **Meaning**: the min-max terminal distance equals the gap between the target center and the near edge of the Apollonius circle. **Extensibility**: directly inherits the convex-target/multi-pursuer generality of [16].

### 5.4 Lemma 3 (p. 3, eq. (19)–(22)): Attacker's dominance region w.r.t. the Turret
**Statement.** With $D$ ignored, $\theta_A \in (0, 2\pi)$, $T$ turning CCW ($u_T = 1$), and $\mathcal{P} = (0,\infty)\times(-\pi,\pi]$ (angles w.r.t. the look angle),
$$\mathcal{R}_{A/T} = \Big\{(r,\theta) \in \mathcal{P} \;\Big|\; r^2 + r_A^2 - 2 r r_A \cos(\theta - \theta_A) \le \frac{\nu^2\theta^2}{\omega^2}\Big\} \quad (19).$$
**Interpretation/derivation logic.** Law of cosines: $A$ reaches $(r,\theta)$ in time $\| \cdot \|/\nu$ (the left side over $\nu$ is the straight-line distance), while $T$ needs slew time $|\theta|/\omega$; the region collects points $A$ can arrive at before $T$ can point at them. Boundary (eq. (20)) from the equality, solving the quadratic in $r$:
$$r = r_A\cos(\theta - \theta_A) \pm \sqrt{\tfrac{\nu^2\theta^2}{\omega^2} - r_A^2\sin^2(\theta - \theta_A)}.$$
Two non-negative real roots require (eqs. (21)–(22)):
$$\theta \in \big[-\tfrac{\pi}{2} + \theta_A,\ \tfrac{\pi}{2} + \theta_A\big],\qquad r_A \ge \frac{\nu|\theta|}{\omega} \ge r_A |\sin(\theta - \theta_A)|.$$
The left inequality of (22) means $T$ can turn through $\theta$ within the time $A$ needs to traverse $r_A$; it is **assumed** satisfied for the remainder ("$A$ starts sufficiently far from $T$"). The $\theta$-domain boundaries of $\mathcal{R}_{A/T}$ are where the right inequality of (22) is tight. **Proof**: omitted, see [15]. Geometric/analytic; global within its angular domain.

### 5.5 Barrier function and Lemma 4 (p. 3, eqs. (23)–(24)): the A-vs-T game of kind
- **Auxiliary function (eq. (23))**: $g(r) = \sqrt{\omega^2 r^2/\nu^2 - 1} + \sin^{-1}\!\big(\tfrac{\nu}{r\omega}\big)$, with $g(\nu/\omega) = \pi/2$.
**Lemma 4.** If $\theta_A < \theta_B(r_A)$ where
$$\theta_B(r) = \sqrt{\omega^2 r^2/\nu^2 - 1} - \cos^{-1}\!\big(\tfrac{\nu}{r\omega}\big) \quad (24),$$
then $A$ cannot guarantee reaching $r \le \nu/\omega$ without getting captured by $T$.
**Proof approach.** (1) From [17], the barrier separating "$A$ can reach the distance $\nu/\omega$ from $T$" from "not" is $\theta_B(r) = g(r) - g(\nu/\omega)$; (2) $g(\nu/\omega) = \pi/2$; (3) substituting (23) at $r = r_A$ and using $\sin^{-1}(z) - \pi/2 = -\cos^{-1}(z)$ yields (24). Decisive identity: $g(r_A) - g(\nu/\omega)$.
**Physical meaning.** The speed-ratio circle $r = \nu/\omega$ is where $A$'s angular rate about $T$ ($\nu/r$) matches $T$'s max slew rate $\omega$; inside it $A$ out-turns the Turret. The barrier $\theta_B(r_A)$ is the critical initial bearing: $\theta_A < \theta_B(r_A)$ ⇒ $T$ (as sole defender) can force capture; $\theta_A > \theta_B(r_A)$ ⇒ $A$ can penetrate to the speed-ratio circle. Under $\nu < \omega$ with unit target radius, $\nu/\omega < 1$ lies **inside** the target disk, so "reaching $r \le \nu/\omega$" is tantamount to $A$ winning; equivalently $\theta_B$ is the imported [17] win/loss barrier of the A-vs-T subgame. **Extensibility**: identical barrier machinery transfers to any alignment-based capture model.

### 5.6 Lemma 5 (p. 3, eqs. (25)–(26)): angular domain of $\mathcal{R}_{A/T}$
**Statement.** For $\theta_A > 0$ WLOG with $\theta_A > \theta_B(r_A)$: (1) there exists exactly one angle $\underline{\theta} \in (\max\{0, \theta_A - \tfrac{\pi}{2}\}, \theta_A]$ and exactly one angle $\bar{\theta} \in (\theta_A, \theta_u]$ such that
$$\frac{\nu\theta}{\omega} = r_A|\sin(\theta - \theta_A)| \quad (25),\qquad \theta_u = \cos^{-1}\!\Big(\frac{\nu}{\omega r_A}\Big) + \theta_A \quad (26);$$
(2) the range $[\underline{\theta}, \bar{\theta}]$ is the $\theta$-domain of $\mathcal{R}_{A/T}$ per (20).
**Proof.** Omitted, see [15]. **Meaning**: the two roots of (25) mark where the slew-demand curve $\nu\theta/\omega$ enters/exits tangency with the sine envelope; they bound the bearing sector over which the dominance boundary exists. Note $\theta_B(r_A) > 0$ is required (Lemma 5 presumes $\theta_A > \theta_B(r_A)$), while Theorems 1–2 below use the complementary case; the two lemmas jointly delimit when the $\mathcal{R}_{A/T}$ geometry is usable.

### 5.7 Lemma 6 (p. 4, eqs. (27)–(29)): T-solo capture Value and strategy
**Statement.** When $D$ has no effect on the outcome under equilibrium play,
$$V(x) = r^*_{A_f} - 1 = g^{-1}\big(g(r_A) - \theta_A\big) - 1 \quad (27),$$
with $A$'s equilibrium heading
$$\hat u_A^* = \sin^{-1}\!\Big(\frac{\nu}{\omega r_A}\Big) \quad (28),$$
measured CW w.r.t. the line from $A$ to $T$; the equilibrium capture point is
$$p_T^* = (r^*_{p_T}, \theta^*_{p_T}) = \Big(r^*_{A_f},\ \frac{\omega(r_A - r^*_{A_f})}{\nu}\Big) \quad (29).$$
**Proof.** Omitted, see [15], [17]. **Analytic up to one numerical step**: $g$ is transcendental, so $g^{-1}$ has no analytic form and $r^*_{A_f}$ must be obtained numerically (stated p. 4). **Meaning**: $A$'s guaranteed capture radius under optimal T-evasion is $r^*_{A_f} = g^{-1}(g(r_A) - \theta_A)$ — the barrier-to-Value reduction characteristic of [17]; consistency check: $\theta_A \to \theta_B(r_A)$ gives $g(r_A) - \theta_A \to \pi/2$, i.e., $r^*_{A_f} \to \nu/\omega$. **Caveat (not stated in the letter)**: a positive Value additionally needs $r^*_{A_f} > 1$, i.e., $\theta_A < g(r_A) - g(1)$ with $g(1) = \sqrt{\omega^2/\nu^2 - 1} + \sin^{-1}(\nu/\omega) > \pi/2$ when $\nu < \omega$; see §7.

### 5.8 Simultaneous capture (p. 4, eqs. (30)–(35))
**Setup.** $A$ is captured by $T$ and $D$ simultaneously: $T$'s LOS aligns with $A$ and $D$ coincident — typically when $A$ is "between" $T$ and $D$. Because of Lemma 1 (fixed aim points), the equilibrium capture point must lie on **both** dominance boundaries, and among intersections it must be the one closest to the origin:
$$A_f^* = \min_r \big\{(r,\theta) \in \partial\mathcal{R}_{A/T} \cap \partial\mathcal{R}_{A/D}\big\} \quad (30).$$
**Computation.** Apollonius circle in polar form (eq. (31)), $r_c = \|c\|$, $\theta_c = \mathrm{atan2}(y_c, x_c)$:
$$r = r_c\cos(\theta - \theta_c) \pm \sqrt{\rho^2 - r_c^2\sin^2(\theta - \theta_c)},\qquad \theta \in \Big[\theta_c - \sin^{-1}\tfrac{\rho}{r_c},\ \theta_c + \sin^{-1}\tfrac{\rho}{r_c}\Big].$$
Define the "bottom halves" (eq. (32)):
$$t(\theta) = r_A\cos(\theta - \theta_A) - \sqrt{\tfrac{\nu^2\theta^2}{\omega^2} - r_A^2\sin^2(\theta - \theta_A)},\qquad d(\theta) = r_c\cos(\theta - \theta_c) - \sqrt{\rho^2 - r_c^2\sin^2(\theta - \theta_c)}.$$
$t(\theta) = d(\theta)$ admits no closed-form solution ⇒ numerical root-find, restricted to the intersection of the two domains (eq. (33)):
$$\theta^*_{A_f} \in \Big[\max\big\{\underline{\theta},\ \theta_c - \sin^{-1}\tfrac{\rho}{r_c}\big\},\ \min\big\{\bar{\theta},\ \theta_c + \sin^{-1}\tfrac{\rho}{r_c}\big\}\Big]\ \text{s.t.}\ d\big(\theta^*_{A_f}\big) = t\big(\theta^*_{A_f}\big).$$
Value and capture point (eqs. (34)–(35)):
$$V(x) = d\big(\theta^*_{A_f}\big) - 1,\qquad p^*_{DT} = \big(d(\theta^*_{A_f}),\ \theta^*_{A_f}\big).$$
**Character**: geometric construction + one-dimensional transcendental root-find; no closed form (explicitly stated). **Meaning**: the team concedes neither agent's solo aim point; the capture radius is read off either boundary curve at the matched bearing.

### 5.9 Lemma 7 (p. 4, eqs. (36)–(37)): when D's aim point is safe from T
**Statement.** For $\theta_A < \theta_B(r_A)$, the point $p_D^*$ lies in $\mathcal{R}_{A/T}$ if
$$\|p_D^*\| > \frac{\nu}{\omega} \quad (36),\qquad \text{and}\qquad \frac{\|A - p_D^*\|}{\nu} \le \frac{\mathrm{atan2}(y^*_{p_D},\, x^*_{p_D})}{\omega} \quad (37).$$
**Proof approach.** Direct time-to-go comparison of $A$ vs. $T$ to the point $p_D^*$; validity hinges on the assumptions precluding $A$'s path from entering the region $r < \nu/\omega$ where $A$ holds the angular-rate advantage (stated in the proof). **Meaning**: $A$ reaches $D$'s optimal intercept point no later than $T$ can slew onto that bearing, so the Turret cannot improve on D-solo capture — the enabling condition for branch 1 of Theorem 1. Sufficient ("if") condition, used as a check; necessity not claimed.

### 5.10 Lemma 8 (p. 4, eqs. (38)–(39)): when T's aim point is safe from D
**Statement.** Define $p^\dagger$ as the point on $\partial\mathcal{R}_{A/D}$ (the Apollonius circle) along $A$'s A-vs-T equilibrium heading (28). For $\theta_A < \theta_B(r_A)$, the point $p_T^*$ lies in $\mathcal{R}_{A/D}$ if
$$\|p^\dagger\| > \frac{\nu}{\omega} \quad (38),\qquad \text{and}\qquad \frac{\|A - p^\dagger\|}{\nu} > \frac{\mathrm{atan2}(y_p^\dagger,\, x_p^\dagger)}{\omega} \quad (39).$$
**Proof.** "Similar to that of the previous Lemma and is omitted." **Interpretation.** If $A$ commits to the T-evasion heading (28), $D$'s earliest possible interception is $p^\dagger$; condition (39) says $A$'s time-to-go to $p^\dagger$ exceeds $T$'s slew time to that bearing, so $T$ aligns (and captures $A$ at $p_T^*$ on the way) before $D$'s interception materializes; (38) keeps the whole comparison outside the $r < \nu/\omega$ angular-advantage zone, as in Lemma 7. **Practical point (stated)**: $p^\dagger$ is obtainable from pure geometry, hence cheaper than $p_T^*$ which needs the numerical evaluation of $r^*_{A_f}$.

### 5.11 Theorem 1 (p. 4, Termination Determination)
**Statement.** For the game (1)–(6) with $T$ turning CCW and $\theta_A < \theta_B(r_A)$:
1. if the closest point of the Apollonius circle to $T$ is inside $A$'s dominance region w.r.t. $T$, i.e., $p_D^* \in \mathcal{R}_{A/T}$ (checked via Lemma 7), then **solo capture by $D$** is optimal, with $p_D^*$ from (18) and the solution of Lemma 2;
2. if the closest point of $\partial\mathcal{R}_{A/T}$ to $T$ is inside the Apollonius circle, i.e., $p_T^* \in \mathcal{R}_{A/D}$ (checked via Lemma 8), then **solo capture by $T$** is optimal, with $p_T^*$ from (29) and the solution of Lemma 6;
3. otherwise **simultaneous capture** by $T$ and $D$ is optimal; all agents head to $p^*_{DT}$ from (35) and $V(x) = \|p^*_{DT}\| - 1$.
**Proof approach.** (1)–(2): each $p^*$ is the closest point of $A$'s dominance region w.r.t. the capturing agent; if that point lies inside $A$'s dominance region w.r.t. the *other* agent, capture there necessarily occurs before the other agent can catch $A$, and $A$ can do no better — any deviation from heading to $p^*$ lets the capturing agent capture even farther from $T$ (dominance-region maximality argument). (3): if neither solo aim point is safely reachable by $A$, an intersection $\partial\mathcal{R}_{A/T} \cap \partial\mathcal{R}_{A/D}$ exists by construction and is the equilibrium capture point (closest point of $\mathcal{R}_{A/T} \cap \mathcal{R}_{A/D}$ to $T$); the decisive monotonicity facts are that $d(\theta)$ of (32) is monotonically increasing for $\theta > \theta^*_{p_{DT}}$ and $t(\theta)$ monotonically increasing for decreasing $\theta < \theta^*_{p_{DT}}$ (asserted, not derived in the letter). **Character**: closed-form geometric classification; global within the stated regime ($\theta_A < \theta_B(r_A)$, CCW). **Proof gap (mild)**: the monotonicity claims and the exclusion of states with $r^*_{A_f} \le 1$ from branch 2 are not demonstrated in the letter (full proofs delegated to [15]).

### 5.12 Theorem 2 (p. 5, eqs. (40)–(42), Full Solution)
**Statement.** Let $V^+(x)$ denote the solution with $T$ turning CCW. When $\theta_A < \theta_B(r_A)$, $V^+$ is given by Theorem 1; otherwise
$$p^* = (x_p^*, y_p^*) = \operatorname*{argmin}_{p \in F} \|p\| \quad (40),\qquad V^+ = \|p^*\| - 1,$$
where the feasible set is
$$F = \Big\{(x,y) \in \mathcal{R}_{A/D} \setminus S \;\Big|\; \frac{\|A - (x,y)\|}{\nu} \le \frac{\mathrm{atan2}(y,x)}{\omega}\Big\} \quad (41),$$
$S$ being the target set ($r \le 1$) and its shadow w.r.t. $A$; excluding $S$ discards points of $\mathcal{R}_{A/D}$ whose intercept path would cross the target. $V^-(x)$, the CW solution, is obtained by the same process with $A$ and $D$ mirrored about $T$'s look angle. The game's solution is
$$V(x) = \max\{V^+, V^-\} \quad (42),$$
with the equilibrium capture point and strategies of the maximizing direction. **Proof**: omitted for space, see [15]. **Dispersal surface discussion (p. 5)**: if $V^+ = V^-$ the state lies on the Dispersal Surface; both $T$ turning directions are equally optimal, $T$ is free to choose, and it is **assumed** the choice is communicated to $D$ (same team) so both aim at the proper capture point; $A$ can only guess, and a wrong guess costs a small performance penalty (capture closer to $T$). It is shown in [15, Appendix] that the $\mathcal{R}_{A/T}$ corresponding to the direction $T$ actually turns remains a subset of the initial-time $\mathcal{R}_{A/T}$ (and likewise for the opposite direction), hence $A$ has no way to exploit the singularity (cf. [18]).
**Case $\theta_A > \theta_B(r_A)$ (pp. 4–5).** The $\mathcal{R}_{A/T}$ geometry "is not well defined" there (the bearing-domain construction of Lemma 5 breaks down because $A$ can penetrate the speed-ratio circle); the game is instead solved by the constrained closest-point problem (40)–(41) — intersect D's interception constraint ($A$-beats-$D$ points) with T's slew constraint ($A$ arrives before alignment) and take the nearest-to-target feasible capture point. The authors label this a cursory treatment (§7).

### 5.13 Summary of the hybrid turret+defender model and winning-region characterization
The "hybrid" aspect is precisely the union capture set (3) combining a **position predicate** ($D$-collision, polar-angle coincidence) with an **alignment predicate** ($T$-slew, $\theta_A = 0$ at $r_A > 1$), plus the mixed control sets in (1) (continuous heading angles vs. normalized turn rate). The winning-region characterization is not delivered as a partition of the state space (explicitly left open) but as a per-initial-condition decision procedure: compute $c, \rho$ (14); check (22) LHS; compute $\theta_B(r_A)$ (24); if $\theta_A < \theta_B(r_A)$ run Lemma 7 then Lemma 8 checks to select Theorem 1's branch, else solve (40)–(41); mirror for the opposite slew direction and take the max (42). Values: $\|c\| - \rho - 1$ (closed form), $g^{-1}(g(r_A)-\theta_A) - 1$ (1-D numerical inversion), $d(\theta^*_{A_f}) - 1$ (1-D root-find), $\|p^*\| - 1$ (constrained minimization).

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
**Author-stated (p. 5–6, Conclusion; p. 1):**
1. The case with $A$ inside the barrier ($\theta_A > \theta_B(r_A)$, i.e., $A$ able to reach the speed-ratio circle w.r.t. $T$) received only "a cursory treatment"; a detailed outcome analysis is left for future work.
2. The partition of the state space into regions of $A$-winning, solo capture, and simultaneous capture is left for future work — the solution is a decision procedure per initial condition, not a closed-form state-space map.
3. Task allocation for many-vs-many engagements is out of scope (follow-on studies); task decomposition, sensing, and higher-level coordination are assumed solved a priori — only motion control is treated.
4. Several proofs (Lemmas 2, 3, 5, 6; Theorem 2) are omitted for space and delegated to the arXiv companion [15].
5. On the dispersal surface, T–D communication of the chosen slew direction is assumed; $A$ faces a guessing game (resolved favorably only via the [15, Appendix] monotonicity argument).

**Critical observations (with page/eq. anchors):**
6. **Unit consistency and the speed-ratio circle (p. 2; Lemma 4).** The assumptions $\nu < \mu$ **and** $\nu < \omega$ compare a linear speed with an angular rate; with the unit target radius this makes $\nu/\omega < 1$, so the speed-ratio circle lies inside the target disk and "reaching $r \le \nu/\omega$" coincides with target penetration. The barrier $\theta_B$ is therefore effectively the imported [17] A-vs-T win/loss boundary; the letter's phrasing ("reach the speed ratio circle") obscures this equivalence.
7. **Implicit positivity condition on the T-solo Value (Lemmas 4, 6; Theorem 1 branch 2).** $V = r^*_{A_f} - 1 > 0$ requires $r^*_{A_f} > 1 \iff \theta_A < g(r_A) - g(1)$ with $g(1) = \sqrt{\omega^2/\nu^2 - 1} + \sin^{-1}(\nu/\omega) > \pi/2$ when $\nu < \omega$. This is strictly stronger than $\theta_A < \theta_B(r_A) = g(r_A) - \pi/2$. In the band $g(r_A) - g(1) < \theta_A < \theta_B(r_A)$ the T-solo capture radius (27) falls below the target radius, and the letter neither states the requirement nor shows that the Lemma 8 test excludes such states (nor defines $p^\dagger$ when the heading ray (28) first enters the target set). **Proof gap** inherited from the delegation to [15].
8. **Sufficient-only checks and an asserted monotonicity (Theorem 1 proof, p. 5).** Lemmas 7–8 are stated as "if" conditions and used as classifiers; the "otherwise ⇒ simultaneous capture" branch rests on the monotonicity of $d(\theta)$ for $\theta > \theta^*_{p_{DT}}$ and of $t(\theta)$ for $\theta < \theta^*_{p_{DT}}$, asserted without derivation in the letter.
9. **Feasible set may be empty (eq. (41)).** For states where $A$ can force target penetration against both defenders, $F$ can be empty and the cost $J = r_{A_f} - 1$ (eq. (5)) presumes team capture, so $A$-winning outcomes are outside the represented value structure; Theorem 2's $V^+ = \|p^*\| - 1$ silently presumes $F \neq \emptyset$.
10. **Numerical dependencies (pp. 4–5).** Three steps are inherently numerical ($g^{-1}$ inversion for $r^*_{A_f}$; the transcendental $t(\theta) = d(\theta)$; the constrained minimization (40)) and none is analyzed (uniqueness of the root in (33), convergence, sensitivity), even though the termination classification's exclusivity ("otherwise" branch) leans on curve properties.
11. **Typo (eq. (17), p. 3).** The $\sin u_A^*$ numerator is printed $y^*_{p_A}$; by symmetry with the cosine entry it must be $y^*_{p_D}$.
12. **Perfect information and point-mass abstractions.** Capture radii are zero (coincidence/alignment), sensing is perfect, and the Turret's slew-limited dynamics are first-order; extending to nonzero capture radii, latency, or second-order turret dynamics is not addressed.

## 8. Citation Value
**Role in the literature.** This letter is the first equilibrium solution of a **mixed-kinematics cooperative defense team** — a stationary turn-constrained Turret fused with a holonomic mobile Defender — against a simple-motion Attacker, and as such it is the natural hub joining three strands in this archive: Apollonius-circle guarding games (Isaacs [4]; Lee–Bakolas [16]; Dorothy et al. [18]), turret/turn-constrained defense (Akilan–Fuchs [7]; Von Moll et al. turret-runner-penetrator [13]; circular target defense barrier theory [17]), and heterogeneous-team perimeter/point defense (Shishika et al. [12]). Its reusable assets are: the two-predicate capture set (3), the dominance-region pair $\mathcal{R}_{A/D}$ (Apollonius) vs. $\mathcal{R}_{A/T}$ (slew-limited, eq. (19)), the barrier $\theta_B(r)$ with the transcendental $g(r)$ (23)–(24), and the three-branch termination classification (Theorem 1) with the turning-direction max (42).
**Suggested citation sentences.**

[P086] solved the min-max terminal-distance differential game between a turn-rate-limited turret plus a mobile defender and a single attacker, proving that under equilibrium play every agent proceeds to a fixed aim point and deriving closed-form checks — based on the mutual containment of the Apollonius circle and the turret's slew-limited dominance region — that select among Defender-solo, Turret-solo, and simultaneous capture."
- "The termination-mode classification and the barrier $\theta_B(r) = \sqrt{\omega^2 r^2/\nu^2 - 1} - \cos^{-1}(\nu/(r\omega))$ of [P086] extend the circular-target-defense barrier theory of [P0xx = Von Moll et al., TAC 2023] to heterogeneous defender teams, and provide the per-agent aim points around which task-allocation layers for many-vs-many turret defense can be built."
