## 2. Research Background

Surveillance–evasion is a classical branch of trajectory optimization that began with Koopman's search-and-screening theory [1], where a range-limited observer (visual, radar, sonar) searched for targets of unknown location using probabilistic mobile search. Dobbie and Taylor [2]–[4] recast surveillance–evasion as a differential game in the sense of Isaacs [5], considering a *faster, turn-limited* observer and defining detection, surveillance, and escape regions; Lewin and Breakwell [6] then studied the game of degree (maximizing contact time given initial contact), Lewin and Olsder [7] changed the contact region to a 2-D cone, and Greenfeld [9] treated the two-car equal-speed/equal-turn-radius case; search under uncertainty was recently revisited as a differential game by Gilles and Vladimirsky [11], and a pure-pursuit observer variant by the authors in [12]. In parallel, pursuit of a *superior (faster) evader* was studied by Breakwell [13] with point capture, by Hagedorn–Breakwell [14] and Szõts et al. [15] (game of approach), and by containment formations with finite capture radius [16]–[18] — the latter being necessary because, as Chernous'ko [19] proved, point capture of a faster evader is impossible even with multiple pursuers; the finite capture radius is structurally the same object as the observation disk used here (both produce the Cartesian oval/Apollonius circle machinery). Multiphase pursuit–evasion problems (successive capture [20], [21], capture-the-flag attack/retreat [22], [28]–[30], hybrid/switching dynamics [24], [25], turret lock-on [26], [27], delayed target assignment [31], [32], [23]) had all been studied, but **not** for surveillance–evasion. This article is the journal extension of the authors' observation-phase-only conference paper [33] (ACC 2020) and its 3-D version [34] (JGCD 2021), adding the approach phase so that the whole engagement is solved over the entire state space.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap

**Problem.** A slower observer $O$ (speed $v_O$, speed ratio $\alpha = v_O/v_T \in (0,1)$) against a faster *fixed-course* (nonmaneuvering, constant-speed) target $T$ (speed normalized to $v_T = 1$). The observer controls only its instantaneous heading $\psi_O$ and carries a circular observation set of radius $R$ (eq. (2)); it must (i) approach and make contact with the faster target (Phase-I) and (ii) maximize the time the target stays inside the observation disk (Phase-II). The fixed-course assumption is justified [authors' claim, p. 4148] by two realistic cases: the target is unaware of the observer, or the target is far less maneuverable so its trajectory is approximately a straight line — an assumption the authors note also appears in examples in Shneydor's missile-guidance book [35] and in Barton–Eliezer's pure-pursuit pursuit curves [36].

**Gap [authors' claim]**: prior surveillance–evasion work solved only the observation phase given initial contact ([33], [34]; Lewin–Breakwell's game of degree [6]); how to *approach* a faster target so as to set up the observation phase, and the relation between the two phases over the whole state space, was open. **[Verified from cited text]**: Ref. [33] is titled "Maximum observation of a faster non-maneuvering target by a slower observer" (ACC 2020, pp. 100–105) and [34] "Maximum observation of a target by a slower observer in three dimensions" (JGCD 2021) — both, by title and by the paper's own description ("This article extends prior work concerning only the observation phase, solved in [33] and [34]"), are observation-phase-only; the gap statement is consistent. The coupling mechanism claimed — "the value of Phase-II is treated as the terminal cost for Phase-I, giving rise to the optimal control for the whole scenario via the principle of dynamic programming" (p. 4148) — is standard backward induction, and the paper indeed executes it (Section V). Note this is a **one-sided optimal-control problem** (the target has no control), not a two-player differential game; the "evasion" is exogenous (fixed course).

## 4. Core Contributions

1. **Theory**: A complete closed-form solution of a two-phase optimal-control problem (approach + observation) for a slower holonomic observer maximizing observation time of a faster fixed-course target; the full state space is partitioned into three regions with distinct optimal strategies (Theorem 1, eqs. (58)–(60)).
2. **Modeling**: A fixed-course-target surveillance model in relative-frame coordinates (eqs. (1)–(5)) where the observation disk of radius $R$ plays the role of a finite capture/sensor range, making the problem the "observation analog" of finite-range pursuit of a superior evader; explicit connection to the Apollonius circle / Cartesian oval machinery of containment games [17].
3. **Geometric tool**: The **Decision Line (DL)** — the locus of points whose Apollonius circle (w.r.t. the target, speed ratio $\alpha$) is tangent to the target's straight-line path (Definitions 1–2, Lemma 6) — a straight line through the target at angle $\theta_{DL} = \cos^{-1}\alpha$ from the $\hat{x}$-axis that yields a closed-form, feedback-implementable partition into $\mathscr{B}_1$ (no observation possible), $\mathscr{B}_2$ (limited observation), $\mathscr{B}_3$ (maximum observation) (Definitions 4–6, Lemmas 7–10).
4. **Algorithm/strategy**: Closed-form optimal headings: straight-line Phase-I approach aimed at the lowest reachable disk point (Lemmas 11–13) and constant-bearing Phase-II "stand-on" course (Lemma 1), with closed-form approach and observation times (Lemmas 11, 14).
5. **Experiment/validation**: Three fully worked numerical scenarios (Section VI, Table I, Figs. 7–9, Tables II–III) covering all three regions, with states at the critical times $t_1, t_2, t_f$, verifying the geometric construction end-to-end.
6. **Methodological claim [authors', p. 4148]**: "a closed-form approach for solving a multiphase optimal control problem" — i.e., solve the endgame first (PMP + transversality), use its value as Phase-I reward, and characterize Phase-I by geometry rather than by solving a two-point BVP.

## 5. Key Mathematical Results

### 5.0 Model, information structure, cost, termination (Section II, p. 4149, eqs. (1)–(5))

- **State** (eq. (1)): $\mathbf{x}(t) = [x_O(t),\, y_O(t),\, y_T(t)]^\intercal \in \mathbb{R}^3$. Cartesian frame with $\hat{y}$-axis aligned with the target's velocity (WLOG); target at $(0, y_T)$, observer at $(x_O, y_O)$.
- **Speeds**: target speed normalized to $1$; observer speed $\alpha = v_O/v_T$, $0 < \alpha < 1$ (observer strictly slower).
- **Control** (p. 4149): $u(t) = \psi_O(t)$, the observer's instantaneous heading — holonomic, constant-speed, unlimited turn rate (kinematic point-mass).
- **Dynamics** (eq. (3)): $\dot{x}_O = \alpha\cos\psi_O$, $\dot{y}_O = \alpha\sin\psi_O$, $\dot{y}_T = 1$.
- **Observation set** (eq. (2)): disk $\mathscr{O} = \{\mathbf{x}(t) \mid x_O^2(t) + (y_O(t)-y_T(t))^2 - R^2 \le 0,\ t\in[t_2,t_f]\}$, $R>0$; realistic readings: visual contact range, sensor range, or communication range (p. 4148).
- **Terminal manifold / termination** (eq. (4)): escape when $R^2 - x_O^2(t) - (y_O(t)-y_T(t))^2 < 0$, $t \ge t_2$; since the target is faster, escape is unavoidable and determines $t_f$.
- **Cost** (eq. (5)): $\psi_O^* = \arg\min J = \int_{t_2}^{t_f}(-1)\,dt = t_2 - t_f$, i.e., maximize $t_{obs} = t_f - t_2$; approach time $t_{apr} = t_2 - t_1$, $t_1 = 0$, $t_f = t_{apr} + t_{obs}$.
- **Information structure**: perfect state information; the target is nonstrategic (no control), so this is a one-player optimal control problem solved by backward induction ("in the best tradition of dynamic programming", p. 4149), not a zero-sum differential game.
- **Notation for the geometry (Fig. 1, pp. 4149–4150)**: $T$ = target at $t_1$ (origin); $L$ = target at $t_2$ = first contact point (on the disk boundary); $M$ = observer at $t_2$; $Q$ = observer at $t_f$; $S$ = target at $t_f$; $F, H$ = target/observer at an intermediate $t \in (t_2, t_f)$. $\lambda_{TO,2} \in [-\pi,\pi]$ = relative bearing from target to observer at $t_2$ (measured from $+\hat{y}$); $I$ = the disk point that makes contact; $W = (x_O, y_O - R)$ = bottom of the observation disk (Definition 3).

### 5.1 Phase-II (observation phase), Section III, pp. 4149–4151

PMP machinery: Hamiltonian (eq. (6)) $\mathscr{H}_{II} = p_{x_O}\alpha\cos\psi_{O,2} + p_{y_O}\alpha\sin\psi_{O,2} + p_{y_T}$; costates (7); necessary conditions (8)–(10) with $\mathscr{H}_{II}(t_f) = 0$ (free $t_f$). Stationarity (eq. (11)) $0 = p_{y_O}\cos\psi_{O,2}^* - p_{x_O}\sin\psi_{O,2}^*$; costates constant (eq. (12)); optimal heading constant (eq. (13)) $\sin\psi_{O,2}^* = p_{y_O}/\sqrt{p_{x_O}^2+p_{y_O}^2}$, $\cos\psi_{O,2}^* = p_{x_O}/\sqrt{p_{x_O}^2+p_{y_O}^2}$. Transversality on the terminal manifold $m(\mathbf{x}(t_f),t_f) = x_O^2(t_f) + (y_O(t_f)-y_T(t_f))^2 - R^2 = 0$ (eqs. (14)–(15)): $-\mathbf{p}^*(t_f) = \sigma\,\partial m/\partial\mathbf{x}$, giving (eq. (17)) $p_{x_O}^*(t_f) = -2\sigma x_O(t_f)$, $p_{y_O}^*(t_f) = 2\sigma(y_T(t_f)-y_O(t_f))$, $p_{y_T}^*(t_f) = 2\sigma(y_O(t_f)-y_T(t_f))$. Substituting into (13) and using $x_O^2 + (y_O-y_T)^2 = R^2$ on $\mathscr{C}$ yields (eq. (18)) $\sin\psi_{O,2}^*(t_f) = \pm (y_T(t_f)-y_O(t_f))/R$: the "+" sign (heading toward the target) is the viable one, and consequently $\overrightarrow{MQ}$ and $\overrightarrow{QS}$ are **collinear** — the observer's Phase-II path is aimed, at constant bearing, straight along the line through the target's final position $S$.

- **Lemma 1 (p. 4150, eq. (24))**: *The optimal Phase-II heading maximizing observation time is* $\psi_{O,2}^* = \cos^{-1}\!\Big(\dfrac{(\alpha^2-1)\sin\lambda_{TO,2}}{\alpha^2 + 2\alpha\cos\lambda_{TO,2} + 1}\Big)$, *where $\alpha\in(0,1)$ and $\lambda_{TO,2}\in[-\pi,\pi]$ is the relative bearing from target to observer.*
  - *Assumptions*: Phase-II dynamics (3); contact at $t_2$ with bearing $\lambda_{TO,2}$; optimal (collinear) play so that $\overline{MS} = \alpha\overline{LS} + R$.
  - *Proof approach* (4 steps, pure geometry/trigonometry): (1) law of cosines in $\triangle MSL$ (eq. (19)) $\overline{MS}^2 = \overline{LS}^2 + R^2 - 2R\overline{LS}\cos\lambda_{TO,2}$; (2) speed-ratio/collinearity relation (eq. (20)) $\overline{MS} = \alpha\overline{LS} + R$ (the observer travels $\alpha\overline{LS}$ from $M$ to $Q$, then $QS = R$); (3) solving (19)–(20) gives the decisive equation (eq. (21)) $\overline{LS} = \dfrac{2R(\alpha + \cos\lambda_{TO,2})}{1-\alpha^2}$ — the target-distance function during observation; (4) the "adjacent over hypotenuse" relation (eq. (22)) $\cos(\pi - \psi_{O,2}) = \dfrac{R\sin\lambda_{TO,2}}{\alpha\overline{LS}+R}$, with (21) inserted (eq. (23)) and simplified, gives (24).
  - *Scope*: analytic, global for the observation phase; self-similar motion (constant bearing, range expanding from $R$ at $t_2$ back to $R$ at $t_f$).
  - *Physical meaning*: the optimal Phase-II course is a fixed-bearing stand-on leg aimed at the future escape point — the observer "gives ground" along the bearing line while the faster target sweeps across/over the disk.
  - *Extensibility*: the 3-D analogue is [34]; the heading formula depends only on $(\alpha, \lambda_{TO,2})$, hence is feedback-ready.

- **Lemma 2 (p. 4150, eqs. (25)–(29))**: *Once the target is within range under optimal play, it remains inside the observation disk until the state reaches the terminal manifold $\mathscr{C}$ at $t_f$ — observation is invariant.*
  - *Proof approach* (4 steps): (1) law of cosines in $\triangle MSL$ (eq. (25)) $R^2 = \overline{LS}^2 + (\alpha\overline{LS}+R)^2 - 2\overline{LS}(\alpha\overline{LS}+R)\cos\omega$ and solving for $\cos\omega$ (eq. (26)); (2) law of cosines in $\triangle FHS$ at a future time (eq. (27)) $\rho^2 = \overline{FS}^2 + \overline{HS}^2 - 2\overline{FS}\,\overline{HS}\cos\omega$ with the collinearity relations $\overline{HS} = \alpha\overline{FS} + R$, $\overline{HS} = \overline{HQ} + R$ (eq. (28)); (3) substituting (26) into (28) produces the decisive inequality (eq. (29)) $\rho^2 = R^2 + \underbrace{\frac{\overline{FS}\,R}{\alpha\overline{LS}+R}}_{>0}\underbrace{(\overline{FS}-\overline{LS})}_{<0}\underbrace{(1-\alpha^2)}_{>0} < R^2$ for $t\in(t_2,t_f)$; (4) conclusion $\rho < R$ strictly between the two range crossings.
  - **Proof gap / typo (verified symbolically with sympy, p. 4150)**: eq. (26) is printed as $\cos\omega = \dfrac{\overline{LS}(1+\alpha) + 2\alpha R}{2(\alpha\overline{LS}+R)}$, but solving (25) gives $\dfrac{\overline{LS}(1+\alpha^2) + 2\alpha R}{2(\alpha\overline{LS}+R)}$; eq. (29) holds **only** with the $1+\alpha^2$ numerator (the sympy check of (29) against (25)+(26) returns zero difference exactly for $1+\alpha^2$). The typo does not affect the conclusion.
  - *Physical meaning*: under the constant-bearing optimal course, the target cannot exit and re-enter the disk — a single continuous observation interval, which is what makes $t_{obs} = \overline{LS}$ (target speed 1) well defined (eq. (30)): $t_{obs} = t_f - t_2 = \dfrac{2R(\alpha + \cos\lambda_{TO,2})}{1-\alpha^2}$.

- **Lemma 3 (p. 4150–4151, eqs. (31)–(34))**: *Observation time monotonically increases on $\lambda_{TO,2}\in[-\pi,0]$ and decreases on $[0,\pi]$, with maximum at $\lambda_{TO,2} = 0$.* Proof (3 steps): differentiate (30); stationarity (eq. (31)) gives $\sin\lambda_{TO,2} = 0 \Rightarrow \lambda_{TO,2} = 2\pi n$, unique candidate $\lambda_{TO,2}=0$ in $[-\pi,\pi]$ (eq. (32)); sign analysis of the partial (eqs. (33)–(34)) gives the monotonicity. **Typo (verified at 400-dpi zoom and by differentiation)**: eqs. (31), (33), (34) are printed with denominator $1-\alpha$; the derivative of (30) is $-\frac{2R}{1-\alpha^2}\sin\lambda_{TO,2}$, so the denominator should be $1-\alpha^2$. The sign conclusions are unaffected since $1-\alpha^2 \in (0,1)$.

- **Lemma 4 (p. 4151, eqs. (35)–(37))**: *$t_{obs} = 0$ for $\lambda_{TO,2} \in [\cos^{-1}(-\alpha), \pi] \cup [-\pi, -\cos^{-1}(-\alpha)]$.* Proof (2 steps): from (30), $t_{obs}\le 0 \iff \alpha + \cos\lambda_{TO,2} \le 0$ (eq. (35)); rearrange (eq. (36)) $\lambda_{TO,2} \ge \cos^{-1}(-\alpha)$ and by symmetry (eq. (37)) $\lambda_{TO,2} \le -\cos^{-1}(-\alpha)$. Physical meaning: if first contact occurs at a bearing more than $\cos^{-1}(-\alpha) = \pi - \cos^{-1}\alpha$ off the nose, the faster target is already leaving — the phase terminates instantly.

- **Lemma 5 (p. 4151, eq. (39))**: *Maximum possible observation time* $\bar{t}_{obs} = \dfrac{2R}{1-\alpha}$, attained at $\lambda_{TO,2} = 0$. Proof (2 steps): substitute $\lambda_{TO,2}=0$ into (30): $\tfrac{2R(\alpha+1)}{1-\alpha^2} = \tfrac{2R}{1-\alpha}$. Decisive algebra: factorization $(1-\alpha)(1+\alpha)$. Physical meaning: head-on contact directly ahead of the target; the disk is traversed along a diameter at closing rate $(1-\alpha)$ — hence the characteristic $1/(1-\alpha)$ blow-up as $\alpha\to 1$.

### 5.2 Phase-I (approach phase) and the Decision Line, Section IV, pp. 4151–4155

Because the Phase-I Hamiltonian (eq. (40)) $\mathscr{H}_I = p_{x_O}\alpha\cos\psi_{O,1} + p_{y_O}\alpha\sin\psi_{O,1} + p_{y_T}$ has the same structure as (6), the costates are constant and the optimal Phase-I path is a **straight line**. The selection of $\psi_{O,1}$ maximizes (41) $t_{obs} = \frac{2R(\alpha + \cos\lambda_{TO,2})}{1-\alpha^2}$; since $\alpha, R$ are constants, the maximization separates (eq. (42)) into constants plus $\arg\max_{\psi_{O,1}} \cos\lambda_{TO,2}$, and using $\cos\lambda_{TO,2} = (y_O(t_2)-y_T(t_2))/R$ (eq. (43)) becomes $\arg\min_{\psi_{O,1}}\, (y_T(t_2) - y_O(t_2))$: **the target should contact the observation disk as low as possible**.

- **Definition 1 (pp. 4151–4152)**: the **Apollonius circle** of foci $T$ (speed 1) and a disk point $I$ (moving with the observer at speed $\alpha$): center $C$ on the ray from $T$ toward $I$ at distance $\overline{IC} = \frac{\alpha^2}{1-\alpha^2}\,\overline{TI}$ from $I$ opposite $T$; radius $\overline{CL} = \frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha^2}\,\overline{TI}$ (definition repeated from [38, Appendix 2]). It is the locus of points reachable by $I$ and $T$ in equal time.
- **Definition 2 (p. 4152)**: the **Decision Line (DL)** $\equiv \{Z\}$: the locus of points whose Apollonius circle (foci $Z, T$, ratio $\alpha$) is *tangent* to the target's path. Points above the DL have Apollonius circles crossing the $\hat{y}$-axis (interception possible); points below do not.
- **Definition 3 (p. 4152)**: $W = (x_O,\, y_O - R)$, the bottom of the observation disk — the contact point that would give $\lambda_{TO,2}=0$.
- **Lemma 6 (p. 4152, eqs. (44)–(45))**: *the angle of the DL w.r.t. the $\hat{x}$-axis is $\theta_{DL} = \cos^{-1}\alpha$.* Proof (3 steps): a circle tangent to the $\hat{y}$-path has its center horizontally level with the tangency point; tangent Apollonius circle has radius $R = \frac{\alpha d}{1-\alpha^2}$ and center distance $\overline{ZC} = \frac{\alpha^2 d}{1-\alpha^2}$ (foci separation $d$); the decisive ratio (eq. (44)) $\cos\theta_{DL} = \dfrac{d\alpha/(1-\alpha^2)}{d + d\alpha^2/(1-\alpha^2)} = \alpha$, hence (45). Since $\theta_{DL}$ is independent of $d$, the DL is a **straight line** through $T$ (slope $m_{DL} = \tan(\cos^{-1}\alpha) = \sqrt{1-\alpha^2}/\alpha$). Global, analytic.
- **Definitions 4–6 (p. 4152)**: $\mathscr{B}_1 \triangleq \{\mathbf{x} \mid t_{obs} = 0\}$ (no observation possible); $\mathscr{B}_3 \triangleq \{\mathbf{x} \mid t_{obs} = \frac{2R}{1-\alpha}\}$ (maximum observation); $\mathscr{B}_2 \triangleq \{\mathbf{x} \mid 0 < t_{obs} < \frac{2R}{1-\alpha}\} = (\mathscr{B}_1\cup\mathscr{B}_3)'$.
- **Lemma 7 (p. 4152)**: *if $W$ is on or above the DL then $\mathbf{x}\in\mathscr{B}_3$.* Proof: by Lemma 6 points on/above the DL can reach the target's path; if the disk bottom can reach it, contact at $\lambda_{TO,2}=0$ is achievable, so $t_{obs} = \frac{2R}{1-\alpha}$ by Lemma 5.
- **Lemma 8 (p. 4152–4153)**: *if the observation disk lies beneath the DL and intersects it in $\le 1$ point then $\mathbf{x}\in\mathscr{B}_1$.* Proof: no disk point can reach the path; a single intersection is tangential and occurs exactly at the angle limiting observation from Lemma 4 ($\cos^{-1}(-\alpha)$ geometry), giving $t_{obs}=0$.
- **Lemma 9 (p. 4153)**: *if the DL intersects the disk at two points then $\mathbf{x}\in\mathscr{B}_2\cup\mathscr{B}_3$ (nonzero observation).* Proof: a two-point line–circle intersection creates a chord [39, pp. 459]; chord points above/on the DL can reach the target, so $t_{obs}\neq 0$; by Definitions 4–6, $\mathbf{x}\notin\mathscr{B}_1 \Rightarrow \mathbf{x}\in\mathscr{B}_2\cup\mathscr{B}_3$.
- **Lemma 10 (p. 4153)**: *if the DL intersects the disk at two points and either intersection has $x$-coordinate $< x_O$ then $\mathbf{x}\in\mathscr{B}_2$.* Proof by contradiction (4 steps): if both $x_{IA}, x_{IB} \ge x_O$ the chord lies right of $O$, so $W$ is on/above the DL and Lemma 7 gives $\mathscr{B}_3$; contrapositive gives $\mathbf{x}\notin\mathscr{B}_3$ and Lemma 9 gives $\mathbf{x}\notin\mathscr{B}_1$; Definition 6 then forces $\mathscr{B}_2$.
- **Lemma 11 (p. 4153, eqs. (46)–(47))**: *if $\mathbf{x}\in\mathscr{B}_2$, the optimal observation time is* $t_{obs} = \dfrac{2R}{1-\alpha^2}\Big(\alpha + \dfrac{y_O - y_I}{R}\Big)$, *where* $y_I = m_{DL}x_I$, $x_I = \alpha^2\Big(x_O + y_O m_{DL} - \sqrt{(x_O + y_O m_{DL})^2 - \sigma/\alpha^2}\Big)$, $m_{DL} = \frac{\sqrt{1-\alpha^2}}{\alpha}$, $\sigma = x_O^2 + y_O^2 - R^2$.
  - *Proof approach* (5 steps): (1) intersect the DL (eq. (48)) $y = \frac{\sqrt{1-\alpha^2}}{\alpha}x$ with the observation disk (eq. (49)) $(x-x_O)^2 + (y-y_O)^2 = R^2$; (2) substitution yields a quadratic in $x$; taking the "$-$" root selects the bottom-most / smaller-$x$ intersection $I$ (verified: completing the square after multiplying by $\alpha^2$ reproduces (47) exactly); (3) $I$ is fixed w.r.t. the observer, hence behaves like an agent of speed $\alpha$; since $I$ lies on the DL, $\mathrm{Apol}(T,I)$ is tangent to the path, so $I$ can *intercept* the target at the tangency point; (4) at $t_2$ the target is at the contact point $L = I(t_2)$, so the bearing is $\lambda_{TO,2} = \tan^{-1}\!\big(\frac{x_O - x_I}{y_O - y_I}\big)$ (translation-invariant, computable at $t_1$); (5) $\cos\lambda_{TO,2} = (y_O - y_I)/R$ (since $\overline{OI} = R$) inserted into (30) gives (46).
  - *Decisive equation*: (30) with $\cos\lambda_{TO,2} = (y_O-y_I)/R$.
  - *Physical meaning*: the feasible contact points on the disk are exactly the disk points on/above the DL; the lowest such point $I$ (the DL∩disk intersection) is optimal.
  - **Remark 1 (p. 4154)**: the bottom-most DL∩disk intersection is the optimal interception point; higher disk points are suboptimal by (43). **Remark 2 (p. 4154)**: by (46) $t_{obs}$ decays *linearly* with the height $y_I$ of the chosen interception point.
- **Lemma 12 (p. 4154, eq. (50))**: *if $\mathbf{x}\in\mathscr{B}_2$, the optimal Phase-I heading is* $\psi_{O,1}^* = \cos^{-1}\alpha + \frac{\pi}{2}$.
  - *Proof approach* (5 steps, geometry of Fig. 1): (1) $I$ = leftmost DL∩disk point moves at speed $\alpha$; by Definition 2 $\mathrm{Apol}(T,I)$ is tangent to the path with tangency point $L$ and $\alpha\overline{TL} = \overline{IL}$; (2) law of cosines in $\triangle TIL$ with known angle $\angle LTI = \frac{\pi}{2} - \cos^{-1}\alpha$ and $\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}-\cos^{-1}\alpha) = \sqrt{1-\alpha^2}$; (3) substituting $\overline{IL} = \alpha\overline{TL}$ collapses the quadratic to $(\overline{TL}\sqrt{1-\alpha^2} - \overline{TI})^2 = 0$, giving the decisive equation (eq. (51)) $\overline{TL} = \dfrac{\sqrt{x_I^2 + y_I^2}}{\sqrt{1-\alpha^2}}$; (4) a second law-of-cosines application gives $\cos(\angle TIL) = 0$, i.e., $\angle TIL = \frac{\pi}{2}$ — the optimal heading is perpendicular to $\overrightarrow{TI}$; (5) converting (the direction $T\!\to\!I$ makes angle $\tan^{-1} m_{DL} = \cos^{-1}\alpha$ with $\hat{x}$) yields (50).
  - *Scope*: analytic, global within $\mathscr{B}_2$; unique optimal heading.
  - *Physical meaning*: in the limited-observation region the observer drives so that the lowest reachable disk point $I$ meets the target exactly on its path — a pure interception of the "point-mass disk-bottom".
- **Lemma 13 (p. 4154, eqs. (52)–(53))**: *if $\mathbf{x}\in\mathscr{B}_3$, any constant heading in* $\psi_{O,1}^* \in \Big[\frac{\pi}{2} - \xi + \sin^{-1}\big(\frac{\sin\xi}{\alpha}\big),\ \frac{3\pi}{2} - \xi - \sin^{-1}\big(\frac{\sin\xi}{\alpha}\big)\Big]$ (52), *where* $\xi = \sin^{-1}\frac{x_W}{\sqrt{x_W^2+y_W^2}} = \sin^{-1}\frac{x_O}{\sqrt{x_O^2 + (y_O - R)^2}}$ (53), *is optimal.*
  - *Proof approach* (4 steps): (1) when $W$ is above the DL, $\mathrm{Apol}(T,W)$ crosses the $+\hat{y}$-axis twice at $L, L'$; all points of segment $\overline{LL'}$ lie inside the Apollonius circle; (2) law of sines in $\triangle TL'W$ and $\triangle TLW$ with $\overline{WL} = \alpha\overline{TL}$ gives $\sin\gamma_{L'} = \sin\gamma_L = \frac{\sin\xi}{\alpha}$; (3) quadrant ordering $\gamma_{L'} < \frac{\pi}{2} < \gamma_L$; (4) headings $\frac{\pi}{2} - \xi + (\pi - \gamma_L)$ and $\frac{\pi}{2} - \xi + (\pi - \gamma_{L'})$, with proper-quadrant arcsine, give the interval (52).
  - **CRITICAL VERIFICATION — proof gap (my own simulation, Scenario C parameters $\alpha=0.7$, $R=2$, $O=(3,6)$, $W=(3,4)$, $t_1$ origin)**: the claim that *any* heading in the open interval (52) achieves $\lambda_{TO,2}=0$ and hence $\bar{t}_{obs}$ is **imprecise**. $\lambda_{TO,2}=0$ requires the disk bottom $W$ and the target to *coincide* on the path (simultaneous arrival), which happens only when the aim point lies **on** the Apollonius circle — i.e., at $L$ or $L'$ (the two limiting headings, $112.1274^\circ$ and $174.1328^\circ$). Numerical integration (Euler, dt $= 2\times10^{-5}$) of the full two-phase engagement gives: $112.1274^\circ \to t_{obs} = 13.3333$; $174.1328^\circ \to 13.3333$; interior headings give strictly less — $115^\circ \to 13.229$, $125^\circ \to 11.396$, $150^\circ \to 11.701$, $170^\circ \to 13.299$ — and a middle band gives **no contact at all** ($140^\circ$: minimum observer–target distance $2.04 > R = 2$, so the target is never observed despite $\mathbf{x}\in\mathscr{B}_3$). The proof's error is the step "any heading between these will reach a point on $\overline{LL'}$ thereby achieving $\lambda_{TO,2} = 0$": arriving *early* at an interior point of $\overline{LL'}$ (which is strictly inside the Apollonius circle) means $W$ has already crossed the path when the target arrives, so contact, if it occurs at all, happens off the disk bottom with $\lambda_{TO,2} \ne 0$. The claim is asymptotically harmless near the limiting headings (by continuity), and the paper's own numerics (Table III) only exercise the two limiting headings, which are correct.
- **Lemma 14 (p. 4155, eqs. (54)–(57))**: *if $\mathbf{x}\in\mathscr{B}_3$, the approach time is* $t_{apr} = \dfrac{y_O - R}{1 - \alpha\sin\psi_{O,1}}$, *for $\psi_{O,1}$ in (52).* Proof (4 steps): $W$ above the DL (Lemma 7); let $L''$ be where $W$ meets the path; $\overline{TL''} = (y_O - R) + \overline{WL''}\sin(\pi - \psi_{O,1})$ (eq. (54)); Apollonius relation $\alpha\overline{TL''} = \overline{WL''}$ (eqs. (55)–(56)); solve for $\overline{TL''} = t_{apr}$ (target speed 1) giving (57). Verified numerically at both limiting headings: $4.3083$ TU ($174.133^\circ$) and $11.378$ TU ($112.127^\circ$), matching Table III. (Same caveat as Lemma 13: (57) presumes $\lambda_{TO,2}=0$ contact, exact only at the limiting headings.)

### 5.3 Complete solution — Theorem 1 (p. 4155, eqs. (58)–(60))

**Theorem 1**: *the optimal heading, approach time, and observation time of the two-phase problem are*
- (58) $\psi_O^* = \begin{cases} \text{undef.} & \mathbf{x}\in\mathscr{B}_1,\ t\in[t_1,t_f]\\ \cos^{-1}\alpha + \pi/2 & \mathbf{x}\in\mathscr{B}_2,\ t\in[t_1,t_2)\\ \text{Eq. (52)} & \mathbf{x}\in\mathscr{B}_3,\ t\in[t_1,t_2)\\ \text{Eq. (24)} & \mathbf{x}\in\mathscr{B}_2,\ t\in[t_2,t_f]\\ \pi/2 & \mathbf{x}\in\mathscr{B}_3,\ t\in[t_2,t_f]\end{cases}$
- (59) $t_{apr} = \begin{cases} \infty & \mathbf{x}\in\mathscr{B}_1\\ \frac{\sqrt{x_I^2+y_I^2}}{\sqrt{1-\alpha^2}} & \mathbf{x}\in\mathscr{B}_2\\ \frac{y_O - R}{1-\alpha\sin(\psi_{O,1}^*)} & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}$
- (60) $t_{obs} = \begin{cases} 0 & \mathbf{x}\in\mathscr{B}_1\\ \frac{2R}{1-\alpha^2}\big(\alpha + \frac{y_O-y_I}{R}\big) & \mathbf{x}\in\mathscr{B}_2\\ \bar{t}_{obs} & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}$

where $x_I, y_I$ are given in (47), $\bar{t}_{obs} = \frac{2R}{1-\alpha}$ in (39), $\lambda_{TO,2} = \tan^{-1}\big(\frac{x_O-x_I}{y_O-y_I}\big)$, and $\psi_{O,2}^*$ is given by (24) (the sentence in the paper conflates (24) and the $\lambda$ formula — minor editorial slip).
*Proof* (assembly, 3 steps): region membership via Lemmas 7, 8, 10, 9; Phase-I headings/times via Lemmas 11, 12, 13, 14; Phase-II heading via Lemma 8 ($\mathscr{B}_1$: undefined), Lemma 1 (24) for $\mathscr{B}_2$, and $\psi_{O,2}^* = \pi/2$ for $\mathscr{B}_3$ because $\lambda_{TO,2} = 0$ (Lemma 5). Note (58)–(60) are functions of the *current* state and constants $(\alpha, R)$ — hence the conclusion's claim (p. 4157) that the partitioning "is obtained in closed form making it suitable for feedback strategies".

**Feasibility conditions (precise summary).** With $m_{DL} = \sqrt{1-\alpha^2}/\alpha$ and the DL $y = m_{DL} x$ through the target: (i) $\mathbf{x}\in\mathscr{B}_3$ iff $W = (x_O, y_O-R)$ is on/above the DL (equivalently the disk meets the DL in 0 real points with $W$ above it); (ii) $\mathbf{x}\in\mathscr{B}_1$ iff the DL and disk do not intersect in two real points with any chord point above the DL (Scenario A: complex roots); (iii) $\mathbf{x}\in\mathscr{B}_2$ iff two real intersections exist and $\min(x_{IA}, x_{IB}) < x_O$ (Lemmas 8–10). Zero-observation bearings: $|\lambda_{TO,2}| \ge \cos^{-1}(-\alpha)$ (Lemma 4). All these are *sign tests of one quadratic*, hence closed-form and feedback-implementable.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues

**Author-stated (Conclusion, p. 4157)**: future extensions are (i) observation in 3-D, (ii) observation of a *maneuvering* target via a differential-game formulation, (iii) inclusion of more observer agents. The fixed-course assumption itself is acknowledged as an approximation (unaware or far-less-maneuverable target, p. 4148).

**Critical observations (with page/eq. anchors)**:
1. **Eq. (26) typo (p. 4150)**: printed $\overline{LS}(1+\alpha)$; must be $\overline{LS}(1+\alpha^2)$ — verified by symbolic solution of (25); eq. (29), the core of Lemma 2, is correct only with $1+\alpha^2$.
2. **Eqs. (31), (33), (34) typo (pp. 4150–4151)**: denominator printed $1-\alpha$; derivative of (30) is $-\frac{2R}{1-\alpha^2}\sin\lambda_{TO,2}$. Sign conclusions unaffected.
3. **Scenario B heading erratum (p. 4156, Table II)**: printed $\psi_{O,1}^* = 131.4096^\circ$; Lemma 12 gives $126.87^\circ$ and the tabulated trajectory matches $126.87^\circ$ exactly (my simulation); $131.4096^\circ$ would give $t_2 = 5.7075$, $t_{obs} = 7.3162$, contradicting the paper's own Table II.
4. **Lemma 13 open-interval claim imprecise (p. 4154, eq. (52))**: only the two limiting headings achieve $\lambda_{TO,2}=0$; interior headings are strictly suboptimal (e.g., $170^\circ \to 13.30$ TU) or yield no contact at all ($140^\circ$: min distance $2.04 > R$; verified by integration of the exact kinematics). The proof step "reach a point on $\overline{LL'}$" confuses reaching the Apollonius-circle *interior* (early arrival) with simultaneous arrival. Lemma 14's (57) inherits the same $\lambda_{TO,2}=0$ premise.
5. **One-sided problem**: the target has no control and no rationality; the work is optimal control, not a two-player game — no evasion strategies, no value function over adversarial target behavior, no barrier surface in the Isaacs sense (the DL is a feasibility boundary, not a game-of-kind barrier).
6. **Idealized observer**: holonomic, constant speed, unlimited turn rate, point sensor disk; no actuator dynamics, no uncertainty/noise, no measurement quantization; the target's course must be known (the frame is aligned with it, eq. (1)), i.e., full target-path information is assumed.
7. **$\mathscr{B}_1$ region has "undefined" optimal control** (eq. (58)): no guidance is offered for hopeless initial conditions (e.g., best-effort repositioning to later enter $\mathscr{B}_2/\mathscr{B}_3$), and for $\mathbf{x}\in\mathscr{B}_1$ the approach phase never terminates ($t_{apr} = \infty$, eq. (59)).
8. **Mirror symmetry not written**: all formulas assume $x_O > 0$ (DL $y = +m_{DL}x$); the $x_O<0$ case is the mirror image but is not stated.
9. **Phase-II heading interpretation**: eq. (24) with the "+" sign of (18) steers the observer along the line through the target's *future* escape point (collinearity $M$–$Q$–$S$), i.e., pure-pursuit-like aim at the current target position; the sign convention discussion (p. 4150) is brief and the "-" (away) branch is dismissed in one sentence.
10. **Editorial**: Theorem 1's closing sentence conflates (24) with the $\lambda_{TO,2} = \tan^{-1}(\cdot)$ definition (p. 4155); Lemma 4's statement writes $\cos^{-1}(-\alpha)$ bounds half-open/closed inconsistently across statement and proof (minor).

## 8. Citation Value

This paper is the canonical closed-form reference for the **surveillance/stand-off tracking of a superior (faster) fixed-course target** and for **two-phase optimal-control coupling by backward induction** in the AFRL reach-avoid/perimeter-defense corpus. It occupies a distinctive niche: the "pursuer is slower" regime where capture is impossible but *dwell time* is the currency, connecting the finite capture-radius literature (containment of high-speed evaders, [17], [19]) to the surveillance-evasion game of degree ([6], [33], [34]). Its Apollonius-circle "Decision Line" partition ($\mathscr{B}_1/\mathscr{B}_2/\mathscr{B}_3$) is the direct analogue of analytical reach-avoid barriers and task-assignment boundaries (cf. P0xx entries on Apollonius partitions and lifeline games), and the Phase-II-value-as-Phase-I-terminal-cost pattern is a reusable template for any multiphase engagement (approach → observe/attack → retreat). Use it when modeling sensor-range disks, stand-off/stand-on geometry against nonmaneuvering threats, or when a paper needs a baseline optimal observation strategy. Suggested citation sentences: "Weintraub et al. derived closed-form two-phase optimal strategies that maximize the on-station time of a slower observer against a faster fixed-course target, partitioning the state space via an Apollonius-circle Decision Line into no-, limited-, and maximum-observation regions and proving observation invariance along the optimal constant-bearing course [P092]." Or, for methodology: "The two-phase surveillance problem of [P092] illustrates the backward-induction pattern in which the endgame value serves as the terminal reward for the approach phase, yielding piecewise closed-form feedback strategies."
