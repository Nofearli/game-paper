## 2. Research Background
The Target-Attacker-Defender (TAD) game is the three-player pursuit-evasion paradigm, traceable to Boyell's moving-target defense ([11], Boyell 1976, claimed on p. 1 as the introduction of the TAD game) and matured by the Garcia–Casbeer–Pachter school: state-feedback optimal strategies for active defense were established in [8] (Garcia, Casbeer, Pachter, IEEE TAC 64(2):553–568, 2019) and [15] (JOTA 191(2):675–699, 2021), the equal-speed barrier in [18] (TAES 54(2):706–721, 2018), and variable speed ratios in [13] (Liang et al., Automatica 102:58–71, 2019). All of these assume **point capture by the Attacker** ($r=0$): the Attacker must coincide with the Target. In practice (missile blast radius, vehicle collision/attack zone) the Attacker only needs to reach a neighborhood of radius $r>0$ of the Target. Prior capture-radius work either prescribes the Attacker's guidance law (pure pursuit / proportional / optimal guidance in [19], Garcia et al., JGCD 38(8):1510–1520, 2015 — Game of Degree only, no barrier), or treats a faster Attacker with role switching ([14], Liang, Deng, Lu, IEEE TAC 66(2):902–909, 2021 — no explicit barrier, no Game of Degree). The paper is motivated by intelligent-vehicle scenarios (p. 1, Sec. I; Remark 1, p. 3): military convoy escort, defense vehicles launching shells against attacking vehicles, ambulance convoy protection; Remark 2 (p. 3) justifies the asymmetric radii (A has $r>0$, D has zero) by an example where the lower-value vehicle acts as Defender.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
**Problem**: the planar "simple motion" TAD game (Isaacs) with holonomic vehicles, $V_A=V_D=1$, $\gamma=V_T/V_A<1$, Attacker capture radius $r>0$, Defender capture radius $0$. Termination set $\mathcal T=\mathcal T_1\cup\mathcal T_2$ (2) with $\mathcal T_1=\{\mathbf x\,|\,\|\mathbf x_T-\mathbf x_A\|_2\le r\}$ (3) (A wins) and $\mathcal T_2=\{\mathbf x\,|\,\|\mathbf x_D-\mathbf x_A\|_2=0\}$ (4) (T–D team wins). Two problems (p. 3–4): **Problem 1 (Game of Kind)** — given $\mathbf x_0$, does the state hit $\mathcal T_1$ or $\mathcal T_2$ first (divide $\mathbb R^2$ of Target positions into Attacker's winning zone $\mathcal Z_c$ and Target–Defender's zone $\mathcal Z_t$ separated by barrier $\mathcal B$); **Problem 2 (Game of Degree)** — inside $\mathcal Z_c$, find the saddle point $(\beta^*,\delta^*,\zeta^*)$ of $V(\mathbf x_0):=\max_{\beta(\cdot)}\min_{\{\delta(\cdot),\zeta(\cdot)\}}\mathcal J$ (7) with terminal cost $\mathcal J=\Psi(\mathbf x_f)=\|\mathbf x_{T_f}-\mathbf x_{D_f}\|_2$ (5)–(6) (A maximizes, T–D cooperatively minimize the terminal Target–Defender separation).

**Gap — [authors' claim]** (p. 1–2, contributions 1–3): (i) [8],[13],[15],[18] all treat point capture ($r=0$) for A, so no optimal feedback strategy nor barrier exists for the equal-speed TAD game with $r>0$; (ii) versus literature that decides the barrier by intersecting **a straight line with an Apollonius circle** ([18]: line = the A–D perpendicular bisectorY-axis logic; [13]), this paper must intersect **A's "ultimate interception curve" with an Apollonius oval**, which "is quite challenging", and yields a strictly larger winning zone for A (the compared zones are subsets); (iii) versus [19]: that work solves only the Game of Degree under prescribed A guidance laws, gives no Game of Kind, and does not prove Nash equilibrium strictly, whereas this paper does both; (iv) versus [14]: that work needs $V_A>V_D$ and provides neither an explicit barrier function nor a Game of Degree, while this paper handles $V_A=V_D$ and solves both games.
**[Verified from cited text]**: the printed titles/venues of [8] (TAC 2019, "Design and analysis of state feedback optimal strategies for the differential game of active defense"), [13] (Automatica 2019, "A differential game for cooperative target defense"), [14] (TAC 66(2), 2021, role switch), [15] (JOTA 2021, "The complete differential game of active target defense"), [18] (TAES 54(2), 2018) and [19] (JGCD 38(8), 2015) are consistent with the authors' characterization, and [8]/[15]'s HJI-verified Nash treatment and [18]'s equal-speed barrier are known features of those papers in this library. The claim that the winning zone for $r>0$ contains that for $r=0$ is supported inside the paper only qualitatively (Figs. 2–4, Remark 4, p. 4–5; Fig. 11, Example 5) — no formal containment theorem or area measure is given.

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Theory (Game of Degree, Theorem 1, p. 5)**: first optimal state-feedback strategies $(\beta^*,\delta^*,\zeta^*)$ (11)–(13) for the equal-speed TAD game with non-zero Attacker capture radius, together with the capture point $\mathbf z^*(\mathbf x)$ defined by the two scalar equations (14)–(15) and a $C^1$ value function (16) that is **proved** to satisfy the HJI equation (17), hence a state-feedback Nash (saddle-point) equilibrium — extending the $r=0$ HJI methodology of [8],[15] to $r>0$.
2. **Theory (Game of Kind, Theorem 2, p. 7–8)**: an explicit barrier characterization (50)–(51) built from (a) the **Attacker's ultimate (limiting) interception curve** $y=f(x)=\pm\sqrt{r^2-x^2}\,(1-x_A/x)$, $x\in[-r,0)$ (56) — the locus of points A can just capture when it is simultaneously intercepted on the A–D perpendicular bisector — and (b) the tangency/separation analysis between this fixed curve and the A–T Apollonius oval (10)/(72), solved via a polar parametrization (72)–(80) borrowed from [24] (Yan, Shi, Zhong, IJC 2021).
3. **Modeling**: non-zero capture radius only for the Attacker ($r>0$), zero for the Defender (3)–(4), with the Apollonius **oval** replacing Isaacs' Apollonius circle (Lemma 1, (9)–(10)); Remark 4 + Figs. 2–4 show A's winning region strictly enlarges with $r>0$.
4. **Experiment (simulation, Sec. V, p. 10–12)**: five autonomous-vehicle examples — four Game-of-Degree runs comparing optimal strategies vs. pure pursuit in a unilateral-deviation matrix (Table II), and one Game-of-Kind comparison of the new barrier against the $r=0$ barrier of [8] under identical geometry (Fig. 11).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results

### 5.0 Dynamics, information, cost, termination (p. 2–4)
- **Dynamics (1)**: Isaacs' simple motion, $\dot x_A=\cos\beta,\ \dot y_A=\sin\beta$; $\dot x_D=\cos\delta,\ \dot y_D=\sin\delta$; $\dot x_T=\gamma\cos\zeta,\ \dot y_T=\gamma\sin\zeta$, with initial conditions, $\mathbf x=(\mathbf x_A,\mathbf x_D,\mathbf x_T)\in\mathbb R^6$, controls $\beta,\delta,\zeta\in[-\pi,\pi]$ (Attacker/Defender/Target headings), speeds $V_A=V_D=1$, $V_T=\gamma$, $\gamma=V_T/V_A<1$ (p. 2–3).
- **Termination (2)–(4)**: $\mathcal T=\mathcal T_1\cup\mathcal T_2$; A captures T iff $\|\mathbf x_T-\mathbf x_A\|_2\le r$ (3); T–D wins iff $\|\mathbf x_D-\mathbf x_A\|_2=0$ (4) — **asymmetric radii**: only A has a non-zero capture radius.
- **Information structure** (p. 3): perfect-information differential game; all agents know (1) and the current state $\mathbf x(t)$, use causal (state-feedback) strategies, and do not know the opponents' current decisions — so no discriminatory/stroboscopic strategies are needed; Isaacs' condition holds because the Hamiltonian (8) decouples in the three controls (min$_\beta$ max$_{\zeta,\delta}\,\mathscr H=$ max$_{\zeta,\delta}$ min$_\beta\,\mathscr H$, p. 4).
- **Cost (5)–(7)**: terminal payoff $\Psi(\mathbf x_f)=\|\mathbf x_{T_f}-\mathbf x_{D_f}\|_2$; value $V(\mathbf x_0)=\max_\beta\min_{\delta,\zeta}\mathcal J$ (7). Remark 3 (p. 4): since A's capture of T is inevitable inside $\mathcal Z_c$, D's rational goal is to get as close to T as possible at $t_f$; if A deviates from optimal, the state can cross $\mathcal B$ into $\mathcal Z_t$ and T escapes.

### 5.1 Lemma 1 (Evasion Region) (p. 4, eqs. (9)–(10); attributed to [24])
**Statement**: for the A–T pair alone, the Target's evasion region and its boundary (the **Apollonius oval** with capture radius $r$) are
$$\mathcal Z_e=\{\mathbf z\mid \|\mathbf x_T-\mathbf z\|_2<\gamma(\|\mathbf x_A-\mathbf z\|_2-r)\}\ (9),\qquad \mathcal A^r=\{\mathbf z\mid \|\mathbf x_T-\mathbf z\|_2=\gamma(\|\mathbf x_A-\mathbf z\|_2-r)\}\ (10).$$
For $r=0$, (10) reduces to Isaacs' Apollonius circle (Isaacs 1965, Sec. 6.7). Interpretation: $\mathbf z$ is reachable by T strictly before A can get within $r$ of it. Example (p. 4): $\mathbf x_A=(3.5,3)$, $\mathbf x_T=(5,0)$, $\gamma=0.7$, $r=1$ (Figs. 2–3); Remark 4 + Fig. 4 (p. 5): A's $r>0$ winning area (oval boundary) strictly contains the $r=0$ one (circle boundary) — the gray crescent is area A "no longer needs to worry about".

### 5.2 Theorem 1 (Game of Degree; optimal strategies, value function, HJI/Nash) (p. 5–7, eqs. (11)–(17))
**Statement**: given the TAD game (1)–(7), the optimal state-feedback strategies are
$$\cos\beta^*=\frac{x^*(\mathbf x)-x_A}{\|\mathbf z^*(\mathbf x)-\mathbf x_T\|_2},\quad \sin\beta^*=\frac{y^*(\mathbf x)-y_A}{\|\mathbf z^*(\mathbf x)-\mathbf x_T\|_2}\ (11)$$
$$\cos\delta^*=\frac{x^*(\mathbf x)-x_D}{\|\mathbf z^*(\mathbf x)-\mathbf x_D\|_2},\quad \sin\delta^*=\frac{y^*(\mathbf x)-y_D}{\|\mathbf z^*(\mathbf x)-\mathbf x_D\|_2}\ (12)$$
$$\cos\zeta^*=\frac{x^*(\mathbf x)-x_T}{\|\mathbf z^*(\mathbf x)-\mathbf x_T\|_2},\quad \sin\zeta^*=\frac{y^*(\mathbf x)-y_T}{\|\mathbf z^*(\mathbf x)-\mathbf x_T\|_2}\ (13)$$
where the **capture point** $\mathbf z^*(\mathbf x)=(x^*(\mathbf x),y^*(\mathbf x))$ solves
$$\|\mathbf z-\mathbf x_T\|_2=\gamma(\|\mathbf z-\mathbf x_A\|_2-r)\ (14)$$
(the Apollonius-oval condition (10): $\mathbf z^*$ is T's terminal position) and
$$\|\mathbf z-\mathbf x_A\|_2\big[(\mathbf z-\mathbf x_D)(y-y_T,x_T-x)^\top\big]-\gamma\|\mathbf z-\mathbf x_T\|_2\big[(\mathbf z-\mathbf x_D)(y-y_A,x_A-x)^\top\big]-\|\mathbf z-\mathbf x_D\|_2\big[(\mathbf z-\mathbf x_A)(y-y_T,x_T-x)^\top\big]=0\ (15)$$
where $(\mathbf z-\mathbf x_D)(y-y_T,x_T-x)^\top$ denotes the dot product $(x-x_D)(y-y_T)+(y-y_D)(x_T-x)$. The $C^1$ value function
$$V(\mathbf x)=\|\mathbf z^*(\mathbf x)-\mathbf x_D\|_2-\tfrac{1}{\gamma}\|\mathbf z^*(\mathbf x)-\mathbf x_T\|_2\ (16)$$
satisfies the HJI equation $\tfrac{\partial V(\mathbf x)}{\partial\mathbf x}\cdot\mathbf f(\mathbf x,\beta^*,\delta^*,\zeta^*)=0$ (17); hence (11)–(13) form a state-feedback Nash equilibrium.
- **Assumptions**: dynamics (1) with $V_A=V_D$, $\gamma<1$; $x_0\in\mathcal Z_c$ (state reaches $\mathcal T_1$ first); perfect information; terminal manifold $\mathcal T_1$ (capture), $t_f$ free.
- **Proof steps** (p. 5–7): (1) Isaacs' main equation 1 (Isaacs 1965, ch. 4): $\min_\beta\max_{\zeta,\delta}\mathscr H=0$ (18) with Hamiltonian $\mathscr H=\gamma\lambda_{x_T}\cos\zeta+\gamma\lambda_{y_T}\sin\zeta+\lambda_{x_D}\cos\delta+\lambda_{y_D}\sin\delta+\lambda_{x_A}\cos\beta+\lambda_{y_A}\sin\beta$ (8). (2) Stationarity gives headings in costates (19)–(21) (note: (21) prints "sin $\delta^*$" where $\sin\zeta^*$ is meant). (3) Costate dynamics $\dot\lambda=-\partial\mathscr H/\partial\mathbf x$ give $\dot\lambda_{x_T}=\dot\lambda_{y_T}=\dot\lambda_{x_D}=\dot\lambda_{y_D}=\dot\lambda_{x_A}=\dot\lambda_{y_A}=0$: all costates constant, hence **all optimal headings constant** (straight-line optimal trajectories). (4) The TPBVP in $\mathbb R^{12}$ is closed with the augmented terminal payoff $\Psi_a(\mathbf x_f):=\|\mathbf x_{D_f}-\mathbf x_{T_f}\|_2+\nu(\|\mathbf x_{A_f}-\mathbf x_{T_f}\|_2-r)$ (22), $\nu$ a Lagrange multiplier; transversality $\lambda(t_f)=\partial\Psi_a/\partial\mathbf x$ (23) yields (24)–(26). (5) Terminal identification $x\equiv x_{T_f},\ y\equiv y_{T_f}$ (27). (6)–(8) Rewriting (28)–(30); D's heading expressed first via terminal states (31), then, by constancy, via the current state: D **aims at the capture point** $\mathbf z$: $\cos\delta^*=(x-x_D)/\|\mathbf x_D-\mathbf z\|_2$ (32); likewise A and T head to $\mathbf z$ (33)–(34). (9) State–costate matching (35)–(36); eliminating $\nu$ from (28)+(30) gives $\lambda_{x_T}+\lambda_{x_A}=\frac{x-x_{D_f}}{\|\mathbf x_{A_f}-\mathbf z\|_2}$ (37), $\lambda_{y_T}+\lambda_{y_A}=\frac{y-y_{D_f}}{\|\mathbf x_{A_f}-\mathbf z\|_2}$ (38). (10) Free-$t_f$ relation $t_f-t=\tfrac1\gamma\overline{TI}=\tfrac1\gamma\|\mathbf x_T-\mathbf z\|_2$ (39) (T's remaining time to $\mathbf z$ at speed $\gamma$). (11) D's terminal position from current state (40). (12) Solving the four equations (35)–(38) for the four (A,T) costates gives (41), e.g. $\lambda_{x_A}=\frac{x-x_A}{\|\mathbf x_D-\mathbf z\|_2}\cdot\frac{x-x_D-\frac{x-x_T}{y-y_T}(y-y_D)}{x-x_A-\frac{x-x_T}{y-y_T}(y-y_A)}$. (13) Isaacs' main equation 1, $\mathscr H|_{t_f}\equiv0$ (42), after substituting (32)–(34),(41) becomes the scalar condition (43), which "simplify[s]... to (15)"; combining (14) and (15) pins the optimal capture point $I^*:(x^*,y^*)\in\mathcal A^r$. (14) **Nash verification**: $V$ (16) is $C^1$ "because neither of the preceding or following terms can be zero"; implicit function theorem on (10) gives $\frac{dy^*}{dx^*}=-\frac{\cos\zeta^*-\gamma\cos\beta^*}{\sin\zeta^*-\gamma\sin\beta^*}$ (44); the chain-rule gradient of $V$ (45) has numerator that collapses (46) to exactly the left side of (15) divided by $\|\mathbf x_T-\mathbf z\|_2\|\mathbf x_A-\mathbf z\|_2\|\mathbf x_D-\mathbf z\|_2$, hence $\frac{dV}{dx^*}=0$ (47); the partials are $\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_A}=\frac{\partial V}{\partial y_A}=0$, $\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_D}=-\cos\delta^*$, $\frac{\partial V}{\partial y_D}=-\sin\delta^*$, $\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_T}=\tfrac1\gamma\cos\zeta^*$, $\frac{\partial V}{\partial y_T}=\tfrac1\gamma\sin\zeta^*$ (48); therefore $\frac{\partial V}{\partial\mathbf x}\cdot\mathbf f=0+0-\cos^2\delta^*-\sin^2\delta^*+\gamma\cdot\tfrac1\gamma\cos^2\zeta^*+\gamma\cdot\tfrac1\gamma\sin^2\zeta^*=-1+1=0$ (49). $\square$
- **Decisive equations**: (46) (the $dV/dx^*$ numerator equals the LHS of (15), so (15) is exactly the stationarity that makes $V$ stationary in the capture point) and (49) (the HJI cancellation, which works *because* the $\tfrac1\gamma$ factor sits in (16) and $\gamma$ scales T's terms in (8)).
- **Scope/meaning**: global within $\mathcal Z_c$; analytic up to solving two scalar equations (14)–(15) for $\mathbf z^*(\mathbf x)$; all three players head in straight lines to the same capture point; $V$ = (D's distance to the capture point) − (T's remaining time-to-capture scaled by $1/\gamma$) = the terminal T–D separation under optimal play; A maximizes it, T–D minimize it. Note the printed denominator in (11) is $\|\mathbf z^*(\mathbf x)-\mathbf x_T\|_2$ while the proof's own (33) uses $\|\mathbf x_A-\mathbf z\|_2$ — an apparent typo in (11); likewise (26)/(30)/(37)–(38) print $\|\mathbf x_{A_f}-\mathbf x_{T_f}\|_2$ (resp. $\|\mathbf x_{A_f}-\mathbf z\|_2$) as the denominator of the first $\lambda_{x_T}$ term, whereas differentiating (22) w.r.t. $\mathbf x_{T_f}$ requires $\|\mathbf x_{D_f}-\mathbf x_{T_f}\|_2$ (resp. $\|\mathbf x_{D_f}-\mathbf z\|_2$); the costate solution (41) is consistent with the corrected denominators (all prefactors $1/\|\mathbf x_D-\mathbf z\|_2$), supporting the typo reading.
- **Extensibility**: the template (two-sided PMP + augmented terminal payoff + HJI verification) is that of [8],[15],[18]; the new content is the oval (14) in place of the circle and the extra stationarity equation (15). The proof nowhere uses $\gamma<1$ except through the oval's existence; the equal-speed assumption on A and D is not used in Theorem 1 at all (it enters the Game of Kind).

**Remark 5 (existence, p. 7)**: real solutions of (10)&(15) exist: at $x=x_T$ the oval (14) has two branches $y_h>y_T>y_l$; writing the LHS of (15) as $\mathcal F(x,y)$, $\mathcal F(x_T,y_h)>0$ and $\mathcal F(x_T,y_l)<0$, so by the intermediate value theorem some $(x^*(\mathbf x),y^*(\mathbf x))$ with $\mathcal F=0$ exists. (Existence only — uniqueness/continuity of the root, needed for $V$ to be a well-defined $C^1$ *function* of $\mathbf x$, is asserted via the implicit function theorem but no non-degeneracy condition $\partial\mathcal F/\partial y\ne0$ is verified.)

### 5.3 Theorem 2 (Game of Kind; barrier) (p. 7–10, eqs. (50)–(81))
**Setting** (p. 7): a reference frame with the $X$-axis along the instantaneous $\overline{AD}$ and the $Y$-axis its perpendicular bisector, so $A=(x_A,0)$, $D=(-x_A,0)$, and the generic Target position is $T=(x_T,y_T)$.
**Statement**: for specified $r$, $\gamma$, and A/D positions, the barrier curve dividing the plane into $\mathcal Z_c$ and $\mathcal Z_t$ is
$$x+P(\phi^*)\cos\phi^*-f^{-1}\big(y+P(\phi^*)\sin\phi^*\big)=0\ (50)$$
where $\phi^*(x,y)\in(\phi_{min},\phi_{max})$ is the unique solution of
$$\frac{\dot P(\phi)\cos\phi-\sin\phi P(\phi)}{\big(\dot P(\phi)\sin\phi+\cos\phi P(\phi)\big)^2}=\dot f^{-1}\big(y+P(\phi)\sin\phi\big)\ (51)$$
with
$$f(x)=\pm\sqrt{r^2-x^2}\Big(1-\frac{x_A}{x}\Big),\quad x\in[-r,0)\ \ (\text{p. 8; identical to (56)}),$$
$$P(\phi)=\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma^2}\Big(\gamma(\mathbf z-\mathbf x_A)\mathbf e-r+\sqrt{\big(\gamma(\mathbf z-\mathbf x_A)\mathbf e-r\big)^2+(1-\gamma^2)\big(\|\mathbf z-\mathbf x_A\|_2^2-r^2\big)}\Big),\quad \mathbf e=(\cos\phi,\sin\phi)^\top,$$
and $f^{-1}$ the inverse of (56), guaranteed to exist by monotonicity but given no closed form (Remark 6, p. 10).
**Proof idea and steps** (p. 8–10): (1) *Barrier-case geometry* (Fig. 5, p. 8): when T starts on $\mathcal B$, at the terminal moment A captures T at $I(x^*,y^*)$ on the Apollonius oval (10) **while** D intercepts A at $L(0,y_L)$ on the $Y$-axis, and the two points are exactly one capture radius apart: $\sqrt{(x^*)^2+(y^*-y_L)^2}=r$ (53); by Lemma 1 of [25] ("P. Waszy, M. Pachterz and K. Pham", MED 2019 — author names as printed appear garbled) A, L, I are collinear, so with slope $k=\frac{y_L-0}{0-x_A}=-y_L/x_A$ of line $AL$ (52) and $\frac{y^*-y_L}{x^*}=k$ (54), the limit capture point is $x^*=-\frac{r}{\sqrt{1+k^2}}$, $y^*=y_L-\frac{kr}{\sqrt{1+k^2}}$ (55). (2) Eliminating $k,y_L$ gives the **Attacker's ultimate interception curve** $y=f(x)=\pm\sqrt{r^2-x^2}(1-\frac{x_A}{x}),\ x\in[-r,0)$ (56) — a fixed curve depending only on $x_A$ and $r$; if T can reach left of (56) it escapes A. (3) *Monotonicity*: $\dot y=-\frac{x}{\sqrt{r^2-x^2}}(1-\frac{x_A}{x})+\frac{x_A}{x^2}\sqrt{r^2-x^2}>0$ (57); (56) is symmetric about the $X$-axis, decreasing on $y\in(-\infty,0)$ and increasing on $y\in(0,+\infty)$. (4) *Reduction to curve comparison*: for fixed A, the A–T oval (10) varies with $\mathbf x_T$ while (56) is fixed; three positional relations — separated (A wins without interception), tangent (limit case: capture and interception coincide; still $\mathcal Z_c$), intersecting (T aims into the lens-shaped region left of both curves and escapes; $\mathcal Z_t$). Define the potential $\mathcal F(\mathbf z)=\|\mathbf x_A-\mathbf z\|_2-\frac{\|\mathbf x_T-\mathbf z\|_2}{\gamma}-r$ (58) with $\mathcal F>0\Leftrightarrow\mathbf z\in\mathcal Z_t$, $\mathcal F=0\Leftrightarrow\mathbf z\in\mathcal A^r$ (59) and gradient (60). Extreme points of the oval: $G$ ($x$-min, (61)), $H$ ($x$-max, (62)), $J$ ($y$-min, (63)), $K$ ($y$-max, (64)) (Fig. 6, p. 9); by symmetry only the left arc $\widehat{JGK}$ must lie right of (56), written $x=g(x_T,y_T,y)$ in two branches $g_1$ ($\widehat{JG}$, $y\in[y_{min},y_G]$) and $g_2$ ($\widehat{GK}$, $y\in[y_G,y_{max}]$) (65), with $g$ decreasing on $(y_1,y_0)$ and increasing on $(y_0,y_2)$, $y_0=y_G$, $y_1=y_{min}$, $y_2=y_{max}$. (5) *One-dimensional minimization*: with $x=f^{-1}(y)$ in two branches (66) and $\mathscr G(y)=g(x_T,y_T,y)-f^{-1}(y)$, A wins iff $\mathscr G(y)\ge0\ \forall y\in[y_1,y_2]$. Three cases by the sign of $y_0$: Case 1 ($y_0>0$): $\dot{\mathscr G}(y)<0$ on $[y_1,y_0]$ (67), $\dot{\mathscr G}(y_0)<0<\dot{\mathscr G}(y_2)$ (68), so a unique $y^*\in[y_0,y_2]$ with $\dot{\mathscr G}(y^*)=0$ (69) is the minimum; Case 2 ($y_0<0$): mirror image (70), unique $y^*\in[y_0,y_1]$; Case 3 ($y_0=0$): minimum at $y^*=y_0=0$ (71). $\mathscr G(y^*)=0$ is the barrier $\mathcal B$. (6) *Polar reformulation* (p. 10, following [24]): $\mathcal A^r=\{\mathbf z=\mathbf x_T+P\mathbf e\mid P=\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma^2}\big(\gamma(\mathbf x_T-\mathbf x_A)^\top\mathbf e-r+\sqrt{(\gamma(\mathbf x_T-\mathbf x_A)^\top\mathbf e-r)^2+(1-\gamma^2)(\|\mathbf x_T-\mathbf x_A\|_2^2-r^2)}\big),\ \mathbf e=(\cos\phi,\sin\phi)^\top,\ \phi\in[0,2\pi]\}$ (72), $P\ge0$; capture point $x^*=x_T+P(\phi)\cos\phi^*,\ y^*=y_T+P(\phi)\sin\phi^*$ (73); slopes $\frac{dy^*}{dx^*}=\frac{\dot P\sin\phi+P\cos\phi}{\dot P\cos\phi-P\sin\phi}$ (74), $\frac{dx^*}{dy^*}=\frac{\dot P\cos\phi-P\sin\phi}{\dot P\sin\phi+P\cos\phi}$ (75); domain ends $\phi_{min}$ (76), $\phi_0$ (77), $\phi_{max}$ (78); the stationarity $\dot{\mathscr G}(y)=0$ becomes (79) — the same equation as (51) with $y_T$ in place of $y$ — and the minimum value is $\mathscr G(y)_{min}=x_T+P(\phi^*)\cos\phi^*-f^{-1}(y_T+P(\phi^*)\sin\phi^*)$ (80). (7) Substituting $x_T\to x$, $y_T\to y$ in (72) and (80) yields (81)=(50): the Game of Kind is solved. $\square$
- **Decisive intermediate result**: the equivalence "curve (10) right of curve (56) $\Leftrightarrow$ $\mathscr G(y)\ge0$ on $[y_1,y_2]$" plus the three-case uniqueness of the minimizer $y^*$ (67)–(71), which converts a 2-D geometric separation problem into the scalar equation (51) whose solution maps through (50) to the barrier point; traversing all $\phi$ traces the whole barrier (Remark 6, p. 10).
- **Local/global**: the barrier is global (divides the whole plane for the given A/D geometry), and is a curve in the Target's initial-position plane (a cross-section of the 6-D state space, as only $\mathbf x_T$ varies). Analytic up to solving (51) numerically; $\dot f^{-1}$ and $f^{-1}$ have no printed closed form.
- **Physical meaning**: the barrier is the locus of Target positions for which, under optimal play, A simultaneously (i) gets within $r$ of T and (ii) is intercepted by D (both events at $t_f$) — a draw; right of it A wins, left of it T–D wins. Because the capture point on the barrier lies on the limiting intercept curve (56) rather than on a straight line, A's winning zone for $r>0$ strictly contains the $r=0$ zone obtained by the line/circle construction of [8] (Figs. 4, 11).
- **Proof gaps / print defects** (all verified against the rendered PDF): (i) *Stationarity mismatch*: the first-order condition for $\min_{[y_1,y_2]}\mathscr G(y)=g-f^{-1}$ is $\frac{dx^*/d\phi}{dy^*/d\phi}=\dot f^{-1}(y^*)$ (first power, i.e. slope of oval = slope of (56) using (74)–(75)), but (51)/(79) as printed carry $(\dot P\sin\phi+\cos\phi P)^2$ in the denominator — i.e. an extra factor $1/(dy^*/d\phi)$. Both (51) and (79) print the squared form consistently, so either the derivation of $\dot{\mathscr G}$ contains a slip or a nonstandard convention is used; the barrier actually plotted in Fig. 11 cannot disambiguate this. (ii) (76)–(78) define $\phi_{min},\phi_0,\phi_{max}$ by conditions that are internally inconsistent with (61)–(64): (76) requires $\frac{dy^*}{dx^*}=0$ and $\frac{dy^*}{dx^*}>0$ simultaneously, and (78) uses $\frac{dx^*}{dy^*}=0$ for $\phi_{max}$ although the $y$-max point $K$ has a horizontal tangent (so $\frac{dy^*}{dx^*}=0$ expected). (iii) The claim that D intercepts A exactly on the $Y$-axis (the A–D perpendicular bisector) is the standard equal-speed Apollonius argument but is invoked via Fig. 5 and [25, Lemma 1] without a formal proof; the semipermeability of the resulting barrier (that neither side can force crossing) is argued geometrically ("T can choose any point in the left area... as the target point", p. 8) rather than proven. (iv) The three positional relations are misprinted as "intersect, tangent, and intersect" (p. 8; first should be "separate/detached").

## 6. Simulation and Experiments
**Scenario** (p. 10–11): land warfare with autonomous vehicles — the attacking unmanned vehicle (tank or self-destructing vehicle) tries to capture the target vehicle; the defending vehicle tries to intercept the attacker. Common initial configuration for all five examples: $A=(5,0)$, $D=(-5,0)$, $T=(3.5,3)$, capture radius $r=1$, speed ratio $\gamma=0.7$ ($V_A=V_D=1$ implied). **Parameter table (as printed)**: those five values are the only parameters given; integration scheme, step size, solver for (14)–(15)/(51), software platform and hardware are **UNREPORTED**. Baseline strategies: the pure-pursuit (PP) guidance laws of [13]; proposed: optimal strategy (OS) (11)–(13). Metrics: winner and cost $V$ from (16); capture point $I$ from (14)–(15).
- **Example 1** (all OS; Fig. 7, p. 12): straight-line trajectories; $I=(-0.598,\,5.042)$; $V=0.1775$. Validates Theorem 1's equilibrium value in $\mathcal Z_c$ (A captures T un-intercepted).
- **Example 2** (A,T = OS; D = PP; Fig. 8): curved trajectories; $I=(0.9905,\,6.9863)$; $V=3.6037$. A Defender's unilateral deviation raises the cost (3.6037 > 0.1775) — D is worse off, consistent with the minimization role.
- **Example 3** (T = OS; A,D = PP; Fig. 9): $I=(0.9115,\,6.7105)$; $V=3.5192$. Compared with Example 2, when A deviates its own cost drops (3.5192 < 3.6037), consistent with A's maximization role.
- **Example 4** (D,T = OS; A = PP; Fig. 10): A loses even though $x_0\in\mathcal Z_c$; "D and T can rendezvous at the gray dots $I=(-0.8045,\,4.1344)$"; winner Defender, $V=0$ (Table II). Validates Remark 3's warning that unilateral sub-optimality of A can flip the outcome.
- **Table II (p. 11, as printed)** — rows: Example 1 OS/OS/OS → Attacker, $V=0.1775$; Example 2 OS/PP/OS → Attacker, $V=3.6037$; Example 3 PP/PP/OS → Attacker, $V=3.5192$; Example 4 PP/OS/OS → Defender, $V=0$. (OS = optimal strategy, PP = pure pursuit; columns = Attacker's, Defender's, Target's strategy.)
- **Example 5** (Game of Kind; Fig. 11, p. 12): Theorem 2's barrier drawn for the common configuration; the region right of the barrier is $\mathcal Z_c$, left is $\mathcal Z_t$; against it, the barrier of [8] is recomputed "under the same initial configuration (capture radius $r=0$)". **What is not validated**: no Monte-Carlo/robustness runs, no measurement of computational cost of solving (14)–(15)/(51), no comparison of trajectories against [19]'s capture-radius strategies, and no hardware experiments.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
**Author-stated**: conclusion (p. 12) frames the work as "a theoretical analysis for vehicle rescue and vehicle engagement issues, and lays the foundation for our future research on multi-player TAD games"; Remark 6 concedes no closed form for $f^{-1}$. No other limitations are acknowledged.
**Critical observations (with page/eq.)**:
1. **Typos in load-bearing equations** (all verified on rendered pages): (11)'s denominator $\|\mathbf z^*(\mathbf x)-\mathbf x_T\|_2$ contradicts the proof's (33) ($\|\mathbf x_A-\mathbf z\|_2$); (21) prints "sin $\delta^*$" for the Target's sine; (42) prints "$\gamma\lambda\sin\zeta^*$" for $\gamma\lambda_{y_T}\sin\zeta^*$; (26)/(30)/(37)–(38) print $\|\mathbf x_{A_f}-\mathbf x_{T_f}\|_2$-type denominators where differentiation of (22) requires $\|\mathbf x_{D_f}-\mathbf x_{T_f}\|_2$-type ones (the solved costates (41) are consistent with the corrected form); Theorem 2's (51)/(79) squared denominator vs. the first-power first-order condition (Sec. 5.3 gap (i)); (76)/(78) internal inconsistency (Sec. 5.3 gap (ii)).
2. **Existence vs. uniqueness of $\mathbf z^*(\mathbf x)$** (Remark 5, p. 7): only existence of a root of (14)&(15) is shown (IVT); uniqueness and $C^1$ dependence on $\mathbf x$ — required for $V$ (16) to be a genuine $C^1$ value *function* and for (11)–(13) to be well-defined feedbacks — is not proven (no non-degeneracy check of the implicit-function step).
3. **The Game-of-Kind argument is geometric, not analytic**: the barrier follows from a tangency/separation analysis of two curves (p. 8–10); semipermeability of $\mathcal B$ and the claim that D holds the $Y$-axis interception point are asserted through Fig. 5 and [25, Lemma 1], not proven; no formal statement that states on $\mathcal B$ end in a draw under (11)–(13) (asserted in Sec. II-B, p. 3).
4. **Restrictive regime**: equal speeds $V_A=V_D$ (the whole barrier construction rests on the A–D Apollonius "circle" being the straight $Y$-axis) and $\gamma<1$; $\gamma\ge1$ and $V_A\ne V_D$ are open, as is a non-zero capture radius for D (Remark 2 explicitly fixes D's radius at zero).
5. **Containment claim not quantified**: "the winning zone of A obtained in this paper is larger" (p. 2) is supported only by Figs. 4/11; no area metric, no measure of how the gain scales with $r$.
6. **Simulation reporting**: platform, numerical solvers for the two scalar equations, and step sizes unreported; single-run examples only; Table II's Example 4 entry "$V=0$" with winner "Defender" is consistent with the terminal T–D separation vanishing, but the phrase "D and T can rendezvous" (p. 11) is imprecise about what happens to A.
7. **Editorial defects**: in-text pointer "In Table II, we provide a list of symbols" (p. 2) should read Table I (the symbol table); Example 4's heading says "while T adopts a pure pursuit guidance law" where A is meant (p. 11); Fig. 11 legend cites "[5]" instead of "[8]" (p. 12); ref. [25]'s author list "P. Waszy, M. Pachterz and K. Pham" appears garbled as printed.

## 8. Citation Value
This paper is the capture-radius ($r>0$) extension of the equal-speed Garcia–Casbeer–Pachter TAD lineage: it keeps the [8]/[15] two-sided-PMP-plus-HJI machinery but replaces the Apollonius circle by the Apollonius oval (Lemma 1, (9)–(10), from Yan–Shi–Zhong's lifeline work [24]) and replaces the straight-line barrier test of [13]/[18] by a tangency test between the A–T oval and the Attacker's ultimate interception curve $y=\pm\sqrt{r^2-x^2}(1-x_A/x)$ on $x\in[-r,0)$ (56), yielding both a Game-of-Degree Nash feedback (Theorem 1) and a Game-of-Kind barrier (Theorem 2) for $V_A=V_D$, $V_T<V_A$, A-radius $r>0$, D-radius $0$. In the knowledge base it should be cited as (a) the reference point for **non-zero capture radius in TAD/active-defense games** alongside [19] (prescribed A guidance, no barrier) and [14] (faster A, no Game of Degree), (b) an example of **barrier construction by curve-vs-curve tangency** rather than line-vs-circle intersection, and (c) evidence that a lethal radius enlarges the Attacker's winning region (Figs. 4, 11). Suggested citation sentence: "Shi et al. solved the Game of Degree and constructed the barrier for the equal-speed TAD game with a non-zero Attacker capture radius, showing via an ultimate-interception-curve/Apollonius-oval tangency analysis that the point-capture winning zone of [8] is a strict subset of the finite-radius one [P080]." Caution: several printed equations contain typos ((11), (21), (26)/(30)/(37)–(38), (42), (51)/(79) squared-denominator issue), so the corrected forms documented in Secs. 5.2–5.3 and 7 should be used when quoting formulas.
