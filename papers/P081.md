## 2. Research Background
The target-attacker-defender (TAD) game — an Attacker $A$ tries to capture a Target $T$ while a Defender $D$, aided by $T$, tries to intercept $A$ — was introduced for maneuvering-target guidance [11 of the paper] and popularized by Boyell's torpedo-defense study [10] and the "lady-bandits-bodyguard" model [12]; Garcia et al. then built the canonical equal-speed solution chain ([13] JGCD 2015, [14] Automatica 2017, [1] IEEE TAC 2020, [15] JOTA 2021), in which [1] constructs **barrier surfaces** solving the game of kind. Speed-differential variants were solved by Liang et al. ([2] Automatica 2019 cooperative target defense; [16] TAC 2021 role switching; [3] TAC 2022 territorial-constrained defender) and Garcia's cooperative counter to Liang's switching strategy [17]. All of these works assume **point capture (zero capture radius)**. The authors argue — echoing [18] (cooperative missile guidance for active defense, TAES) — that real missiles/aircraft have a blast (capture) radius, so both $A$ and $D$ should carry non-zero radii, which changes the capture curve from an **Apollonius circle to an Apollonius oval** [20] and, crucially, lets $A$ destroy $T$ **outside** the $D$–$A$ interception curve. This paper is a geometric game-of-kind analysis of that non-zero-radius, fast-defender TAD game.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
- **Problem** (Sec. 2.1): three simple-motion players in the plane, $\dot{\mathbf{x}}_A = v_A\mathbf{u}_A$, $\dot{\mathbf{x}}_D = v_D\mathbf{u}_D$, $\dot{\mathbf{x}}_T = v_T\mathbf{u}_T$ (eq. (1)), constant speeds with $v_D > v_A > v_T$, speed ratios $\sigma = v_A/v_D < 1$ and $\varrho = v_T/v_A < 1$, headings $(\delta,\vartheta,\varphi)$ as controls; non-zero capture radii $R_A$ (of $A$) and $R_D$ (of $D$). Termination in $\mathcal{L}_1 = \{\mathbf{x}\,|\,\|\mathbf{x}_A-\mathbf{x}_T\|_2 \le R_A,\ \|\mathbf{x}_A-\mathbf{x}_D\|_2 > R_D\}$ (eq. (2), $A$ wins), $\mathcal{L}_2 = \{\mathbf{x}\,|\,\|\mathbf{x}_A-\mathbf{x}_D\|_2 \le R_D,\ \|\mathbf{x}_A-\mathbf{x}_T\|_2 > R_A\}$ (eq. (3), $D$–$T$ team wins), or $\mathcal{L}_3 = \{\mathbf{x}\,|\,\|\mathbf{x}_A-\mathbf{x}_D\|_2 = R_D,\ \|\mathbf{x}_A-\mathbf{x}_T\|_2 = R_A\}$ (eq. (4), draw). Game of kind: decide, for each initial state, whether the outcome is $\mathcal{L}_1$, $\mathcal{L}_2$ or $\mathcal{L}_3$; the boundary between the two winning regions is the **barrier surface** to be determined.
- **[Authors' claim]** (i) Existing TAD games with a slower attacker ([2], [19], plus [1,13,15,16,17,3]) model capture as point capture with zero radius; in reality both $A$ and $D$ (missiles) may have blast radii (p. 1, citing [18] on significance). (ii) With non-zero radius the capture curve shifts "from an Apollonius circle to an Apollonius oval, making the center distance approach unsuitable" (p. 1–2, Sec. 2.2). (iii) "with a zero capture radius, $A$ can only capture $T$ if the Apollonius circles of $A$-$D$ and $A$-$T$ are inclusive and inscribed. If they intersect (Fig. 1), $T$ can escape within $\angle M'TN'$" — but with non-zero radius "$A$'s firepower extends beyond the Apollonius oval of $A$-$D$ ... Even if $T$ escapes this oval, it may still be destroyed by $A$ due to the overlap of $A$-$T$ and $A$-$D$ ovals" (p. 2). Hence "existing methods based on center-to-center distance [are] inadequate for solving the game of kind problem in TAD games" (Abstract). (iv) The barrier can no longer be obtained from the tangency of two circles via center distances as in [2,19]; one needs the new farthest-firepower-curve construction (Contribution 2, p. 2).
- **[Verified from the paper's own derivations]** The mechanism of (iii) is internally consistent with the paper's own construction: the firepower curve (eq. (9)) lies strictly outside the $A$-$D$ interception oval $B_1$ (eq. (5)) — each point is $I \in B_1$ pushed outward along the ray from $A$ by $R_A$ — so $T$ can indeed be destroyed at points beyond $B_1$ (confirmed by the derivation and Figs. 1, 4). The prior-work assumptions (i) are standard and consistent with [2,19] as archived in this library (both are zero-capture-radius fast-defender TAD games whose barriers are tangency conditions between Apollonius **circles**). Claims about [18], [20], [21] are not independently verifiable from this PDF.
- **Gap filled**: first archive entry that solves the **game of kind** (winning-region partition + barrier) for the TAD game with **both** $A$ and $D$ having non-zero capture radii under $v_D > v_A > v_T$, by replacing circle-tangency algebra with (a) a constructed "farthest firepower coverage curve" for $A$ and (b) a tangency (min-of-difference-function) analysis between two irregular ovals. Note the paper solves **only the game of kind** — no payoff, no game of degree, no optimal-control expressions inside $A$'s winning region (unlike [1], which also derived optimal strategies).

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Modeling**: TAD game of kind with non-zero capture radii for **both** $A$ and $D$ and a faster defender ($v_D > v_A > v_T$), terminal sets $\mathcal{L}_1$–$\mathcal{L}_3$ (eqs. (2)–(4)) — extending the zero-radius settings of [2,19] (p. 2, Contribution 1).
2. **Theory — new geometric object**: **Definition 1 + Lemma 3**: the *Attacker's farthest firepower coverage curve* $G(x,y)=0$ (eq. (9)) — the locus of points $C$ where $A$ can still destroy $T$ exactly when $D$ closes to within $R_D$ of $A$; constructed from the $A$-$D$ Apollonius-oval interception curve $B_1$ (eq. (5)) inflated radially by $R_A$ (pp. 3–4).
3. **Algorithm**: **Algorithm 1** (p. 4) — decides whether a given initial state belongs to $A$'s or the $D$–$T$ team's winning region (or the barrier) from the sign of $G(\mathbf{x}_T)$ and the number of intersections of the $A$-$T$ capture curve $F(x,y)=0$ (eq. (7)) with the firepower curve $G(x,y)=0$ (pp. 4, Remark 1).
4. **Theory — main result**: **Theorem 1** (pp. 4–6) — the barrier surface is characterized implicitly by $\mathcal{F}(x^*)-\mathcal{K}(x^*)=0$ (eq. (11)) where $x^*$ is the unique stationary point of the difference function $\mathcal{M}(x)=\mathcal{F}(x)-\mathcal{K}(x)$ (eqs. (12), (22)), i.e., the locus of $T$'s positions at which the $A$-$T$ capture oval is **tangent** to $A$'s farthest firepower coverage curve; proved via strict convexity + implicit-function theorem + intermediate value theorem in three cases (eqs. (23)–(29)).
5. **Experiment (numerical)**: three barrier-curve comparisons (Figs. 7–9) quantifying, qualitatively, how each player's capture radius reshapes the winning regions relative to the zero-radius baselines of [2,19] (Sec. 4, pp. 6–8).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results (exhaustive)

### 5.0 Dynamics, information structure, termination (Sec. 2.1, pp. 2, eqs. (1)–(4))
- **Dynamics (eq. (1))**: planar simple motion $\dot{\mathbf{x}}_A = v_A\mathbf{u}_A$, $\dot{\mathbf{x}}_D = v_D\mathbf{u}_D$, $\dot{\mathbf{x}}_T = v_T\mathbf{u}_T$ with $\mathbf{x}_i=(x_i,y_i)$, unit controls $\mathbf{u}_A=(\cos\delta,\sin\delta)$, $\mathbf{u}_D=(\cos\vartheta,\sin\vartheta)$, $\mathbf{u}_T=(\cos\varphi,\sin\varphi)$; speeds constant with the standing assumption $v_D > v_A > v_T$; $\sigma = v_A/v_D < 1$, $\varrho = v_T/v_A < 1$; capture radii $R_A, R_D > 0$.
- **Termination (eqs. (2)–(4))**: $\mathcal{L}_1$ ($A$ captures $T$, $A$ not yet intercepted: $\|\mathbf{x}_A-\mathbf{x}_T\|_2 \le R_A$ **and** $\|\mathbf{x}_A-\mathbf{x}_D\|_2 > R_D$); $\mathcal{L}_2$ ($D$ intercepts first); $\mathcal{L}_3$ (simultaneous, both equalities — draw). The three-way question "will the state first arrive at $\mathcal{L}_1$, $\mathcal{L}_2$, or $\mathcal{L}_3$" defines the game of kind; $A$'s (resp. $D$–$T$'s) winning region = states from which $A$ (resp. the team) can guarantee victory; the separating boundary is the barrier surface (p. 2).
- **Information structure**: not explicitly discussed; the construction and Lemma 1 imply perfect state information and open-loop straight-line optimal play (heading angles constant under optimal play).
- **Implicit assumptions (unstated)**: initial states outside all capture discs ($\|\mathbf{x}_A-\mathbf{x}_T\|_2 > R_A$, $\|\mathbf{x}_A-\mathbf{x}_D\|_2 > R_D$); in the normalized frame of Sec. 3.1, $A$ at the origin with $D$ on the negative $x$-axis and $x_D + R_D < 0$ (else the $x$-range formula below degenerates).

### 5.1 Preliminary lemmas and the two Apollonius ovals (Sec. 2.2, p. 3, eqs. (5)–(8))
- **Lemma 1 ([19])**: "In the TAD differential game (1), $T$, $A$, and $D$ follow straight-line paths under optimal play, with their controls remaining constant." Imported from the zero-radius fast-defender game [19]; its transfer to the non-zero-radius setting is assumed, not re-proven (see §7).
- **$D$-pursues-$A$ interaction (eqs. (5)–(6))**: for $\mathbf{z}=(x,y)$ any plane point, the capture curve (barrier) of the $D$–$A$ pair and $A$'s escape region are
  $$\mathcal{B}_1 = \{\mathbf{x}\,|\,\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}_A\|_2 = \sigma(\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}_D\|_2 - R_D)\},\qquad \mathcal{T}_1 = \{\mathbf{x}\,|\,\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}_A\|_2 < \sigma(\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}_D\|_2 - R_D)\}.$$
- **$A$-pursues-$T$ interaction (eqs. (7)–(8))**: capture curve and $T$'s escape region
  $$\mathcal{B}_2 = \{\mathbf{x}\,|\,\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}_T\|_2 = \varrho(\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}_A\|_2 - R_A)\},\qquad \mathcal{T}_2 = \{\mathbf{x}\,|\,\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}_T\|_2 < \varrho(\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}_A\|_2 - R_A)\}.$$
- Per [20]: with non-zero radius these curves are **Apollonius ovals** (Cartesian ovals); for zero radius they reduce to the Apollonius circles of [2,19]. Time-to-reach reading: on $\mathcal{B}_1$ ($\mathcal{B}_2$) the two vehicles arrive simultaneously, $D$'s (resp. $A$'s) arrival meaning closing to within $R_D$ (resp. destroying at range $R_A$).
- **Lemma 2 ([21])**: "The escape area $\mathcal{T}_1$ and $\mathcal{T}_2$ are strictly convex." (Cited from the lifeline game paper; reused in Theorem 1's proof as strict convexity of the interception and capture curves.)
- **Motivating example (Sec. 2.2, Fig. 3)**: $A$–$T$ pair only, with $T$'s slower speed ratio $0.6$ and $R_A=1$; the printed text says "$A$ is at $(0,2)$, $T$ is at $(2,2)$" but the plotted figure shows $A$ at $(2,0)$, $T$ at $(2,2)$ (the text coordinate is a typo). Zero-radius barrier (black) vs non-zero-radius barrier (blue): the red area is the additional winning region gained by $A$ from its capture radius — the paper's quantitative motivation for studying non-zero radii.

### 5.2 The Attacker's farthest firepower coverage curve (Sec. 3.1, pp. 3–4, eqs. (9)–(10))
- **Definition 1 (p. 3)**: "When the attacker destroys the target at $C(x^*,y^*)$ and is subsequently intercepted by the defender at $I(x_I,y_I)$, a point on the oval (5), $C$ represents the boundary of the Attacker's farthest firepower. The collection of all such points $C(x^*,y^*)$ [is] the Attacker's farthest firepower coverage curve." So: $I$ ranges over the $A$-$D$ interception oval $\mathcal{B}_1$; $C$ is the co-located destruction point.
- **Lemma 3 (p. 3, eq. (9))**: *Given the initial positions and non-zero capture radii of $A$ and $D$, the farthest firepower coverage curve of $A$ is*, in the frame with $A$ at the origin and $\overrightarrow{DA}$ along the positive $x$-axis ($D=(x_D,0)$):
  $$G(x,y) = \sqrt{\Big(x-\tfrac{R_A x}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2}}-x_D\Big)^{2} + \Big(y-\tfrac{R_A y}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2}}\Big)^{2}} - R_D - \frac{1}{\mu}\sqrt{\Big(x-\tfrac{R_A x}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2}}\Big)^{2} + \Big(y-\tfrac{R_A y}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2}}\Big)^{2}} \tag{9}$$
  (rendered image of p. 3 confirms $-R_D$ sits **outside** the first radical and the coefficient is printed as $1/\mu$; see proof gap below on $\mu$ vs $\sigma$).
- **Proof (pp. 3–4)**: by symmetry of the $A$-$D$ oval about the $x$-axis, restrict to the upper half plane. The interception point $I$ lies on $\mathcal{B}_1$ while $T$ is destroyed at $C$ with $\|C-I\| = R_A$ and (eq. (10)) $\sqrt{(x_C-x_I)^2+(y_C-y_I)^2}=R_A$, $\ y_C/x_C = y_I/x_I = (y_C-y_I)/(x_C-x_I)$ — i.e., $A$ (origin), $I$, $C$ are collinear, justified "based on Lemma 1 in [20]" (the collinear triple is written $A, A', C$; $A'$ is only defined in Fig. 4, never in the text). Substituting $x_C \to x$, $y_C \to y$ into the collinearity + oval membership gives (9).
- **[Verification, my derivation — not in the paper]**: writing $\hat{\mathbf{z}} = \mathbf{z}/\|\mathbf{z}\|$ and $I(\mathbf{z}) = \mathbf{z} - R_A\hat{\mathbf{z}}$ (the point one radius $R_A$ inward from $\mathbf{z}$ toward $A$), the zero set of (9) is exactly $\{I(\mathbf{z}) \in \mathcal{B}_1\}$ — i.e., the firepower curve is the **radial outward offset of the $A$-$D$ Apollonius oval by $R_A$**: in polar form about $A$, $\rho_{\mathrm{fp}}(\theta) = \rho_{\mathcal{B}_1}(\theta) + R_A$. For this zero set to equal $\mathcal{B}_1$'s offset, the coefficient in (9) must be $1/\sigma$ (from (5): $\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{x}_D\|_2 - R_D = \tfrac{1}{\sigma}\|\mathbf{w}\|_2$ on $\mathcal{B}_1$), **not** $1/\mu$.
- **Timing semantics (my reconstruction; the paper does not spell this out)**: $A$ heading along a ray meets $I \in \mathcal{B}_1$ exactly when $D$ closes to within $R_D$ of $I$; at that instant a target at $C = I + R_A\hat{\mathbf{z}}$ is exactly at $A$'s destruction range $\Rightarrow$ simultaneous $\mathcal{L}_3$-type ending. A target position strictly inside the curve on that ray is destroyed strictly before interception ($\mathcal{L}_1$); strictly outside, never ($\mathcal{L}_2$).
- **Domain (used in Theorem 1's proof, p. 5)**: setting $y=0$ in (5), the interception oval spans $x \in \left[\tfrac{\sigma(x_D+R_D)}{1+\sigma},\ \tfrac{\sigma(x_D+R_D)}{\sigma-1}\right]$ (endpoints on the $x$-axis; correct for $x_D+R_D<0$), so the firepower curve (9) spans $x \in \left[\tfrac{\sigma(x_D+R_D)}{1+\sigma}-R_A,\ \tfrac{\sigma(x_D+R_D)}{\sigma-1}+R_A\right]$ — the oval's range extended by $\pm R_A$.

### 5.3 Algorithm 1 — winning-region membership test (Sec. 3.1, p. 4, Remark 1)
- **Algorithm 1** (inputs: $\mathbf{x}_A,\mathbf{x}_D,\mathbf{x}_T$, "velocity ratio $\sigma$ between $A$ and $D$, velocity ratio $\mu$ between $T$ and $A$, capture radius $r$ of $A$, and capture radius $R$ of $D$" — note the notation drift $\mu, r, R$ vs. the $\varrho, R_A, R_D$ of Sec. 2.1):
  1. compute the capture curve $F(x,y)=0$ of $A$-$T$ per (7) and the firepower curve $G(x,y)=0$ per (9);
  2. **if** $G(x_T,y_T) \ge 0$ → state is in the $D$–$T$ winning area (Remark 1: "$G(x_T,y_T)\ge 0$ means that the initial position of $T$ is on or outside the farthest firepower coverage curve of $A$" — $T$ can escape outright);
  3. **elseif** $F(x,y)=0$ and $G(x,y)=0$ have **two** distinct solutions → $D$–$T$ winning area (the two ovals intersect: $T$ has an escaping heading, cf. Fig. 6);
  4. **elseif** they have **one** solution → current state is **on the barrier curve** (tangency, Fig. 4);
  5. **else** (no intersection) → $A$'s winning area (capture oval strictly inside the firepower region, Fig. 5).
- **Geometric logic (Remark 1 + Sec. 3.1)**: $A$ wins iff $T$'s escape oval $\mathcal{B}_2$-interior $\mathcal{T}_2$ cannot reach beyond the firepower curve before $A$'s capture; $T$ escapes iff the capture curve crosses the firepower curve; tangency = draw ($\mathcal{L}_3$) = barrier.
- **Proof gap (sign inconsistency, verified against renders of p. 3–4)**: with the printed $G$ of (9) (and $\mu$ read as $\sigma$), $G > 0$ holds **inside** the firepower curve (at $\mathbf{z}$ near $A$: $G = \|\mathbf{z}'-\mathbf{x}_D\|_2 - R_D - \tfrac{1}{\sigma}\|\mathbf{z}'\|_2 > 0$ for the interior point $\mathbf{z}' = I(\mathbf{z})$; numerical check with the simulation parameters gives $G(1.5,0.5) \approx +1.93$ for a state where $A$ obviously wins) — contradicting Algorithm 1 step 3 and Remark 1, which treat $G \ge 0$ as "on or outside". Either the inequalities in Algorithm 1/Remark 1 must be flipped, or (9) should be negated; as printed, the formula and the algorithm are mutually inconsistent. The qualitative logic itself (inside/outside/intersect/tangent) is coherent.

### 5.4 Theorem 1 — barrier surface (Sec. 3.2, pp. 4–6, eqs. (11)–(29))
- **Theorem 1 (p. 4)**: *Given the initial positions of $A$ and $D$, speed ratios, and $A$'s capture radius, the barrier surface that divides $A$'s winning region from the $D$–$T$ team's can be defined as*
  $$\mathcal{F}(x^*) - \mathcal{K}(x^*) = 0 \tag{11}$$
  *where $x^*$ (function of $(x_T,y_T)$) is the unique solution of*
  $$\mathcal{F}'(x) - \mathcal{K}'(x) = 0. \tag{12}$$
  (Note: the stated hypotheses omit $R_D$, yet the firepower curve (9) depends on it; the proof and Figs. 7–9 use both radii.)
- **Proof, part 1 (curve reduction to functions, p. 5)**: the interception oval (5) is symmetric about the $x$-axis, hence so is (9); only the half-plane of $T$'s initial position needs analysis (mirror image otherwise). Only $T$ **inside** the firepower curve is interesting (outside ⇒ no barrier, $D$–$T$ wins). Three positional relations between the firepower curve (9) and the capture curve (7) are possible: "inward deviation" ($A$ wins, Fig. 5 — printed as "infidelity", a typo), "intersection" ($D$–$T$ wins, Fig. 6), "inward tangent" (draw, Fig. 4). Because both curves are irregular, the relations are converted to an **extremum problem of a difference function**: min $>0$ / min $<0$ ⇔ separation / intersection; min $=0$ ⇔ tangency. Setting $y=0$ gives the oval (5)'s $x$-range $\left[\tfrac{\sigma(x_D+R_D)}{1+\sigma}, \tfrac{\sigma(x_D+R_D)}{\sigma-1}\right]$ and (9)'s range extended by $\pm R_A$; strict convexity (Lemma 2 + "easily deduce[d]" for (9)) plus endpoint vertical tangents ("slope at the left endpoint is $-\infty$, at the right endpoint $+\infty$" — sign convention sloppy for the upper branch, where they are $+\infty$/$-\infty$) yield a **unique $x_0$** with zero slope of curve (9); since $\mathrm{d}y/\mathrm{d}x = -G_x/G_y$ and $G_y \ne 0$ on the open interval (correct: $G_y=0$ only at the left/right extremes), the implicit function theorem gives the functional form of the firepower curve
  $$y = \mathcal{F}(x),\quad x \in \Big(\tfrac{\sigma(x_D+R_D)}{1+\sigma}-R_A,\ \tfrac{\sigma(x_D+R_D)}{\sigma-1}+R_A\Big). \tag{13}$$
- **Proof, part 2 (potential function and extremal points of the capture oval, eqs. (14)–(20), p. 5)**: introduce
  $$\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{z}) = \|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}_T\|_2 - \varrho(\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}_A\|_2 - R_A), \tag{14}$$
  with $\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{z})<0 \Leftrightarrow \mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{T}_2$, $\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{z})=0 \Leftrightarrow \mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{B}_2$ (eq. (15)) and gradient (eq. (16))
  $$\mathcal{S}_x(\mathbf{z}) = \tfrac{x-x_T}{\|\mathbf{x}_T-\mathbf{z}\|_2} - \varrho\tfrac{x-x_A}{\|\mathbf{x}_A-\mathbf{z}\|_2},\qquad \mathcal{S}_y(\mathbf{z}) = \tfrac{y-y_T}{\|\mathbf{x}_T-\mathbf{z}\|_2} - \varrho\tfrac{y-y_A}{\|\mathbf{x}_A-\mathbf{z}\|_2}.$$
  The $x$-extremal points of the oval (7) are characterized as $G(x_{\min},y_G) = \{\mathcal{S}=0,\ \mathcal{S}_x=0,\ \mathcal{S}_y<0\}$ (eq. (17)) and $H(x_{\max},y_H) = \{\mathcal{S}=0,\ \mathcal{S}_x=0,\ \mathcal{S}_y>0\}$ (eq. (18)); the $y$-extremal points as $J(x_J,y_{\min}) = \{\mathcal{S}=0,\ \mathcal{S}_x<0,\ \mathcal{S}_y=0\}$ (eq. (19)) and $K(x_k,y_{\max}) = \{\mathcal{S}=0,\ \mathcal{S}_x>0,\ \mathcal{S}_y=0\}$ (eq. (20)). **These four characterizations systematically swap $\mathcal{S}_x$ and $\mathcal{S}_y$**: at an $x$-extremal point of a level set the tangent is vertical, so the gradient is horizontal ($\mathcal{S}_y = 0$), and at a $y$-extremal point $\mathcal{S}_x = 0$ — exactly the opposite of (17)–(20) (verified against the render; a global typo, since the subsequent monotonicity reasoning uses the correct geometric facts). Only the upper arc $\widehat{GHK}$ of (7) is analyzed ("the point on curve (7) with the smallest distance from $A$'s farthest firepower coverage curve must be above the straight line $GH$" — asserted, not proven), giving by the implicit function theorem
  $$y = \mathcal{K}(x),\quad x\in(x_{\min},x_{\max}). \tag{21}$$
- **Proof, part 3 (difference function and the three cases, eqs. (22)–(28), pp. 5–6)**: define
  $$\mathcal{M}(x) = \mathcal{F}(x) - \mathcal{K}(x),\quad x\in(x_{\min},x_{\max}). \tag{22}$$
  $\min\mathcal{M} > 0$ ⇒ curve (9) always above curve (7) ⇒ separation ⇒ $A$ wins; $\min \mathcal{M} < 0$ ⇒ intersection ⇒ $T$ escapes; $\min\mathcal{M} = 0$ ⇒ tangency ⇒ draw ⇒ barrier. The monotonicity of $\mathcal{F}$ changes at $x_0$ (the firepower curve's apex abscissa), giving three cases on the position of $x_0$ relative to $(x_{\min},x_{\max})$:
  - **Case 1 ($x_0 < x_{\min}$)**: $\mathcal{F}'(x)<0$ on $(x_{\min},x_{\max})$; with $\mathcal{K}'(x)>0$ on $(x_{\min},x_k)$, $\mathcal{M}'<0$ there; on $(x_k,x_{\max})$ "based on monotonicity" $\mathcal{M}'(x_k)<0$, $\mathcal{M}'(x_{\max})>0$ (eq. (23)); by the intermediate value theorem there is $x^*\in(x_k,x_{\max})$ with $\mathcal{M}'(x^*)=0$ (eq. (24)) — the minimum of $\mathcal{M}$.
  - **Case 2 ($x_{\max} < x_0$)**: $\mathcal{F}'(x)>0$ on $(x_{\min},x_{\max})$; $\mathcal{K}'(x)<0$ on $(x_k,x_{\max})$ so $\mathcal{M}'>0$ there; on $(x_{\min},x_k)$, $\mathcal{M}'(x_k)>0$, $\mathcal{M}'(x_{\min})<0$ (eq. (25)); IVT gives $x^*\in(x_{\min},x_k)$ with $\mathcal{M}'(x^*)=0$ (eq. (26)).
  - **Case 3 ($x_{\min}<x_0<x_{\max}$)**: "based on monotonicity, we can obtain $\mathcal{M}'(x_{\min})>0$, $\mathcal{M}'(x_{\max})<0$ (eq. (27)); therefore, based on (25) [sic — should be (27)] and the intermediate value theorem, there exists $x^*\in(x_{\min},x_k)$ that satisfies $\mathcal{M}'(x^*)=0$ (eq. (28))". **The printed inequalities of (27) are sign-reversed**: at $x_{\min}$ the upper branch of the capture oval leaves with slope $+\infty$ so $\mathcal{M}'(x_{\min}^+) = -\infty < 0$, and at $x_{\max}$, $\mathcal{M}'(x_{\max}^-) = +\infty > 0$; the corrected signs still give a stationary point by IVT, but in $(x_{\min},x_{\max})$, not specifically in $(x_{\min},x_k)$.
- **Conclusion of the proof (p. 6)**: $\mathcal{M}$ "has a unique $x^*$ extremum in the interval $(x_{\min},x_{\max})$, which is the minimum value"; $\mathcal{M}(x^*)>0$ / $<0$ correspond to $A$'s and the $D$–$T$ team's winning areas, and the barrier surface is determined by
  $$\mathcal{F}(x^*) - \mathcal{K}(x^*) = 0 \tag{29}$$
  (identical to (11)). **Remark 2**: explicit closed forms of $\mathcal{F},\mathcal{K}$ are never needed — by the implicit function theorem (13) and (21) exist for any given parameter set.
- **Decisive intermediate objects**: eq. (22) ($\mathcal{M}$ = difference of the two branch functions) and eqs. (12)/(24)/(26)/(28) (stationarity $\mathcal{F}'=\mathcal{K}'$); the barrier condition itself is (11)/(29).
- **Scope/locality**: fully analytic-implicit; the barrier is a **locus of $T$ positions** obtained by moving $\mathbf{x}_T$ until (11)–(12) hold ("by fixing $A$ and $D$'s positions and adjusting $T$'s location ... the point set where the capture curve of $A$-$T$ is tangent to $A$'s farthest firepower coverage curve", p. 4); no closed-form parametrization, and the resulting curve is symmetric about the $A$-$D$ line by construction.
- **Physical meaning**: the barrier is the set of target positions from which the target can exactly (and only exactly) reach the boundary of the attacker's effective firepower region at the moment of the defender's interception — the natural non-zero-radius replacement of the "two Apollonius circles tangent" condition of [2,19].
- **Proof gaps (all verified against page renders)**:
  1. *$\mu$ vs $\sigma$ in (9)*: the coefficient must be $1/\sigma$ (firepower curve derives from the $A$-$D$ oval (5)); as printed it is $1/\mu$, while $\mu$ elsewhere ("velocity ratio $\mu$ between $T$ and $A$", Algorithm 1 line 1) denotes $\varrho$ — a wrong ratio would make the constructed curve simply wrong; charitable reading: typo for $\sigma$.
  2. *Sign inconsistency* between (9) and Algorithm 1/Remark 1 (detailed in §5.3).
  3. *Uniqueness of $x^*$*: the IVT argument proves **existence** of a stationary point of $\mathcal{M}$; uniqueness ("a unique $x^*$ extremum") is asserted from strict convexity of the two curves, but the difference of two strictly convex functions need not be quasiconvex — no rigorous uniqueness proof is given (the endpoint-derivative limits only pin the outermost sign change).
  4. *Swapped partials* in (17)–(20) (see above) and reversed endpoint signs in (27); cross-reference "(25)" in Case 3 should read "(27)"; eq. (29) duplicates (11).
  5. *Upper-arc reduction*: the claim that the closest point of $\mathcal{B}_2$ to the firepower curve lies on $\widehat{GHK}$ (above chord $GH$) is asserted without proof; similarly, the comparison is meaningful only if $(x_{\min},x_{\max}) \subseteq \mathcal{F}$'s domain (13) — an unstated assumption (a capture oval poking out sideways past $\mathcal{F}$'s $x$-range would escape the $\mathcal{M}$-analysis, although Algorithm 1's full-curve intersection test would still detect it).
  6. *Convexity of the radial offset*: "According to the construction of the farthest firepower coverage curve of $A$ in Lemma 3, we can easily deduce that curve (9) is also strictly convex" — no proof; a radial offset of a convex oval about an interior point is not convex in general, so this step (which underwrites the unique apex $x_0$ and the case analysis) is unproven.
  7. *Game-theoretic sufficiency*: no strategy construction or verification argument shows that the derived regions are exactly the winning regions under optimal play by all three players (Lemma 1's straight-line optimality is imported from the zero-radius game [19]); the argument is a geometric composition of the static oval $\mathcal{T}_2$ with the static firepower region, i.e., a well-motivated heuristic reduction, as in [2,19] but with less algebraic closed-form support.
  8. *Notation collisions*: $F$ = capture curve function in Algorithm 1 but $y=\mathcal{F}(x)$ = **firepower** branch in (13); $G$ = firepower curve function in (9)/Algorithm 1 but also the name of the point $G(x_{\min},y_G)$ in (17); $K$ = point $K(x_k,y_{\max})$ in (20) vs $\mathcal{K}(x)$ in (21); Algorithm 1's "$\mu, r, R$" vs Sec. 2.1's "$\varrho, R_A, R_D$".

### 5.5 Summary of strategy/value content
No payoff function, no capture-time value, no game of degree, and no feedback strategies are produced anywhere: the paper's entire formal output is the qualitative partition (game of kind) via Definition 1, Lemma 3 (eq. (9)), Algorithm 1, and Theorem 1 (eqs. (11)–(12)). The equilibrium-strategy content is limited to Lemma 1's imported statement that optimal play is straight-line, and the figures depict straight-line trajectories (Figs. 4–6) consistent with the draw/win/lose configurations.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
- **Author-stated**: the paper treats only the single-defender single-attacker planar case with $v_D>v_A>v_T$; "This work also sets the stage for future research on multi-player TAD games" (Conclusion, p. 8). No other limitations are acknowledged.
- **Game-theoretic rigor (critical)**: the winning-region claims rest on Lemma 1 (straight-line optimal play) imported from the zero-radius fast-defender game [19] and on a static composition of $T$'s escape oval with the firepower region; there is no strategy synthesis, no verification/sufficiency argument (Isaacs-type or otherwise), and no game-of-degree solution inside $A$'s region — the barrier is derived only as the tangency locus (11)–(12).
- **Internal inconsistencies (all verified against renders)**: (i) $1/\mu$ vs $1/\sigma$ in eq. (9); (ii) sign of $G$ vs Algorithm 1 step 3 / Remark 1 (§5.3); (iii) swapped $\mathcal{S}_x/\mathcal{S}_y$ in eqs. (17)–(20); (iv) reversed endpoint inequalities in eq. (27) and the "(25)" cross-reference in Case 3; (v) Theorem 1's hypotheses omit $R_D$; (vi) Sec. 2.2 example text "$A$ is at $(0,2)$" vs Fig. 3 showing $A$ at $(2,0)$; (vii) Fig. 8/Fig. 9 captions contradict their own legends/scenario texts; (viii) garbled word "infidelity" (Fig. 5 caption and p. 5) for the containment/separation relation; (ix) Algorithm 1's $\mu, r, R$ vs. $\varrho, R_A, R_D$, and the ambiguous $R=0.7$/$r=0.8$ assignment in Sec. 4.
- **Unproven analytic steps**: uniqueness of the stationary point $x^*$ of $\mathcal{M}$; strict convexity of the radial-offset firepower curve (9); the upper-arc ($\widehat{GHK}$) reduction; containment $(x_{\min},x_{\max})\subseteq\mathcal{F}$'s domain (§5.4 gaps 3, 5, 6).
- **Modeling scope**: planar, simple motion, constant speeds, perfect information, point-mass engagement kinematics; no turn-rate/first-order actuation limits (unlike [14]); capture modeled as isotropic discs ("capture net" narrative notwithstanding); the draw set $\mathcal{L}_3$ (eq. (4)) is measure-zero and its equality conditions are treated only implicitly through tangency.
- **Validation depth**: three barrier plots, no quantitative metric (winning-region areas never computed), no baseline beyond the zero-radius special case, no reproducibility artifacts (platform, code, solver tolerances, and the numerical procedure for sweeping $\mathbf{x}_T$ all unreported); reference [20]'s author names are printed as "P. Waszy, M. Pachterz" (garbled) and [17]'s journal as "Atuomatica" (sic).

## 8. Citation Value
This paper is the archive's reference for **barrier-surface determination in the TAD game with non-zero capture radii and a faster defender** — the natural "next step" citation after the zero-radius fast-defender barrier papers [P-references: Garcia IET 2017; Liang Automatica 2019] and the active-defense TAC line [Garcia TAC 2020]. Its reusable assets are (i) the explicit **farthest-firepower-coverage curve** (eq. (9)) — a radial $R_A$-offset of the $A$-$D$ Apollonius oval capturing the fact that the attacker can kill at range beyond its own interception point, (ii) the **oval-vs-oval tangency** template (difference function $\mathcal{M}$ (22), stationarity (12), tangency condition (11)) that replaces circle-tangency algebra when capture curves are Apollonius ovals, and (iii) the qualitative winning-region monotonicity results (larger $R_A$ grows $A$'s region; larger $R_D$ grows the $D$–$T$ region; Figs. 7–9). Use it in Introductions to justify studying capture-radius effects in target defense, and in Related Work as the geometric game-of-kind branch (as opposed to HJI/quantitative approaches); cite with care given the sign/notation inconsistencies documented in §5 and §7. Suggested citation sentences: "Shi et al. [P081] addressed the game of kind for a TAD game in which both attacker and defender have non-zero capture radii ($v_D>v_A>v_T$): they constructed the attacker's farthest firepower coverage curve as a radial offset of the attacker–defender Apollonius oval and derived the barrier surface as the locus of target positions where the attacker–target capture oval is tangent to this curve." Or, as a gap statement: "Existing geometric TAD barriers determine tangency between Apollonius circles via center distances [2,19]; once capture radii are non-zero the capture curves become Apollonius ovals and the attacker's lethal envelope extends beyond the interception curve, so the center-distance approach fails [P081]."
