## 2. Research Background
Pursuit-evasion (PE) differential games, since Isaacs' framework, are conventionally attacked with the Hamilton–Jacobi–Isaacs (HJI) equation and retrogressive path equations, which are hard to solve and non-intuitive for nonlinear dynamics. A classical trade-off device is to give the two players heterogeneous kinematics: the pursuer is faster but less agile, the evader slower but more maneuverable — a configuration the authors argue is bioinspired by real predator–prey locomotion (cheetahs, birds, fish shoals; refs. [16]–[18], [27]). Isaacs' "isotropic rocket" (damped double-integrator pursuer vs single-integrator evader) is the canonical instance of this configuration, but its classical solution via the Hamiltonian and retrogressive path equations is, by the authors' reading, incomplete, and later revisits (Bernhard [28]; Lewin & Olsder [29]) also covered only parts of the game. Isochron-based (Apollonius-circle-type) geometry has been used almost exclusively for simple kinematics; for complex kinematics the isochrone intersections become irregular and lack theoretical analysis (e.g., the double-integrator TAD game of [37]).

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
**Problem**: two-player PE game in the plane: pursuer P with damped double integrator (damping µ, acceleration bound u_pm, capture radius ϵ ≥ 0), evader E with single integrator (speed bound v_em). P minimizes capture time, E maximizes it; payoff is the capture time; full-state feedback information structure. The authors want (i) an intuitive geometric method replacing HJI/retrogressive-path machinery, (ii) optimal strategies + value where capture is guaranteed (game of degree), and (iii) a decisive game-of-kind characterization (capture vs evasion) over all parameter/state configurations.

**Gap claims and verification status**:
- [Authors' claim, p. 2]: "as far as the authors are aware, for the PE game where the pursuer is with damped double integrator dynamics and the evader is with single integrator dynamics, a full solution is still absent in the literature." [Not independently re-verified against primary sources, but consistent with the paper's own survey: Isaacs [2] treated the isotropic rocket only partially ("the problem was not completely solved, where some validations and cases lacked full investigations", p. 2); Bernhard [28] "was also not finished completely, as the author said" (p. 2); related works [7], [29], [30] treat surveillance-evasion or multi-pursuer variants without full analysis.]
- [Authors' claim, p. 2]: "the isochron-based method has not been used to study PE games of players with heterogeneous dynamics." [Verified as a fair reading of their survey: isochrones were used for simple motions (Apollonius circle [31]–[35]), for steered-vehicle minimum-time control [36], and for double-integrator TAD without theoretical analysis of intersections [37].]
- [Authors' claim, p. 3, Remark 4]: existing works [2], [7], [30] "mainly considered these two cases (Cases A-1∼A-2)", i.e., only 2 of the 16 configurations they enumerate. [Consistent with their Table I discussion; not re-verified against those primary texts.]
- The genuine technical gap: with damped double-integrator pursuer isochrones, the intersection curve Π(x,y)=0 (eq. (18)) is transcendental, the evader's dominant region can be multiply connected or concave (occlusion regions, hollows, Fig. 2), so the simple-motion Apollonius argument (star-shaped absolutely dominant region, closed-form capture point) breaks down; the paper's contribution is a geometric apparatus that survives this irregularity.

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Method (modeling/analysis)**: an isochron-based framework for heterogeneous kinematics. Both players' optimal isochrones are circles (Lemma 2); the moving-center, growing-radius extended pursuer isochrones and the static-center evader isochrones generate an intersection curve whose external/internal tangency (ET-IT) events structure the whole game. Three independent factors are isolated: motion capabilities (u_pm/µ vs v_em), capture capability (ϵ via the superiority function F(τ), eq. (21)), and initial states (through τ̃, the first internal-tangency time).
2. **Taxonomy (theory)**: a complete division of the game into **16 cases** (Table I, p. 7; labels A-1…A-8, B-1…B-6, C-1, C-2) by superiority class × position of τ̃ relative to the roots τ1, τ2 of F(τ) × geometric features of the intersection (dominant region / disjoint regions / occlusion region(s) / hollow / infinite curve), each illustrated in Fig. 5(a)–(p).
3. **Optimal strategies and value (theory/algorithm)**: closed-form target points f_e, f_p (Lemma 3, eq. (23)–(25)), feedback Nash pursuit/evasion strategies (Theorem 1, eq. (26)–(27)), value V = τ* = min root of Q(τ), validated by direct substitution into the HJI equation (Appendix I-C, eq. (34)–(39)); extension to partially superior and weaker pursuers (Theorem 2, Corollaries 2–3) and a numerical root solver (Algorithm 1, MATLAB `vpasolve`-based).
4. **Evasion (theory/algorithm)**: escape-direction set Θ_e for the never-tangent / infinite-dominant-region cases, and a **Tangent-Evasion Strategy** (Theorem 3, Algorithm 2) keeping the inter-player distance ≥ ϵ forever even against a faster, partially superior pursuer; plus a target-oriented pursuit heuristic (Algorithm 3).
5. **Game of kind (theory)**: necessary-and-sufficient-style characterizations of guaranteed capture (Theorem 5) and guaranteed evasion (Theorem 6) in terms of internal tangency at finite τ̃ and superiority for all τ < τ̃.
6. **Experiments + biology link**: three simulation scenarios (including one pursuer vs five evaders), showing Nash optimality (Table II) and emergent swerve/sideway prey behaviors explained as optimality (Fig. 10(d)–(f)).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results (exhaustive)

### 5.0 Dynamics, information structure, cost, termination (pp. 3, Sec. II)
- Kinematics eq. (1): ẋe = v_ex, ẏe = v_ey; ẋp = v_px, ẏp = v_py; v̇_px = −µv_px + u_p cos θ_p, v̇_py = −µv_py + u_p sin θ_p. Controls: u_e = (v_ex, v_ey) ∈ U_e = {v : ‖v‖ ∈ [0, v_em]}; u_p = (u_p, θ_p) ∈ U_p = [0, u_pm] × [0, 2π). P's velocity is not explicitly bounded; steady-state speed is u_pm/µ. State x = (xe, ye, xp, yp, vpx, vpy)ᵀ. Damping µ > 0 shared by both velocity components (heterogeneity = order + agility asymmetry).
- Termination: M(x) = (xp−xe)² + (yp−ye)² − ϵ²; terminal set M(x) ≤ 0 (capture radius ϵ ≥ 0).
- Cost eq. (2): J = t_f = ∫₀^{t_f} 1 dt; saddle value min_{u_p} max_{u_e} J. Hamiltonian eq. (3): H = λᵀg_e(xe,u_e) + γᵀg_p(xp,u_p) + 1 with λ ∈ R², γ ∈ R⁴. HJI eq. (4): −∂V/∂t = (∂V/∂x)ᵀ(g_e, g_p) + 1 = 0. Nash condition eq. (5): J(u*_p, u_e) ≤ J(u*_p, u*_e) ≤ J(u_p, u*_e). Information: perfect state feedback; all final strategies are feedback laws.

### 5.1 Lemma 1 (p. 3; proof Appendix I-A, p. 14, eq. (29)–(32))
**Statement**: for game (1)(2), the optimal P strategy is maximum acceleration along a constant direction (u*_p = u_pm, θ*_p constant); the optimal E strategy is maximum speed along a constant direction (‖v*_e‖ = v_em, constant components).
**Assumptions**: dynamics (1), cost (2), capture not yet occurred.
**Proof approach**: (1) write H explicitly; (2) costate ODEs (29): λ̇1 = λ̇2 = γ̇1 = γ̇2 = 0, γ̇3 = −γ1 + µγ3, γ̇4 = −γ2 + µγ4 ⇒ λ1, λ2, γ1, γ2 constant, γ3 = γ1/µ + k3 e^{µt}, γ4 = γ2/µ + k4 e^{µt}; (3) transversality γ3(t_f) = ν ∂M/∂v_px = 0, γ4(t_f) = ν ∂M/∂v_py = 0 (M independent of velocities) ⇒ γ1/γ2 = k3/k4 = γ3/γ4 ≜ k34; (4) E's maximization is the linear program (31) max_{u_e} λ1 v_ex + λ2 v_ey over the convex U_e ⇒ optimum on the boundary, constant direction [printed "‖u*_e‖ = u_pm" on p. 14 is a typo for v_em]; (5) ∂H/∂u_p = 0 yields (32): u*_p = ½u_pm(1 − sign(γ3 cos θ_p + γ4 sin θ_p)), tan θ*_p = γ4/γ3 = 1/k34 ⇒ θ*_p constant; (6) second-order condition ∂²H/∂θ_p² = −γ3u_p cos θ_p − γ4u_p sin θ_p > 0 forces u*_p = u_pm.
**Decisive relation**: γ3/γ4 = k34 constant, eq. (32). **Local/global**: global, analytic. **Meaning**: bang-bang max-effort play; justifies reducing the reachable-set (isochrone) computation to max-effort trajectories. **Extensibility**: the constant-direction property is what makes isochrones exactly circular for any τ.

### 5.2 Definition 1 (p. 3–4) and Lemma 2 (p. 4, eq. (6)–(15)) — the isochrones
**Definition 1**: the isochron of time τ ∈ (0, ∞) of player i is the set of points i can reach at τ under the different (optimal-direction) strategies; the cluster over τ is the player's isochrones. Remark 1: τ is a predicted horizon, distinct from real time t.
**Lemma 2 statement**: under optimal strategies, E's isochron of time τ is the circle I_e(τ) = {(x,y): Γ_e = 0} with Γ_e(x,y,τ) = (x−x_ce)² + (y−y_ce)² − R_e(τ)² (eq. (6)–(7)), center c_e = (xe, ye)ᵀ (10), radius R_e = v_em τ (11); P's isochron is I_p(τ) with Γ_p(x,y,τ) = (x−x_cp)² + (y−y_cp)² − R_p(τ)² (eq. (8)–(9)), center c_p(τ) = (x_p − (v_px/µ)(e^{−µτ}−1), y_p − (v_py/µ)(e^{−µτ}−1))ᵀ (12), radius R_p(τ) = (u_pm/µ)(τ + (e^{−µτ}−1)/µ) (13).
**Proof approach**: (1) E at max speed ⇒ concentric circles; (2) integrate P's ODE at u_pm with constant θ*_p ⇒ x_p(t) = (u_pm cos θ*_p/µ)t + (k_px1/µ)e^{−µt} + k_px2 (eq. (14)); (3) eliminate the constants via x_p(0), v_p(0) to get cos θ*_p and sin θ*_p as functions of (x, y, t) (eq. (15)); (4) impose cos²θ*_p + sin²θ*_p = 1 ⇒ Γ_p = 0.
**Decisive equation**: (15). **Local/global**: global, analytic, exact (no linearization). **Meaning** (Remark 2): all isochrones are circles; P's centers move with τ (memory of current velocity), E's do not — the heterogeneity is entirely in the center motion and the radius growth law. **Definition 2 (p. 4, eq. (16)–(17))**: extended isochron I^ϵ_p(τ): same center c_p(τ), radius R^ϵ_p = R_p + ϵ — the reach-**or-touch** set, exploiting the capture radius.

### 5.3 Intersection Π, dominant region, target-point program (p. 4–5, eq. (18)–(20))
- Eliminating τ between Γ^ϵ_p(x,y,τ) = 0 and Γ_e(x,y,τ) = 0 (τ = √((x−xe)²+(y−ye)²)/v_em for points on I_e) gives the intersection curve Π(x,y) = 0, eq. (18): (x − x_p + (v_px/µ)(e^{−µ√((x−xe)²+(y−ye)²)/v_em} − 1))² + (y − y_p + (v_py/µ)(e^{−µ√((x−xe)²+(y−ye)²)/v_em} − 1))² − ((u_pm/µ)(√((x−xe)²+(y−ye)²)/v_em + (e^{−µ√((x−xe)²+(y−ye)²)/v_em} − 1)/µ) + ϵ)² = 0 — transcendental in (x, y).
- **Definition 3 (p. 5)**: E's dominant region D = {(x,y): Π(x,y) > 0}; justified by checking Π(x_e, y_e) = (x_p−xe)² + (y_p−ye)² − ϵ² > 0 pre-capture, so e ∈ D. If D is star-shaped at e (e z ⊂ D ∀z ∈ D) it is an **absolutely dominant region** D_a. With double-integrator P, D_a may not exist: points can be reached by P twice (first arrival earliest), so D can be multiply connected or concave — encircling **occlusion regions** O (reachable by P first, Fig. 2(b)) or exhibiting **hollows** H (concave boundary parts, Fig. 2(c)).
- E's target-point program eq. (19): f_e = argmax_{q ∈ I} ‖q − e‖ s.t. Π(x,y) > 0 for all (x,y) on segment qe (safety along the path). P's program eq. (20): f_p on I_p(τ′) at distance ϵ from f_e, where f_e ∈ I_e(τ′).
- **ET–IT generation principle (p. 5, Fig. 3)**: regarding the isochrones as circles with moving center/growing radius, each arc of the intersection I is born at an external tangency (ET) or internal tangency (IT) event and dies at an ET or IT event ("ET-IT process"); arcs ending in IT encircle a dominant region of one player, and the internally tangent point is the farthest safe point — hence the candidate target point.

### 5.4 Definition 4 and Proposition 1 (p. 6, eq. (21)–(22)) — superiority
**Definition 4**: P is superior to E at time τ if R_p(τ) + ϵ ≥ R_e(τ); absolutely superior if this holds ∀τ > 0; partially superior if it holds for some τ′ and fails for some τ″. (P can never be absolutely inferior, see proof.)
**Proposition 1 statement**: superiority depends only on (ϵ, µ, v_em, u_pm) — not on states x. P is absolutely superior iff either (1) u_pm/µ > v_em and ϵ ≥ v_em/µ − (1/µ)(u_pm/µ − v_em)·log(1 − µv_em/u_pm)  (printed eq. (22)); or (2) u_pm/µ = v_em and ϵ ≥ u_pm/µ². Otherwise P is partially superior.
**Proof approach** (function F(τ) = R_p + ϵ − R_e, eq. (21)): (1) F(0) = ϵ > 0 ⇒ P never absolutely inferior; (2) case u_pm/µ < v_em: F′(τ) = (u_pm/µ)(1 − e^{−µτ}) − v_em < 0 and lim_{τ→∞}F < 0 ⇒ exactly one root τ1, superior on [0, τ1] (Fig. 4(a)); (3) case u_pm/µ > v_em: F′ = 0 has the unique solution τ_m = −(1/µ)log(1 − µv_em/u_pm), the global minimum; absolute superiority ⟺ F(τ_m) ≥ 0, which the authors write as (22); otherwise two roots τ1 < τ2 with inferiority window (τ1, τ2) (Fig. 4(d)(e)); (4) case u_pm/µ = v_em: F′ < 0, lim_{τ→∞}F = ϵ − u_pm/µ² ⇒ absolutely superior iff ϵ ≥ u_pm/µ², else one root τ1 (Fig. 4(b)(c)).
**Decisive equation**: F(τ_m) ≥ 0 ⟺ (22). **Local/global**: global in τ, parameter-only (state-free) — the key structural insight.
**Erratum flag (verified numerically against the paper's own data)**: with the printed minus sign, (22) at Scenario I parameters (p. 12: u_pm = 5, v_em = 2.5, µ = 1, ϵ = 0.7771) gives threshold v_em/µ − (1/µ)(u_pm/µ − v_em)log(1 − µv_em/u_pm) = 2.5 + 2.5·0.6931 = 4.233 ≫ ϵ, which would classify P as partially superior — contradicting the paper's own statement "F(τ_m) = 0.01 > 0, thus P is absolutely superior". Direct computation gives F(τ) = 2.5τ + 5e^{−τ} − 4.2229, minimum F(τ_m) = F(0.6931) = 0.01. The consistent threshold is **ϵ ≥ v_em/µ + (1/µ)(u_pm/µ − v_em)·log(1 − µv_em/u_pm)** (log argument in (0,1), so the added term is negative), equivalently ϵ ≥ v_em/µ − (1/µ)(u_pm/µ − v_em)·log(u_pm/(u_pm − µv_em)); the Scenario II value F(τ_m) = −0.2348 (p. 12) is likewise reproduced only by this corrected form. So printed (22) (and the same formula under Fig. 4(d)/(e)) carries a sign typo on the log term. The classification logic itself is correct.
**Meaning**: a single scalar test on the capture radius decides whether P can eventually fence E in from anywhere; e.g., in the equal steady-speed case P needs ϵ ≥ u_pm/µ². **Extensibility**: any kinematics with closed-form R_p(τ), c_p(τ) admits the same F-test.

### 5.6 Lemma 3 (p. 7, eq. (23)–(25)) — closed-form target points (absolutely superior, stronger P; Cases A-1/A-2)
**Statement**: if P is absolutely superior, E's target point f_e = (x_fe, y_fe)ᵀ and P's target point f_p = (x_fp, y_fp)ᵀ are
x_fe = [(R_p(τ̃)+ϵ)x_ce − R_e(τ̃)x_cp(τ̃)] / (R_p(τ̃)+ϵ−R_e(τ̃)), y_fe likewise (23);
x_fp = [R_p(τ̃)x_ce − (R_e(τ̃)−ϵ)x_cp(τ̃)] / (R_p(τ̃)+ϵ−R_e(τ̃)), y_fp likewise (24);
where τ̃ is a root of Q(τ) = ‖c_p(τ) − c_e(τ)‖² − (R_p(τ)+ϵ−R_e(τ))² = 0 (25).
**Assumptions**: absolute superiority (R^ϵ_p(τ) ≥ R_e(τ) ∀τ), so at the first IT the evader isochron is inside the extended pursuer isochron and I₀ encircles D_a.
**Proof approach**: (1) verify the first internally tangent point satisfies the three target-point conditions of (19) — on I, the segment ef_e meets I only at f_e, and f_e is the farthest such point (via the ET–IT analysis: the intersection arc I₀ ends at f_e on E's largest isochron); (2) collinearity of c_p, c_e, f_e at internal tangency (Fig. 6): x_fe − x_cp = ((R_p+ϵ)/R_e)(x_fe − x_ce) ⇒ solve the two linear equations for (23); (3) f_p lies on segment c_p f_e with ‖f_p f_e‖ = ϵ: x_fe − x_fp = (ϵ/(R_p+ϵ))(x_fe − x_cp) ⇒ (24); (4) internal tangency ⟺ center distance equals radius difference ⇒ Q(τ̃) = 0.
**Decisive equation**: Q(τ) = 0 (25) — transcendental (contains e^{−µτ} inside squared norms), solved numerically.
**Local/global**: global; analytic up to one scalar root-find. **Meaning**: converts the nonlinear constrained program (19)–(20) into a 1-D root solve + two linear formula evaluations — the paper's headline "geometric approach avoiding nonlinear constrained optimization". **Extensibility**: any circular-isochrone game with monotone radius laws.

### 5.7 Lemma 4, Remark 3, Lemma 5, Lemma 6, Algorithm 1, Corollary 1 (pp. 8–9) — root structure and capture-time selection
- **Lemma 4 (p. 8)**: (i) Q(τ) has at least one root in [0, ∞) whenever u_pm/µ ≠ v_em [proof: continuity, Q(0) = (xe−xp)²+(ye−yp)²−ϵ² > 0 pre-capture, lim_{τ→∞}Q < 0]; (ii) a positive root τ₀ is an internal tangency if R^ϵ_p(τ₀) ≠ R_e(τ₀), and a coincidence of the two circles if R^ϵ_p(τ₀) = R_e(τ₀).
- **Remark 3 (p. 8)**: at coincidence, infinitely many "tangent points" exist; the natural choice is on the extended line pe (E directly away, P straight in) — the degenerate tail-chase configuration.
- **Lemma 5 (p. 8–9; proof Appendix I-B, p. 14, Fig. 12)**: if the intersection ends in IT at τ′₁ then Q′(τ′₁) < 0; if it starts from IT at τ′₂ then Q′(τ′₂) > 0; if the circles coincide at τ′₃ then Q′(τ′₃) = 0. [Proof: place f_e on the center line; for τ = τ′₁ + δ continuity puts I_e inside I^ϵ_p, forcing Q < 0; for τ = τ′₁ − δ, f_e outside I_e, Q > 0; a two-circle collinearity argument (c_p q_p = (R_p+ϵ)/R_e · c_e q_e) excludes the alternatives, so Q decreases through τ′₁.]
- **Lemma 6 (p. 9)**: if P is absolutely superior, Q has no multiple root, and its minimum (unique) root τ̃ satisfies Q′(τ̃) < 0. [Every root has Q′ ≠ 0 by Lemma 4(ii)+Lemma 5; Q(0) > 0 + continuity ⇒ decreasing at the first root.]
- **Algorithm 1 (p. 9, Solving-for-roots)**: recursive search of Q's roots on [T₀, T_M] using MATLAB `vpasolve`, splitting the interval at each found root; τ̃ = min T_s.
- **Corollary 1 (p. 9)**: the capture time τ* satisfies (I) R_p(τ*) + ϵ ≥ R_e(τ*) and (II) Q′(τ*) ≤ 0 [from Lemma 3 and Lemma 5; equalities hold in the coincidence case of Remark 3]. For absolutely superior P, (I) holds automatically and Lemma 6 gives (II), so τ̃ is the capture time.

### 5.8 Theorem 1 (p. 9, eq. (26)–(27); proof Appendix I-C, pp. 14–15, eq. (33)–(39)) — main result
**Statement**: for game (1)(2) with P absolutely superior and u_pm/µ > v_em (Cases A-1, A-2), the optimal feedback pursuit strategy is (26): u_p = u_pm, cos θ_p = (x_ce(τ*) − x_cp(τ*))/(R_p(τ*)+ϵ−R_e(τ*)), sin θ_p likewise; the optimal feedback evasion strategy is (27): v_ex = (x_ce(τ*) − x_cp(τ*))/(R_p(τ*)+ϵ−R_e(τ*))·v_em, v_ey likewise; τ* = τ̃ = min T_s from Algorithm 1; the value is V = τ*.
**Assumptions**: absolute superiority (R_p + ϵ − R_e > 0 always ⇒ denominators well defined); stronger P only to place the case in A-1/A-2.
**Proof approach**: (1) well-definedness + Corollary 1 conditions via Lemma 4/Lemma 6; (2) derive E's law: E runs at full speed toward f_e, and since ‖c_e − c_p‖ = R_p + ϵ − R_e at τ = τ* (Q = 0), v_ex = (d_cx/√(d_cx²+d_cy²))v_em = (d_cx/(R_p+ϵ−R_e))v_em (eq. (33) = (27)); (3) derive P's law from the isochrone parametrization (15): cos θ_p = (x_fp − x_cp)/R_p = (x_ce − x_cp)/(R_p+ϵ−R_e) — note both players aim along the **same** direction c_e − c_p (Remark 4: recovers Isaacs' isotropic-rocket result [2] by a different, intuitive route); (4) **HJI validation**: implicitly differentiate Q(V, x) = 0 w.r.t. all six state components; e.g. w.r.t. x_e (eq. (34)) with d_cx = x_e − x_p + (v_px/µ)(e^{−µV}−1) = x_ce − x_cp (35), d_cy (36), dR = (u_pm/µ)(V + (e^{−µV}−1)/µ) + ϵ − v_em V = R_p + ϵ − R_e (37), obtaining all six gradients (38): ∂V/∂x_e = d_cx/W, ∂V/∂y_e = d_cy/W, ∂V/∂x_p = −d_cx/W, ∂V/∂y_p = −d_cy/W, ∂V/∂v_px = ((e^{−µV}−1)/µ)·d_cx/W, ∂V/∂v_py likewise, where W = dR(u_pm/µ(1 − e^{−µV}) − v_em) + (d_cx v_px + d_cy v_py)e^{−µV} and W = −Q′(τ*) > 0 by Lemma 5 (strictly, W = −Q′/2; the paper states W = −Q′(τ*), a harmless factor), so all gradients exist; (5) substitute (26)(27)(38) into the HJI equation (4): Σ ∂V/∂x·ẋ + 1 = (1/W)[(v_em + ((e^{−µV}−1)/µ)u_pm)dR − (d_cx v_px + d_cy v_py)e^{−µV}] + 1 = 0 (eq. (39)), using v_em + (e^{−µV}−1)u_pm/µ = −(u_pm/µ(1−e^{−µV}) − v_em) and Q(V)=0 ⇒ ‖c_e−c_p‖ = dR; since V has no explicit t-dependence, ∂V/∂t = 0 and the HJI equation is satisfied.
**Decisive equation**: (39) = 0 (HJI verification) with the gradient denominator W = −Q′(τ*) > 0.
**Local/global**: global capture guarantee from any pre-capture state in the case; analytic except one scalar transcendental root-find; strategies are genuine state feedback (they depend on x through c_p, c_e and τ*(x), eq. (25)). **Physical meaning**: both players dash along the instantaneous Apollonius direction; if the initial gap is large the play converges to a tail-chase (Remark 5, refs. [39]–[42]). **Proof gap**: none found; the chain (34)→(38)→(39) was re-derived and is internally consistent. **Extensibility**: template for validating geometric value functions against HJI.

### 5.9 Theorem 2 and Corollary 2 (p. 10) — partially superior, τ̃ ≤ τ1 (Cases A-3; also B-4, C-1)
**Theorem 2**: if Q(τ) has a minimum root τ̃ < τ1 (P superior on the whole relevant window), the optimal strategies are again (26)(27) with τ* = τ̃ and V = τ̃.
**Proof approach**: (1) Condition (II) of Corollary 1: τ̃ < τ1 is the unique minimum root, Q′(τ̃) < 0; (2) the crux is persistence: under (26)(27) the HJI equation holds (Theorem 1's proof), hence **dτ̃/dt = −1** (the value decreases at unit rate) while τ1 depends only on parameters, **dτ1/dt = 0**; (3) therefore τ̃ < τ1 is invariant along the play, and F(τ) > 0 on (0, τ1) keeps Condition (I) (R_p + ϵ > R_e) true, so the strategies stay well defined.
**Decisive inequality**: dτ̃/dt = −1 < 0 = dτ1/dt. **Remark 6 (author's, p. 10)**: unlike Theorem 1, here the guarantee is not restart-capable — if P deviates from (26), E can steer x into another case and escape; the capture guarantee is strategy-dependent, a genuinely dynamic (not static) superiority.
**Corollary 2**: if τ̃ = τ1 (circles coincide at the tangency, Q′(τ̃) = 0, Remark 3), P can drive the game into τ̃ < τ1 merely by accelerating in E's current direction; once play starts, time-to-target shrinks so τ̃ < τ1.

### 5.10 Theorem 3 (p. 10) — Tangent-Evasion Strategy (Cases A-4, A-5: τ1 < τ̃ < τ2)
**Statement**: if P is partially superior and at least one occlusion region O exists for E, then E following the Tangent-Evasion Strategy (move along the tangent from e to an occlusion region, toward the farthest reachable intersection point q_s, Algorithm 2) keeps its distance from P no smaller than ϵ forever — i.e., avoids capture indefinitely against any P strategy.
**Proof approach**: (1) on the critical set Π(x_e, y_e) = 0 ⟺ (x_p−xe)² + (y_p−ye)² − ϵ² = 0 (capture imminent), consider Π̇(x_e, y_e) = 2[(x_p−xe)(v_px−v_ex) + (y_p−ye)(v_py−v_ey)]; (2) claim: E can make Π̇ ≥ 0 by steering along the tangent of the occlusion-region boundary, and this instantaneous rate is independent of P's current control input (u_p, θ_p) (it involves v_p, a state); (3) hence Π(x_e, y_e) ≥ 0 is an invariant barrier ⇒ distance ≥ ϵ; (4) conservatively E may play for radius ϵ̄ = ϵ + δ to avoid grazing the boundary.
**Decisive inequality**: Π̇(x_e, y_e) ≥ 0 at Π = 0. **Proof gap (my observation)**: step (2)–(3) is under-argued — Π̇ depends on v_p, which P controls over time, so "independent of P's strategy" holds only instantaneously; and the existence of a tangent direction achieving (p−e)·(v_p − v_e) ≥ 0 for every v_p is asserted, not proven (the tangent of the occlusion boundary need not a priori dominate an arbitrarily directed v_p in the inner-product sense). The conclusion is plausible (and simulation-supported, Fig. 10) but the barrier argument as printed is not airtight. Related to the distance-keeping strategy of [43].
**Local/global**: global safety (non-capture), no capture-time value claimed. **Meaning**: against a faster but partially superior pursuer, the evader survives by circling tangentially around occlusion regions — the origin of the observed swerve/sideway prey behavior.

### 5.11 Theorem 4 (p. 10–11) — case transition for τ̃ ≥ τ2 (Cases A-6…A-8)
**Statement**: in a tail-chase state, with P stronger and partially superior and τ̃ > τ2, under strategies (26)(27) the case converts to τ1 < τ̃ < τ2 "soon" (i.e., P's persistent optimal pursuit eventually hands the advantage window to E).
**Proof approach (informal, author-acknowledged)**: WLOG tail-chase along the x-axis ⇒ Q(τ*) = 0 becomes |x_ce − x_cp(τ*)| = |R_p(τ*) + ϵ − R_e(τ*)| (28); the tangency point lies on the extended line pe (Fig. 8); as the gap shrinks, τ̃ decreases toward τ2; at τ̃ = τ2 the circles coincide; assuming "inertia of movement" (E keeps direction, P keeps accelerating) for a small δ, τ̃(t+δ) < τ2 ⇒ case A-4/A-5. The authors state explicitly: "A more general theoretical proof still requires future work" (p. 11), and argue that for τ̃ > τ2 the distance is usually large so the play converges to tail-chase, validated by simulations.
**Decisive equation**: (28). **Proof gap**: the inertia assumption and the x-axis WLOG; transition claim for non-tail-chase configurations is simulation-supported only.

### 5.12 Weaker pursuer (Sec. IV-C, p. 11–12): escape set and the counterintuitive wins
- **Escape direction set (p. 11)**: Θ_e = {θ_e ∈ [0, 2π) : Π(e + k(cos θ_e, sin θ_e)) > 0 ∀k > 0} — directions along which every future E position stays in D. In Cases B-1, B-3 (Q has no root — isochrones never internally tangent, intersection an infinite curve splitting the plane into two dominance half-regions), B-5, B-6, C-2 (τ̃ > τ1 ⇒ the arc ending at τ̃ encircles a **dominant region of P**, leaving E an infinite region), E escapes with (v_em cos θ_e, v_em sin θ_e), θ_e ∈ Θ_e; P's best practical reply is θ_p = θ_e (shadowing, hoping for mistakes).
- **Corollary 3 (p. 12)**: if P is absolutely superior with u_pm/µ = v_em (Case B-2) and Q has a minimum root τ̃, the optimal strategies are (26)(27) with τ* = τ̃, V = τ*. [Proof: Lemma 6 ⇒ Q′(τ̃) < 0; Corollary 1 conditions hold; same analysis as Theorem 1.]
- **Pursuer wins while weaker (p. 12)**: in Cases B-4 and C-1 (τ̃ ≤ τ1) Theorem 2 and Corollary 2 apply verbatim — capture is guaranteed even though u_pm/µ ≤ v_em. This is the paper's "counterintuitive result": a slower, weaker pursuer with a sufficient capture radius ϵ still wins from favorable geometries, because superiority is judged at the tangency horizon τ̃, not at steady speed.

### 5.13 Theorem 5 and Theorem 6 (p. 12) — game of kind
- **Theorem 5 (capture guaranteed)**: capture can be guaranteed iff (1) the extended pursuer isochron and the evader isochron are internally tangent at some finite τ̃, and (2) P is superior for all τ < τ̃. [Proof by enumeration: exactly six cases of Table I satisfy both — A-1, A-2, B-2 (absolutely superior) and A-3, B-4, C-1 (superior on (0, τ1] with τ̃ ≤ τ1) — all covered by Theorem 1, Theorem 2, Corollary 3.]
- **Theorem 6 (evasion guaranteed)**: E avoids capture if either (1) the isochrones are never internally tangent (Cases B-1, B-3; use Θ_e directions), or (2) they are internally tangent at some finite τ̃ but P is inferior at some τ′ < τ̃ (eight cases: A-4…A-8, B-5, B-6, C-2; use Θ_e for B-5/B-6/C-2 and the Tangent-Evasion Strategy for A-4…A-8, with the ϵ̄ = ϵ + δ perturbation to strict safety).
- Together, Theorems 5–6 + the case-wise analysis claim a **complete solution** (game of kind for all parameter/state configurations; game of degree in all six capture cases).

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
**Author-stated**:
1. Theorem 4's transition argument is informal — "A more general theoretical proof still requires future work" (p. 11); the tail-chase case is typical, not general.
2. The Tangent-Evasion guarantee is critical-case (distance exactly ϵ) and is patched by the conservative radius ϵ̄ = ϵ + δ (p. 10) rather than a strict barrier proof.
3. Future work (p. 14): a general framework for multiple players with single/double integrator dynamics; the evader-to-pursuer **allocation** problem (only pairwise capture conditions are provided); extending the isochron method to other dynamic models.
4. Scenario III's multi-evader policy (capture in order of capture times) is a heuristic demonstration, not a theorem; each evader is assumed ignorant of the pursuer's strategy.

**Critical observations (with page/eq.)**:
1. **Sign inconsistency in printed eq. (22) (p. 6)**: as printed, the absolute-superiority threshold v_em/µ − (1/µ)(u_pm/µ − v_em)·log(1 − µv_em/u_pm) evaluates to 4.233 at the paper's own Scenario I parameters, contradicting the paper's classification "F(τ_m) = 0.01 > 0, thus P is absolutely superior" (p. 12). The correct identity, reproduced by both scenarios, is ϵ ≥ v_em/µ **+** (1/µ)(u_pm/µ − v_em)·log(1 − µv_em/u_pm) (log term negative), i.e., with log argument u_pm/(u_pm − µv_em). Same typo repeats in the Fig. 4(d)/(e) condition captions. The Proposition's logic and all downstream case assignments are unaffected once the corrected form is used.
2. **Typo in Lemma 1's proof (p. 14, after eq. (31))**: "‖u*_e‖ = u_pm" should read ‖u*_e‖ = v_em (U_e's boundary); the LP constraint in (31) is also misprinted as "‖u_e‖ ∈ U_e".
3. **Theorem 3's barrier step is not rigorous (p. 10)**: Π̇(x_e, y_e) involves v_p, which P controls along the trajectory, so "independent of P's strategy" is only instantaneous; the existence of an evasion direction with Π̇ ≥ 0 for every current v_p is asserted without proof. The invariance of Π(x_e, y_e) ≥ 0 — hence the theorem — rests on this gap (mitigated by simulations, Fig. 10).
4. **Static taxonomy vs dynamic switching**: the 16 cases classify a state snapshot; states migrate between cases during play (Corollary 2, Theorem 4 address only two transitions). A complete hybrid analysis of case-switching under non-optimal play is open; Remark 6 concedes the capture guarantee of Theorem 2 is conditional on P never deviating.
5. **Computational content of the "analytic" solution**: τ*(x) requires solving the transcendental Q(τ) = 0 (25) at every feedback instant (Algorithm 1, numerical `vpasolve`, no complexity/real-time analysis); the root-selection rule (Corollary 1) and the existence/uniqueness of Θ_e directions are constructive but not analyzed for degenerate geometries (tangencies of higher order, ϵ = 0 boundary where D_a arguments change).
6. **Model scope**: one shared damping µ; P's speed is unbounded explicitly (only the steady state u_pm/µ caps it, and transient overshoot toward u_pm/µ²(1/µ)... in fact initial speed can exceed it); E is omnidirectional with instant velocity — no actuation lag, no bounded acceleration for E; state feedback assumes perfect full-state information (no measurement noise/partial observability).
7. **HJI validation covers only the capture cases** (Theorem 1/2/Corollary 3 chain); for evasion cases no viscosity/verification argument is given that Θ_e or the Tangent-Evasion strategy is optimal in any value sense (they are feasibility/safety results, correctly framed as such).

## 8. Citation Value
**Paragraph role**: this is the definitive reference for (i) isochrone-based geometric solution methods extended beyond simple (single-integrator) kinematics to a second-order damped pursuer, (ii) the complete game-of-kind taxonomy (16 cases) for the damped-double-integrator vs single-integrator PE game — the modern, thorough treatment of Isaacs' isotropic rocket configuration, and (iii) optimality-based explanations of prey evasion behaviors (swerve/sideway, circling, tail-chase) and of the counterintuitive "weaker but winning" pursuer phenomenon mediated by capture radius ϵ. It is equally citable for its negative-space observations: isochrone intersections of heterogeneous players are transcendental and generate multiply connected dominant regions (occlusion regions, hollows), breaking naive Apollonius arguments.
**Suggested citation sentences**:
- "Li, Wang and Xie [P053] give a complete isochron-based solution to the pursuit–evasion game between a damped double-integrator pursuer and a single-integrator evader: closed-form target points, feedback Nash strategies and the value V = τ* (validated against the HJI equation) in all capture-guaranteed cases, and tangent-evasion strategies that let a slower evader survive a faster, partially superior pursuer indefinitely."
- "As shown in [P053], the intersection of the isochrones of heterogeneous players is generally transcendental and may partition the evader's dominant region into multiply connected sets with occlusion regions and hollows, so that the outcome is decided by the first internal-tangency time τ̃ relative to the roots of F(τ) = R_p(τ) + ϵ − R_e(τ) rather than by steady-speed comparison alone."
