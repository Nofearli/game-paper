## 2. Research Background
The target defense differential game abstracts counter-UAV scenarios in which an invader tries to enter a protected area while defenders try to capture it outside (Motivation citations: Michel & Holland 2018; Sathyamoorthy 2015). Since Isaacs' formulation of the HJI equation, analytical solutions coexist with numerical ones (level-set/fixed-point HJI solvers, nonlinear programming, ADP/RL), but numerical approaches suffer the curse of dimensionality, and RL/ADP handle this game awkwardly because its objective has no integral term (p. 1). Analytical work therefore leans on geometric constructs, chiefly the invader's dominance region: an Apollonius circle for zero capture range, a Cartesian oval (fourth-order curve) for non-zero capture range (Garcia & Bopardikar 2021). The specific game studied here — defenders moving freely in an unbounded plane around an arbitrary convex target — has long had a known geometric solution ("head toward the tangency point of the dominance-region boundary with the best target contour"), but that solution had only been justified rigorously in special cases; intuition without proof can fail, as shown historically by the two-evader tie-breaking subtlety (Breakwell & Hagedorn 1979) and by the multi-pursuer intuitive law being optimal on only part of the state space (Pachter et al. 2020).

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
**Problem.** Prove that the classical geometric feedback law and value for the multi-defender single-invader (MDSI) target defense game satisfy the Isaacs HJI equation, under the *general* assumptions: arbitrary convex C² target area (not just circles/lines/polygons), non-zero capture range r > 0, any number n of defenders, defenders faster than the invader, all players single-integrators with bounded speed in an unbounded 2-D plane.

**Gap** [authors' claim, p. 2]: the geometric solution of this game exists (Isaacs 1999; Fu & Liu 2020; Garcia, Casbeer & Pachter 2020) but has been proved only for special cases — linear target (Garcia, Von Moll, Casbeer & Pachter, CDC 2019), circular and polygonal targets (Pachter, Garcia & Casbeer, JGCD 2017), convex target with a *single* defender (Lee & Bakolas 2021, arXiv) — and all three proofs assumed **zero capture range**. [Verified from cited text: not independently re-verified; this characterization of the three references is the authors' own and is consistent with this paper's abstract/introduction. Their related-work survey of differential-game variants (attack-range games, slower-pursuer capture, perimeter defense, lifeline games, pp. 1–2) is likewise the authors' account.]

**Three technical challenges** claimed and resolved (p. 2): (i) the arbitrarily convex target φ has no closed form, so its partial derivatives cannot be written explicitly — unlike Lee & Bakolas (2021), no distance-to-target construction is used; (ii) the non-zero capture range makes ∂D_I a fourth-order curve, so the closed-form analytic representation of the critical intermediate variable (the tangency point p*) used in the prior proofs is unavailable here — and not needed; (iii) with multiple defenders the suggested value function is not differentiable everywhere, so continuity is proved and the HJI equation is satisfied in the **viscosity sense** (Crandall & Lions 1983).

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Theory (main)**: Theorem 2.1 — the geometric control law (2.7) and geometric value (2.8) solve the MDSI HJI equation (2.9a) with boundary conditions (2.9b)–(2.9d), under A1–A5, i.e., under strictly more general conditions (convex target of arbitrary shape, r > 0, arbitrary n) than all prior justifications [authors' claim].
2. **Modeling/proof technique**: a derivative-free-form approach — the tangency point p* is handled as an *implicitly defined* function of player positions via system (3.11) (Stationarity + boundary equation), differentiated implicitly (3.13), with all expressions organized in local orthonormal-style frames F_{α*}, F_{β*} aligned with the defender-to-p* and invader-to-p* directions (3.4)–(3.6); a determinant identity (3.20)/(B.5) collapses the value-gradient to the one-line form (3.21).
3. **Theory (structure)**: a complete degeneration classification of the MDSI game (Sections 4.2, 4.4): every MDSI state reduces either to an SDSI game (p* on a smooth segment of ∂D_I) or to a non-degenerate 2DSI game (p* at a vertex of ∂D_I); continuity of the value across switching cases gives the viscosity-solution property.
4. **Convexity lemma**: Lemma 2.1 / Appendix C — the Cartesian-oval dominance region D_I = ∩_j D_I^j is convex, proved by an explicit curvature sign computation (independent, reusable result).
5. **Experiment (simulation only)**: barrier construction for an ellipse and a superellipse target (Fig. 9) and 4-defender trajectory simulations demonstrating both degeneration modes (Fig. 10).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results (exhaustive)

### 5.0 Setting, dynamics, information, cost, termination (Section 2, pp. 2–3)
- **Target** (A1, eq. (2.1), p. 2): A = {[x,y]ᵀ | φ(x,y) ≤ 0}, φ: R² → R twice continuously differentiable, A convex.
- **Dynamics** (A2, eq. (2.2), p. 2): single integrators with fixed speed magnitudes — ẋ_j = v_{xj}, ẏ_j = v_{yj}, √(v_{xj}²+v_{yj}²) = v_D (j = 1,…,n); same for the invader with speed v_I. Controls are the velocity vectors; information structure is perfect state feedback (the strategies (2.7) are functions of all player positions).
- **Speed ratio** (A3, eq. (2.3)): v_D ≥ v_I, "or a > 1", a = v_D/v_I. (Printed as stated; note the ≥/> inconsistency — Appendix C uses a ≥ 1. See §7.)
- **Capture** (p. 2): ∃j ∈ {1,…,n} s.t. ‖p_I − p_j‖ ≤ r; A4: r > 0. **Remark 2.1** (p. 2): the proof has no requirement on r, hence remains valid for r = 0.
- **Value / termination** (eq. (2.4), p. 2): with [x_I^c, y_I^c]ᵀ the invader's location upon capture,
  V(x_1,y_1,…,x_n,y_n,x_I,y_I) = min_{v_I} max_{v_1,…,v_n} φ(x_I^c, y_I^c).
  The invader wins iff V < 0 (it reaches/gets captured inside A where φ < 0); V = 0 on ∂A; the surface {V = 0} is the barrier (§4.5, p. 7). Boundary conditions of the HJI: V = 0 (2.9b) whenever φ(x_I,y_I) = 0 (2.9c, invader on target boundary) and min_j‖p_j − p_I‖ = r (2.9d, at least one defender within capture range) — captured immediately at value 0.
- **Dominance region** (eq. (2.5), p. 3): D_I^j = {[x,y]ᵀ | d(x,y,x_j,y_j,x_I,y_I) ≥ 0} with d = R_j(x,y) − aR_I(x,y) − r, R_j = √((x−x_j)²+(y−y_j)²), R_I = √((x−x_I)²+(y−y_I)²); overall D_I = ∩_{j=1..n} D_I^j. On ∂D_I, d = 0 gives R_j = aR_I + r (a Cartesian oval, fourth order in coordinates).
- **Auxiliary problem** (eq. (2.6), p. 3): P_aux(D_1,…,D_n): min φ(x,y) s.t. [x,y]ᵀ ∈ D_I. The **geometric strategy** (eq. (2.7)) is v_j^g = (v_D/R_j(x*,y*))·[x*−x_j, y*−y_j]ᵀ, v_I^g = (v_I/R_I(x*,y*))·[x*−x_I, y*−y_I]ᵀ, i.e., every player heads to p*; the **geometric value** (eq. (2.8)) is V^g = g(x*, y*) (g = φ composed with the solution map of P_aux).
- **HJI equation** (eq. (2.9a), p. 3): min_{v_I} max_{v_1,…,v_n} {Σ_{j=1}^n (∂V/∂x_j ẋ_j + ∂V/∂y_j ẏ_j) + ∂V/∂x_I ẋ_I + ∂V/∂y_I ẏ_I} = 0, with boundary conditions (2.9b)–(2.9d).

### 5.1 Lemma 2.1 (p. 3; proof in Appendix C, pp. 9) — convexity of the dominance region
**Statement.** For given player locations, D_I = ∩_{j=1..n} D_I^j is convex.
**Assumptions.** A2–A4 (fixed-speed integrators, a ≥ 1, r > 0); no convexity of A needed.
**Proof approach (Appendix C).** (1) Intersection of convex sets is convex, so reduce to the SDSI case. (2) Convexity is frame-independent: place the x-axis along the vector D→I (Fig. C.11) so that y_D = y_I = 0. (3) Since ∂D_I is given implicitly by d(x,y,…)=0, it suffices to show the implicit boundary curve y(x) is concave for y > 0 and convex for y < 0. (4) With slope k = −d_x/d_y (eq. (3.3)), d²y/dx² = dk/dx = (d_y k_x − d_x k_y)/d_y. (5) Substitute first-order derivatives d_x = cos α − a cos β, d_y = sin α − a sin β (A.6) and k_x = ρ_D sin α + aρ_I sin β, k_y = −ρ_D cos α − aρ_I cos β (A.9), where ρ_D = (a cos(α−β) − 1)/((d_y)²R_D), ρ_I = (cos(α−β) − a)/((d_y)²R_I) (eq. (3.5)). This gives
  d_y·d²y/dx² = −ρ_D(a cos(α−β) − 1) + aρ_I(cos(α−β) − a) = −(a cos(α−β)−1)²/((d_y)²R_D) + a(cos(α−β)−a)²/((d_y)²R_I).
(6) **Decisive inequality**: multiply by (d_y)³R_I and use R_I/R_D < 1 (on ∂D_I, d = 0 ⇒ R_D = aR_I + r > R_I):
  (d_y)³R_I d²y/dx² = −(a cos(α−β)−1)² R_I/R_D + a(cos(α−β)−a)² > −(a cos(α−β)−1)² + (cos(α−β)−a)² = (a²−1)(1−cos²(α−β)) ≥ 0.
(7) Hence sign(d²y/dx²) = sign(d_y). In the chosen frame |sin α| = |y|/R_D < |y|/R_I = |sin β|, so d_y = sin α − a sin β < 0 for y > 0 and > 0 for y < 0; therefore d²y/dx² < 0 for y > 0 and > 0 for y < 0. ∎
**Properties.** Global/analytic; the inequality is strict for a > 1 and degenerate to ≥ for a = 1. **Physical meaning**: the invader's safe maneuvering region is convex, which guarantees the auxiliary problem P_aux is well-posed. **Caveat**: step (6)–(7) implicitly need d_y ≠ 0 (implicit-curve representation); points of vertical tangency are not discussed (see §7).
**Extensibility**: the curvature-sign technique applies to any superlevel set {R_D − aR_I − r ≥ 0}, e.g., reach-avoid Apollonius/Cartesian-oval regions with capture radius.

### 5.2 Lemma 2.2 (p. 3) — well-posedness of the auxiliary problem
**Statement.** P_aux(D_1,…,D_n) is convex and has a unique solution p* = [x*, y*]ᵀ ∈ ∂D_I (∂D_I the boundary of D_I).
**Proof.** "Obvious due to the convexity of φ and D_I, and assumption A5" (A5: A ∩ D_I = ∅ at game start, p. 3, which rules out the trivial winning case A ∩ D_I ≠ ∅ where the invader wins regardlessly). ∎ Global, analytic existence/uniqueness; the minimizer lies on the boundary because the interior of D_I lies strictly away from A and φ increases away from A.

### 5.3 Theorem 2.1 (p. 3) — main result
**Statement.** For the target defense game with dynamics (2.2), target area (2.1) and assumptions A1–A5, the geometric control law (2.7) and value function (2.8) solve the HJI equation (2.9a) under boundary conditions (2.9b)–(2.9d).
**Proof architecture** (Sections 3–4 + Appendices A–C): SDSI case verified differentiably (Section 3), 2DSI non-degenerate case verified differentiably (Section 4.3), general MDSI case reduced by degeneration and closed by a continuity/viscosity argument (Section 4.4). Detail below.

#### 5.3.1 Lemma 3.1 (p. 4, eq. (3.7)) — tangency and sign
**Statement.** For the SDSI game under A1–A5, φ_{x*}/φ_{y*} = d_{x*}/d_{y*} > 0.
**Proof** (3 steps): (1) by convex optimization theory, ∂D_I is tangent at p* to the contour ∂A′ = {φ(x,y) = φ*} (Fig. 3); (2) hence ∇φ = [φ_{x*}, φ_{y*}]ᵀ and ∇d = [d_{x*}, d_{y*}]ᵀ are parallel at p*; (3) by the definitions of φ (increasing out of A) and d (increasing into D_I), both increase along the direction from p* into the dominance region, so the two gradients point the same way — giving equality of ratios with positive sign. ∎ Local, geometric. Physical meaning: p* is the point of D_I closest to A (in the level-set sense), and the shared normal points from A into D_I. This positivity is later exactly the sign condition that makes the HJI verification pass (Remark 3.2 shows the maximizing twin solution would give φ_{y*}/d_{y*} < 0 and fail the HJI).

#### 5.3.2 Lemma 3.2 (p. 4, eq. (3.9)) — what the HJI equation demands
**Statement.** For control laws v_D, v_I and function V to solve the re-organized HJI equation min_{v_I} max_{v_D} {(∂_{p_D}V)ᵀv_D + (∂_{p_I}V)ᵀv_I} = 0 (eq. (3.8)), it must be satisfied that
  v_D = v_D ∂_{p_D}V/‖∂_{p_D}V‖, v_I = −v_I ∂_{p_I}V/‖∂_{p_I}V‖ (3.9a), and a‖∂_{p_D}V‖ = ‖∂_{p_I}V‖ (3.9b).
**Proof approach**: max over v_D of a linear function over the speed disk is attained in the gradient direction (giving v_D‖∂_{p_D}V‖), min over v_I in the anti-gradient direction (giving −v_I‖∂_{p_I}V‖); equating the two (condition for the min-max to vanish) yields (3.9b) after dividing by v_I = v_D/a. Used as the verification checklist in Step IV.

#### 5.3.3 Lemma 3.3 (p. 4, eq. (3.11)) — implicit system defining p*
**Statement.** Under A5, a necessary condition for [x*, y*]ᵀ to solve P_aux(D) is
  φ_{x*} + φ_{y*} k(x*, y*, x_D, y_D, x_I, y_I) = 0 (3.11a), d(x*, y*, x_D, y_D, x_I, y_I) = 0 (3.11b),
where k = −d_x/d_y is the slope of ∂D_I (eqs. (3.1)–(3.3)).
**Proof**: p* ∈ ∂D_I gives (3.11b); parameterize ∂D_I as y(x) via d = 0, substitute into φ so P_aux becomes an unconstrained problem in x; the necessary condition dφ/dx = 0 is exactly (3.12) evaluated at (x*, y*). ∎ This replaces any closed-form expression of p* — the key maneuver that removes challenge (i)/(ii).

#### 5.3.4 Step II (p. 4–5, eqs. (3.13)–(3.15)) — implicit differentiation of p*
Differentiating (3.11) w.r.t. x_D gives a 2×2 linear system in (x*_{x_D}, y*_{x_D}) (eq. (3.13)); solving for all eight partials x*_{x_D}, y*_{x_D}, x*_{y_D}, y*_{y_D}, x*_{x_I}, y*_{x_I}, x*_{y_I}, y*_{y_I} yields eqs. (3.15a)–(3.15h), e.g.
  x*_{x_D} = −K*ᵀ∇φ_{y*} cos α*/Δ + (ρ*D − a ẽ_{α*}ᵀR₋ẽ_{β*}) φ_{y*}/Δ,  x*_{x_I} = aK*ᵀ∇φ_{y*} cos β*/Δ − (a²ρ*I + a e_{α*}ᵀR₋e_{β*}) φ_{y*}/Δ,
with K* = [1, k*]ᵀ, ∇φ_{x*} = [φ_{xx*}, φ_{yx*}]ᵀ, ∇φ_{y*} = [φ_{xy*}, φ_{yy*}]ᵀ, R₊ = diag(ρ*D, ρ*I), R₋ = diag(ρ*D, −ρ*I), Δ = det of (3.13) (eq. (3.14)); e_α = [cos α, sin α]ᵀ, ẽ_α = [sin α, cos α]ᵀ (eq. (3.6)). Intermediate form (B.1)–(B.2) with G_{x*} = K*ᵀ∇φ_{x*} + k_{x*}φ_{y*}, G_{y*} = K*ᵀ∇φ_{y*} + k_{y*}φ_{y*}, Δ = d_{y*}G_{x*} − d_{x*}G_{y*}. **Remark 3.1** (p. 5): (3.15) is ill-defined at Δ = 0, "however, Δ will be canceled out in ∂_{p_D}V^g, ∂_{p_I}V^g." Local, analytic, algebraic.

#### 5.3.5 Step III (p. 5, eqs. (3.16)–(3.21)) — the decisive collapse of the value gradient
Chain rule (3.10): ∂_{p_D}V^g = [x*_{x_D}φ_{x*} + y*_{x_D}φ_{y*}; x*_{y_D}φ_{x*} + y*_{y_D}φ_{y*}], similarly for p_I. Substituting (3.15a)–(3.15d) gives (3.16): the first two terms align with e_{α*}; the remaining terms are written in the frame F_{α*} (x-axis along e_{α*}) via dot/cross products (3.18), which evaluate to (3.19): [γ¹_D]_{F_{α*}} = −[k_{y*}; ρ*D d_{y*}]/Δ, [γ²_D]_{F_{α*}} = [k_{x*}; ρ*D d_{x*}]/Δ (details in Appendix B.3, eqs. (B.6)–(B.7), e.g. e_{α*}·γ¹_D Δ = ρ*D cos α* + aρ*I cos β* = −k_{y*}, e_{α*}×γ¹_D Δ = −ρ*D d_{y*}). Because d_{x*}/d_{y*} = −k*, the second component of [∂_{p_D}V^g]_{F_{α*}} vanishes, and the **key determinant identity**
  K*ᵀ H_{φ*} K* + φ_{y*}(k* k_{y*} + k_{x*}) = Δ/d_{y*}  (3.20)
(proved in Appendix B.2: both sides equal the expansion of Δ = d_{y*}(K*ᵀ∇φ_{x*} + k_{x*}φ_{y*} + k*(K*ᵀ∇φ_{y*} + k_{y*}φ_{y*})); along ∂D_I this is d_{y*}·d/dx(φ_{x*} + k*φ_{y*}) = d_{y*}·d/dx(dφ*/dx) = d_{y*} d²φ*/dx²|_{∂D_I}, eq. (B.4)) collapses the defender-side gradient to
  [∂_{p_D}V^g]_{F_{α*}} = [φ_{y*}/d_{y*}; 0]  (3.21a),
and, by the mirrored computation of Appendix B.4 (eqs. (B.8)–(B.9)),
  [∂_{p_I}V^g]_{F_{β*}} = −a[φ_{y*}/d_{y*}; 0]  (3.21b).
**Decisive intermediate results**: (3.20) and (3.21). Physical meaning: ∂_{p_D}V^g is exactly v_D-magnitude-scaled a⁻¹ of ∂_{p_I}V^g and both are aligned with the p*-directions — precisely the structure (3.9) demands: the defender's gradient points from defender to p* (V increases if the defender advances toward p*, shrinking D_I), the invader's gradient is a times larger and opposite (V decreases as the invader advances toward p*).

#### 5.3.6 Step IV (p. 5, §3.3.4) — verification and classical differentiability (SDSI)
Comparing (3.21a)–(3.21b) with Lemma 3.2 and the frame definitions: v_D^g = v_D e_{α*} (defender heads to p*), v_I^g = v_I e_{β*} (invader heads to p*), the directions match (3.9a) iff φ_{y*}/d_{y*} > 0, guaranteed by Lemma 3.1; the magnitude condition a‖∂_{p_D}V^g‖ = ‖∂_{p_I}V^g‖ holds by (3.21). **Finiteness/differentiability** (p. 5): "since φ, d are second-order differentiable, φ is convex, d is concave, and [x*, y*]ᵀ is not an extremum of d, thus φ_{y*}/d_{y*} must be finite. This means V^g is differentiable, thereby is the classic solution of the HJI equation." — See §7: the claim "d is concave" is not established and is generally false (d = R_D − aR_I − r is a difference of convex functions; only quasiconcavity follows from Lemma 2.1); the non-extremality assertion is also unproven. This is the weakest link of the SDSI proof (proof gap, flagged below).
**Remark 3.2** (p. 5): (3.13) is only necessary and has another solution maximizing φ; the two are distinguished by sign(φ_{y*}/d_{y*}) — if p* maximized φ, φ_{y*}/d_{y*} < 0 and the HJI equation would NOT be satisfied. This is the precise sense in which the geometric (minimizing) solution is the game-theoretic one.

#### 5.3.7 2DSI game (Section 4, pp. 5–7) — degeneration classification, Lemma 4.1, non-degenerate proof
- **Degeneration enumeration** (§4.2, p. 6, Figs. 5–6): D_I¹ and D_I² always intersect (they share the invader). If one contains the other, the game is an SDSI game with the smaller region. Otherwise the game is degenerate (SDSI) or non-degenerate depending on the target location: only when the solution of P_aux(D_1,D_2) lands on p⋆ — the *closer intersection point* of ∂D_I¹ and ∂D_I² — is the game genuinely 2DSI (both defenders move toward p⋆ and capture happens at the vertex, farther from the target than either SDSI capture); at the critical target position p₂* = p⋆ the game is both 2DSI and SDSI.
- **Lemma 4.1** (p. 6, eq. (4.2)): for the HJI equation (4.1) min_{v_I} max_{v_1,v_2} {(∂_{p_1}V)ᵀv_1 + (∂_{p_2}V)ᵀv_2 + (∂_{p_I}V)ᵀv_I} = 0, necessarily v_1 = v_D ∂_{p_1}V/‖∂_{p_1}V‖, v_2 = v_D ∂_{p_2}V/‖∂_{p_2}V‖, v_I = −v_I ∂_{p_I}V/‖∂_{p_I}V‖, and **a‖∂_{p_1}V‖ + a‖∂_{p_2}V‖ = ‖∂_{p_I}V‖** — the invader's gradient magnitude is the *sum* of the a-scaled defender magnitudes (replacement of (3.9b) for two defenders).
- **Implicit system** (4.3): d₁(x⋆,y⋆,x_1,y_1,x_I,y_I) = 0, d₂(x⋆,y⋆,x_2,y_2,x_I,y_I) = 0 at the vertex p⋆. Differentiating w.r.t. x_1 (4.4) and solving gives (4.5): x⋆_{x_1} = d¹_{x_1} d²_{y⋆}/Ω, y⋆_{x_1} = −d¹_{x_1} d²_{x⋆}/Ω, with Ω = d¹_{y⋆}d²_{x⋆} − d¹_{x⋆}d²_{y⋆} the determinant; repeating for the other variables gives the full list (4.6), e.g. x⋆_{x_1} = −cos α₁⋆ d²_{y⋆}/Ω, x⋆_{x_I} = a cos β⋆(d²_{y⋆} − d¹_{y⋆})/Ω.
- **Gradients** (4.7): with ∇g⋆ = [g_{x⋆}, g_{y⋆}]ᵀ, ∂_{p_1}V^g = −(∇g⋆×∇d²⋆) e₁⋆/Ω, ∂_{p_2}V^g = (∇g⋆×∇d¹⋆) e₂⋆/Ω, ∂_{p_I}V^g = a(∇g⋆×∇d²⋆ − ∇g⋆×∇d¹⋆) e_{β⋆}/Ω. (Notation slip: this line redefines Ω as ∇d¹⋆×∇d²⋆, the negative of the (4.5) definition; the sign analysis that follows is consistent with Ω = ∇d²⋆×∇d¹⋆, i.e., the (4.5) definition. See §7.)
- **Sign determination** (p. 6, Fig. 7): since p⋆ ≠ p*_j, φ decreases along tangent t₁ of ∂D_I¹ at p⋆ and along −t₂ of ∂D_I²; ∇d⋆ʲ ⊥ tʲ and points into the dominance region; hence ∇g⋆ lies between ∇d¹⋆ and ∇d²⋆, whence ∇g⋆×∇d¹⋆ < 0, ∇g⋆×∇d²⋆ > 0, ∇d²⋆×∇d¹⋆ < 0, and (4.7) gives ∂_{p_1}V^g > 0, ∂_{p_2}V^g > 0, ∂_{p_I}V^g < 0 (coefficients along e₁⋆, e₂⋆, e_{β⋆}). Lemma 4.1 follows algebraically: a‖∂_{p_1}V‖ + a‖∂_{p_2}V‖ = a(∇g⋆×∇d²⋆ − ∇g⋆×∇d¹⋆)/|Ω| = ‖∂_{p_I}V‖. Directions: both defenders and the invader head to p⋆, matching (2.7) with p* → p⋆.
- **Finiteness at tangency** (pp. 6–7): for transversal intersection Ω ≠ 0 trivially. When ∂D_I¹, ∂D_I² are tangent at p⋆ (Ω → 0), ∇g⋆, ∇d¹⋆, ∇d²⋆ become colinear; using ‖∇g⋆×∇d¹⋆‖ ∼ ∠(∇d¹⋆,∇g⋆) > 0 (same order as the included angle, since the vectors are finite and non-zero), and the additivity ∠(∇d¹⋆,∇d²⋆) = ∠(∇d¹⋆,∇g⋆) + ∠(∇g⋆,∇d²⋆) (nested vectors), the limits ∠(∇d¹⋆,∇g⋆)/∠(∇d¹⋆,∇d²⋆) and ∠(∇g⋆,∇d²⋆)/∠(∇d¹⋆,∇d²⋆) are finite, so (4.7) stays finite as Ω → 0. The argument is correct in substance (each sub-angle is ≤ the total angle) but is phrased heuristically ("there is no reason one of … approaches zero at the same order … but the other does not").

#### 5.3.8 MDSI closure (Section 4.4, p. 7) — viscosity solution
∂D_I is piecewise smooth, each segment belonging to some ∂D_Iʲ (Fig. 8): if p* sits on a smooth segment the MDSI game degenerates to an SDSI game; if p* sits at a vertex it degenerates to a 2DSI game — so the HJI equation (2.9a) holds within each case (differentiably). Since differentiability of V^g is not assured at case switches, the paper proves **V^g continuous**: under a small deviation of player locations, (a) an SDSI-degenerate game stays SDSI-degenerate with the same j (p* = p*_j persists; continuity inherited from the SDSI solution map), and (b) a 2DSI-degenerate game remains degenerate with either or both of D_{j₁}, D_{j₂}, because other vertices of ∂D_I are a finite distance away so p* cannot jump; switches between SDSI/2DSI happen only when p*_1, p*_2, p⋆ overlap, where continuity follows from continuity of p*_1, p*_2, p⋆. Hence V^g is a continuous (piecewise-classical) solution and solves the HJI equation in the **viscosity sense** (Crandall & Lions 1983). **Proof gap (minor)**: the viscosity sub/supersolution inequalities across the switching surfaces are asserted from "piecewise classical + continuous" rather than verified; this is a standard argument but is not written out.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
**Author-stated** (Section 5, p. 7):
- The second-order differentiability requirement on φ can possibly be relaxed; "The authors' supposition is that continuity is sufficient" (a conjecture, not proven).
- Multiple invaders are out of scope: a task-assignment problem is needed to decide the capture order (Fu & Liu 2021), and the number of players changes upon a capture.
- Remark 2.1 asserts validity for r = 0, but all simulations use r = 2 > 0.

**Critical observations (with page/eq. references)**:
1. **"d is concave" is unjustified and generally false** (p. 5, §3.3.4): d = R_D − aR_I − r is a difference of convex functions; Lemma 2.1 only establishes convexity of the superlevel set D_I = {d ≥ 0} (quasiconcavity of d). The finiteness claim for φ_{y*}/d_{y*} — hence the classical-differentiability conclusion of the SDSI proof — rests on this sentence plus the unproven assertion that "[x*, y*] is not an extremum of d". A rigorous argument would need, e.g., ∇d(p*) ≠ 0 together with the geometry of the tangency (Lemma 3.1) to rule out d_{y*} = 0, or a symmetric treatment swapping the roles of x and y (the k = −d_x/d_y parametrization silently assumes the boundary has no vertical tangency at p*).
2. **Notation slip in (4.7)** (p. 6): Ω is defined as the determinant d¹_{y⋆}d²_{x⋆} − d¹_{x⋆}d²_{y⋆} in (4.5) but re-stated as Ω = ∇d¹⋆×∇d²⋆ immediately before (4.7); these differ by sign. The subsequent sign analysis is internally consistent with the (4.5) definition (Ω = ∇d²⋆×∇d¹⋆ < 0).
3. **Typo in (A.9)** (p. 8): the last derivative is printed "k_{x_I} = aρ_I cos β"; by symmetry with the other entries it must be k_{y_I} = aρ_I cos β (verified visually).
4. **Assumption A3 inconsistency** (p. 2, eq. (2.3)): "v_D ≥ v_I, or a > 1" — v_D ≥ v_I corresponds to a ≥ 1, while a > 1 is used elsewhere (Appendix C needs only a ≥ 1 with r > 0 for strict inequality; equal speeds a = 1 make (a²−1)sin²(α−β) = 0 attainable and are not discussed).
5. **Heuristic limit argument at tangency** (pp. 6–7, §4.3): the finiteness of (4.7) as Ω → 0 is argued by "there is no reason one of … but the other does not"; the angle-nesting identity quoted does make each sub-angle ≤ the total angle, but existence of the two ratio limits is asserted, not derived (e.g., via monotonicity or explicit asymptotics).
6. **Δ = 0 cancellation** (Remark 3.1, p. 5): for the SDSI case the cancellation of Δ in (3.21) is asserted; unlike the 2DSI tangency discussion, no limiting argument is provided for the SDSI case where ∂D_I is tangent to a φ-contour at p* (i.e., where Lemma 3.3's stationarity has a degenerate solution).
7. **Viscosity step abbreviated** (p. 7, §4.4): continuity of V^g is argued, but the viscosity sub-/super-solution inequalities on switching surfaces are not explicitly verified; also the terminal payoff when the invader successfully enters A is handled only through the boundary conditions (2.9b)–(2.9c) and the V < 0 interpretation of §4.5, not through a formal terminal-cost definition.
8. **Scope**: 2-D, unbounded plane, perfect information, single-invader; fixed-speed integrators only (A2); no noise, delay, or sensing constraints; simulation-only validation with no quantitative performance study.

## 8. Citation Value
This is the *rigorization* paper for the geometric/barrier solution of target-defense games: it is the natural citation whenever one uses dominance regions (Apollonius circles or Cartesian ovals with non-zero capture radius), "head-to-the-tangency-point" feedback laws, or piecewise value functions for multi-defender target guarding, and needs to claim HJI/viscosity legitimacy rather than intuition. Cite it (a) as the general-case justification covering arbitrary convex targets, non-zero capture range and any number of faster defenders, superseding the special-case proofs for linear coastlines (Garcia et al. 2019), circular/polygonal targets (Pachter et al. 2017) and single-defender convex targets (Lee & Bakolas 2021); (b) methodologically, for its implicit-differentiation + local-frame machinery that obtains value-function gradients without closed-form expressions of the tangency point — a pattern transferable to other games whose value depends on an implicitly defined point; and (c) as evidence that intuitive pursuit laws require optimality proof, per its motivating counterexamples. Example citation sentence: "The geometric target-defense solution we employ is justified in the viscosity sense for arbitrary convex target areas, non-zero capture radii, and any number of faster defenders [Fu & Liu, Automatica 2023]."
