## 2. Research Background

Reach-avoid (RA) differential games model an evasion agent trying to enter a goal region while a pursuit team tries to intercept it before penetration; applications cited include aerospace, control, networks, and robotics (p. 18605). The geometric machinery for such games — Voronoi partitions, Apollonius circles, and Cartesian ovals when capture radii are nonzero — yields the evader's *safe region* (SR), the set of points the evader reaches before any pursuer regardless of actions, from which optimal strategies follow under **perfect information** ([1], [10], [16], [17], [20] in the paper's numbering). The authors argue that perfect mutual knowledge of positions and speeds is too restrictive in practical adversarial scenarios, and they place the work in the TASE "Note to Practitioners" tradition: autonomous multi-agent systems for robotics, defense, and surveillance need pursuit strategies that are robust to observation uncertainty (p. 18605). The specific practical twist is **asymmetric observations**: the evader knows everything, while the pursuers only obtain unbiased, noise-corrupted estimates of the evader's speed ratio and range, with noise variance growing with distance. The paper is the journal-length follow-up of the same group's work on RA games under imperfect observation information (cf. their 2024 conference/journal line) and is validated both in simulation and on an indoor multi-AGV hardware platform.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap

**Problem.** In a 2D RA game where the line y = 0 (operationally y = φE after accounting for the evader's capture radius) splits the space into play region and goal region Ωgoal, Np pursuers {P1,…,PNp} with speeds vPk > vE cooperate to intercept one evader E before yE < φE. Information is asymmetric: E has perfect information; pursuer Pk only has unbiased Gaussian estimates of the speed ratio γk = vE/vPk and of the range dk = ‖xE − xPk‖2, with variances monotonically increasing in dk (eq. (2), p. 18606). The pursuers must (i) build a usable surrogate of the evader's SR from noisy data, (ii) decide winnability and the interception strategy in the 1v1 subgame, and (iii) coordinate the coalition, using as few pursuers as possible, so that the interception point is pushed as far from Ωgoal as possible.

**Gap, as stated by the authors (with verification status):**
- [Authors' claim, p. 18606]: most RA differential game methods "require perfect information in order to obtain the SR and to derive optimal strategies" — **consistent with the cited corpus** ([13], [19], [20] construct HJI barrier surfaces for the 2-player game; [1], [17], [21]–[23] decompose multiplayer RA games into 1v1 subgames solved by HJI/geometric partitions; all perfect-information works).
- [Authors' claim, p. 18606]: imperfect-information pursuit works either do not examine how uncertainty affects the evader's reachable set/SR — [31] (uncertain pursuer speeds, simulated annealing) and [32] (unknown evader max speed, Apollonius) "do not examine how uncertainty affects the evader's reachable set or SR" — or, when reachable-set estimation under bounded uncertainty is treated ([33], mixed monotonicity + high-gain observers), conservatism is not connected to RA winning conditions; [18] (Oyler et al., CDC 2015) computes dominance-boundary sensitivity to parameter perturbation, but "the robustness of SR estimation under uncertain information has not been thoroughly analyzed" and "there is a lack of research on how to mitigate the impact of uncertainty on cooperative strategies within a pursuit coalition" — **authors' claim; plausible given the cited list; not independently re-verified beyond it**.
- [Authors' claim, p. 18606]: coalitions do not always help — [13] shows two cooperative pursuers push capture farther from the target, but [1] shows at most three pursuers are needed in the 3D RA game, and [34] shows a two-pursuer coalition can be *less* effective than individual pursuit; nevertheless "these studies focus on perfect information scenarios and do not explore the geometric conditions under which a pursuer actively contributes to the coalition" — **consistent with [34]'s result as summarized; the geometric-condition gap under uncertainty is the paper's own positioning**.

## 4. Core Contributions

1. **Theory — RESR with 3σ guarantees**: a robust estimation of the safe region (RESR) built from pursuer-side uncertainties in γ and d, with closed-form boundary/closure (Theorem 1) and boundedness/strict-convexity properties (Lemma 1), supported by the sensitivity analysis (11)–(14) of the Cartesian-oval boundary radius w.r.t. the game parameters.
2. **Theory — 1v1 winning condition and optimal interception**: an estimated interception point (EIP) defined as the closest point of the RESR to Ωgoal, a feedback "optimal interception strategy" (OIS) with an uncertainty-compensation term (Theorem 2, eq. (33)) guaranteeing ẏI ≥ 0 (the EIP never sinks toward the goal), plus a proximity strategy (PS, eq. (39)) for the case where the RESR already intersects the goal region, which shrinks the RESR by closing distance.
3. **Concept — effective coalition (EC)**: Definitions 4–5 and Theorem 3 characterize exactly when adding pursuers strictly improves the interception point (CIP farther from Ωgoal than every individual EIP), enabling minimal-resource coalition formation under asymmetric information.
4. **Algorithm — receding horizon cooperation with strategy switching** (Algorithm 1, p. 18612): at each decision instant the team recomputes all RESRs, their intersection Bc, and switches among OIS / single-pursuer assignment / PS according to triggered geometric conditions, under distance-related observation uncertainty.
5. **Experiment — indoor multi-AGV RA testbed** (perception–planning–control pipeline: NOKOV motion capture, onboard Nvidia TX1, ROS cmd_vel, PID tracking) validating the strategy hierarchy in four scenarios (p. 18614–18615).

### 5.0 Dynamics, information structure, winning conditions

- **Dynamics (p. 18606, eq. (1))**: decoupled simple motion in R², ẋPk = vPk uPk, xPk(0) = x⁰Pk (k = 1,…,Np); ẋE = vI uE, xE(0) = x⁰E. **Printed typo**: the evader speed appears as v_I; it is vE (used everywhere else, e.g., "vE ∈ R+ denote the speeds of Pk and E"). Controls uPk, uE ∈ U = {u ∈ R² : ‖u‖2 = 1} (unit-norm heading); speeds constant with vPk > vE, so 0 < γk = vE/vPk < 1.
- **Capture / termination (p. 18606)**: E is captured iff ‖xPk − xE‖ < φPk for some k (∃k ∈ {1,…,Np}); E wins iff yE(t) < φE (goal region reached through the demarcation line y = φE that accounts for E's capture radius φE); "If the evader reaches Ωgoal, the evader wins, otherwise the pursers win" [sic — typo for "pursuers"].
- **Assumption 1 (Initial Deployment, p. 18606)**: ‖xPk(0) − xE(0)‖ > φPk ∀k and yE(0) > φE.
- **Assumption 2 (Constant Speed, p. 18606)**: no player changes speed during the game (so v̄E = γ̄vP under the estimates).
- **Information structure (p. 18606, eq. (2))**: dk = ‖xE − xPk‖2; Pk receives unbiased estimates γ̃k = γk + ργ, ργ ∼ N(0, σγ²), d̃k = dk + ρd, ρd ∼ N(0, σd²), where "σγ and σd are monotonically positively correlated with dk" — i.e., the farther the evader, the noisier the observation. This is the sole channel through which asymmetric information enters the analysis; E's own model stays noise-free.
- **Perfect-information winning condition (p. 18608, top)**: with SR closure cl(A) (eq. (6)), "P can win the game if cl(A) ∩ {y = φE} = ∅, while E can win if cl(A) ∩ {y = φE} ≠ ∅, both under optimal strategy [20]" (Lee & Bakolas, IEEE CSL 2021).

### 5.1 Perfect-information SR and its polar form (pp. 18607–18608, eqs. (3)–(9))

- **Definition 1 (Safe Region [20])**: A = {x ∈ R² : f(x) < 0} (eq. (3)) with the **Cartesian oval** barrier (eq. (4)): f(x) = ‖x − xE‖2 − γ‖x − xP‖2 + γφP; gradient (eq. (5)) ∇xf(x) = (x − xE)/‖x − xE‖2 − γ(x − xP)/‖x − xP‖2 for x ≠ xE, xP; closure cl(A) = {x : f(x) ≤ 0} (eq. (6)).
- Polar coordinates with origin at xE and zero azimuth along the line of sight: x = xE + re (eq. (7)), e = (cos(θ+θ0), sin(θ+θ0)), θ ∈ [−π, π), θ0 = arctan((yE−yP)/(xE−xP)). Substituting into f(x) = 0 gives the quadratic (eq. (8)): (1 − 1/γ²) r² − 2(φP/γ − d cos θ) r + d² − (φP)² = 0 (printed "1/γφP" means φP/γ), whose relevant root is (eq. (9)) r = [(φP/γ − d cos θ) − √((φP/γ − d cos θ)² − (1 − 1/γ²)(d² − (φP)²))]/(1 − 1/γ²). (I re-derived (8) independently: (1−γ²)r² + 2(γφP − γ²d cos θ)r + γ²(φP² − d²) = 0 divided by −γ² — correct.)

### 5.2 Sensitivity linearization of the boundary radius (p. 18608, eqs. (10)–(16))

- Auxiliary quantity (eq. (10)): c = √((φP)² + d²(1/γ² − sin²θ) − (2/γ)φP d cos θ).
- Partial derivatives of the boundary radius r w.r.t. the uncertain parameters: ∂r/∂γ (eq. (11)) = [(3 − γ²)φP − 2γ(d cos θ + c)]/(γ² − 1)² − [φP/γ·(d² − (φP)²)·(1/γ) − φP]/(c(γ² − 1)) — "characterizes the sensitivity to speed ratio estimation of E"; ∂r/∂d (eq. (12)) = (γ²/(γ² − 1))[−cos θ + (d(γ² sin²θ − 1) + γφP cos θ)/(cγ²)] — "sensitivity to position estimation of P relative to E".
- First-order propagation (eq. (13)): with z = [γ, d]ᵀ, r(z) = r(z̄) + [∂r/∂γ, ∂r/∂d]ᵀ|z̄ (z − z̄). Treating z as Gaussian with mean z̄ = [γ̄, d̄]ᵀ and covariance Σz, the boundary distance is Gaussian r̃ = r̄ + ω, r̄ = r(z̄), ω ∼ N(0, σr²) with variance (eq. (14)) σr² = [∂r/∂γ, ∂r/∂d]|z̄ Σz [∂r/∂γ, ∂r/∂d]ᵀ|z̄, Σz = diag((σγ)², (σd)²) (components independent by construction of (2)).
- Boundary point (eq. (15)): x = x̄E + r̄e + ωe = x̄ + ∆x with x̄ = x̄E + r̄e, ∆x = ωe. The 3σ uncertainty set (eq. (16)): Pω∈[±3σr](∆x) = {x ∈ R² : x = we, ∀w ∈ [−3σr, 3σr], e = (cos(θ+θ0), sin(θ+θ0)), ∀θ ∈ [−π, π)}.
- **Remark 1 (p. 18608)**: the 3σ principle means "there is a 99.73% probability that the evader's SR can be accounted for within the 3σ confidence interval, assuming a normal distribution of the observation noise [35]" — the guarantee is probabilistic (Gaussian tails), not worst-case.

### 5.3 Definition 2 (RESR) and the uncertain barrier (p. 18608, eqs. (17)–(18))

- **Definition 2 (Robust Estimation of the Safe Region)**: given xP, x̄E, γ̄, the RESR is "the set of all possible positions in R² that E may reach without being captured, regardless of P's strategy": Ã = ∪∆x∈Pω∈[±3σr](∆x) {x̄ ∈ R² : f̃(x̄, ∆x) < 0} (eq. (17)), with barrier (eq. (18)): f̃(x̄, ∆x) = ‖x̄ − x̄E‖2 − γ̄‖x̄ − xP‖2 + γ̄φP + (1 − γ̄)ω‖e‖2.
- **How uncertainty enters (modeling observation)**: since ‖e‖2 = 1, the uncertain term is the additive offset +(1 − γ̄)ω; at ω = −3σr the barrier becomes f̃ = f_nominal − 3σr(1 − γ̄), i.e., the pursuer behaves as if its effective capture radius were reduced from φP to φP − 3σr(1 − γ̄)/γ̄, which enlarges the estimated SR — the conservative direction. **Proof gap (modeling, p. 18608)**: the radial perturbation r̃ = r̄ + ω derived in (13)–(15) is *asserted* to be equivalent to the barrier offset (1 − γ̄)ω in (18); the two are not identical (a barrier-value offset δ shifts the radial boundary by ≈ δ/|∂f/∂r|, not by δ), and no derivation reconciles them. The RESR is therefore a specific first-order conservative inflation of the SR, and the "3σ of the boundary radius" interpretation is approximate.

### 5.4 Theorem 1 (pp. 18608–18609, eqs. (19)–(25)): closed-form boundary and closure of the RESR

**Statement.** For the RESR Ã defined in (17), the boundary and closure with the 3σ confidence interval are ∂(Ã) = ∪θ∈[−π,π) {x̄ ∈ R² : f̃(x̄, ∆x(−3σr)) = 0} (eq. (19)) and cl(Ã) := ∪θ∈[−π,π) {x̄ ∈ R² : f̃(x̄, ∆x(−3σr)) ≤ 0} (eq. (20)), where ∆x(−3σr) = −3σr·e, e = (cos(θ+θ0), sin(θ+θ0)).

**Proof approach** (direct set-inclusion argument):
1. The boundary/closure candidates correspond to ω = −3σr; it suffices to show that for any ω1 ∈ (−3σr, 3σr] the partial RESR Ã1 = ∪{x̄ : f̃(x̄, ∆x) < 0} ⊆ Ã (eq. (21)).
2. Fix ω = ω1: barrier f̃1(x̄) = ‖x̄ − x̄E‖2 − γ̄‖x̄ − xP‖2 + γ̄φP + ω1(1 − γ̄) (eq. (22)); for ω = −3σr: f̃(x̄) = … + γ̄φP − 3σr(1 − γ̄) (eq. (23)).
3. For any x̄1 ∈ Ã1, f̃1(x̄1) < 0 (eq. (24)).
4. **Decisive inequality (eq. (25))**: f̃(x̄1) = f̃1(x̄1) − (3σr + ω1)(1 − γ̄) < −(3σr + ω1)(1 − γ̄) ≤ 0, because 3σr + ω1 ∈ (0, 6σr] and 0 < γ̄ < 1. Hence x̄1 ∈ Ã.
5. Setting f̃(x̄) = 0 and ≤ 0 gives (19)–(20); physically, ∂(Ã) is "the set of positions that both P and E can reach simultaneously under the 3σ uncertainty from P's perspective", and cl(Ã) is where E arrives before P (p. 18609).

**Properties**: global, analytic, exact for the *defined* RESR (given the (18) inflation convention). Fig. 2 (p. 18610) confirms visually that the black 3σ RESR encloses the red actual SR; the union over ω is attained at ω = −3σr (largest set), i.e., the RESR is the outer envelope of all SRs compatible with the 3σ uncertainty.

### 5.5 Lemma 1 (p. 18609, eqs. (26)–(32)): boundedness and strict convexity of cl(Ã)

**Statement.** For the RESR Ã with respect to P and E, its closure cl(Ã) is bounded and strictly convex.

**Proof approach** (polar convexity criterion, cf. [1]):
1. Work with f̃(x̄) = ‖x̄ − x̄E‖2 − γ̄‖x̄ − xP‖2 + γ̄φP − 3σr(1 − γ̄) (eq. (26)); abbreviate κ := γ̄φP − 3σr(1 − γ̄) (the effective capture-radius term).
2. **Boundedness**: polar form x̄ = x̄E + re yields the quadratic (eq. (27)): (γ̄² − 1)r² − 2((γ̄φP − 3σr(1 − γ̄)) − γ̄²d cos θ) r + γ̄²d² − (γ̄φP − 3σr(1 − γ̄))² = 0 — **printed with γ̄d cos θ, a typo**; the g₁ definition in (28) and ∂²g₁/∂θ² = γ̄²d cos θ later in the proof require γ̄²d cos θ, and my independent recomputation (repeating the 5.1 reduction with γ → γ̄, φP → κ) confirms γ̄²d. With root (eq. (28)) r(θ) = (g₁(θ) − g₂(θ))/(γ̄² − 1), g₁(θ) = κ − γ̄²d cos θ, g₂(θ) = √(g₁(θ)² − (γ̄² − 1)(γ̄²d² − κ²)); "the radius r(θ) > 0 is bounded, thus the closure cl(Ã) is bounded" (positivity of r(θ) for all θ follows from x̄E being interior when d > φP: f̃(x̄E) = −γ̄(d − φP) − 3σr(1 − γ̄) < 0).
3. **Strict convexity**: use the polar criterion — a set with polar boundary r(θ) is strictly convex iff r² + 2(r′)² − r r″ > 0 for all θ (definition from [1]; Remark 2 notes the algebraic convexity definition [37] is hard to apply directly).
4. Chain rule (eq. (29)): ∂g₂/∂θ = (g₁/g₂)∂g₁/∂θ; first/second derivatives of r(θ) (eq. (30)).
5. **Decisive identity (eq. (31))**: r² + 2(r′)² − r r″ = ((g₁ − g₂)/(γ̄² − 1))² [1 + (1/g₂)∂²g₁/∂θ² + ((g₂ − g₁)/g₂³)(∂g₁/∂θ)²] ≥ ((g₁ − g₂)/(γ̄² − 1))² [1 + (1/g₂)∂²g₁/∂θ²] (I verified the factoring by expanding — the cross-term bookkeeping is correct). The dropped last term is nonnegative iff g₂ ≥ g₁, which holds whenever γ̄d ≥ κ, i.e., γ̄(d − φP) + 3σr(1 − γ̄) > 0 — true for valid game states d > φP (Assumption 1) but **implicit, not stated**.
6. Case analysis on ∂²g₁/∂θ² = γ̄²d cos θ = −g₁ + κ: (i) if g₁ < κ then the bracket 1 + (1/g₂)∂²g₁/∂θ² > 0 trivially; (ii) if g₁ ≥ κ, then (eq. (I verified (32)'s algebra; it holds unconditionally for d > 0.)
7. Conclusion: r² + 2(r′)² − r r″ > 0 for all θ ⟹ cl(Ã) strictly convex. □

**Physical meaning**: the pursuer's conservative SR estimate is a nice strictly convex body — this uniqueness/convexity is what makes the EIP well-defined and the projection/KKT arguments in Theorem 2 legitimate. Analytic, global. Extensibility: the same reduction shows the RESR is the Cartesian oval of the *inflated* game (γ̄, κ), so every perfect-information oval property transfers.

### 5.6 Definition 3 and Theorem 2 (p. 18610, eqs. (33)–(37)): optimal interception strategy

- **Definition 3 (Estimated Interception Point, p. 18610)**: if cl(Ã) ∩ Ωgoal = ∅, the EIP xI = [xI, yI]ᵀ is "the unique point in cl(Ã) that is closest to Ωgoal" (equivalently the lowest point of the strictly convex cl(Ã), since Ωgoal lies below the line y = φE).
- **Theorem 2 (Optimal Interception Strategy)**. Consider P and E under model (1) with cl(Ã) ∩ Ωgoal = ∅. If P adopts the feedback strategy uP = v/‖v‖2 with (eq. (33)) **v = γ̄(xI − xP)/‖xI − xP‖2 − 3(1 − γ̄)(∂σr/∂d)(x̄E − xP)/‖x̄E − xP‖2**, then P guarantees that cl(Ã) does not approach Ωgoal, i.e., ẏI ≥ 0, for any uE ∈ U.

1. By Lemma 1 and Definition 3, xI is the unique minimizer of min x̄y s.t. f̃(x̄, ∆x(−3σr)) ≤ 0; KKT conditions (eq. (34)): [0, −1]ᵀ = λ∇x f̃(xI, ∆x(−3σr)), λ ≥ 0, λ f̃(xI, ∆x(−3σr)) = 0; slackness ⟹ λ > 0 and f̃(xI, ·) = 0 (constraint active).
2. **Total time derivative of the active constraint** (eq. 5. **Claimed conclusion**: since ∂σr/∂d > 0 and 0 < γ̄ < 1, the bracket is ≥ 0; then −ẏI = [0, −1]ᵀ ẋI = λ∇x f̃(xI, ∆x(−3σr))ᵀ ẋI ≤ 0, i.e., ẏI ≥ 0. □

**Proof gaps (Theorem 2, p. 18610–18611)**: (a) the pivotal sentence reads "Therefore, it follows from and that the derivative ∂σr/∂d > 0 and 0 < γ̄ < 1…" — a **dropped reference** (p. 18611); (b) the positivity of the bracket in (37) — a difference of two square roots — does not follow from β1 ≥ β0, cos β0 ≥ cos β1, ∂σr/∂d > 0, γ̄ < 1 in any printed monotonicity argument: the first root carries the larger noise coefficient a/γ̄ (a := 3(1 − γ̄)∂σr/∂d), which enlarges it through the squared term but also strengthens its negative cross-term −2(a/γ̄)cos β0; for small a and near-equal angles the printed expressions can give a negative bracket (e.g., γ̄ = 0.8, a = 0.1, β0 = β1 = 60°). The theorem may still hold under additional unprinted geometric relations between β0, β1 and the RESR geometry, but as printed **the last step is under-argued**; (c) ∂σr/∂d > 0 itself is asserted, not derived — it depends on the distance-correlation of σγ, σd in (2) plus (11)–(12). (d) Step (c) is attributed to "expanding the two-paradigm calculus" [sic].

**Physical meaning**: the first term of (33) aims at the robust interception point; the second term is an uncertainty-compensation bias — since ∂σr/∂d > 0, pointing the heading so as to reduce d shrinks σr and hence the RESR, keeping the EIP from sinking toward the goal. The guarantee ẏI ≥ 0 is a *set-invariance-type* condition: the evader's worst-case reachable set never creeps into Ωgoal, so interception occurs before penetration. Local feedback law, analytic; the strategy depends only on estimates pursuers actually possess (γ̄, d̄, x̄E, σr), i.e., it is implementable under the asymmetric information pattern. **Remark 3 (p. 18611)**: the 3σ-based RESR makes this "a robust strategy … ensuring that the strategy can effectively handle uncertainties with a high probability" — the guarantee is probabilistic, not deterministic.

### 5.7 Proximity strategy (p. 18611, Section III-D, eqs. (38)–(39))

When cl(Ã) ∩ Ωgoal ≠ ∅ there is **no guaranteed winning strategy** from the pursuer's perspective ("he does not know whether the current SR intersects Ωgoal or not"). Define the **proximity point (PP)** x′I ∈ argmin_{x∈{∂(Ã)∩(y=φE)}} ‖x − xP‖ (eq. (38)) — the intersection point of the RESR boundary with the guard line closest to the pursuer — and the feedback proximity strategy uP = (x′I − xP)/‖x′I − xP‖2 (eq. (39)). Mechanism: because σγ, σd increase with d (eq. (2)), closing the distance compresses the RESR ("as the pursuer approaches the proximity point, cl(Ã) shrinks and may be separated from the line y = φE"), after which the OIS of Theorem 2 takes over. The paper explicitly distinguishes this from the proximity strategy of [27], which counters an evader's distance-maintaining behavior; here proximity is an *information-gathering* maneuver that "reduces RESR by minimizing observation noise variance" (p. 18611).

### 5.8 Evader's approaching strategy (p. 18611, Section III-E, eqs. (40)–(42))

The evader has perfect information and plays the [20] optimal strategy on its true SR: if cl(A) ∩ Ωgoal = ∅, head straight to the **optimal approach point (OAP)** x† solving min_x y s.t. f(x) ≤ 0 (eq. (40)); if cl(A) ∩ Ωgoal ≠ ∅, x† solves min_x ‖xE − x‖ s.t. f(x) ≤ 0, y = φE (eq. (41)) — the closest reachable point on the guard line. Feedback: uE = (x† − xE)/‖x† − xE‖ (eq. (42)). This gives the strongest-case adversary for evaluating the pursuers' robust strategies; the EIP xI generally shifts away from the evader's scheduled target x† because of uncertainty (p. 18611), which motivates coalitions.

### 5.9 Effective coalition (p. 18611–18612, Section IV, eq. (43), Definitions 4–5, Theorem 3)

- Setting: "for the purpose of this analysis, three pursuers P1, P2 and P3 are considered"; coalitions Pc over index sets c ⊆ {1,2,3}, |c| ≥ 2.
- **Coalition RESR intersection (eq. (43))**: Bc := ∩_{k∈c} cl(Ãk) — "the robust estimation of region for evader that can move without being captured by any pursuer in the coalition under uncertainties". Intersecting individual RESRs compresses the evader's estimated safe region.
- **Definition 4 (Cooperative Interception Point, CIP)**: if Bc ∩ Ωgoal = ∅, xIc = [xIc, yIc]ᵀ is the unique point in Bc closest to Ωgoal.
- **Definition 5 (Effective Coalition, EC)**: Pc is an effective coalition for countering E iff Bc ∩ Ωgoal = ∅ **and** xIc ≠ xIk for all k ∈ c. Fig. 5 (p. 18612) illustrates: in (a) xIk ∉ Bc for k ∈ {1,2} and the CIP is farther from Ωgoal than both EIPs (EC forms); in (b) xI2 ∈ B so P1 adds nothing and xIc = xI2 — P2 should intercept alone. "Thus, not all pursuit coalitions contribute to enhancing interception performance, which is the significance of our introduction of the concept of an effective coalition" (p. 18612).
- **Theorem 3 (Properties of an effective coalition, p. 18612)**: if Pc is an EC for E, then ∀k ∈ c, if xIk ∉ Bc, the CIP xIc of Pc is farther from Ωgoal than the EIP xIk of cl(Ãk).
  **Proof** (two cases, set-theoretic): (1) if ∀k, xIk ∉ Bc, then xIc ∈ Bc ≠ xIk and Pc is an EC "according to Definition 4.2" [**dangling reference as printed — no Definition 4.2 exists; context indicates Definition 5**]; since Bc ⊆ cl(Ãk) by (43) and xIk is the (unique) closest point of cl(Ãk) to Ωgoal, every point of Bc — including xIc — is farther; (2) if ∃k with xIk ∈ Bc, then since Bc ⊆ cl(Ãk), xIk and xIc are the lowest points of cl(Ãk) and Bc respectively, so xIk = xIc and Pc is *not* effective. □ The proof also delivers the working characterization: **Pc is effective ⟺ (Bc ∩ Ωgoal = ∅ and) every member's EIP lies outside Bc**; this is the test Algorithm 1 uses to prune redundant pursuers.

### 5.10 Algorithm 1 (p. 18612): receding horizon strategy for the pursuit coalition

**Require**: x̄E, d̄k, γ̄k, xPk (k = 1,…,Np), σγ, σd. **Ensure**: uPk. At each decision instant (receding horizon, sample time T):
1. Compute each cl(Ãk) from (10)–(15) and (20); compute Bc from (43).
2. If Bc ∩ Ωgoal = ∅: (a) if the current Pc is an EC, determine the (minimal) index set c′ forming the EC via Definition 5, compute xIc′ via Definition 4, and apply the OIS (33) with xIc′ for k ∈ c′; (b) else, assign only the pursuer k′ with the farthest EIP xIk′ to intercept E alone using (33) — "we expect pursuers to intercept the evader as far from Ωgoal, using as few pursuers as possible" (p. 18612).
3. If Bc ∩ Ωgoal ≠ ∅: every pursuer computes its proximity point x′Ik ∈ argmin_{x∈{∂(Ãk)∩(y=φE)}} ‖x − xPk‖ (line 13) and applies the PS (39).
The evader runs (40)/(41) + (42). The receding-horizon re-planning is necessary because "the RESR and the interception point are dynamic as the game evolves from the pursuers' perspective" (p. 18612); the distance-related uncertainty makes the policy switch PS → EC → single-pursuer OIS as d decays. No convergence/complexity analysis is given; the coalition enumeration is combinatorial in Np (2^Np − 1 subsets, albeit analyzed for Np = 3).

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues

**Author-stated:**
- "At present, this method cannot be used directly in the multi-pursuer versus multi-evader game scenario. Future studies will investigate the scalability of this method" (Note to Practitioners, p. 18605); the 3v3 experiment decouples the game by an *assumed* task assignment ("task assignment is not the focus, and we assume that the task assignment has been completed", p. 18613).
- Conclusion (p. 18615): future work — extension to multiplayer-vs-multiplayer RA games.
- Implicit scope: 2D simple motion, constant speeds (Assumption 2), faster pursuers (0 < γk < 1), probabilistic (Gaussian, 3σ) guarantees only.

**Critical observations (with page/eq.):**
1. **Modeling gap between (13)–(15) and (18)** (p. 18608): the linearized boundary-radius noise r̃ = r̄ + ω is converted into the additive barrier offset +(1 − γ̄)ω‖e‖2 (‖e‖2 = 1) without derivation; a barrier offset δ and a radial offset are not the same perturbation (δ shifts the boundary by ≈ δ/|∂f/∂r|). The RESR is thus a particular conservative inflation — equivalent to shrinking the pursuer's capture radius to φP − 3σr(1 − γ̄)/γ̄ — and the "3σ of the boundary radius" reading is approximate.
2. **Theorem 2 proof gaps** (pp. 18610–18611): (i) sign inconsistency between (35) (term with (xP − x̄E)ᵀ) and (36)/(33) (term −3(1 − γ̄)(∂σr/∂d)(x̄E − xP)/‖x̄E − xP‖) — exactly one is a typo; (ii) the decisive sentence has a dropped reference ("it follows from and that…", p. 18611); (iii) the final inequality — positivity of the difference of square roots in (37) — is not established by the printed facts (β1 ≥ β0, cos β0 ≥ cos β1, ∂σr/∂d > 0, 0 < γ̄ < 1) alone; the a/γ̄-scaled first norm need not dominate for small noise amplitude a = 3(1 − γ̄)∂σr/∂d. The conclusion ẏI ≥ 0 is therefore not fully verifiable from the printed argument.
3. **∂σr/∂d > 0 is asserted**, not derived; it depends on the assumed monotone positive correlation of σγ, σd with dk in (2) — the theorem's applicability is tied to this specific noise model.
4. **Eq. (27) typo** (p. 18609): γ̄d cos θ should be γ̄²d cos θ (inconsistent with (28) and with direct recomputation). Eq. (1) prints ẋE = vI uE (v_I for v_E); "Definition 4.2" (p. 18612) is a dangling reference; Section IV header "UNDER RESE" (twice, pp. 18609/18611) for RESR; "the pursers win" (p. 18605).
5. **Lemma 1's final bound drops the term ((g₂ − g₁)/g₂³)(g₁′)²** in (31); nonnegativity requires g₂ ≥ g₁, which holds for d > φP (Assumption 1) but is nowhere stated (p. 18609).
6. **Guarantee semantics**: all winning conditions are 3σ/probabilistic (Remark 1, p. 18608; Remark 3, p. 18611) under unbounded Gaussian noise — no worst-case (bounded-error) alternative is given; tail events beyond 3σ are excluded by assumption, and nothing prevents occasional σr underestimation that would invalidate the EIP invariance.
7. **Simulation outcomes**: in 2 of 3 uncertainty settings the evader wins (Fig. 6(b), (c)) — the framework guarantees "farther interception" and minimal resource usage, not game victory; no quantitative comparison against baselines (e.g., [18]'s sensitivity-based dominance or plain Apollonius pursuit ignoring noise) is provided.
8. **Coalition logic is combinatorial** (2^Np − 1 subsets tested each cycle; analyzed for Np = 3, p. 18611) and Theorem 3's proof is a near-immediate set argument; no complexity or real-time guarantees are reported (relevant since Algorithm 1 runs on an embedded TX1).
9. **Reporting gaps**: experiment initial positions UNREPORTED in text; no repeated-trial statistics; the evader's experimental strategy is only "a similar receding strategy" (p. 18614), not exactly (40)–(42); reference [15] (Garcia & Bopardikar, ACC 2021) is listed but never cited in the body (p. 18616).

## 8. Citation Value

**Paragraph role**: this is the archival reference for *pursuit under asymmetric observations* in RA games — i.e., the point in the literature where Apollonius/Cartesian-oval safe-region geometry is made robust to measurement noise and then exploited for *minimal-resource coalition formation*. It complements perfect-information geometric solutions ([1], [13], [17], [20]), uncertainty-aware reachable-set work ([18], [32], [33]), and experimental pursuit testbeds ([27]), and it is one of the few RA papers with a full sim-to-hardware pipeline (mocap + AGVs). Its effective-coalition concept is a citable counterpoint to "more pursuers always help" results ([34]).

**Ready-to-adapt citation sentences**: "Fang et al. [P028] study a multiple-pursuer–one-evader reach-avoid differential game in which the evader has perfect information while the pursuers hold only distance-dependent noisy estimates of the speed ratio and range; they construct a 3σ robust estimation of the evader's safe region (RESR) with provable boundedness and strict convexity, derive a feedback interception strategy with an uncertainty-compensation term that keeps the estimated interception point away from the goal region, and coordinate the team through an effective-coalition notion and a receding-horizon algorithm validated on an indoor multi-AGV testbed." Alternatively, for the gap sentence: "Existing safe-region-based RA strategies presuppose perfect information [1], [13], [20]; the RESR framework of [P028] is among the first to couple a statistically guaranteed safe-region estimate with a provable interception invariant (ẏI ≥ 0) and a minimal-pursuer coalition rule, albeit with guarantees that remain probabilistic (3σ) and a final invariance step that is only sketchily argued."
