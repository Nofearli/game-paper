## 2. Research Background

The target defense differential game (TDDG) — an attacker ($A$) trying to approach a static target $T$ and a defender ($D$) trying to intercept $A$ as far from $T$ as possible — originates in Isaacs' work [1] and underpins interception-missile and anti-drone scenarios [15]–[21]. Classical analytical solutions (Apollonius-circle dominance regions, target-attacker-defender constructions, active defense [5], convex target areas [26], [7], flow fields [27], perimeter-constrained defenders [6], [29], and MAMD extensions [10], [30]–[34]) almost universally model players as constant-speed single integrators, so one party always holds a fixed speed advantage. Numerical HJI solutions suffer the curse of dimensionality, and intelligent methods (ADP [22], [23], DRL [24], [35], [36]) cannot certify a strict Nash equilibrium and are hyperparameter-dependent. This article pushes the analytical program to second-order plants: both players have variable speed driven by bounded forces, so neither party has an a priori speed advantage and the dominance geometry must be re-derived from an effective ratio.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap

**Problem** [authors' formulation, p. 1851–1852]: a two-player zero-sum SOTDDG with dynamics (1) (heading-controlled velocity direction + force-driven speed), saturation constraints (2), nonanticipative information structure (each agent knows both current states and current control inputs, p. 1852, citing [4], [5], [6], [7], [10]), capture terminal condition (6), and Mayer payoff $\varphi(\mathbf z_f)=(x_{A_f}-x_T)^2+(y_{A_f}-y_T)^2$ (7)–(8), $V(\mathbf z_0):=\min_{\mathbf u_A}\max_{\mathbf u_D}J$ (9). The goal is closed-form state-feedback Nash equilibrium strategies (headings $\chi^*,\gamma^*$ and forces $F_A^*,F_D^*$) plus the game-of-kind barrier.

**Stated gap** [authors' claim, p. 1851]: "all the research above assumes that the attackers and defenders travel at constant speeds"; "Existing literature that studied TDDGs modeled as second-order systems is based on DRL [35], [36]. The analytical solution of the second-order systems has been rarely studied because of the theoretical difficulties brought by the curse of dimensionality."

**Verified from the cited corpus**: [verified] the archives for [5] (Garcia et al., active defense, TAC 2019), [7] (Fu & Liu, Automatica 2023), [10] (Garcia et al., MAMD, TAC 2021), [26] (Lee & Bakolas, CDC 2021), [27] (Deng et al., TAC 2024) indeed use constant-speed simple motions; the first-order machinery (Apollonius circle (11), nearest-point saddle point) is exactly the toolkit those papers use, so the paper's positioning is accurate. [verified] The genuinely new object here is the *speed-varying* pair: speed becomes a state with bang-bang force input, and the effective speed ratio becomes an endogenous quantity ($\bar\alpha$, the "average speed ratio") rather than a parameter. [authors' claim — partially unverifiable] "to the best of the authors' knowledge, this research analyses and designs the analytical Nash equilibrium strategy for SOTDDG with speed-varying players and with saturation constraints for the first time" (p. 1851) — the game-paper library contains no earlier analytical second-order TDDG with saturated forces, so the claim stands within this library, but the phrase "second-order" must be read narrowly (see §7, item 10).

## 4. Core Contributions

1. **Theory (main)**: first (per authors) analytical Nash-equilibrium solution of a TDDG whose players have second-order (speed-dynamic) plants with force saturation, obtained by an *average speed ratio* reduction: the 6-D SOTDDG with four decision variables is decomposed into (i) a force-allocation problem solved by PMP (bang-bang full acceleration, then coast) and (ii) a 1-D extremal search of the terminal value over $\bar\alpha$ — no HJI PDE is solved numerically (Abstract, p. 1850; §IV-A, p. 1857–1858).
2. **Theory (verification)**: a continuously differentiable terminal value function w.r.t. the ratio (Lemmas 1–2, p. 1856), used to argue via the HJI equation that the constructed strategies are the Nash equilibrium (Theorems 1–3; eqs. (35), (36), (44), (67)).
3. **Theory (unification)**: for the FOTDDG, a single algebraic expression of the saddle point, dominance circle and value valid for *arbitrary* speed ratio $\alpha=v_D/v_A\neq 1$ (Theorem 1, eqs. (14)–(16)), with the degenerate equal-speed case as Theorem 2 ((37)–(38)) and continuity at $\alpha=1$ stitched by Lemma 2 ((45)); "rigorous proof from an algebraic perspective that aligns with geometric intuition" (p. 1851).
4. **Theory (game of kind)**: closed-form barrier surface for the SOTDDG — an offset Apollonius circle when $\bar\alpha_{\max}\neq 1$ (68) or two parallel lines offset by $\pm r_T$ from the perpendicular bisector when $\bar\alpha_{\max}=1$ (69), claimed "universally applicable" for arbitrary motion processes (p. 1859).
5. **Modeling/algorithm (extension, sketch-level)**: generalization to $\dot v=g(\mathbf u_2,t)$ with $n$ attackers and $m$ defenders (74)–(78); headings stay constant and forces bang-bang if $g$ is $z$-independent; MAMD solved by Hungarian matching ($n=m$) or SASD+DASD decomposition ($n<m<2n$) (p. 1859–1860).
6. **Experiment**: numerical illustrations only — a ratio sweep (Fig. 6) validating continuity/monotonicity of $V(\alpha)$ and one SOTDDG run (Fig. 7) showing Nash trajectories against the three dominance boundaries (initial-speed, max-speed, optimal-ratio).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results (exhaustive)

### 5.0 Model and information structure (pp. 1851–1852)

- **Dynamics (1)** (second-order in speed; heading is a *control*, not a state):
$$\dot x_A=v_A\cos\chi,\ \dot y_A=v_A\sin\chi,\ \dot x_D=v_D\cos\gamma,\ \dot y_D=v_D\sin\gamma,\ \dot v_A=F_A/m_A,\ \dot v_D=F_D/m_D$$
with headings $\chi,\gamma\in\mathbb R$, forces $F_A,F_D\in\mathbb R$, masses $m_A,m_D$; target $T$ static, a disk of radius $r_T$ (Fig. 1). So "second-order" concerns only the speed channel; the heading channel is kinematic (no turn-rate dynamics). The extension (74) replaces $\dot v$ by $g(\mathbf u_2,t)$ (true abstract double integrator).
- **Constraints (2)**: $v_A\le v_{A\max}$, $|F_A|\le F_{A\max}$, $v_D\le v_{D\max}$, $|F_D|\le F_{D\max}$; controls $\mathbf u_A:=\{\chi,F_A\}$, $\mathbf u_D:=\{\gamma,F_D\}$ (3); state $\mathbf z:=[\mathbf x,\mathbf y,\mathbf v]^\top$ (4); $\mathbf z_0$ (5).
- **Termination (6)**: $\psi(\mathbf z_f):=\sqrt{(x_{A_f}-x_{D_f})^2+(y_{A_f}-y_{D_f})^2}=0$ — capture at coincidence (no capture radius for $D$). Printed typo in the where-line: "$y_{D_f}=x_D(t_f)$" (p. 1852; should be $y_D(t_f)$).
- **Cost (7)–(9)**: Mayer form $J=\varphi(\mathbf z_f)= (x_{A_f}-x_T)^2+(y_{A_f}-y_T)^2$ (squared distance from $A$ to the target *center* at interception; $r_T$ plays no role in the game of degree); $V(\mathbf z_0):=\min_{\mathbf u_A}\max_{\mathbf u_D}J$.
- **Assumptions (p. 1852)**: (i) at maximum speed the force is 0 (coast); (ii) $t_f\ge\max\{\frac{v_{A\max}-v_{A0}}{F_{A\max}/m_A},\frac{v_{D\max}-v_{D0}}{F_{D\max}/m_D}\}$ (10) — both players finish accelerating before termination ("to make the formulas concise"); $t_f$ is free. Note 2 distinguishes the game of degree (GoD, payoff (7)) from the game of kind (GoK, §IV-B).
- **Apollonius circle (11)** (constant speeds, ratio $\beta=v_A/v_D<1$): $(x-x_c)^2+(y-y_c)^2=r_c^2$ with $x_c=\frac{1}{1-\beta^2}(x_A-\beta^2 x_D)$, $r_c=\frac{\beta}{1-\beta^2}\sqrt{(x_A-x_D)^2+(y_A-y_D)^2}$; interior = slower player's dominance region (Fig. 2: $v_D>v_A$, green = $D$'s region).

### 5.1 Theorem 1 (p. 1853, eqs. (13)–(16); FOTDDG (12), $v_D\neq v_A$)

**Statement.** Optimal state-feedback strategies (13): both players steer at the saddle point $Q^*$, $\cos\chi^*=\frac{x^*-x_A}{\sqrt{(x^*-x_A)^2+(y^*-y_A)^2}}$, $\cos\gamma^*=\frac{x^*-x_D}{\sqrt{(x^*-x_D)^2+(y^*-y_D)^2}}$ (and sine analogues). Saddle point (14): $x^*=x_c\Big(1-\frac{r_c}{\sqrt{x_c^2+y_c^2}}\Big)$, $y^*=y_c\Big(1-\frac{r_c}{\sqrt{x_c^2+y_c^2}}\Big)$, where (15) $x_c=\frac{1}{1-\alpha^2}(x_D-\alpha^2 x_A)$, $y_c=\frac{1}{1-\alpha^2}(y_D-\alpha^2 y_A)$, $r_c=\big|\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha^2}\big|\,\sqrt{(x_A-x_D)^2+(y_A-y_D)^2}$, $\alpha=v_D/v_A$. Value (16): $V_1=(r_c-\sqrt{x_c^2+y_c^2})^2$ — the squared Euclidean distance from $T$ (origin) to the Apollonius circle at ratio $\alpha$; $Q^*$ is the circumference point nearest $T$. All expressions are *global, closed-form, purely algebraic*; validity requires $T$ in $D$'s dominance region for the GoD to make sense (Note 2).

**Proof approach (steps, p. 1853–1855).**
1. Two-sided extremism principle (TSEP) + Euler equation [1], [10]: costates $\lambda$ and optimal headings constant, $\cos\chi^*=-\lambda_{x_A}/\|\lambda_A\|$, $\cos\gamma^*=\lambda_{x_D}/\|\lambda_D\|$, … (17).
2. Augmented terminal value (18) $\phi+\nu_1(x_{A_f}-x_{D_f})+\nu_2(y_{A_f}-y_{D_f})$; transversality $\lambda(t_f)=\partial\Phi/\partial\mathbf z$ (19); saddle point $Q^*(x^*,y^*)$ with $x^*=x_{A_f}=x_{D_f}$, $y^*=y_{A_f}=y_{D_f}$ (20); headings expressed through $Q^*$ (21).
3. Free $t_f$, autonomous Mayer problem ⇒ $\mathcal H|_{t_f}=0$ giving (22): $v_D/v_A=\|\lambda_A\|/\|\lambda_D\|$. Two unknown multipliers $\nu_1,\nu_2$ ⇒ "PMP does not work according to Isaacs' method" (p. 1853) — geometry is combined.
4. Relations (23)–(24) ($\lambda_{x_A}+\lambda_{x_D}=2x^*$ at $T$=origin); costates solved in terms of $Q^*$ (25); substituting into (22) yields the **decisive algebraic relation (26)**: $\frac{x^*y_D-y^*x_D}{y^*x_A-x^*y_A}=\frac{v_D^2}{v_A^2}$.
5. Parametrize the Apollonius circle (27) $x=x_c+r_c\cos\theta$, substitute into (26) → bottom-of-page identity (28) (ratio of two affine-in-$(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$ expressions $=1/\beta^2$), simplifying to **(29)**: $(1-\beta^2)y_c\cos\theta=(1-\beta^2)x_c\sin\theta\Rightarrow\theta=\arctan(y_c/x_c)$ — $Q^*$ lies on the line $TO_c$.
6. Case "$v_D>v_A$" (printed header reads "Case $v_A>v_D$", p. 1854 — typo, content and Fig. 2 caption fix it): $T$ outside the circle ⇒ $D$ wins; $Q^*$ = nearest circumference point via the quadratic system (30) (circle $\cap$ line $y=\frac{y_c}{x_c}x$) giving (31) $=(14)$. Case "$v_D<v_A$": $\alpha=v_D/v_A<1$, same saddle point, different circle parameters. Unified form for $\alpha\neq 1$: (15) with $\beta=1/\alpha$ substitution when $\alpha>1$.
7. **HJI/Nash verification** (pp. 1854–1855): value rewritten (32) $V_1(\mathbf z)=\big(\frac{\alpha d_{AD}-d_\alpha}{|1-\alpha^2|}\big)^2$ with $d_{AD}=\sqrt{(x_A-x_D)^2+(y_A-y_D)^2}$, $d_\alpha=\sqrt{(x_D-\alpha^2x_A)^2+(y_D-\alpha^2y_A)^2}$, $w=\frac{1}{|1-\alpha^2|}(\alpha d_{AD}-d_\alpha)$; gradients (33) and saddle-point increments (34); the HJI equation $\frac{\partial V_1}{\partial\mathbf z}f(\mathbf z,\chi^*,\gamma^*)=0$ reduces — for $\alpha>1$ — through (35) to $v_A\big[\alpha\sqrt{(\partial V_1/\partial x_D)^2+(\partial V_1/\partial y_D)^2}-\sqrt{(\partial V_1/\partial x_A)^2+(\partial V_1/\partial y_A)^2}\big]=0$, i.e. the $\alpha$-weighted gradient norms of $V_1$ w.r.t. the $A$- and $D$-coordinates are equal; (36) is the analogous identity for $\alpha<1$. Hence "the optimal strategies satisfy the HJI equation in both cases, so they are the Nash equilibrium solutions" (p. 1855).

**Physical meaning**: interior of the $(15)$-circle is the dominance region of the slower-side player under ratio $\alpha$; the attacker can guarantee nothing better than the closest point of that circle to $T$, the defender can force interception there. Extensibility: (14)–(15) is the "arbitrary ratio" template later re-instantiated at the average ratio $\bar\alpha$ in (47)–(49) and offset by $r_T$ in (68).

### 5.2 Theorem 2 (pp. 1855–1856, eqs. (37)–(38); degenerate case $v_D=v_A$)

**Statement.** Strategies (13) (head to the interception point); interception point (37): $x^*=\frac{1}{2}\frac{(x_A^2-x_D^2+y_A^2-y_D^2)(x_A-x_D)}{(y_D-y_A)^2+(x_D-x_A)^2}$, $y^*=\frac{1}{2}\frac{(x_A^2-x_D^2+y_A^2-y_D^2)(y_A-y_D)}{(y_D-y_A)^2+(x_D-x_A)^2}$; value (38): $V_1=\frac{1}{4}\frac{(x_A^2-x_D^2+y_A^2-y_D^2)^2}{(x_A-x_D)^2+(y_A-y_D)^2}$ — the squared distance from $T$ to the perpendicular bisector of $AD$ (the equal-speed dominance boundary, eq. (39)).

**Proof.** Perpendicular bisector (39) as dominance boundary (Fig. 4); substituting (39) into (26) gives (40)–(41); geometric shortest distance: $Q^*$ = bisector ∩ line through $T$ perpendicular to it (42); value (43); HJI verification (44) — note printed (44) uses coefficient $\frac{\alpha d_{AD}}{2w}$ in the $v_D$-numerator while its own denominator uses $\frac{d_{AD}}{2u}$ ($w$ undefined in this proof; $u:=\frac{1}{2}\frac{x_A^2-x_D^2+y_A^2-y_D^2}{d_{AD}}$ is defined) — a typo, should read $2u$ throughout. Decisive intermediate: (26) evaluated on the bisector.

### 5.3 Lemma 1 (p. 1856) — average-speed principle

If the optimal headings are constant, the headings are determined by the ratio of $D$'s and $A$'s **average speeds**: with constant headings both reach $Q^*$ simultaneously so $\frac{DQ^*}{AQ^*}=\frac{v_D}{v_A}$; Theorems 1–2 show the FOTDDG Nash equilibrium is one-to-one with the speed ratio; hence for second-order plants with constant optimal headings the equilibrium depends only on the ratio of average speeds. This is the linchpin transferring §III to §IV. (Proof is one paragraph — a geometric argument, no formal statement about off-equilibrium behavior.)

### 5.4 Lemma 2 (p. 1856, eq. (45)) — continuity/differentiability in the ratio

$x^*(\alpha),y^*(\alpha),V_1(\alpha)$ are continuous at the singular point $\alpha=1$ and differentiable for $\alpha\neq 1$. Proof: the limit is computed by L'Hôpital on $d_\alpha-\alpha d_{AD}$, $\lim_{\alpha\to1}x^*=\frac{x_A-x_D}{2d_{AD}^2}\big[2x_A(x_A-x_D)+2y_A(y_A-y_D)-d_{AD}^2\big]=\frac{(x_A^2-x_D^2+y_A^2-y_D^2)(x_A-x_D)}{2d_{AD}^2}$ (45), which equals Theorem 2's $x^*$; likewise $y^*$; $V=x^{*2}+y^{*2}$ inherits continuity and piecewise differentiability. This supplies the $C^1$-type regularity the authors invoke for the HJI prerequisite (Contribution 2).

### 5.5 Theorem 3 (pp. 1856–1858, eqs. (46)–(50), (65)–(67); SOTDDG main result)

**Statement.** Consider the SOTDDG (1), (6)–(9) with (2), (10). Optimal forces are **bang-bang** (46): $F_A^*=F_{A\max}$ for $t\le t_{A1}$, $0$ for $t>t_{A1}$; $F_D^*=F_{D\max}$ for $t\le t_{D1}$, $0$ after; $t_{A1}=\frac{v_{A\max}-v_{A0}}{F_{A\max}/m_A}$, $t_{D1}=\frac{v_{D\max}-v_{D0}}{F_{D\max}/m_D}$ (acceleration durations, as defined in the theorem statement). Optimal headings are given by (13) (constant, toward the saddle point); the saddle point depends on the initial configuration: **if $\bar\alpha\neq 1$** (47) $x^*=\tilde x_c\big(1-\frac{\tilde r_c}{\sqrt{\tilde x_c^2+\tilde y_c^2}}\big)$, $y^*=\tilde y_c\big(1-\frac{\tilde r_c}{\sqrt{\tilde x_c^2+\tilde y_c^2}}\big)$; **if $\bar\alpha=1$** (48) $=$ (37); where (49) $\tilde x_c=\frac{1}{1-\bar\alpha^2}(x_D-\bar\alpha^2 x_A)$, $\tilde y_c=\frac{1}{1-\bar\alpha^2}(y_D-\bar\alpha^2 y_A)$, $\tilde r_c=\big|\frac{\bar\alpha}{1-\bar\alpha^2}\big|\,d_{AD}$ — i.e. the Theorem 1 construction evaluated at the **average speed ratio** $\bar\alpha:=\tilde v_D/\tilde v_A$ (57). The optimal ratio is (50):
$$\bar\alpha^*=\begin{cases}\dfrac{s_{A1}+v_{D\max}(t_f-t_{A1})}{s_{D1}+v_{A\max}(t_f-t_{D1})}, & p>0\\[2pt] \dfrac{s_{D1}+v_{A\max}(t_{A1}-t_{D1})}{s_{A1}}, & p\le 0 \text{ and } t_{A1}\ge t_{D1}\\[2pt] \dfrac{s_{A1}+v_{D\max}(t_{D1}-t_{A1})}{s_{D1}}, & p\le 0 \text{ and } t_{D1}\ge t_{A1}\end{cases}$$
with $p=\frac{v_{D\max}^2-v_{D0}^2}{F_{D\max}}v_{D\max}-\frac{v_{A\max}^2-v_{A0}^2}{F_{A\max}}v_{A\max}+v_{D\max}v_{A\max}(t_{A1}-t_{D1})$ (50) (sign pattern as printed; see the subscript caveat below). "The value function is a continuous function." Value: if $\bar\alpha_{\max}\neq 1$, (65) $V=\Big[\frac{\bar\alpha d_{AD}-\sqrt{(x_D-\bar\alpha^2x_A)^2+(y_D-\bar\alpha^2y_A)^2}}{|1-\bar\alpha^2|}\Big]^2$; if $\bar\alpha_{\max}=1$, (66) $V=\frac{1}{4}\frac{(x_A^2-x_D^2+y_A^2-y_D^2)^2}{(x_D-x_A)^2+(y_D-y_A)^2}$.

**How speed variation is modeled.** Speeds are states; each player's motion is uniform acceleration ($F=F_{\max}$) then uniform motion (Assumption 1). Average speed $\tilde v_A=\frac{\frac{v_{A\max}^2-v_{A0}^2}{2F_{A\max}/m_A}+v_{A\max}t_{A2}}{t_m}$, $\tilde v_D$ analogously (61), where $t_m$ is the common travel time (60) and $t_{A2},t_{D2}$ the cruising durations; acceleration distances/times $s_{A1}=\frac{v_{A\max}^2-v_{A0}^2}{2F_{A\max}/m_A}$, etc. The whole problem is reduced to maximizing $\bar\alpha$ over the feasible travel time, because $\frac{\partial V}{\partial \tilde v_D}>0$, $\frac{\partial V}{\partial\tilde v_A}<0$ (58) and $\frac{\partial V}{\partial\bar\alpha}=\frac{\partial V}{\partial\tilde v_D}\tilde v_A=-\frac{\partial V}{\partial\tilde v_A}\frac{\tilde v_D}{\bar\alpha^2}$ (59) — a monotone 1-D problem ("the saddle point of $V$ in 2-D space is transformed into finding the maximum point in 1-D space", p. 1858).

**Proof approach (steps).**
1. Hamiltonian (51): $\mathcal H=\lambda_{x_A}v_A\cos\chi+\lambda_{y_A}v_A\sin\chi+\lambda_{x_D}v_D\cos\gamma+\lambda_{y_D}v_D\sin\gamma+\lambda_{v_A}F_A/m_A+\lambda_{v_D}F_D/m_D$.
2. TSEP: headings from costate directions as in (17); forces from switching logic (52): $F_A^*=0$ if $\lambda_{v_A}\ge0$, $F_A^*=F_{A\max}$ if $\lambda_{v_A}<0$; $F_D^*=F_{D\max}$ if $\lambda_{v_D}\ge0$, else $0$.
3. Costate dynamics (53): $\dot\lambda_{x_A}=\dot\lambda_{y_A}=\dot\lambda_{x_D}=\dot\lambda_{y_D}=0$ (position costates constant ⇒ **optimal headings constant**), $\dot\lambda_{v_A}=-\lambda_{x_A}\cos\chi-\lambda_{y_A}\sin\chi$, $\dot\lambda_{v_D}=-\lambda_{x_D}\cos\gamma-\lambda_{y_D}\sin\gamma$.
4. Transversality (54): $\lambda_{x_A}(t_f)=2(x_{A_f}-x_T)+\nu_1$, …, $\lambda_{v_A}(t_f)=\lambda_{v_D}(t_f)=0$; combining (52)–(54) reproduces (21) and gives **(55)**: $\dot\lambda_{v_A}=\sqrt{[2(x^*-x_T)+\nu_1]^2+[2(y^*-y_T)+\nu_2]^2}>0$, $\dot\lambda_{v_D}=-\sqrt{\nu_1^2+\nu_2^2}<0$ ⇒ $\lambda_{v_A}\le0$, $\lambda_{v_D}\ge0$ on $[0,t_f]$ ⇒ both players accelerate at full force until reaching $v_{\max}$, then coast (56). This is the decisive PMP step: it fixes the force channel *independently* of the geometry.
5. Average-speed bookkeeping (57)–(61); ratio as function of travel time (62) $\bar\alpha=\frac{\tilde v_D}{\tilde v_A}=\frac{s_{A1}+v_{D\max}(t_m-t_{A1})}{s_{D1}+v_{A\max}(t_m-t_{D1})}$ **as printed**; derivative (63) $\frac{d\bar\alpha}{dt_m}=\frac{s_{D1}v_{D\max}-s_{A1}v_{A\max}+v_{D\max}v_{A\max}(t_{A1}-t_{D1})}{[s_{D1}+v_{A\max}(t_m-t_{D1})]^2}$ (exactly the derivative of the printed (62)); maximum at the boundary of the feasible travel-time interval (64), piecewise: at $t_m=t_f$ if $p>0$ (ratio increasing), at $t_m=\max(t_{A1},t_{D1})$ if $p\le0$ (ratio decreasing).
6. Value function (65)–(66) by substituting $\bar\alpha=\bar\alpha_{\max}$ into the Theorem 1/2 templates.
7. **HJI/Nash verification (67)**: since $V$ does not contain $v_A,v_D$, $\partial V/\partial v_A=\partial V/\partial v_D=0$, and the 6-D HJI expression collapses to the 4-D FOTDDG form, which vanishes "According to (35), (36), and (44)".
8. Closing interpretation (p. 1858): the 6-D SOTDDG with four decision variables "degenerates to two simpler problems" — solving the controlling forces (done by PMP) and the 1-D extreme-value problem in $\bar\alpha$.

**Proof gap / subscript inconsistency (recorded precisely).** The definitions below (62) fix $s_{A1},t_{A1}$ = *attacker's* acceleration distance/time and $s_{D1},t_{D1}$ = *defender's*. Then (57)+(61) with $t_{A2}=t_m-t_{A1}$, $t_{D2}=t_m-t_{D1}$ logically give $\bar\alpha=\frac{s_{D1}+v_{D\max}(t_m-t_{D1})}{s_{A1}+v_{A\max}(t_m-t_{A1})}$, but printed (62) — and consistently (50), (63), (64) — pair $s_{A1},t_{A1}$ with $v_{D\max}$ in the numerator and $s_{D1},t_{D1}$ with $v_{A\max}$ in the denominator. (63) is the exact derivative of the printed (62), so the swap is systematic from (62) onward, not an isolated slip; in the 2nd/3rd branches of (50)/(64) the $v$-subscripts are additionally crossed (branch 3 as printed is even the reciprocal of the consistent value). The qualitative structure (monotonicity governed by $p$; maximizers at $t_f$ or at the acceleration boundary) is unaffected, but anyone re-implementing must repair the subscripts first. Related typo: in the proof, $t_{A1}=\frac{v_{A\max}-v_{A0}}{2F_{A\max}/m_A}$ (factor 2, p. 1858) contradicts the theorem statement (46) $\frac{v_{A\max}-v_{A0}}{F_{A\max}/m_A}$; the (46) version is the physically correct acceleration time.

### 5.6 Theorem 4 (pp. 1858–1859, eqs. (68)–(73); game of kind)

**Statement.** The barrier surface depends on initial conditions and is either a circle or a straight line. When $\bar\alpha_{\max}\neq 1$, (68) $\sqrt{(x-\tilde x_c)^2+(y-\tilde x_c)^2}=\frac{\bar\alpha_{\max}d_{AD}+(1-\bar\alpha_{\max}^2)r_T}{|1-\bar\alpha_{\max}^2|}$ — **as printed**; the consistent form obtained from (71)–(72) is $\sqrt{(x-\tilde x_c)^2+(y-\tilde y_c)^2}=\frac{\bar\alpha_{\max}d_{AD}+(\bar\alpha_{\max}^2-1)\,r_T}{|\bar\alpha_{\max}^2-1|}=\tilde r_c\pm r_T$ (two typos in (68): the $y$-center should be $\tilde y_c$; the $r_T$ term's sign follows (72)). When $\bar\alpha_{\max}=1$, (69) $(x_A-x_D)x+(y_A-y_D)y-\frac12(y_A^2-y_D^2+x_A^2-x_D^2)\pm d_{AD}r_T=0$ — the perpendicular bisector offset by the target radius; take the line closer to $D$. $D$ guarantees the target safe iff $T$ lies in $D$'s dominance region.

**Proof approach.** Tangency reasoning (Fig. 5): the dominance boundary at the frozen ratio $\bar\alpha_{\max}$ is the Apollonius circle (49); the critical condition for $D$'s success is tangency of the target disk with that boundary: success condition (70) $\sqrt{(x_T-\tilde x_c)^2+(y_T-\tilde x_c)^2}<\tilde r_c\pm r_T$ ("± corresponds to external and internal tangent relations"; note as printed the "$<$" direction only fits the $\bar\alpha_{\max}<1$ internal-tangency case — for $\bar\alpha_{\max}>1$, $D$'s region is the *exterior* and the inequality must reverse); replacing $(x_T,y_T)$ by $(x,y)$ defines the barrier (71); the radius offset unifies as (72) $\tilde r_c\pm r_T=\begin{cases}\frac{\bar\alpha_{\max}}{\bar\alpha_{\max}^2-1}d_{AD}+r_T,&\bar\alpha_{\max}>1\\ \frac{\bar\alpha_{\max}}{1-\bar\alpha_{\max}^2}d_{AD}-r_T,&\bar\alpha_{\max}<1\end{cases}=\frac{\bar\alpha_{\max}d_{AD}+(\bar\alpha_{\max}^2-1)r_T}{|\bar\alpha_{\max}^2-1|}$ (verified at 300-dpi zoom: no square roots — the $\bar\alpha_{\max}^2\pm1$ appear linearly). Equal-ratio case: distance from $T$ to the bisector equals $r_T$ (73). Universality claim (p. 1859): (68)–(69) hold "for an arbitrary motion process, rather than following Assumption 2", with $\bar\alpha_{\max}$ computed per (60)–(64) or analogously — asserted, not proven (see §7).

### 5.7 Extension to general formulation (§IV-C, p. 1859–1860, eqs. (74)–(78)) — sketch, no formal theorem

Dynamics (74): $\dot{\mathbf x}=\mathbf v\cos\mathbf u_1$, $\dot{\mathbf y}=\mathbf v\sin\mathbf u_1$, $\dot{\mathbf v}=g(\mathbf u_2,t)$ with $n$ attackers, $m$ defenders (75); pairwise terminal constraints (76) $x_{A_i}(t_f)=x_{D_j}(t_f)$; cost (77) $J=\sum_{i=1}^n\varphi_i(\mathbf z_f)$. Discussion: if $g(\mathbf u_2)$ does not depend on $\mathbf z$, position costates stay constant (Euler) ⇒ constant optimal headings $\mathbf u_1^*$ and bang-bang forces $\mathbf u_2^*$ (PMP); $V$ is determined by $\bar\alpha$ and the saddle point reduces to the 1-D extreme-point problem as in Theorem 3. MAMD: $n=m$ ⇒ Hungarian-algorithm matching into SASD games; $n<m<2n$ ⇒ SASD + DASD subgames (DASD solved by [7]); per-engagement $\bar\alpha$ follows Theorem 3's procedure. No proof, no barrier, and no experiment are given for this section (the $\mathbf u_D$ line in (78) also prints $n$-indexed lists where $m$ is expected).

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues

**Author-stated.** Analysis confined to the model where headings are controls and speeds obey $F/m$ dynamics (Note 1, p. 1852); Assumptions 1–2 (coast at $v_{\max}$; $t_f$ beyond both acceleration times) needed "to make the formulas concise"; future work: "more general forms of the velocity dynamics" and "more types of players" (p. 1860).

**Critical observations (with loci).**
1. **Systematic subscript swap in the core result** (50)/(62)–(64) (pp. 1857–1858): printed $\bar\alpha(t_m)$ contradicts (57)+(61) as detailed in §5.5; the closed-form $\bar\alpha_{\max}$ — the paper's central quantity — cannot be used verbatim. [verified by re-derivation]
2. **Factor-2 conflict** on $t_{A1},t_{D1}$ between (46) and the proof's definitions below (62) (p. 1858). [verified]
3. **Barrier typos**: (68) and (70)/(71) carry $(y-\tilde x_c)^2$ where $(y-\tilde y_c)^2$ is meant, and (68)'s $(1-\bar\alpha_{\max}^2)r_T$ contradicts (72)'s $(\bar\alpha_{\max}^2-1)r_T$ (the latter is the geometrically correct offset, checked against the tangency cases); (70)'s "$<$" is only valid for $\bar\alpha_{\max}<1$ — for $\bar\alpha_{\max}>1$, $D$'s dominance region is the circle's exterior and the success condition reverses. (pp. 1858–1859) [verified at 300 dpi]
4. **Case-label typo** in Theorem 1's proof: "Case $v_A>v_D$" (p. 1854) should be $v_D>v_A$ (content + Fig. 2 caption); the announcement "we will consider case $v_A>v_D$ and case $v_D<v_A$" states the same condition twice. Also (6)'s "$y_{D_f}=x_D(t_f)$" and (44)'s $2w/2u$ mixing. [verified]
5. **HJI verification gap for the SOTDDG** (67), p. 1858: the reduction to the FOTDDG identities (35), (36), (44) is valid when the speed ratio entering $V$'s formula equals the ratio in $\mathbf f$; with $V$ parameterized by the *frozen* $\bar\alpha$ but $\mathbf f$ using *instantaneous* $v_A(t),v_D(t)$, the cancelling identity (gradient-norm ratio $=\bar\alpha$ vs. speed ratio $v_D/v_A$) fails during acceleration transients. The Nash-property argument is therefore heuristic at the second-order level; no verification theorem / viscosity argument is supplied. [critical reading]
6. **Circularity in the $p>0$ branch** of (50)/(64): $\bar\alpha_{\max}$ is written in terms of the free terminal time $t_f$, which itself depends on $\bar\alpha$ through the saddle-point geometry (47) and the average speeds (61); existence/uniqueness of this fixed point, and the admissible set of $t_m$ in the $p\le0$ branches, are not analyzed. The "analytical solution" is thus closed-form up to one implicit scalar equation in general. [critical reading]
7. **"State feedback" is nominal**: optimality is proven (via costates) only along constant-heading, clock-triggered-force trajectories computed from the initial configuration ("The coordinates of the saddle point depend on the initial configurations", p. 1856); off-equilibrium feedback optimality of (13)/(46) is not established. [critical reading]
8. **Modeling narrowness of "second-order"**: heading is a direct control (no turn-rate/heading dynamics), so missile/UAV motivation [16], [20] overstates physical fidelity; no capture radius for $D$ ($\psi=0$, (6)); the GoD payoff measures distance to the target *center*, so $r_T$ influences only the GoK barrier; forces are never negative in (52) although (2) allows $|F|\le F_{\max}$ (no braking; TSEP case $\lambda_{v_A}\ge0\Rightarrow F_A^*=0$ is inconsistent with a two-sided force set). [critical reading]
9. **Extension §IV-C is declarative**: no theorem, no barrier, no matching-quality analysis for the Hungarian/DASD decomposition; interaction effects (e.g., one defender facing two simultaneous attackers) are assumed away. [critical reading]
10. **Empirical thinness**: two single-run figures, no baseline, no metrics (§6). [verified]
