## 2. Research Background

Multi-agent pursuit-evasion games (PEGs) model one team cooperating to capture rivals and have applications in industrial manufacturing, autonomous driving, spacecraft rendezvous/interception and military warfare (p. 1, refs. [1–5]). Classical differential-game treatments require solving the Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman–Isaacs (HJI) equation, which "is difficult to get the analytical solution" (p. 2, ref. [20]); geometric tools — barriers [11], dominant regions [12–14], and especially the Apollonius circle, which is "real-time and effective" (p. 1, ref. [15]) — give tractable closed-form constructs, and Apollonius-based encirclement formations were previously used mostly for **faster-evader** scenarios (refs. [16, 17]). On the learning side, value-factorization methods such as QMIX [30] handle cooperative multi-agent credit assignment under the centralized-training/decentralized-execution (CTDE) paradigm. This paper combines the two: an Apollonius-circle partition of the state space into dominant regions supplies an *analytic* evader strategy and a *shaping signal* (the evader's surviving dominant area) for a role-based QMIX pursuit policy, with a potential-game layer added to certify Nash equilibrium (NE) existence.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap

**Problem.** $n\ge 2$ pursuers $P_i$ with constant speed $v_p$ cooperate to capture a single, **slower** evader $E$ with constant speed $v_e$ ($\alpha=v_e/v_p<1$, constant), all with simple-motion kinematics (eq. (1), p. 3); positions and speeds are public, strategies private (p. 1). Pursuers must guarantee capture by shrinking the evader's dominant region $S$ to a threshold (Objective 3, p. 3); the evader maximizes survival time by always running to the farthest point of its current dominant region (Problem 1, eq. (2), p. 3); pursuers jointly maximize a cooperative aggregation $\mathbf F$ of sub-objectives $f_i(R_e^t, a_e, a_i)$ (Problem 2, eq. (3), p. 3).

**Gap [authors' claims] vs [verified]**:
1. *[Claim, p. 1–2]* Apollonius-circle methods were used "for faster evader scenarios in most cases" ([16, 17]); this paper handles faster pursuers with a wise evader that counter-strategizes from its dominant region. **[Verified]** [16] "Pursuit-evasion games of high speed evader" (J. Intell. Robot. Syst. 2017) and [17] "Pursuit strategy to capture high-speed evaders using multiple pursuers" (JGCD 2017) are indeed high-speed-evader encirclement works; the speed-role flip is real, though the slower-evader case is the geometrically *easier* one (single Apollonius disk per pursuer contains the evader).
2. *[Claim, p. 2]* "a more efficient neighbor-identifying algorithm than [31]" (Makkapati & Tsiotras, Apollonius allocation, 2020) is proposed for reducing cooperative pursuit time. **[Verified]** No complexity analysis, runtime measurement, or head-to-execution comparison with [31] appears anywhere in the paper — the efficiency claim is unsupported as printed.
3. *[Claim, p. 2]* Potential-game theory was previously applied to cooperative anti-jamming ([19]); extending it to PEGs "expands the application scenarios of the potential game", with NE existence proved. **[Verified]** [19] is a multi-leader one-follower Stackelberg anti-jamming paper (IEEE access-style, 2018); the potential-game-for-PEG transfer is plausible as a gap statement, and the NE proof is given (Theorem 2), but see Section 5 for proof-precision caveats.
4. *[Claim, p. 2]* HJI is analytically intractable, so MARL is adopted for pursuer strategies. **[Verified]** Standard motivation; the paper does not actually solve or approximate any HJI equation, so this is positioning rather than a comparative result.
- **Caveat**: the gap is a *composition* — Apollonius dominant regions (classical, e.g. [7, 15, 31]), ordinal potential games (Monderer–Shapley [32, 35], Lasaulce–Tembine [33]), and QMIX [30] are all established; the contribution is their integration plus the role/reward design, not new geometric or game-theoretic theory.

## 4. Core Contributions

1. **Theory (geometry)**: Characterization of the evader's optimal fixation aimpoint within the Apollonius-partition dominant region — it lies on a two-circle boundary intersection $B_i\cap B_j$ (eq. (15)) or on the ray extension $B_i\cap L(\mathbf x_e,\mathbf x_{\mathbf O_i})$ (eq. (16)) — with a geometric proof (**Theorem 1**, p. 5, Appendix A).
2. **Theory (game theory)**: Formulation of the multi-pursuer/single-evader PEG as an **ordinal potential game** with potential $\Phi$ (eq. (22)) and proof of NE existence for $N_e=1, N_p\ge 2$ (**Theorem 2**, p. 6, Appendix B, eqs. (22)–(28)); equilibrium reachability via the finite improvement property (FIP) [35].
3. **Theory (guarantee)**: Finite-time capture certificate via monotone decrease of the current shortest capture time (CSCT), $\mathrm{d}t_c/\mathrm{d}t<0$ (**Theorem 3**, p. 6, Appendix C).
4. **Algorithms**: (a) Algorithm 1 — $\mathbf N_{\mathbf{ep}}$ neighbor identification (which Apollonius circles actually shape the evader's region), claimed more efficient than [31]; (b) Algorithm 2 — evader aimpoint selection via farthest-point and pairwise-intersection enumeration (eqs. (10)–(14)); (c) Algorithm 3 — dominant-area $S_t$ computation (shoelace polygon + circular segments, Lemma 2 [34]); (d) Algorithm 4 — **role-based backbone QMIX**: pursuers outside $\mathbf N_{\mathbf{ep}}$ freeze (eq. (21)), reward $R=\max_i U_i$ by eq. (17), trained with the QMIX loss (eq. (18)).
5. **Experiment**: Simulations on the authors' original **APES** platform (agents-based pursuit-evasion simulation platform with an integrated DRL framework, p. 6): evader fixation vs self-learned DQN evader (2v1), Apollonius-area pursuit vs greedy distance-minimizing pursuit, and 2v1/4v1/6v1 QMIX training over 20 000 episodes (Figs. 4–11).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results (MOST IMPORTANT)

### 5.0 Model, information structure, reward, termination (Sections 2–4, pp. 3–6)

- **Dynamics** (eq. (1), p. 3): $\dot x_i = v_p\cos\theta_i$, $\dot y_i = v_p\sin\theta_i$, $\dot x_e = v_e\cos\theta_e$, $\dot y_e = v_e\sin\theta_e$ — constant-speed simple motion; controls are the instantaneous heading angles $\theta_i,\theta_e$; $\alpha = v_e/v_p <1$ constant.
- **Information structure**: positions and speeds shared globally ("location and speed information are shared, but the respective strategies are private", p. 1); the evader's strategy is **scripted** (fixation, Section 3.2), only pursuers are learned. Neighbor indicator $\delta_i = 1$ iff $i\in\mathbf N_{\mathbf{ep}}$, the set of pursuers whose Apollonius circles contribute to the evader's current dominant region (p. 2).
- **Apollonius partition** (eqs. (6)–(9), pp. 3–4): for each pursuer $i$, the Apollonius circle $\{(x,y):(x-cx_i)^2+(y-cy_i)^2 = r_i^2\}$ (eq. (7)) with
  $$cx_i=\tfrac{1}{1-\alpha^2}(x_e-\alpha^2 x_i),\qquad cy_i=\tfrac{1}{1-\alpha^2}(y_e-\alpha^2 y_i),\qquad r_i=\tfrac{\alpha}{1-\alpha^2}\sqrt{(x_e-x_i)^2+(y_e-y_i)^2}\quad (8)$$
  is the locus where evader and pursuer $i$ arrive simultaneously; the evader's dominant region (disk membership, eq. (6)) is the **intersection of all $n$ disks**:
  $$R_e^t=\{\mathbf x\mid (x-cx_i)^2+(y-cy_i)^2\le r_i^2,\ \forall i\in N_p\}\quad (9)$$
  i.e., points the evader reaches no later than any pursuer. Fig. 3 (p. 4) shows $R_e^t$ for 2 and 4 pursuers: with 2 pursuers only the interior circle matters; with 4, all circles contribute — $R_e^t$ depends on time **and** on $|\mathbf N_{\mathbf{ep}}|$.
- **Utility / reward** (eq. (17), p. 5): $U_i=-\big[(1-\delta_i)\|\mathbf x_i(t)-\mathbf x_i(t-1)\|_2 + S_t\big]$, where $S_t$ = area of $R_e^t$ at time $t$. Non-neighbor pursuers are additionally frozen (eq. (21), p. 6: $S'_{x_i}=S_{x_i}$, $S'_{y_i}=S_{y_i}$ if $i\notin\mathbf N_{\mathbf{ep}}$), so their movement cost is 0; neighbors pay no movement cost ($\delta_i=1$). Consequently **every** $U_i = -S_t$ and the team reward of Section 4 item 3 (p. 6), $R=\max\{U_1,\dots,U_n\}$, equals $-S_t$: the "new approach to designing rewards compared to traditional QMIX" (p. 2) is, in effect, a negative-dominant-area reward.
- **Pursuer action space** (eq. (19), p. 6): $\theta_i = 90\,k + 6\,n$, $n\in A_i=\{0,1,\dots,14\}$, $k\in\{0,1,2,3\}$ set by the evader's relative azimuth — 15 discrete actions; next position $S'_{x_i}=S_{x_i}+\cos(\theta_i/180^\circ\pi)v_p$, $S'_{y_i}=S_{y_i}+\sin(\theta_i/180^\circ\pi)v_p$ (eq. (20)). Evader action set: continuous headings $\{0^\circ,\dots,360^\circ\}$ from the global state.
- **Termination**: capture when the evader's dominant region "converges to a small threshold value" (Objective 3, p. 3; restated p. 5) — the numerical threshold is **never reported**.
- **QMIX training** (eq. (18), p. 6): $L(\theta)=\sum_{i=1}^{b}\big(y_i^{tot}-Q_{tot}(\boldsymbol\tau,\mathbf u,s;\theta)\big)^2$, $y^{tot}=r+\gamma\max_{\mathbf u'}Q_{tot}(\boldsymbol\tau',\mathbf u',s';\theta^-)$ with target-network parameters $\theta^-$; CTDE, joint action–value $Q_{tot}$ mixed monotonically from per-agent $Q_i$ (QMIX [30]). Inputs: "joint observation of $i$th pursuer and evader, and previous action" (p. 2).

### 5.1 Theorem 1 (p. 5, eqs. (15)–(16); proof Appendix A, pp. 9–10)

**Statement.** *Algorithm 2 guarantees the evader's optimal reaction, heading to its current farthest safe point, to extend its life. The point $\mathbf x_t$ farthest from the evader in its dominant region at time $t\ge 0$ satisfies*
$$\mathbf x_t = B_i\cap B_j,\ i\in A_{ep},\ j\in A_{ep}\quad(15)\qquad\text{or}\qquad \mathbf x_t = B_i\cap L(\mathbf x_e,\mathbf x_{\mathbf O_i}),\ i\in A_{ep}\quad(16)$$
where $B_i$ is the $i$th Apollonius boundary (eq. (7)) and $L(\mathbf x_e,\mathbf x_{\mathbf O_i})$ is the extension of the segment from the evader position through the Apollonius center $O_i$.
- **Assumptions**: current-state Apollonius partition (eqs. (7)–(9)); $\alpha<1$; the "dominant region" is the intersection (9) whose boundary consists of arcs of the active circles.
- **Proof approach** (Appendix A): purely geometric. Key property: the line $L_i$ through $P_i$ and $E$ is the **perpendicular bisector** of any chord $M_i$ of circle $i$ drawn perpendicular to it (since $O_i$ lies on $EP_i$ by eq. (8)). For the 2v1 case (Fig. 12 left): with $Q_1,Q_2$ the vertices of $R_e^t$, case analysis on directed arcs — if free point $m$ lies on arc $\widehat{Q_1 b}$, $\max_m\|\mathbf x_E-\mathbf x_m\|_2=\|\mathbf x_E-\mathbf x_{Q_1}\|_2$; if on arc $\widehat{bc}$, $\max=\|\mathbf x_E-\mathbf x_c\|_2$ with monotone increase along $\widehat{bc}$; if on arc $\widehat{cQ_2}$, monotone decrease — where $c$ is on the extension of $EO_i$. Decisive intermediate equalities: $\|\mathbf x_E-\mathbf x_b\|_2=\|\mathbf x_E-\mathbf x_{Q_1}\|_2$ (first circle) and $\|\mathbf x_E-\mathbf x_{Q_1}\|_2=\|\mathbf x_E-\mathbf x_a\|_2$ (second circle), both from the perpendicular-bisector property. Conclusion: the maximum over the boundary is attained at a **vertex** (two-circle intersection, eq. (15)) or at the **ray point** (eq. (16)); the general $n$-pursuer case repeats the argument per boundary arc between consecutive vertices $Q_k\in B_i\cap B_j\cap R_e^t$ (Fig. 12 middle/right).
- **Scope**: global maximum over the *current* region; analytic; recomputed each time step (receding-horizon/greedy fixation — not a trajectory-level optimum over a horizon).
- **Physical meaning**: the evader's best instant escape direction is either toward a "corner" of its surviving region (where two pursuers arrive simultaneously) or straight away from the nearest shaping pursuer along the center ray.
- **Extensibility**: the characterization is model-independent for any equal-time-partition geometry; it feeds Algorithm 2 (candidate set $C_N$ = per-circle farthest points via eqs. (10)–(11); $C_M$ = pairwise intersections in $R_e^t$ via eqs. (13)–(14); $A=\max$ over candidates of distance from $\mathbf x_e$).
- **Proof gap**: (i) the index set $A_{ep}$ in eqs. (15)–(16) is **never defined** (presumably $=\mathbf N_{\mathbf{ep}}$; notation slip); (ii) the $n$-pursuer extension is argued "with a specific scenario in Fig. 12 (middle and right)" — the arc decomposition is by picture, not an exhaustive combinatorial case proof; (iii) arc monotonicity of $\|\mathbf x_E-\mathbf x_m\|_2$ is asserted, though it is elementary (distance to a fixed point along a circle is unimodal on any arc not containing the antipode of the ray point).

### 5.2 Lemma 1 [19], Lemma 2 [34], Lemma 3 [31] (cited results, pp. 3, 5, 6)

- **Lemma 1** (p. 3, cited to [19]): every potential game admits at least one NE. Provides the bridge from "the game is an ordinal potential game" (Theorem 2) to NE existence. (The canonical source for this fact is Monderer–Shapley [35], also cited.)
- **Lemma 2** (p. 5, cited to [34] = Boyd–Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization): (1) intersections of convex sets are convex; (2) the overlapping region of multiple circles is composed of circular arcs. Used to justify Algorithm 3's decomposition of $R_e^t$ into a polygon (shoelace) plus circular-segment areas.
- **Lemma 3** (p. 6, cited to [31]): if a pursuer is active at $t'$ it need not remain active for $t>t'$, but a pursuer **redundant** at $t'$ stays redundant for all $t>t'$. Justifies the role-allocation step of Algorithm 4 (freeze outsiders once redundant).

### 5.3 Theorem 2 (p. 6; proof Appendix B, pp. 9–10, eqs. (22)–(28))

**Statement.** *There exists a Nash equilibrium for the cooperative-pursuit evasion game $G=[N,A,U]$ with a potential function $\phi$, where $A=(a_i,a_{-i},a_e)$, $N=N_e\cup N_p$, if $N_e=1$, $N_p\ge 2$.*
- **Definitions used** (p. 3): NE (eq. (4)): $U_i(\delta_i,a_i^*,a_{-i}^*,a_e^*)\ge U_i(\delta_i,a_i,a_{-i}^*,a_e^*)$ and $U_e(\delta_i,a_e^*,a_{-e}^*)\ge U_e(\delta_i,a_e,a_{-e}^*)$; ordinal potential game (eq. (5)): $\mathrm{sgn}\big(\phi(a_i,a_{-i},a_e)-\phi(a_i',a_{-i},a_e')\big)=\mathrm{sgn}\big(U_i(a_i,a_{-i},a_e)-U_i(a_i',a_{-i},a_e')\big)$ for all $a_i\ne a_i'$ — note the nonstandard quantification in which the **evader simultaneously re-optimizes** ($a_e\to a_e'$) when pursuer $i$ deviates ("evader also changes its action from $a_e$ to $a_e'$ to achieve its goal", p. 3).
- **Proof approach** (Appendix B): candidate potential (eq. (22), p. 9):
  $$\Phi(a_i,a_{-i},a_e)=-\Big[\sum_{i\in N_p}(1-\delta_i)\|\mathbf x_i(t)-\mathbf x_i(t-1)\|_2 + n*S\Big]$$
  Splitting the sum over $\mathbf N_{\mathbf{ep}}$ and its complement and using $\delta_i=1$ on $\mathbf N_{\mathbf{ep}}$ plus the freeze rule eq. (21) for the complement, all movement terms vanish and (eq. (23), p. 10) $\Phi=-\big[\sum_{i\notin\mathbf N_{\mathbf{ep}}}\|\mathbf x_i(t)-\mathbf x_i(t-1)\|_2 + n*S\big]$; after a unilateral deviation of pursuer $i$ with evader best-responding, (eq. (24)) $\Phi(a_i',a_{-i},a_e')=-\big[\sum_{i\notin\mathbf N_{\mathbf{ep}}}\|\cdot\|_2+n*S'\big]$, whence the **decisive intermediate equation**
  $$\Phi(a_i,a_{-i},a_e)-\Phi(a_i',a_{-i},a_e')=n*(S'-S)\quad(25)$$
  On the utility side (eqs. (26)–(27)), for $i\in\mathbf N_{\mathbf{ep}}$ ($\delta_i=1$): $U_i(a_i,\cdot)-U_i(a_i',\cdot)=S'-S$. Since $n>0$, (eq. (28)) $\mathrm{sgn}(\Delta\Phi)=\mathrm{sgn}(\Delta U_i)$ — the ordinal-potential sign condition holds, so by Lemma 1 (and FIP [35] for finiteness) a NE exists.
- **Scope**: pointwise (single-step) comparison at fixed $t$; analytic; requires $N_p\ge 2$ (with $N_p=1$ the potential degenerates) and $N_e=1$ (single evader).
- **Physical meaning**: all neighbor pursuers' utilities are the *same monotone function* $-S$ of the evader's surviving dominant area, so any unilateral improvement for one pursuer improves the common potential — pure incentive alignment; the potential is effectively $\Phi\equiv -nS_t$ up to identically-zero movement terms.
- **Extensibility**: the construction (potential = common team objective scaled by team size) transfers to any cooperative game whose individual utilities share a common team-level statistic.
- **Proof gaps / imprecisions**: (i) eq. (26) contains the undefined notation $\|\mathbf x_i(t)-\mathbf x_i(t-1)\|_2'$; (ii) the proof keeps the neighbor set $\mathbf N_{\mathbf{ep}}$ **fixed** across the deviation ($\delta_i=1$ before and after), but a changed $a_i'$ can change the partition and hence membership; (iii) deviations of pursuers *outside* $\mathbf N_{\mathbf{ep}}$ are handled only verbally ("the $i$th pursuer's action will not influence the pursuers' decision who are not belong to $\mathbf N_{\mathbf{ep}}$", p. 9) — for such $i$, $S'=S$ and $\Delta U_i=\Delta\Phi=0$, which is consistent but not written out; (iv) the evader's own utility never enters the sign condition — the verified object is a potential game over the **pursuer team** with the evader clamped to best response, so the full two-team NE claim rests on this asymmetric, best-response-potential reading of Definition 2; the paper does not discuss the evader's payoff alignment with $\Phi$.

### 5.4 Theorem 3 (p. 6; proof Appendix C, pp. 10–11)

**Statement.** *Pursuers' cooperative strategies guarantee capture in finite time under Algorithm 4, where the evader takes the fixation strategy.*
- **Proof approach** (Appendix C): reduction + monotonicity. If several pursuers capture simultaneously their CSCT (current shortest capture time) coincides, so it suffices to analyze one pursuer. Geometry (Fig. 13, p. 10): with $O$ the center of the $i$th Apollonius circle and $M$ the capture point, "pursuer, evader, center $O$ and capture point $M$ are all on the same line". CSCT:
  $$t_c=\frac{\|\mathbf x_M-\mathbf x_e\|_2}{v_e}=\frac{\|\mathbf x_M-\mathbf x_i\|_2}{v_p}=\frac{r+\|\mathbf x_e-\mathbf x_O\|_2}{v_e}=\frac{r+\|\mathbf x_i-\mathbf x_O\|_2}{v_p}$$
  With $v_e=\alpha v_p$ and collinearity ($\|\mathbf x_i-\mathbf x_O\|=\|\mathbf x_i-\mathbf x_e\|+\|\mathbf x_e-\mathbf x_O\|$), the decisive intermediate chain is
  $$(1-\alpha)\big(r+\|\mathbf x_e-\mathbf x_O\|_2\big)=\alpha\|\mathbf x_i-\mathbf x_e\|_2\ \Rightarrow\ \|\mathbf x_e-\mathbf x_O\|_2=\tfrac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\|\mathbf x_i-\mathbf x_e\|_2-r$$
  so the radius cancels and $t_c=\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\frac{\|\mathbf x_i-\mathbf x_e\|_2}{v_e}$, giving $\frac{\mathrm{d}t_c}{\mathrm{d}t}=\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\frac{1}{v_e}\frac{\mathrm{d}\|\mathbf x_i-\mathbf x_e\|_2}{\mathrm{d}t}$. Differentiating the distance and substituting $v_e=\alpha v_p$, $\theta_e=\theta_i$,
  $$\frac{\mathrm{d}\|\mathbf x_i-\mathbf x_e\|_2}{\mathrm{d}t}=\frac{(x_i-x_e)(v_p-v_e)\frac{1}{\cos\theta_e}}{\sqrt{(x_i-x_e)^2+(y_i-y_e)^2}}$$
  using $(y_i-y_e)=(x_i\tan\theta_i-x_e\tan\theta_e)$ and $\mathrm{sgn}(x_i-x_e)=-\mathrm{sgn}(\cos\theta_e)$ (same-direction motion), hence
  $$\frac{\mathrm{d}t_c}{\mathrm{d}t}=-\Big|\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\frac{1}{v_e}\Big|\cdot\frac{\big|(x_i-x_e)(v_p-v_e)\frac{1}{\cos\theta_e}\big|}{\sqrt{(x_i-x_e)^2+(y_i-y_e)^2}}<0\quad(\text{p. 11})$$
  Strict monotone decrease of CSCT under the engagement geometry ⇒ capture in finite time.
- **Assumptions**: $0<\alpha<1$; $v_p>v_e>0$; **same-direction motion** $\theta_e=\theta_i$ (line-of-sight/head-on geometry); $\cos\theta_e\ne 0$; collinearity of $P_i,E,O,M$.
- **Scope**: analytic but geometry-conditional; the capture criterion proved (capture-point distance shrinking) is not formally linked to the operational termination rule of the algorithm (dominant-area $S_t$ below a threshold, Objective 3).
- **Physical meaning**: under the fixation evader and line-of-sight pursuit, every step shortens the worst-case remaining capture time — the standard Apollonius capture argument dressed as a Lyapunov-like monotonicity.
- **Extensibility**: the CSCT-monotonicity template applies to any constant-speed-ratio PE pair with straight-line engagement; extending to learned (non-line-of-sight) pursuer policies would require a different argument.
- **Proof gaps**: (i) the substitution $(y_i-y_e)=x_i\tan\theta_i-x_e\tan\theta_e$ (p. 10) drops the initial-position offsets $(y_i^0-y_e^0)$ and $x$-offsets; it is valid only if both players' straight-line paths are rays through the coordinate origin, or if positions are measured along the common line of sight — neither is stated; (ii) $\theta_e=\theta_i$ holds exactly only when both players move along the line joining them; the evader's fixation heading is along the ray $E\to O_i$ (which **is** collinear with $EP_i$ by eq. (8), so evader heading is indeed along $\pm(x_i-x_e)$), but the *learned* pursuer heading equals $\theta_e$ only if training converges to pure pursuit — the proof silently assumes the learned policy attains this geometry; (iii) the simultaneous-capture CSCT-equality reduction is asserted, not proved; (iv) $\mathrm{sgn}(x_i-x_e)=-\mathrm{sgn}(\cos\theta_e)$ is a positional-configuration claim (evader ahead of pursuer along the heading) that holds on the line-of-sight geometry but is stated as a blanket fact.

### 5.5 The four algorithms (pp. 4–6) — how the Apollonius partition enters the MARL loop

- **Algorithm 1 ($\mathbf N_{\mathbf{ep}}$ neighbor-identifying, p. 4)**: input circles $(\mathbf c=(cx,cy),\mathbf r,n)$; start from $L=B_i$ and iteratively keep only boundary points $L'=\{L[k]\mid\|L[k],\mathbf c[j]\|_2\le r[j]\}$ inside every other circle; pursuer $i$ is a neighbor (status=True) iff some of its boundary survives all inclusions. This implements "find which parts of Apollonius boundaries are contained by all the other Apollonius circles" (p. 4). Note the output line prints a scalar `status` although the pseudo-code manipulates per-pursuer flags $s[j]$ (minor inconsistency).
- **Algorithm 2 (aimpoint selection, p. 5)**: for each neighbor circle, farthest point from $E$ on that circle along the line $y-cy_i=k(x-cx_i)$, $k=(cy_i-y_e)/(cx_i-x_e)$ (eq. (10)), or $x=cx_i$ if $k$ does not exist (eq. (11)) — i.e., the ray point of Theorem 1; pairwise intersections of neighbor circles via eq. (12)–(14) ($D=\sqrt{(cx_2-cx_1)^2+(cy_2-cy_1)^2}$, and the standard two-circle intersection formulas, verified correct against $x=\frac{r_1^2-r_2^2+D^2}{2D}$ + perpendicular offset $h$); keep candidates inside $R_e^t$; return the candidate farthest from $\mathbf x_e$. **Typo as printed (p. 4)**: "given center of each circle $O_1(cx_1,cx_1),O_2(cx_2,cx_2)$" — should be $(cx_1,cy_1),(cx_2,cy_2)$.
- **Algorithm 3 (dominant-area computation, p. 5)**: cases — no intersections $\Gamma$: $S=\pi\min(r_1,r_2)^2$ (smaller disk contained); $\mathrm{card}[\Gamma]\le 2$: sector/triangle decomposition with the chord–center-line crossing test; more intersections: clockwise-sorted shoelace polygon $A_1$ plus per-circle circular-segment areas $A_2$ ($area2=2\arcsin(x_c/2r_c)r_c^2/2-\frac12 r_c^2\sin(2\arcsin(x_c/2r_c))$), $S=A_1+A_2$. Grounded in Lemma 2 [34]. This supplies the scalar $S_t$ inside utility eq. (17) and hence the MARL reward.
- **Algorithm 4 (role-based backbone QMIX, p. 6)**: per episode and step — (i) determine $R_e^t$ by eq. (9); (ii) evader moves to its Algorithm-2 aimpoint; (iii) roles by Algorithm 1; (iv) pursuers choose actions $u\in A$ from the $Q$ set of the evaluate agent network; (v) instant reward $R=\max(r_i),\forall i\in N_p$ "by equation (17)"; (vi) store transition in buffer $D$ (capacity $M$); (vii) update by eq. (18); target networks updated every $p$ steps; zero-padding after `done`; step limit $L$, $K$ training runs. The **Apollonius partition enters the MARL algorithm in three places**: the reward ($S_t$ from Algorithm 3), the role mask (Algorithm 1 → eq. (21) freezing redundant pursuers, implementing Lemma 3), and the scripted opponent (Algorithm 2 fixation evader inside the environment dynamics). FIP of the ordinal potential game [35] is invoked to argue that the optimum-reaction-based Algorithm 4 converges to NE (p. 6) — an argument by analogy, not a convergence proof of the deep-learning dynamics (no statement connecting gradient TD learning to FIP is attempted).

## 6. Simulation and Experiments

- **Platform**: APES, the authors' original agents-based pursuit-evasion simulation platform integrated with a deep-RL framework; environments randomly generated; initial positions change every episode (pp. 6–7). No hardware experiments; no external benchmark environment (e.g., StarCraft II SMAC despite citing QMIX's SMAC success).
- **Parameters AS PRINTED**: $v_p=6$, $v_e=3$, $\alpha=v_e/v_p=0.5$ (from [7], p. 6). Evader action set $\{0^\circ,\dots,360^\circ\}$; pursuer action set $A_i=\{0,1,\dots,14\}$ with $\theta_i=90k+6n$ (eq. (19)). QMIX hyperparameters: buffer capacity $M$, training number $K$, step limit $L$, target-net update period $p$ appear **symbolically only** (Algorithm 4); learning rate, discount $\gamma$, batch size $b$, network/mixer architecture, $\epsilon$-greedy schedule: **UNREPORTED**. Capture threshold on $S_t$: **UNREPORTED**. Domain size/bounds: **UNREPORTED**. Training length: **20 000 episodes** for the QMIX scenarios (p. 7, Figs. 8–9).
- **Experiments and claim→figure mapping**:
  1. **Evader strategy (2v1)** — Fig. 4 (p. 7): average capture steps per 100-episode block, pursuers fixed; DQN-self-learned evader is caught at ~**17th step** on average vs ~**50 steps** survival under Algorithm 2 (fixation). Validates: the Apollonius-partition fixation strategy "extends its life to a great extent" (p. 2). Metric: average steps; baseline: DQN evader.
  2. **Pursuer strategy (2v1)** — Fig. 5 (p. 7): evader fixed to Algorithm 2; greedy distance-minimizing pursuit catches at ~**51st step** vs ~**49 steps** for the Algorithm-3 (dominant-area-minimizing) strategy, over 1000 training episodes with 300-episode averaging. Validates: "pursuer can take fewer time steps ... using the strategies with Algorithm 3" (p. 7) — a marginal (~4%) improvement; the x-axis label "episodes*600" is inconsistent with the stated 300-episode buckets over 1000 episodes.
  3. **Backbone QMIX training (2v1/4v1/6v1)** — Figs. 8–9 (p. 8): 20 000 episodes; episode steps converge after ~1000 episodes to ~**32** (2v1), ~**18** (4v1), ~**14** (6v1); rewards (negative, from eq. (17) structure) converge to ≈ **−20** (2v1, visibly unstable), ≈ **−4** (4v1), ≈ **−2.5** (6v1). Validates: scalability/robustness claim "the more pursuers, the algorithm has better performance on robustness and stability" (p. 7) and the role-based reward design (p. 2). Figs. 6–7 (p. 8): qualitative snapshots of 4v1 and 6v1 encirclement with Apollonius circles drawn per pursuer.
  4. **Motion process (4v1)** — Figs. 10–11 (pp. 9): $P_4$ (far initial position) becomes redundant after few steps; $P_2$ stops when the evader moves away; $P_1$ and $P_3$ capture "almost simultaneously"; within-episode reward (Fig. 11) rises monotonically from ≈ −0.5 toward 0 as $S_t$ shrinks. Validates: role switching (Lemma 3 in action), dominant-region shrinkage (Objective 3), and reward–termination coupling.
- **Baselines used**: DQN evader (for the evader-side comparison), greedy distance-minimizing pursuers (for the pursuer-side comparison). **Missing baselines**: vanilla QMIX without role/reward design, VDN, MAPPO, or the [31] Apollonius-allocation algorithm — so neither the "more efficient than [31]" claim nor the QMIX-modification benefit is isolated.
- **Metrics**: average capture steps; episode reward. No variance/seeds/statistics reported.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues

**Author-stated** (p. 8): future work targets "robotic multi-agent system under interference conditions" — i.e., the current model has no noise, interference, or real-robot validation; "Data availability: No data was used" (p. 11).

**Critical observations (with page/eq.)**:
1. **Reproducibility**: no numerical RL hyperparameters (learning rate, $\gamma$, $M$, $K$, $L$, $p$, batch size, network sizes — all symbolic in Algorithm 4, p. 6) and no capture-area threshold value (Objective 3, p. 3) — the reported convergence numbers cannot be reproduced.
2. **Capture criterion mismatch**: termination is defined by dominant-area threshold $S_t$ (p. 3, p. 5), but Theorem 3 proves monotone decrease of a distance-based CSCT (Appendix C); the two criteria are never formally connected.
3. **Theorem 3 proof gaps**: the derivative step $(y_i-y_e)=x_i\tan\theta_i-x_e\tan\theta_e$ (p. 10) silently assumes origin-anchored straight-line paths; the same-direction assumption $\theta_e=\theta_i$ (p. 10) is exact only for line-of-sight pursuit, which is a property of a *pure-pursuit* policy, not of the learned QMIX policy; the simultaneous-capture CSCT reduction is unproven (pp. 10–11).
4. **Theorem 2 scope**: the potential verification covers only pursuer deviations with the neighbor set held fixed (Appendix B, pp. 9–10); evader deviations are excluded by construction (best-response clamping, p. 3, Definition 2 discussion); undefined notation $\|\cdot\|_2'$ in eq. (26) (p. 10).
5. **Reward design is weaker than claimed**: with eq. (21) freezing non-neighbors and $\delta_i=1$ for neighbors, all $U_i=-S_t$ (eq. (17)), so the team reward $\max_i U_i$ (p. 6) is identically $-S_t$; the "new approach to designing rewards compared to traditional QMIX" (p. 2) amounts to a negative-area potential-based shaping signal; no ablation against a plain $-S_t$ or distance-based reward is provided.
6. **Unsupported efficiency claim**: "a more efficient neighbor-identifying algorithm than [31]" (p. 2) — no complexity analysis or timing comparison anywhere.
7. **Marginal pursuer-side gain**: 51 vs 49 capture steps (Section 5.2, p. 7) is a ~4% improvement with no statistical treatment.
8. **Idealized information structure**: full global state available to all agents (p. 1, p. 6); no sensing range, communication, observation noise, obstacles, or domain bounds; evader uses continuous headings while pursuers get 15 discrete actions (p. 6) — an asymmetry that favors the scripted evader in Fig. 4 comparisons.
9. **Notational defects**: $A_{ep}$ undefined in eqs. (15)–(16) (p. 5); circle centers misprinted as $O_1(cx_1,cx_1),O_2(cx_2,cx_2)$ (p. 4); "sgn" typeset as broken ligatures in eqs. (5), (28); Algorithm 1 returns a scalar `status` though it computes per-pursuer flags (p. 4); Fig. 5 axis "episodes*600" vs text "each 300 training episodes ... 1000 train episodes" (p. 7).
10. **Equilibrium–learning disconnect**: FIP guarantees finite convergence of *best-response improvement paths* in finite games [35]; it says nothing about deep Q-learning with function approximation, replay, and target networks — Theorem 2's NE existence and Algorithm 4's empirical convergence are not causally linked (p. 6).

## 8. Citation Value

**Paragraph role**: P097 is a *hybrid geometry-plus-learning* reference: it imports the classical Apollonius-circle dominant-region partition (Isaacs-lineage tools also used in P-periphery works like Dorothy et al. "One Apollonius circle is enough", and Makkapati–Tsiotras' Apollonius allocation [31]) into a CTDE multi-agent RL pipeline, wraps the pursuit side in an ordinal-potential-game NE-existence argument, and adds a role-freezing mechanism motivated by the active/redundant structure of [31]. It is best cited (a) as a representative of "analytic-partition-shaped reward/role design for MARL pursuit" in surveys of learning-based differential games, (b) as an instance of potential-game formulations of pursuit-evasion (alongside [19, 32, 33, 35]), and (c) as a source for the concrete Apollonius-partition machinery — center/radius formulas (eq. (8)), dominant region (eq. (9)), aimpoint characterization (eqs. (15)–(16)), and area computation (Algorithm 3) — reusable by papers that need a differentiable or analytic evader model or a team objective $-S_t$ for shaping. Its guarantees (Theorems 2–3) should be cited with the caveat that the potential-game verification is pursuer-side and the finite-time proof assumes line-of-sight geometry. Example citation sentence: "Xue et al. [P097] shape cooperative pursuit with an Apollonius-partition potential, rewarding pursuers with the negative area of the evader's dominant region and training a role-based QMIX; the resulting fixation-evader benchmarks show capture in 32/18/14 steps for 2v1/4v1/6v1, although the NE-existence proof covers only pursuer-side deviations and the finite-time guarantee presumes line-of-sight engagement."
