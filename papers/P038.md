## 2. Research Background

Capture-the-flag is the canonical two-objective adversarial scenario: an Attacker must reach a target (flag) and then withdraw to safety while a Defender tries to intercept. Robotic CTF testbeds and matches ([10] Blake et al. ICRA 2004; [11]–[13] RoboFlag) exposed that autonomy relied on human input, motivating differential-game treatment in the line of Isaacs [14]. The CTF scenario was first posed as a differential game by Huang, Ding, Zhang, Tomlin [15] (TCST 2015) and [16] (ICRA 2011), who solved it numerically via HJI computations with objective functionals over desired/undesired sets; [17] (Wang et al., TCDS 2024) addressed multi-agent CTF *matching* assuming faster defenders; [18] (Zhou et al., IECON 2021) attacked the two-phase structure but only numerically approximated the transition phase and the Game of Kind; [19] (Lee & Bakolas, ACC 2023) treated a reconnaissance game with a similar two-phase (approach–retreat) structure. This paper is the journal-level, closed-form completion of that program: it is the extended version of the authors' CDC 2018 conference paper [24] (Garcia, Casbeer, Pachter, "The capture-the-flag differential game", pp. 4167–4172), adding boundary solutions and — decisively — the transition phase that connects the two phases and unlocks the overall Game of Kind.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap

**Problem.** Two players, Attacker A and Defender D, equal speeds $V_A = V_D = 1$ (normalized, [authors' assumption, p. 272]), simple motion, compact convex rectangular game set $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ with flag $F=(0,0)$ and safe zone $S=\{x \ge x_s\}$; boundaries $\underline{y}$ (lower) and $\bar{y}$ (upper) along $y$. Phase I (Attack): A tries to capture $F$ (termination set $T_{A1}$, eq. (3)); D tries to intercept ($T_{D1}$, eq. (4)). Phase II (Retreat), conditional on A winning Phase I: A tries to reach $S$ ($T_{A2} = \{x \mid x_A = x_s\}$, eq. (12)) before interception ($T_{D2}$, eq. (13)). The full Game of Kind (who wins from any initial state, accounting for D's repositioning between phases) and the Games of Degree (terminal payoff values and feedback saddle-point strategies in each phase) are sought in closed form.

## 4. Core Contributions

1. **Theory (Game of Degree, Phase I)**: closed-form state-feedback saddle-point strategies (16), optimal interception point (17), and $C^1$ Value function (18) for the Attack phase in $\mathcal{R}_{D1}$, with a rigorous verification proof (continuity, continuous differentiability, HJI satisfaction), including the constrained/boundary solution when the unconstrained aimpoint leaves $\Omega$ (eqs. (25)–(32), p. 276).
2. **Theory (Game of Degree, Phase II)**: closed-form feedback strategies (33) and piecewise Value (34) for the Retreat phase in $\mathcal{R}_{D2}$, continuous everywhere and $C^1$ except on a dispersal surface $\mathcal{D} = \{\mathbf{x} \mid y_A = y_D\}$, with HJI verification (pp. 277–279).
3. **Modeling novelty (Transition Phase, Section IV)**: formulation of D's inter-phase repositioning problem — when $x_0 \in \mathcal{R}_{A1}$, D concedes interception and optimizes its heading over the fixed time $t_f = AF/V_A$ so as to minimize the Phase II aimpoint abscissa $x^*$ (optimization (46)–(47)); solved in closed form (Theorems 5–6) including the constrained case where the optimal endpoint must stay in the closed upper half plane.
4. **Theory (Game of Kind, Section V)**: barrier functions $B_1$ (75) and $B_2$ (78) with an angular regime condition (76)–(77) that together characterize from any initial state whether A can capture the flag *and* escape to $S$; cross-sections of the 4-D barrier are circular arcs (80), (82) (Fig. 9).
5. **Experiment/illustration (Section VI)**: closed-loop implementation showing (i) time-invariance of the saddle-point interception point under optimal play and (ii) the security-strategy property — a non-optimal Attacker is intercepted farther from $S$ than the Value ($x_A(t_f) = 4.73 < V = 5.34$).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results (exhaustive)

### 5.1 Dynamics, information, costs, termination (pp. 272–274)

- Dynamics (1): $\dot{x}_A = \cos\theta_A$, $\dot{y}_A = \sin\theta_A$, $\dot{x}_D = \cos\theta_D$, $\dot{y}_D = \sin\theta_D$; $\theta_A,\theta_D \in [-\pi,\pi)$; speeds normalized to 1 (assumption $V_A = V_D$, p. 272). State $\mathbf{x} = (x_A, y_A, x_D, y_D) \in \mathbb{R}^4$; perfect-state, nonanticipative information; feedback (closed-loop) strategies, continuously recomputed (Remarks pp. 278, 286).
- Termination: Phase I $T_1 = T_{A1} \cup T_{D1}$ (2) with $T_{A1} = \{x_A^2 + y_A^2 = 0\}$ (3) (flag capture) and $T_{D1} = \{(x_A-x_D)^2 + (y_A-y_D)^2 = 0\}$ (4) (interception). Phase II $T_2 = T_{A2} \cup T_{D2}$ (11) with $T_{A2} = \{x_A = x_s\}$ (12), $T_{D2}$ as (13). Framed via two-termination-set games [25]–[27] (p. 273).
- Costs (Mayer/terminal only): Phase I $\Phi(\mathbf{x}_f) = x_{Af}^2 + y_{Af}^2$ (6) — squared distance from A to F at interception; $V(\mathbf{x}_0) = \min_{u_A}\max_{u_D} J$ (7). Phase II $\Phi(\mathbf{x}_f) = x_{Af}$ (14) with $V(\mathbf{x}_0) = \max_{u_A}\min_{u_D} J$ (15) — "A aims at maximizing $x_{Af}$ while D tries to minimize it" (p. 274); i.e., terminal proximity of the capture point to $S$.
- Hamiltonian (p. 273): $H = \lambda_{x_A}\cos\theta_A + \lambda_{y_A}\sin\theta_A + \lambda_{x_D}\cos\theta_D + \lambda_{y_D}\sin\theta_D$; decoupled in $(\theta_A,\theta_D)$, hence $\min_{\theta_A}\max_{\theta_D} H = \max_{\theta_D}\min_{\theta_A} H$ and Isaacs' condition holds (p. 273).
- Winning regions. Phase I (p. 274, unnumbered): $\mathcal{R}_{D1} = \{\mathbf{x} \mid x_D^2 + y_D^2 < x_A^2 + y_A^2\}$, $\mathcal{R}_{A1} = \{\mathbf{x} \mid x_D^2 + y_D^2 > x_A^2 + y_A^2\}$ — equal-speed Apollonius (perpendicular-bisector) condition on flag distance. Phase II (p. 277, unnumbered): $\mathcal{R}_{D2} = \{\mathbf{x}\mid (x_D-x_s)^2+(y_D-\underline{y})^2 < (x_A-x_s)^2+(y_A-\underline{y})^2\} \cap \{\mathbf{x}\mid (x_D-x_s)^2+(y_D-\bar{y})^2 < (x_A-x_s)^2+(y_A-\bar{y})^2\}$; $\mathcal{R}_{A2}$ the same with $>$ and $\cup$ — D wins Phase II iff it is closer than A to *both* corners $(x_s,\underline{y})$, $(x_s,\bar{y})$ of the safe-zone line segment (point-capture dominance over a segment).

### 5.2 Theorem 1 (p. 273) — straight-line optimality, Phase I

- **Statement**: For $x \in \mathcal{R}_{D1}$, headings of A and D are constant under optimal play; optimal trajectories are straight lines.
- **Assumptions**: dynamics (1); $x \in \mathcal{R}_{D1}$ so termination is by (4).
- **Proof approach** (4 steps): (i) Isaacs' Main Equation 1, $\min_{\theta_A}\max_{\theta_D} H = 0$; (ii) stationarity $\partial H/\partial\theta = 0$ gives $\cos\theta_A^* = -\lambda_{x_A}/\sqrt{\lambda_{x_A}^2+\lambda_{y_A}^2}$, $\sin\theta_A^* = -\lambda_{y_A}/\sqrt{\lambda_{x_A}^2+\lambda_{y_A}^2}$ (9) and $\cos\theta_D^* = \lambda_{x_D}/\sqrt{\lambda_{x_D}^2+\lambda_{y_D}^2}$, $\sin\theta_D^* = \lambda_{y_D}/\sqrt{\lambda_{x_D}^2+\lambda_{y_D}^2}$ (10); (iii) costate dynamics $\dot{\lambda} = -\partial H/\partial x$ give $\dot{\lambda}_{x_A} = \dot{\lambda}_{y_A} = \dot{\lambda}_{x_D} = \dot{\lambda}_{y_D} = 0$ (H has no state dependence); (iv) constant costates ⇒ constant optimal headings (9)–(10) ⇒ straight lines. Decisive equation: $\dot\lambda \equiv 0$. Local result (within $\mathcal{R}_{D1}$), analytic; physical meaning: no state-dependent terms ⇒ no curvature-inducing mechanism.

### 5.3 Theorem 2 (p. 274) — straight-line optimality, Phase II

- Identical statement for Phase II with $x \in \mathcal{R}_{D2}$ and termination (13). **No proof is given** (the symbol □ follows the statement; p. 274: "the co-state and the Hamiltonian in Phase II can be written in the same way as in Phase I"). The identical Hamiltonian/costate structure makes this immediate; formally it is asserted by analogy.

### 5.4 Theorem 3 (p. 274, eqs. (16)–(18)) — Phase I Game of Degree in $\mathcal{R}_{D1}$

- **Statement**: For $\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{R}_{D1}$ the optimal state-feedback strategies are
$$\cos\theta_A^* = \frac{x^*-x_A}{\sqrt{(x^*-x_A)^2+(y^*-y_A)^2}},\quad \sin\theta_A^* = \frac{y^*-y_A}{\sqrt{(x^*-x_A)^2+(y^*-y_A)^2}}$$
and similarly for D with $(x^*,y^*)$ replacing and $D$-components (16), where the optimal interception point is
$$x^* = \frac{x_A^2+y_A^2-x_D^2-y_D^2}{(x_A-x_D)^2+(y_A-y_D)^2}\,\frac{x_A-x_D}{2},\qquad y^* = \frac{x_A^2+y_A^2-x_D^2-y_D^2}{(x_A-x_D)^2+(y_A-y_D)^2}\,\frac{y_A-y_D}{2}\;(17)$$
and the Value function is
$$V(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{4}\,\frac{(x_A^2+y_A^2-x_D^2-y_D^2)^2}{(x_A-x_D)^2+(y_A-y_D)^2}\;(18)$$
which is $C^1$ and satisfies the HJI equation.
- **Assumptions**: $x \in \mathcal{R}_{D1}$; $(x_A-x_D)^2+(y_A-y_D)^2 \neq 0$ (else already captured, p. 274); equal speeds; point flag at origin.
- **Proof approach** (geometry + verification, 5 steps): (i) by Theorem 1 both players fly straight, so the interception point $I$ lies on the orthogonal bisector (OBS) of segment $AD$; the OBS is $y - y_o = m_o(x - x_o)$ (19) with $m_o = -(x_A-x_D)/(y_A-y_D)$ (20), midpoint $(x_o,y_o)$ (21); (ii) the payoff $\|I-F\|^2$ is minimized over the OBS at the foot of the perpendicular from $F$: intersect (19) with $y = mx$, $m = -1/m_o = (y_A-y_D)/(x_A-x_D)$ (22), yielding (17) — the point on the OBS closest to $F$ is the common aimpoint; (iii) $V = x^{*2}+y^{*2}$ simplifies to (18); continuity holds since the denominator $\neq 0$ on $\mathcal{R}_{D1}$; (iv) $C^1$: the four partials (p. 275, unnumbered display) are continuous for $(x_A-x_D)^2+(y_A-y_D)^2 \ne 0$; (v) HJI verification: the key identities $x^*-x_A = -\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_A}\cdot\frac{(x_A-x_D)^2+(y_A-y_D)^2}{x_A^2+y_A^2-x_D^2-y_D^2}$ (and analogues for the other three components, p. 275) convert the optimal headings into normalized negatives of the Attacker gradient blocks and positives of the Defender blocks; then $\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_A}\cos\theta_A^* + \frac{\partial V}{\partial y_A}\sin\theta_A^* = -\sqrt{(\partial V/\partial x_A)^2 + (\partial V/\partial y_A)^2}$ (23) and the analogous Defender term is $+\sqrt{(\partial V/\partial x_D)^2 + (\partial V/\partial y_D)^2}$; the decisive equation is (24): the two gradient norms are *equal* (both equal $\frac{|x_A^2+y_A^2-x_D^2-y_D^2|}{[(x_A-x_D)^2+(y_A-y_D)^2]^{3/2}}\sqrt{\tfrac14(x_A^2+y_A^2-x_D^2-y_D^2)^2 + (x_A^2+y_A^2)(x_D^2+y_D^2 - x_Ax_D - y_Ay_D) + (x_D^2+y_D^2)(x_A^2+y_A^2 - x_Ax_D - y_Ay_D)}$), so $\frac{\partial V}{\partial \mathbf{x}}\cdot f(\mathbf{x},\theta_A^*,\theta_D^*) = 0$ (p. 276 top). The norm equality is exactly where $V_A = V_D$ enters.
- **Local/global, analytic**: analytic closed form; valid on $\mathcal{R}_{D1}$ (D-win side of the Phase I barrier). Physical meaning: D drags the interception point as far from the flag as the geometry allows while A pulls it to the flag; the equilibrium aimpoint is the OBS foot of the perpendicular from F.
- **Extensibility**: the OBS-foot aimpoint construction is the template used throughout the paper (Phases II and transition) and in the authors' active-defense line [9], [28].
- **Remark (p. 276, aimpoint feasibility)**: if the unconstrained aimpoint $(x^*,y^*) \notin \Omega$, "the optimal aimpoint is the point where the orthogonal bisector of AD meets the boundary; because $\Omega$ is convex, there are two such points and the optimal point is the one closest to F."
- **Boundary solutions (p. 276, eqs. (25)–(32); not numbered as a separate theorem)**: case $y^* < \underline{y}$ (Fig. 2; mirror case $y^* > \bar{y}$ analogous). The constrained optimal interception point is $I_b = (x_b, \underline{y})$ on the OBS, with
$$x_b = \frac{\tfrac12(x_D^2+y_D^2-x_A^2-y_A^2) + \underline{y}(y_A-y_D)}{x_D - x_A}\;(25),\qquad V_b(\mathbf{x}) = x_b^2 + \underline{y}^2\;(26)$$
valid while $x_D - x_A \ne 0$ (if $x_D = x_A$ then $m_o = 0$, OBS $= \{y = y_o\}$ and the interior aimpoint $(0, y_o) \in \Omega$). Continuity at the critical state where $y^* = \underline{y}$: substituting $\underline{y} = m x_b$ into (25) gives $x_b = x^*$ (27) and $V_b = (m^2+1)x_b^2 = \frac14\frac{(x_A^2+y_A^2-x_D^2-y_D^2)^2}{(x_A-x_D)^2+(y_A-y_D)^2} = V$ (28). $C^1$ matching: gradients (29) coincide with those of $V$ at the critical state (shown by direct substitution, p. 276). HJI on the boundary region: identities (30)–(31) and headings (32) give $\frac{\partial V}{\partial \mathbf{x}}\cdot f = \|\nabla_A V\| - \|\nabla_D V\| = 0$, the last equality holding because $\sqrt{(x_b-x_A)^2+(\underline{y}-y_A)^2} = \sqrt{(x_b-x_D)^2+(\underline{y}-y_D)^2}$ (equidistance of the OBS point). *Proof gap note*: the boundary case is handled by direct construction and verification; global uniqueness of the constrained saddle point is argued geometrically (closest-to-F of the two convexity-guaranteed boundary intersections), not via a general comparison principle.

### 5.5 Theorem 4 (p. 277, eqs. (33)–(34)) — Phase II Game of Degree in $\mathcal{R}_{D2}$

- **Statement**: For $\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{R}_{D2}$, the optimal state-feedback strategies of A and D are (33), both players aiming at $I^* = (-m y^* + n,\; y^*)$ with
$$\cos\theta_A^* = \frac{-my^*+n-x_A}{\sqrt{(-my^*+n-x_A)^2+(y^*-y_A)^2}},\quad \sin\theta_A^* = \frac{y^*-y_A}{\sqrt{(-my^*+n-x_A)^2+(y^*-y_A)^2}}$$
and the D-analogue (33), where $m = \frac{y_A-y_D}{x_A-x_D}$ and $n = \frac12\frac{x_A^2-x_D^2+y_A^2-y_D^2}{x_A-x_D}$; the aimpoint ordinate is $y^* = \bar{y}$ if $m_o > 0$ and $y^* = \underline{y}$ if $m_o < 0$. The Value function is
$$V_1(\mathbf{x}) = \frac12\,\frac{x_A^2-x_D^2+y_A^2-y_D^2}{x_A-x_D} - \frac{y_A-y_D}{x_A-x_D}\,\bar{y},\ \text{if } m_o > 0;\qquad V_2(\mathbf{x}) = \frac12\,\frac{x_A^2-x_D^2+y_A^2-y_D^2}{x_A-x_D} - \frac{y_A-y_D}{x_A-x_D}\,\underline{y},\ \text{if } m_o < 0 \;(34)$$
$V$ is continuous, continuously differentiable except at the dispersal surface $\mathcal{D} := \{\mathbf{x} \mid y_A = y_D\}$, and satisfies the HJI equation.
- **Assumptions**: $x \in \mathcal{R}_{D2}$ (D closer to both safe-zone corners, so termination is (13)); $x_A \ne x_D$ on $\mathcal{R}_{D2}$ (p. 278); note the aimpoint is on the *boundary* of $\Omega$ (intersection of the OBS with $\underline{y}$ or $\bar{y}$), unlike Phase I's interior foot point.
- **Proof approach** (6 steps): (i) OBS written as $y = m_o x + n_o$ (35) with $n_o = \frac12\frac{x_A^2-x_D^2+y_A^2-y_D^2}{y_A-y_D}$; the interception point is $I^* = (x^*(\bar y), \bar y)$ if $m_o > 0$, $(x^*(\underline y), \underline y)$ if $m_o < 0$ — i.e., the OBS–boundary intersection with the *larger* abscissa (the outcome favorable to A, consistent with $V = \max\min x_{Af}$; both branches select the same "upper-right" intersection); (ii) $V = x^*$ gives (34); continuity: at the singular surface $x^*(\bar y) = x^*(\underline y)$ one has $y_A = y_D$ and $V_1 = V_2$ (p. 278); (iii) partials (36): $\partial V/\partial x_A = \frac{(y_A-y_D)y^* + \frac12[(x_A-x_D)^2 - y_A^2 + y_D^2]}{(x_A-x_D)^2}$, $\partial V/\partial y_A = \frac{y_A-y^*}{x_A-x_D}$, $\partial V/\partial x_D = -\frac{(y_A-y_D)y^* + \frac12[(x_A-x_D)^2 + y_A^2 - y_D^2]}{(x_A-x_D)^2}$, $\partial V/\partial y_D = \frac{y^*-y_D}{x_A-x_D}$; (iv) gradient-heading identities (37)–(40): $-my^*+n-x_A = -\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_A}(x_A-x_D)$, $y^*-y_A = -\frac{\partial V}{\partial y_A}(x_A-x_D)$, etc.; (v) hence optimal headings are gradient-normalized (41)–(43): $\cos\theta_A^* = -\frac{\partial V/\partial x_A}{\sqrt{(\partial V/\partial x_A)^2+(\partial V/\partial y_A)^2}}$, $\cos\theta_D^* = +\frac{\partial V/\partial x_D}{\sqrt{(\partial V/\partial x_D)^2+(\partial V/\partial y_D)^2}}$; (vi) HJI: $\frac{\partial V}{\partial\mathbf{x}}\cdot f = \sqrt{(\partial V/\partial x_A)^2+(\partial V/\partial y_A)^2} - \sqrt{(\partial V/\partial x_D)^2+(\partial V/\partial y_D)^2} = \frac{\sqrt{P(\mathbf{x})}}{(x_A-x_D)^2} - \frac{\sqrt{P(\mathbf{x})}}{(x_A-x_D)^2} = 0$, where $P(\mathbf{x}) = \frac12(x_A-x_D)^2\big[(\underline{y}-y_A)^2+(\underline{y}-y_D)^2 + \frac12(x_A-x_D)^2\big] + \big[\underline{y}(y_A-y_D) - \frac12(y_A^2-y_D^2)\big]^2$ (p. 278) — again the equal gradient norms (equal speeds) close the argument. ("For regular solutions", p. 278.)
- **Singular surface (p. 278–279)**: on $y_A = y_D$, $V_1 = V_2 = \frac12(x_A+x_D)$ (continuity), $\partial V/\partial x_A$ and $\partial V/\partial x_D$ are continuous ($= \tfrac12$), but $\partial V/\partial y_A$: $\frac{y_A-\bar y}{x_A-x_D} \ne \frac{y_A-\underline y}{x_A-x_D} = \partial V_2/\partial y_A$ — a dispersal surface with two saddle-point solutions (aim at either boundary intersection); the state exits $\mathcal{D}$ as soon as the players choose different aimpoints, and remains on it otherwise until the game ends.
- **Remark (p. 279)**: although at Phase II onset $x_A = y_A = 0$ (A just captured the flag), Theorem 4 is stated for general $(x_A,y_A)$ so it can be recomputed in closed loop throughout Phase II against non-optimal play.
- **Physical meaning**: D pins the capture point on the game boundary, as close to the flag side as the OBS geometry forces; A's guaranteed terminal abscissa is (34).

### 5.6 Transition Phase (Section IV, pp. 279–285) and Theorems 5–6

- **Setup**: $x_0 \in \mathcal{R}_{A1}$, i.e., $x_{D0}^2 + y_{D0}^2 > x_{A0}^2 + y_{A0}^2$. Define $d = \sqrt{x_{A0}^2+y_{A0}^2}$ (distance A travels to F) and $w = \frac12(x_{D0}^2+y_{D0}^2 - d^2) > 0$ (p. 279). WLOG $y_{D0} > 0$. Since speeds are equal, at the fixed terminal time $t_f = AF/V_A = d$, D can be anywhere on the circle of radius $d$ about $D_0$: $(x_{D0} + d\cos\theta_D,\; y_{D0} + d\sin\theta_D)$ (44). Constraint $x' := x_{D0} + d\cos\theta_D > 0$ — otherwise $S$ falls in A's dominance region and "A can easily escape" (p. 279).
- **Cost**: with $I' = (x', y')$ D's transition endpoint, the Phase II OBS of segment $FI'$ has $m_o = -x'/y'$, $n_o = (x'^2+y'^2)/(2y')$, and its boundary intersection abscissa is $x = \frac{\tfrac12(x'^2+y'^2) - \underline{y}\,y'}{x'}$ (45). D solves
$$\min J_T(x') \quad \text{s.t. } x' > 0,\; (x'-x_{D0})^2 + (y'-y_{D0})^2 = d^2 \;(46),\qquad J_T(x') = \frac{\tfrac12(x'^2+y'^2) - \underline{y}\,y'}{x'}\;(47)$$
i.e., D minimizes the Phase II aimpoint abscissa $x^*$ — the Value A would inherit. [Justification that this is the right objective, p. 279: D "tries to find the optimal heading that results in a Phase II optimal aimpoint that has x-coordinate as far as possible from S"; the motivating example (Fig. 3, $A_0=(3,-3)$, $D_0=(3,4)$, $\Omega=[-5,10]\times[-6,6]$, $x_s=8$) shows that naive aiming at F yields $x_0 \in \mathcal{R}_{A2}$ (A escapes aiming below $(x_s,-5.326)$), whereas repositioning yields $x_0 \in \mathcal{R}_{D2}$.]
- **Case split**: if $y_{D0} > d$, circle (44) stays above the x-axis, $m_o < 0$ for every $\theta_D$, so the Phase II aimpoint is on $\underline{y}$ (Fig. 4).

**Theorem 5 (p. 280, eqs. (48)–(50))**: *Assume $y_{D0} > d$ and $x_{D0} > -d$. The optimal Transition Phase heading is*
$$\cos\theta_D^* = \frac{x^*-x_{D0}}{\sqrt{(x^*-x_{D0})^2+(\underline{y}-y_{D0})^2}},\quad \sin\theta_D^* = \frac{\underline{y}-y_{D0}}{\sqrt{(x^*-x_{D0})^2+(\underline{y}-y_{D0})^2}}\;(48)$$
*with*
$$x^* = \frac{x_{D0}(w - y_{D0}\underline{y}) - d\sqrt{(w-y_{D0}\underline{y})^2 + \underline{y}^2(x_{D0}^2-d^2)}}{x_{D0}^2 - d^2}\ \text{if } x_{D0}^2 \ne d^2 \;(49),\qquad x^* = \frac{y_{D0}^2(\tfrac{y_{D0}}{2}-\underline{y})^2 - x_{D0}^2\,\underline{y}^2}{2x_{D0}y_{D0}(\tfrac{y_{D0}}{2}-\underline{y})}\ \text{if } x_{D0}^2 = d^2\;(50)$$
- **Proof approach** (7 steps, pp. 280–282): (i) first-order condition $\partial J_T/\partial x' = 0$ (51) with $dy'/dx' = -(x'-x_{D0})/(y'-y_{D0})$ from the circle constraint gives (52); (ii) **decisive geometric property** (53)–(56): writing the line through $D_0$ and $I'$ and evaluating at $y = \underline{y}$, the optimality condition (52) is exactly equivalent to collinearity of $D_0$, $I'$, and $I^*$ — "the optimal heading of D in the Transition Phase is the same as its optimal heading in Phase II"; (iii) hence the optimal aimpoint satisfies
$$\sqrt{(x^*-x_{D0})^2 + (\underline{y}-y_{D0})^2} = \sqrt{x^{*2} + \underline{y}^2} + d\;(57)$$
(D's straight-line distance to $I^*$ = distance A travels from F to $I^*$ plus $d$) — a hyperbola with foci $D_0$ and $F$, evaluated at $y = \underline{y}$; (iv) squaring twice yields the quadratic (58), with roots (59); (v) feasibility: $x_{D0} \le -d$ ⇒ circle (44) misses the half plane $x>0$ ⇒ no feasible solution and "the Defender is then unable to win Phase II"; (vi) Case 1 $x_{D0} > d$ (Fig. 5): both roots real; the D-branch intersection violates (57); the A-branch root is the minus-sign expression (49); (vii) Case 2 $x_{D0} = d$ (Fig. 6): degenerate quadratic, unique intersection on the A-branch at positive abscissa, giving (50) (positivity argued from $\underline y < 0$, $y_{D0} > d$: $2x_{D0}(y_{D0}\underline y - w) < 0$ and $(y_{D0}^2-d^2)\underline y^2 + w^2 - 2w y_{D0}\underline y > 0$); Case 3 $-d < x_{D0} < d$ (Fig. 7): $x_{D0}^2 - d^2 < 0$ flips the root ordering; both intersections are on the A-branch, on opposite sides of the y-axis; the right-hand-side root is (49). Conclusion: "when $y_{D0} > d$ and $x_{D0} > -d$, the optimal heading of the Defender in the Transition Phase is the same as its optimal heading in Phase II" — D flies one straight line through the transition into Phase II.
- **Analytic, global within its stated regime**; physical meaning: the transition optimum collapses the two-phase problem into a single straight-line intercept geometry captured by the hyperbola (57).

**Constrained case (0 < y_D0 ≤ d) and Theorem 6 (p. 282, eq. (60))**: the circle (44) now crosses the x-axis; the unconstrained optimum could give $y_{D0} + d\sin\theta_D^* < 0$ (D ending below the x-axis), in which case A would aim at the $\bar y$ boundary instead — so the additional constraint $y_{D0} + d\sin\theta_D \ge 0$ is imposed; also, for any $y_{D0} > 0$, $\sin\theta_D^* \le 0$, i.e., $\theta_D^* \in [-\pi, 0]$ (p. 282, Fig. 8).

**Theorem 6 (p. 282)**: *Assume $0 < y_{D0} < d$ and $x_{D0} > 0$. If $\theta_D^*$ from (48)–(50) is such that $y_{D0} + d\sin\theta_D^* < 0$, the optimal feasible heading is*
$$\cos\theta_f^* = -\frac{1}{d}\sqrt{d^2 - y_{D0}^2},\qquad \sin\theta_f^* = -\frac{y_{D0}}{d}\;(60)$$
*i.e., D heads to the left x-axis crossing $(x_{D0} - \sqrt{d^2-y_{D0}^2},\,0)$ of circle (44); D's optimal heading is piecewise-constant with one change at the end of the transition, and Phase II starts on the dispersal surface $\mathcal{D} = \{\mathbf{x}\mid y_A = y_D\}$.*
- **Proof approach** (pp. 282–285): (i) $J_T$ in polar form (61); (ii) $dJ_T/d\theta_D = \frac{\alpha + \beta\cos\theta_D + \gamma\sin\theta_D}{(x_{D0}+d\cos\theta_D)^2}$ (62) with $\alpha = d^2(y_{D0}-\underline y)$, $\beta = dx_{D0}(y_{D0}-\underline y)$, $\gamma = d\big[\tfrac12(d^2-x_{D0}^2+y_{D0}^2) - y_{D0}\underline y\big]$ (63); stationarity $\alpha + \beta\cos\theta_D + \gamma\sin\theta_D = 0$ (64) has exactly two admissible solution sets (66)–(67) out of the four formal sign combinations of (65); (iii) Case 1 $x_{D0}>d$: using $\alpha^2 - \beta^2 = d^2(y_{D0}-\underline y)^2(d^2-x_{D0}^2) < 0$ only (66) has $\sin\theta_D < 0$; endpoint signs of $dJ_T/d\theta_D$ at $\theta_D = -\pi, 0$ plus uniqueness of the stationary point give a unique minimum; second-derivative test (68) evaluated at $\theta_D^*$ gives $d^2J_T/d\theta_D^2 = \frac{(\beta^2+\gamma^2)\sqrt{\beta^2+\gamma^2-\alpha^2}}{(x_{D0}+d\cos\theta_D^*)^2(\beta^2+\gamma^2)} > 0$ (69); (iv) if $\theta_D^*$ infeasible, by Fermat's theorem only the boundary values of the feasible range $[\theta_{b1},\theta_{b2}]\cup[\theta_{b3},\theta_{b4}]$ (70) need comparison; $J_T(\theta_{b2}) < J_T(\theta_{b1})$, $J_T(\theta_{b3}) < J_T(\theta_{b4})$, and $J_T(\theta_{b2}) < J_T(\theta_{b3})$ from (71) ⇒ $\theta_f^* = \theta_{b2}$, which is exactly (60); (v) Case 2 $x_{D0}=d$: $\alpha^2-\beta^2 = 0$; the $\sin\theta_D = 0$ candidate forces $\cos\theta_D = -1$, i.e., $x' = 0$, infeasible; the unique extremum is $\sin\theta_D^* = -\frac{2\beta\gamma}{\beta^2+\gamma^2}$, $\cos\theta_D^* = \frac{\gamma^2-\beta^2}{\beta^2+\gamma^2}$, a minimum by (69); if infeasible, $\lim_{\epsilon\to0} J_T(-\pi+\epsilon) = +\infty$ (small-angle expansion, unnumbered display p. 284) ⇒ $\theta_f^* = \theta_{b2}$ again; (vi) Case 3 $0 < x_{D0} < d$: here $\beta^2+\gamma^2-\alpha^2 > 0$ is proven via the sum-of-squares identity (72); the candidate (67) is *infeasible* because $x'(\theta_{D2}) < 0$ — shown through the factorization (74) using $x_{D0}\sqrt{\beta^2+\gamma^2-\alpha^2} < d\gamma$ — while $x'(\theta_{D1}) > 0$; hence unique stationary point $\theta_D^* = \theta_{D1}$, minimum by (69); if infeasible, $\theta_{b1}$ is redefined by $x_{D0} + d\cos\theta_{b1} = 0$ (73), $\lim_{\epsilon\to0}J_T(\theta_{b1}+\epsilon) = +\infty$ (using $y_{D0} - \sqrt{d^2-x_{D0}^2} > 0$), and $J_T(\theta_{b2}) < J_T(\theta_{b3}) < J_T(\theta_{b4})$ ⇒ $\theta_f^* = \theta_{b2}$.
- **Remark (p. 285)**: for $y_{D0} < 0$ Theorems 5–6 hold symmetrically with the aimpoint on $\bar y$.

### 5.7 Game of Kind / Barrier surface (Section V, pp. 285–286) — **no numbered theorem**

The overall winner characterization is given constructively (not stated as a formal theorem):

- **Fact 1 (p. 285)**: if $0 < y_{D0} < d$, D can reach $(x_{D0}-\sqrt{d^2-y_{D0}^2},\,0) \in \Omega$ at the end of the Transition Phase; then the Phase II aimpoint abscissa is $x^* = \frac12(x_{D0}-\sqrt{d^2-y_{D0}^2}) < x_s$: **D intercepts A in Phase II whenever circle (44) intersects the x-axis** (D parks on the perpendicular bisector of $FA'$ that A must cross before $S$).
- **Consequently (p. 285)**: for A to win, D's Phase II solution must be the unconstrained one, which forces D's optimal heading to be constant along the entire game and (57) to hold. Define the **Barrier function**
$$B_1(\mathbf{x}) = \sqrt{(x_{D0}-x_s)^2 + (y_{D0}-\underline{y})^2} - \sqrt{x_s^2 + \underline{y}^2} - \sqrt{x_{A0}^2+y_{A0}^2}\;(75)$$
At the limit case D intercepts A exactly at $(x_s, \underline{y})$: barrier surface $B_1(\mathbf{x}) = 0$. Interpretation: $B_1 < 0$ ⇔ D's straight distance to the corner $(x_s,\underline y)$ is less than A's total path $d + \|F\to(x_s,\underline y)\|$ ⇔ D can win. This holds only for a subset of the state space, selected by the angle condition: with $\varphi = \arctan(\underline y/x_s)$, if $\frac{\pi}{2} \le \arctan\big(\frac{y_{D0}-\underline y}{x_{D0}-x_s}\big) < \varphi$ then the barrier surface is $\{(x_A,y_A)\in\Omega, (x_D,y_D)\in\Omega \mid B_1(\mathbf{x})=0\}$ (76). (Branch caution: $\underline y < 0$, so the inequalities are meaningful only with the arctangent taken in $(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi]$; the paper does not state the branch.)
- **Regime 2 (p. 286)**: if $\varphi \le \arctan\big(\frac{y_{D0}-\underline y}{x_{D0}-x_s}\big) \le \pi$, the barrier surface is $\{\cdot \mid B_2(\mathbf{x}) = 0\}$ (77) with
$$B_2(\mathbf{x}) = \sqrt{x_{D0}^2 + y_{D0}^2} - \sqrt{x_{A0}^2 + y_{A0}^2}\;(78)$$
Here D "is in such position that cannot win Phase II" — the A-branch of the hyperbola (57) crosses $x = x_s$ at $y > \underline y$, so $S$ lies in A's dominance region and the overall barrier coincides with the Phase I (flag-distance) barrier.
- **Cross-sections (p. 286, eqs. (79)–(82), Fig. 9)**: fixing A at $A_0$, the barrier projects to $\{(x,y)\in\Omega \mid B_1(x,y)=0\}$ (79) with $B_1(x,y) = \sqrt{(x-x_s)^2+(y-\underline y)^2} - \sqrt{x_s^2+\underline y^2} - \sqrt{x_{A0}^2+y_{A0}^2}$ (80) — a circle centered $(x_s,\underline y)$ with radius $\sqrt{x_s^2+\underline y^2} + \sqrt{x_{A0}^2+y_{A0}^2}$ — and $\{(x,y)\in\Omega \mid B_2(x,y)=0\}$ (81) with $B_2(x,y) = \sqrt{x^2+y^2} - \sqrt{x_{A0}^2+y_{A0}^2}$ (82) — a circle centered $F=(0,0)$ with radius $\sqrt{x_{A0}^2+y_{A0}^2}$. Fig. 9 shows both arcs plus the $y<0$ mirror; dashed lines mark the $B_1\!\to\!B_2$ switchover.
- **Overall Game of Kind (assembled)**: A wins the full two-phase game iff ($x_0 \in \mathcal{R}_{A1}$, i.e., $B_2 < 0$-complement: $\|D_0 - F\| > \|A_0 - F\|$) **and** the post-transition geometry is in A's favor — which, per Sections IV–V, happens exactly when (i) $x_{D0} > -d$ (transition feasible for D fails) or (ii) regime (77) with $\|D_0\| > \|A_0\|$; otherwise D wins. The paper states this as the barrier pair (76)–(77) rather than a single theorem; the winning-direction inequalities ($B_i < 0$ vs $> 0$) must be read off the text (pp. 285–286), not from a formal proposition.

### 5.8 Phase structure recap (requested explicitly)

- **Attack (Phase I)**: $\Omega \ni x_0$; A $\to F$, D intercepts; termination (3)/(4); Degree solved in $\mathcal{R}_{D1}$ (Thm 3) with boundary refinement (25)–(32); in $\mathcal{R}_{A1}$ no Degree game is solved — D switches to transition.
- **Transition**: fixed horizon $t_f = d$; D trades certain Phase I loss for the best Phase II start; cost (46)–(47); solution Thm 5 (unconstrained, straight-line continuation) / Thm 6 (constrained, head to x-axis point, then Phase II starts on the dispersal surface).
- **Retreat (Phase II)**: A $\to S = \{x_A = x_s\}$, D intercepts; termination (12)/(13); Degree solved in $\mathcal{R}_{D2}$ (Thm 4) with dispersal surface $y_A = y_D$; Game of Kind of the whole game: (76)/(77) barriers.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues

**Author-stated (pp. 286–287, Sec. VI.A "Extensions and future work" + Sec. VII)**:
1. Equal speeds $V_A = V_D$ and point capture (zero capture radius) only; unequal speeds lead to an Apollonius-circle separation and positive capture radius to a hyperbola, where "the optimal aimpoint … may be located inside the game set and not on the intersection of the reachable region with the boundary" — closed forms "will vary significantly" (p. 287).
2. Flag modeled as a point; flag *regions* and other safe-zone shapes left open; other convex $\Omega$ shapes change all closed forms (p. 272).
3. Higher-order dynamics and imperfect state information deferred to methods of [28] (p. 287).

**Critical observations (with page/eq)**:
4. **Printed typo (pp. 275 and 278)**: the dynamics vector is printed $f(\mathbf{x},\theta_A^*,\theta_D^*) = [\cos\theta_A^*\ \sin\theta_A^*\ \cos\theta_D^*\ \sin\theta_A^*]^T$ — the last component should be $\sin\theta_D^*$ (verified against the rendered page; the subsequent expansions on p. 275 bottom use $\sin\theta_D^*$ correctly, so only the vector line is wrong).
5. **Probable typo in Theorem 6, Case 2 (p. 284)**: the unique extremum is first printed as $\sin\theta_D^* = -2\alpha\gamma/(\beta^2+\gamma^2)$, then as $\sin\theta_D^* = -2\beta\gamma/(\beta^2+\gamma^2)$, $\cos\theta_D^* = (\gamma^2-\beta^2)/(\beta^2+\gamma^2)$; since $\alpha = \beta$ when $x_{D0}=d$ and the printed cosine pairs with the $-2\beta\gamma$ form, the $-2\alpha\gamma$ instance is inconsistent (the correct value follows from (66) with $\alpha=\beta$).
6. **No formal Game-of-Kind theorem**: Section V states the barrier pair (75)–(78) with the angular regime selector (76)–(77) but never a proposition with explicitly stated win-region inequalities; the reader must assemble sign conventions (e.g., $B_1<0 \Leftrightarrow$ D wins) from the prose (pp. 285–286). The arctangent branch for $\varphi = \arctan(\underline y/x_s)$ with $\underline y<0$ is unspecified.
7. **Dispersal surfaces unresolved in play terms**: Phase II's $\mathcal{D}=\{y_A=y_D\}$ (p. 278–279) and the post-Theorem-6 onset state admit two saddle-point aimpoints; the paper describes exit semantics but gives no equilibrium-selection or mixed-strategy rule, and the overall Game of Kind along these surfaces inherits that ambiguity.
8. **Phase I Degree in $\mathcal{R}_{A1}$ not solved**: the paper deliberately skips the Game of Degree on the Attacker-win side of the Phase I barrier ("although it is possible to formulate a Game of Degree in $\mathcal{R}_{A1}$…", p. 279), so the inter-phase handoff cost for A (e.g., *where* A chooses to capture the flag to shape Phase II) is not optimized — A's transition-side symmetric problem is untouched.
9. **Transition optimality is one-level**: D minimizes the immediate Phase II Value (47); the argument that this greedy-in-Value repositioning is globally optimal across the two phases rests on the Phase II feedback structure (and is validated by the barrier result), but no formal two-stage dynamic-programming or Stackelberg-level proof across phases is given; A's possible exploitation of D's committed transition heading is handled only through closed-loop recomputation (illustrated, not proved).
10. **Verification-style correctness**: "rigorous correctness" (p. 272) is delivered as continuity + $C^1$ + HJI satisfaction of the candidate Value (classical Isaacs verification); viscosity/comparison-principle formalism and boundary-value matching at $\partial\Omega$ are not treated with PDE-level rigor (acceptable for this geometry, but the claim's strength is bounded accordingly).
11. **Empirical scope**: two trajectory plots, no Monte Carlo, no comparison against the numerical HJI solutions of [15] or [18], and no timestamp/step-size reporting — the quantitative claims (e.g., $V=5.34$, $4.73$) are single-point illustrations.

## 8. Citation Value

This is the definitive closed-form treatment of the two-player capture-the-flag differential game and the natural journal-citation anchor for the "attack–transition–retreat" phase structure: it contributes (i) OBS-foot aimpoint Values with full HJI verification for both phases, (ii) the inter-phase transition concept — D concedes a lost Phase I and optimizes its Phase II entry state, with the transition optimum shown to continue straight into the Phase II aimpoint (hyperbola condition (57)) — and (iii) the overall Game of Kind via the explicit barrier pair $B_1$/$B_2$ (75)/(78). Cite it when a paper needs the canonical two-phase CTF benchmark, an example of connecting Games of Degree across phases through a terminal-cost-matched transition, equal-speed Apollonius/perpendicular-bisector dominance geometry, or a baseline analytic solution to extend to heterogeneous speeds, capture radii, flag regions, or multi-agent matching-based CTF (e.g., alongside [15]/[17] and the follow-on CTF works in this library, such as Deng et al. 2025 multi-defender CTF with convex target regions and Liang et al. 2026 half-plane-flag CTF). Suggested citation sentence: "Unlike the numerical HJI treatment of the two-phase CTF game in [15], [18], Garcia and Casbeer [P038] derive closed-form state-feedback saddle-point strategies for the attack and retreat phases, introduce a transition phase that optimally repositions the Defender once flag capture becomes inevitable, and thereby obtain the analytic Game-of-Kind barrier characterizing whether the Attacker can both capture the flag and escape to the safe zone."
