## 2. Research Background
USVs are small, low-cost, fast, flexible platforms with high military value, and unmanned maritime combat is expected to become mainstream; pursuit-and-escape capability of USVs is identified as a key technology (pp. 1–2). Classical pursuit-evasion solution approaches — environmental numerical solution (Von Moll et al., 2018), bio-inspiration (Madden et al., 2010), force-based (Janosov et al., 2017), control/optimization (Yamaguchi, 2003) — mostly assume known dynamic environments, and analytic solutions become intractable as the number of players grows (p. 1). The authors therefore adopt learning-based game methods, specifically Multi-Agent Deep RL (MA-DRL), which needs neither labeled data nor pre-built maps and suits complex games with unknown dynamics (p. 2). Prior MA-DRL pursuit works (Bilgin & Kadioglu-Urtis 2015; De Souza et al. 2021; Zhang et al. 2022; Wang et al. 2020; Liao 2020; Hüttenrauch et al. 2019) target land robots/drones/swarms; USV instances remain scarce. The paper builds a full Unity3D training-deployment pipeline for a 3-pursuer-vs-1-evader USV game in multi-obstacle waters, with 6-DOF USV dynamics, sensor models, and PID command interfaces.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
**Problem**: three identical pursuing USVs must cooperatively capture a faster-evading USV (max velocity 10 vs 6 m/s in favor of pursuers, but the evader is more agile: max angular velocity 1.2 vs 0.9 rad/s) before it reaches a preset target point (a "military base" on an island), in a sea area with multiple complex irregular obstacles, wind and wave disturbance (p. 4, Sec. 3.1).

**Gap, as stated by the authors** (p. 2): (1) existing MA-DRL pursuit methods are for land robots/drones and need rework for USVs; [authors' claim — the cited works are indeed robot/drone/swarm works, verified from the reference list]; (2) in most prior strategies the evader has no or poor learning ability, so pursuer strategies "are likely to fail if the evader becomes more intelligent"; [authors' claim — plausible for their cited set; De Souza et al. (2021) track maneuvering targets but do not train an adversarial evader, consistent with the claim]; (3) most works assume barrier-free environments and the trained agents lack autonomous obstacle avoidance; [authors' claim — verified for the cited DRL pursuit works, which pursue in open arenas]. The paper fills this by co-training both sides (pursuit via MA-POCA + CTDE; escape via PPO + BC + GAIL), adding obstacle-rich environments, a curriculum-learning schedule, and an adversarial-evolutionary (alternating self-play) training loop over multiple random scenarios (p. 2, Sec. 5.2).

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Environment + USV modeling**: a 6-DOF kinematic/dynamic USV model (Fossen-style, Eqs. (1)–(5)) realized in Unity3D's physics engine, plus a multi-obstacle battle-space model with ray-sensor (pursuer "sonar", FOV 120°, 50 m) and grid-sensor (evader "radar", 5 m resolution, 140 m width) perception (Secs. 2.1–2.2).
2. **Game-theoretic MDP/POMDP formulation** with task-specific action spaces (Eq. (11)), separate pursuit/escape state spaces coupled to the capture conditions (Eqs. (12)–(18)), and shaped dense reward functions encoding simultaneous-arrival encirclement (Eqs. (19)–(26)).
3. **Pursuit algorithm**: CTDE training with the MA-POCA actor–critic (counterfactual COMA-style credit assignment with residual self-attention critic, Eqs. (27)–(32)) so that each pursuing USV is rewarded by its contribution (first into the capture circle / first stable encirclement) rather than an even split (p. 7).
4. **Escape algorithm**: PPO-Clip combined with imitation learning — BC warm-start whose weight αλ decays 1→0 (Eqs. (35)–(36)) — and GAIL providing an intrinsic reward −log D(s,a) from a discriminator against 38,800 manually-collected expert steps (Eqs. (37), Sec. 4.2).
5. **Training methodology**: curriculum learning with explicit difficulty regulators (speed scaling, capture radius, field area, obstacle number/size, Eqs. (38)–(39)) plus an adversarial-evolutionary game training method over multiple random scenarios that alternately freezes one side and trains the other (H_T0→H_T1, R_T0→R_T1, ...), alternating which side trains first each cycle "to guarantee fairness" (Sec. 5.2).
6. **Experiments**: convergence comparisons (vs MA-POCA without CL and vs MA-PPO for pursuit; vs pure PPO for escape), policy-extraction tests, APF-baseline comparisons (evader APF, pursuer APF à la Janosov et al. 2017 in an archipelago, 50 statistical tests), comprehensive H_Tn-vs-R_Tn confrontation tests, and ONNX inference-time profiling for embedded deployment (STM32H747I-DISCO).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results
The paper contains **no theorems, lemmas, propositions, or corollaries** — it is a modeling/algorithm/experiment paper. The "mathematical results" are the model equations, learning objectives, and training regulators. All are recorded below with page and equation numbers; all verified against 200–300 dpi renderings.

### 5.1 USV dynamics and control interface
- **States (p. 3, Eq. (1))**: η1=[x,y,z]^T, η2=[φ,θ,ψ]^T (roll, pitch, yaw), η=[η1,η2]^T; V=[u,v,w]^T, W=[p,q,r]^T, ν=[V,W]^T — 6 DOF between geodetic frame E0 and hull frame B0 (Table 1).
- **Kinematics (p. 3, Eqs. (2)–(3))**: [η̇1; η̇2] = blkdiag(R_EB1(η2), R_EB2(η2))·[V; W]. R_EB2(η2) = [[1, sinφ tanθ, cosφ tanθ],[0, cosφ, −sinφ],[0, sinφ secθ, cosφ secθ]] (standard z-y-x Euler transform). **Proof gap (typo) in Eq. (2) as printed**: R_EB1(η2) row 1 is printed as [cosψcosθ, cosψsinθsinφ − sinψ**cosθ**, cosψsinθsinφ + sinψ**sinθ**] and row 2 col 3 as sinψ**sinθ**sinφ − cosψsinφ; the standard Fossen rotation requires cosφ/sinφ in those slots (row1: [cψcθ, cψsθsφ − sψcφ, cψsθcφ + sψsφ]; row2 col3: sψsθcφ − cψsφ). The printed matrix is not a valid rotation matrix as typeset; clearly θ/φ subscripts were swapped in production.
- **Dynamics (p. 3, Eq. (4))**: (M_RB + M_A)ν̇_r + (C_RB(ν_r) + C_A(ν_r))ν_r + D(ν_r)ν_r + g(η) = F_propulsion + d, with ν_r = [V_r, W]^T the relative velocity; M from Solidworks modeling, C from M and velocity, D fitted from FLUENT square-law drag simulation, g restoring forces, d wind-wave disturbance; parameters follow Song et al. (2022a,b). During simulation Unity3D's physics engine integrates Eq. (4) via AddRelativeForce/AddRelativeTorque (p. 3).
- **Control commands (p. 3, Eq. (5))**: differential-propulsion normalization δ_nd = (δ_nport − δ_nstbd)/(2δ_nmax), δ_nm = (δ_nport + δ_nstbd)/(2δ_nmax). Policy outputs are mapped to these commands by PID steering and speed controllers at deployment (p. 10).

### 5.2 Game formulation, information structure, termination
- **Decision process (pp. 3–4)**: POMDP tuple (N, 𝒮, 𝒪, 𝒜, P, r, γ); 𝒪 = 𝒪¹×⋯×𝒪^N joint observations; for pursuers 𝒮 = 𝒪 (their domain), reducing to (N, 𝒮, 𝒜, P, r, γ) (verified from p. 3 right column / p. 4). P = 𝒮×𝒜×𝒮 ∈ [0,1]; **shared team reward** r = 𝒮×𝒜 ∈ ℝ for pursuers; γ ∈ [0,1]. Return (p. 4, Eq. (6)): R_{i,t} = r_{i,t} + γr_{i,t+1} + γ²r_{i,t+2} + **γ^{t+3}r_{i,3}** + ⋯ = Σ_{k=0}^∞ γ^k r_{i,t+k} — the term γ^{t+3}r_{i,3} is a printed typo for γ³r_{i,t+3}.
- **Action space (p. 4, Eq. (11))**: 𝒜 = {u, v, r} — continuous, identical for both sides. Inconsistency as printed: the text motivates only surge u and yaw rate r ("underactuation ... fails to provide driving force along the y direction") yet the space lists v as well; "where 𝑥, 𝑦 and 𝜓 are defined in Sec. 2.1.1" is another typo (should be u, v, r).
- **Termination conditions (p. 4, Sec. 3.1, unnumbered bullets)**: Pursuit succeeds iff (1) the evader does not reach the preset target within the specified time, OR (2) all three pursuer–evader distances are less than capture radius R_cap, AND (3) the capture angle γ_ab between two arbitrary pursuing USVs is not greater than π (with 3 pursuers equally spaced, pairwise angles = 2π/3, so this is the encirclement-consistency condition). Escape succeeds iff the evader reaches the target point within the time limit without capture.
- **Observation dimensions (pp. 5–6, Eqs. (15), (18))**: pursuer sensor block dim = N_stack · NumRay · (NumDetectableTags + 2) (one-hot tag ID + hit-flag + normalized hit distance per ray); evader grid-observation dim = GridSize(x) · GridSize(y) · NumDetectableTags, fed as a 3-D tensor ("visual observation") to a CNN. Numeric values of N_stack, NumRay, NumDetectableTags, GridSize: **UNREPORTED**.

### 5.3 Reward design (the paper's main shaped-objective results)
- **Pursuer distance reward (p. 6, Eq. (19))**: r_d = −(k_{d1}·d_a + k_{d2}·e^{(d_a − d̄)/σ(d)} − 1), with d_a = d_aT − R_cap remaining capture distance, d̄ = Σ_i d_i/N, and σ(d) = Σ_{i=1}^N √((d_i − d̄)²)/N **as printed** — note this is a mean absolute deviation (no outer root over the mean), not the standard deviation; the exponential term penalizes a pursuer that is farther than the group mean, enforcing simultaneous arrival.
- **Pursuer phase-angle reward (p. 6, Eq. (20))**: r_θ = k_{θ1}r_{θ1} + k_{θ2}r_{θ2}; r_{θ1} = e^{−|γ_ab − 2π/N|} + e^{−|γ_ac − 2π/N|} − 2; r_{θ2} = e^{−Δγ_a} − 1, where Δγ_a = ||γ_ac|| − ||γ_ab||. Drives each pursuer to keep 2π/N = 120° phase separation from its two neighbors → equal-division stable encirclement.
- **Pursuer task reward (p. 6, Eq. (21))**: r_task = r_suc = 100 (capture success) / r_col = −40 (any collision: obstacles, other pursuers, evader, boundaries) / r_step = −20/MaxSteps(pursuit) (per-step living penalty). Total (Eq. (22)): r_all = ω_d r_d + ω_θ r_θ + r_task, ω_d, ω_θ > 0, ω_d + ω_θ = 1.
- **Evader rewards (pp. 6–7, Eqs. (23)–(26))**: per-pursuer distance reward r̂_{d_a} = 1.0 if d_aT ≥ 100; 0.02·d_aT − 1.0 if R_cap ≤ d_aT ≤ 100; 0.02·R_cap − 1.0 otherwise (piecewise-linear repulsion from pursuers, saturating beyond 100 m). Target penalty r̂_p = −0.01·d_TF if d_TF > 0, else 0 (attraction to target point F). Task reward (Eq. (25)): r̂_suc = 300 (target reached) / r̂_fail = −200 (captured) / r̂_col = −100 (collision) / r̂_step = +20/MaxSteps(escape). Total (Eq. (26)): r̂_all = ω̂_d Σ_{a=1}^N r̂_{d_a} + ω̂_p r̂_p + r̂_task, with ω̂_d + ω̂_p = 1.
- Note the asymmetry: pursuer step reward is negative, evader step reward positive — both encourage fast task completion.

### 5.4 Learning objectives (pursuit: MA-POCA under CTDE)
- **Actor–Critic basics (p. 4, Eqs. (7)–(10))**: Critic TD loss J(θ_c) = ½(r + γV_{θ_c}(s_{t+1}) − V_{θ_c}(s_t))²; gradient (Eq. (8)) ∇_{θ_c}J = −(r + γV_{θ_c}(s_{t+1}) − V_{θ_c}(s_t))∇_{θ_c}V_{θ_c}(s_t). Actor objective J(θ_π) = E_{s0}[V_{π_θ}(s0)]; Monte-Carlo policy gradient (Eq. (10)) ∇_{θ_π}J = E_{π_θ}[Σ_{t=0}^T (Σ_{t'=t}^T γ^{t'−t}r_{t'}) ∇_{θ_π} log π_θ(a_t|s_t)].
- **Centralized critic with RSA (p. 7, Eqs. (27)–(29))**: all N USV observations g^i(o_t^i) are entity-encoded and processed by a Residual Self-Attention block; value becomes V_{θ_c}(RSA(g^i(o_t^i))_{1≤i≤N}) (Eq. (27)); critic loss (Eq. (28)) J(θ_c) = (V_{θ_c}(RSA(·)) − y(λ))² (the ½ of Eq. (7) is dropped as printed), with λ-return y(λ) = (1−λ)Σ_{n=1}^∞ λ^{n−1}G_t^(n) and G_t^(n) = Σ_{l=1}^n γ^{l−1}r_{t+l} + γ^n V_{θ_c} (Eq. (29)).
- **Counterfactual credit assignment (p. 7, Eqs. (30)–(32))**: baseline of the j-th pursuer b_j(o_t, a_t^j) = Q_{θ_π}(RSA(g^j(o_t^j), f^i(o_t^i, a_t^i)_{1≤i≤N, i≠j})) — i.e., the joint value of the other agents' (observation, action) pairs with agent j's action excluded (COMA-style counterfactual); advantage Adv_j = y(λ) − b_j(o_t, a_t^j) (Eq. (31)); actor gradient ∇_{θ_π}J = ∇_{θ_π} log π_j(a^j|o^j)·Adv_j (Eq. (32)). Physical meaning: a pursuer that arrives first inside the capture circle or first forms the stable encirclement earns a larger advantage than teammates — the stated solution to the multi-agent credit-assignment problem (p. 7). Same policy parameters shared by all pursuers during centralized training; distributed execution uses only local observations.
- **No formal proof**: convergence is argued architecturally (CTDE resolves environmental non-stationarity; centralized value is "the correct evaluation of the policy function of the joint state", p. 7) and verified empirically (Sec. 6.1). **Proof gap (framing)**: no regret/optimality/convergence analysis is given for the CTDE + counterfactual scheme in this adversarial non-stationary setting; the shared-reward POMDP with alternating opponent updates (Sec. 5.2) violates the stationary-environment assumption that Eq. (10) style gradients rely on.

### 5.5 Learning objectives (escape: PPO + BC + GAIL)
- **PPO-Clip (p. 8, Eq. (33))**: arg max_{θ'} E_{s∼v^{π_k}} E_{a∼π_{θ_k}(·|s)}[min( (π_{θ'}/π_{θ_k})A^{π_{θ_k}}(s,a), clip(π_{θ'}/π_{θ_k}, 1−ε, 1+ε)A^{π_{θ_k}}(s,a) )], A = Q − V. Two printed imprecisions: the subscript "s∼v^{π_k}" (likely the state visitation distribution) and the sentence "ε is the Generalized Advantage Estimation hyperparameter" — ε is the clip range; λ is the GAE parameter.
- **GAE (p. 8, Eq. (34))**: Â_t = δ_t + (γλ)δ_{t+1} + ⋯ + (γλ)^{T−t+1}δ_{T−1}, δ_t = r_t + γV(s_{t+1}) − V(s_t). **Printed typo**: the final exponent is T−t+1 as typeset; the standard GAE truncation is (γλ)^{T−t−1}δ_{T−1}.
- **BC-augmented policy loss (p. 8, Eqs. (35)–(36))**: L_π = L_θ + αλL_BC, where L_θ is the PPO loss (Eq. (33)), α the BC strength, λ a decay coefficient 1→0 (BC fades out once the agent reaches a certain cumulative-reward level), and L_BC = MSE(π_θ(s_e), a_e) over expert pairs (s_e, a_e) ∼ B (Eq. (36)).
- **GAIL (p. 9, Eq. (37))**: min_G max_D E_{x∼p_data}[log D(x)] + E_{z∼p_z}[log(1 − D(G(z)))]; escape policy = generator; discriminator D is a 2-layer, 128-unit FC net outputting a probability scalar; intrinsic reward r̂_GAIL = −log D(s,a) is added to the external r̂_all of Eq. (26) (p. 9). Expert data: 38,800 steps (after frame-skip division) over 1510 episodes of manual evader control, average reward 196.958 (p. 11).

### 5.6 Curriculum learning regulators (pp. 9–10, Eqs. (38)–(39))
- **Difficulty schedule (Eq. (38))**: χ* = χ[λ + (1−λ)κ] if successtime/Maxstep < 1, else χ, applied to evader speed (λ_v), capture radius (λ_r), task-field area (λ_s); κ = succestime/Maxsuccestime is the ratio of cumulative group successes to the success quota; when the quota is reached the parameter freezes at the real-difficulty value.
- **Obstacle schedule (Eq. (39))**: N*_obs = INT{N^max_obs / Maxsuccestime · succestime} (count grows linearly with cumulative successes, rounding down); S^i,*_obs = Range{S^min_obs, S^max_obs} (obstacle radii uniformly re-sampled each stage). Identical regulators are used for escape-side training, with the previously trained pursuit models fixed at real speed/capture radius (p. 10, Sec. 5.1.2).
- **Adversarial-evolutionary loop (p. 10, Sec. 5.2, unnumbered)**: freeze R_T0, train H → H_T1; freeze H_T1, train R → R_T1; iterate H_Tn/R_Tn; alternate which side trains first across cycles for fairness. This is the paper's mechanism against CL-induced overfitting and poor generalization.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
**Author-stated**: none explicitly in a limitations section; the conclusion claims success across all dimensions (p. 19). Implicitly, the 1:1 confrontation rate is presented as evidence of balanced co-evolution, not as a limitation.

**Critical observations (with page/eq.)**:
1. **No theory**: no convergence, optimality, capture-guarantee, or stability result anywhere; all claims rest on Unity3D simulation curves. For a game paper, there is no barrier/Apollonius/HJI-style analysis nor any comparison to a game-theoretic benchmark solution.
2. **Typo-laden equations**: Eq. (2) rotation matrix invalid as printed (θ/φ swaps, p. 3); Eq. (6) γ^{t+3}r_{i,3} (p. 4); Eq. (34) GAE exponent T−t+1 (p. 8); Eq. (33) "ε is the GAE hyperparameter" (p. 8); Table 3 "d_{d2}" for k_{d2} (p. 10); Table 2 "Weight (m)"; Eq. (11) lists v despite the underactuation discussion. Mostly cosmetic, but they force the reader to re-derive intended forms.
3. **Weak encirclement condition**: the third capture condition (capture angle between two arbitrary pursuers ≤ π, p. 4) is nearly automatic for three pursuers; the real encirclement quality is enforced only through the shaped reward r_θ of Eq. (20), so "encirclement" is a learned preference, not a verified topological property.
4. **σ(d) in Eq. (19)** is a mean-absolute-deviation as printed, and the −1 constants inside r_d and r_{θ2} make the zero-points of the rewards scenario-dependent; no sensitivity study on reward coefficients is provided (only single values in Table 3).

6. **Simulation-only deployment**: "deployment" means ONNX inference inside the Unity simulation and a statement about STM32 embeddability (p. 19); no physical USV experiment, no sim-to-real transfer quantification despite the 6-DOF dynamics and wind disturbance modeling.
7. **Fairness of baselines**: the APF baselines use distance-only greedy logic by the authors' own description (p. 16); the pure-PPO escape baseline is trained without any imitation; no ablation isolates GAIL vs BC contributions or the adversarial-evolutionary loop vs plain self-play.
8. **Information asymmetry**: the evader's grid sensor (140 m width) and the pursuers' ray sensor (50 m, 120°) give different world models; whether the game outcome is robust to sensor-range ablation is unexamined.

## 8. Citation Value
**Role in a paper's related-work section**: the canonical citation for *learning-based multi-USV pursuit-evasion in obstacle-rich marine environments with adversarial co-evolution* — i.e., the applied/engineering wing of PE-game research, contrasting with analytic pursuit papers (Apollonius/barrier/reach-avoid). It is also a usable reference for: MA-POCA/CTDE credit assignment in marine agents, PPO+BC+GAIL hybrid escape policies, curriculum + adversarial-evolutionary training against overfitting, and Unity3D/ONNX/STM32 sim-to-embed pipelines for USVs.
**Suggested citation sentences**: "Learning-based USV pursuit-evasion with obstacle avoidance and counterfactual credit assignment has been demonstrated in simulation, with adversarial-evolutionary co-training yielding near-parity between pursuer and evader policies [P072]." or "In contrast to analytic pursuit strategies, Qu et al. (2023) train three pursuing USVs via MA-POCA under CTDE and an evading USV via PPO combined with BC/GAIL imitation, achieving cooperative capture in unseen multi-obstacle waters at ≈0.1–0.2 s per ONNX inference."
