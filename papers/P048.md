## 2. Research Background
Target-guarding games are dynamic optimization problems with conflicting-objective mobile decision makers, classically introduced by Isaacs [2], and recently extended along area/perimeter defense [3]–[14], reconnaissance [15], [16], and moving/maneuvering targets [17]–[20] (survey: [21]). Motivated by Counter-UAS technology [1], the paper addresses the relatively under-studied regime where **attackers outnumber the defender** and a single defender captures multiple attackers **sequentially**; prior work in this regime covers two cooperative attackers with a half-planar target [22]–[24], an arbitrary number of *noncooperative* attackers [25], capture-order optimization [26], and algorithmic treatments [27], [28]. The distinguishing move here is attacker **heterogeneity**: attackers differ both in speed ratio $\nu_i$ and in a **weight** $\theta_i$ representing degree of importance (e.g., allocated resources), and the attacker team is **cooperative**, jointly minimizing the weighted sum $\sum_i \theta_i h(x_{A_i}(t_i))$ of final proximity levels to a convex target area. The analysis style follows the authors' earlier geometric work [9], [10], [13]: candidate equilibrium policies are constructed geometrically and then *validated* as saddle-point equilibria by showing that a value function satisfies the Isaacs equation — here using parametric optimization sensitivity theory [31], as similarly done in [13].

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
**Problem (Section II, p. 234)**: One defender D (unit speed) and $m$ attackers $A_1,\dots,A_m$ (speeds $\nu_i \in (0,1)$) with simple motion in $\mathbb{R}^n$ play over a convex target $\mathcal{T} = \{x : h(x) \le 0\}$ (1). Attackers are captured in a prescribed order (the attacker indexing is *assumed* to equal the capture order, p. 234); the payoff is $J = \sum_{i=1}^m \theta_i h(x_{A_i}(t_i))$ (3) with capture times $t_i = \inf\{t \ge t_{i-1} : x(t) \in \mathcal{C}_i\}$ and capture sets $\mathcal{C}_i = \{x : \|x_{A_i} - x_D\| = 0 \text{ and } h(x_{A_i}) > 0\}$ (4), $\theta_i > 0$, $\sum_i \theta_i = 1$. Problem 1 asks for the **capturable set** $\mathcal{W} \subset \mathbb{R}^{n(m+1)}$ and a feedback strategy pair satisfying the saddle-point condition (5) $J(\gamma_A^\star,\gamma_D;x^0) \le J(\gamma_A^\star,\gamma_D^\star;x^0) \le J(\gamma_A,\gamma_D^\star;x^0)$.

**Gap — [authors' claim]** (p. 233): "In the majority of cases, the number of defenders either equals or exceeds the number of attackers. However, there is relatively limited literature on scenarios where attackers outnumber defenders and where one defender can capture multiple attackers in a sequential manner." **[Verified from cited text titles]**: the cited 1-vs-many line [22]–[28] indeed consists of (i) two cooperative attackers + half-planar target [22]–[24], (ii) arbitrary *noncooperative* attackers [25] (Fu & Liu, AIAA 2021), (iii) capture-order optimization [26], (iv) sequential-arrival/algorithmic variants [27], [28] — so the survey claim is accurate; in all of them attackers are either homogeneous or noncooperative. **[Authors' claim]** (p. 233): "In sharp contrast to the key references above, this paper focuses on scenarios where an arbitrary number of attackers characterized by heterogeneous speed ratios and degrees of importance collaborate to minimize the weighted sum of their final levels of proximity to a convex target area." **[Verified]**: heterogeneity alone appears in perimeter defense [3] (Adler et al.) and 3D heterogeneous reach-avoid [7] (Yan et al., Automatica 2022), and cooperation of two attackers in [23], [24], but the *combination* — arbitrary $m$, heterogeneous speed **and** importance weights, a *jointly* minimized weighted team cost, and a general convex target in $\mathbb{R}^n$ — is not present in any cited reference; the claim holds as scoped. **[Authors' claim on difficulty]** (p. 233): "the equilibrium control policies of the agents can only be obtained through, despite the convexity of the target area, the globally optimal solution of a possibly nonconvex optimization problem." **[Verified from the paper's own Remarks 3–6]**: the feasible set $\mathcal{A}(x)$ of problem (P) is not convex for $m \ge 2$ because the constraint functions $g_i$ (except $i=1$) are concave in the earlier capture points (p. 235), and (P) "often admits more than one (but finitely many) strict global minimizers" (Remark 6, p. 237). The paper's response to this gap: a **sufficient condition (SOSC)** under which (P) has a unique local minimum that is also global, plus **validation** of the geometric candidate policies via parametric sensitivity analysis [31], following the methodology of [13]. Not covered by any cited/claimed prior work: no cited reference establishes saddle-point equilibrium for heterogeneous *cooperative* attacker teams with sequential capture against a general convex target set.

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Modeling**: A one-defender-vs-$m$-attacker target-guarding game in $\mathbb{R}^n$ with a general convex target (1), simple-motion kinematics (2), heterogeneous speed ratios $\nu_i \in (0,1)$ and heterogeneous importance weights $\theta_i > 0$, $\sum_i \theta_i = 1$ entering a **team** (cooperative) performance index (3) minimized by the attacker team and maximized by the defender; sequential capture with exogenous capture order (index = capture order).
2. **Theory (main result, Theorem 2)**: At any state $x \in \mathcal{X}$ (19) for which a local minimizer of the stacked capture-point problem (P) satisfies the Second-Order Sufficient Condition (SOSC), the global optimal value $V$ of (P) is $C^1$ and satisfies the Isaacs equation (21) **in the classical sense** — hence it *is* the Value of the game — and the geometric "head-to-capture-point" policies (15) are saddle-point equilibrium strategies. Supporting machinery: Remarks 2–5 establish compactness/existence, nonconvexity, boundary/LICQ structure, and SOSC $\Rightarrow$ uniqueness of the local (hence global) minimizer; Theorem 1 (imported from Fiacco [31]) supplies $C^1$ sensitivity of the value.
3. **Algorithmic construction**: The $m$-phase hierarchical (Stackelberg-like, backward-recursive) capture-point problems (11)–(13) collapse into a **single-level** nonconvex program (P) with constraints (14) built from *time-extended safe-reachable regions*; solving one program yields all capture points $p_1^\star,\dots,p_m^\star$ and thus all feedback policies (15).
4. **Numerical evidence (Section V)**: A 1-vs-6 instance with deliberately heterogeneous $(\nu_i, \theta_i)$ showing, against the noncooperative baseline of [25] (problem (33)), that **sacrificial cooperation emerges**: low-weight attackers $A_1, A_3, A_5$ end up farther from the target (higher proximity level) so that high-weight attackers $A_2, A_4, A_6$ get closer (lower), validated over $10^3$ random initial configurations (Figs. 3–4).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results

### 5.1 Dynamics, information structure, termination
- **Target set (1, p. 234)**: $\mathcal{T} = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^n : h(x) \le 0\}$, where $h: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ is twice continuously differentiable and convex; its value indicates the level of proximity to $\mathcal{T}$; example $h(x) = \|x\|^2 - r^2$, $r \ge 0$. (For the disk of radius 2 in Section V: $h(x) = \|x\|_2^2 - 4$; note $h$ is *smaller* = *closer* to $\mathcal{T}$, $h = 0$ on $\partial\mathcal{T}$, and cost minimization by A means getting close.)
- **Kinematics (2a)–(2b, p. 234)**: $\dot{x}_{A_i} = \nu_i u_i$, $x_{A_i}(t_0) = x^0_{A_i}$, $i \in [m]$; $\dot{x}_D = v$, $x_D(t_0) = x^0_D$; controls $u_i, v \in \mathcal{U} = \{\xi \in \mathbb{R}^n : \|\xi\| \le 1\}$; speed ratios $\nu_i \in (0,1)$; "D moves with unitary speed and is faster than all attackers."
- **Information structure (p. 234)**: feedback (perfect state) information pattern; both teams measure the global state $x = [x_{A_1}^\top,\dots,x_{A_m}^\top,x_D^\top]^\top$ at all times; piecewise-continuous feedback strategies $\gamma_A = (\gamma_{A_1},\dots,\gamma_{A_m}): \mathbb{R}^{n(m+1)} \to \mathcal{U}^m$ and $\gamma_D: \mathbb{R}^{n(m+1)} \to \mathcal{U}$. No delays/noise modeled.
- **Termination/capture**: sequential capture with $t_i = \inf\{t \ge t_{i-1} : x(t) \in \mathcal{C}_i\}$, $\mathcal{C}_i = \{x : \|x_{A_i} - x_D\| = 0 \text{ and } h(x_{A_i}) > 0\}$ (4) — capture occurs only strictly outside $\mathcal{T}$; **the attacker indexing is assumed to coincide with the capture order** (p. 234) — this is a structural assumption, not an output of the analysis.
- **Payoff (3, p. 234)**: $J(\gamma_A,\gamma_D;x^0) = \sum_{i=1}^m \theta_i h(x_{A_i}(t_i))$, $\theta_i > 0$, $\sum_{i=1}^m \theta_i = 1$; jointly minimized by A, solely maximized by D (zero-sum in the team sense).
- **Problem 1 (p. 234)**: find the capturable set $\mathcal{W} \subset \mathbb{R}^{n(m+1)}$ (initial states where D captures all $m$ attackers) and the equilibrium pair satisfying (5): $J(\gamma_A^\star,\gamma_D;x^0) \le J(\gamma_A^\star,\gamma_D^\star;x^0) \le J(\gamma_A,\gamma_D^\star;x^0)$ for all admissible $\gamma_A,\gamma_D$, $x^0 \in \mathcal{W}$. Theorem 2 "(partially) answers Problem 1" (p. 236): the analyzed region is $\mathcal{X}$ (19), not a full characterization of $\mathcal{W}$.

### 5.2 Preliminary: single-attacker game ($m=1$; Section III, p. 234)
- With only $A_m$ left (at $t = t_{m-1}$), $J^{(m)} = \Phi^{(m)}(x_m(t_m))$ (6), $\Phi^{(m)}(x_m) = \theta_m h(x_{A_m})$ (purely terminal). By first-order necessary conditions for saddle-point equilibria [29, Theorem 8.2], the equilibrium trajectory over the regular region is a **straight line**.
- **Capture-point problem (7, p. 234)** (convex): $\min_x \theta_m h(x) \;\text{s.t.}\; \frac{\|x - x_{A_m}\|}{\nu_m} - \|x - x_D\| \le 0$; its feasible set $\mathcal{A}^{(m)}(x_m) = \{x : \frac{\|x - x_{A_m}\|}{\nu_m} - \|x - x_D\| \le 0\}$ is the **safe-reachable region** of $A_m$ (an Apollonius-type region since $\nu_m < 1$).
- **Value (8)**: $V^{(m)}(x_m) = \min\{\theta_m h(x) : x \in \mathcal{A}^{(m)}(x_m)\}$ "satisfies the related Isaacs equation [2] in the classical sense over the capturable set" $\mathcal{X}^{(m)} = \{x_m : V^{(m)}(x_m) > 0\}$ (9) — attributed to the literature ([12], [13]), not re-proved here.
- **Equilibrium strategies (10a)–(10b)**: $\gamma^\star_{A_m}(x_m) = \frac{p_m^\star(x_m) - x_{A_m}}{\|p_m^\star(x_m) - x_{A_m}\|}$, $\gamma^\star_D(x_m) = \frac{p_m^\star(x_m) - x_D}{\|p_m^\star(x_m) - x_D\|}$, where $p_m^\star$ solves (7): both players align with the line of sight to the *current* optimal capture point.

### 5.3 Phase decomposition and the single-level program (Section IV-A, p. 235)
- **Backward recursion (11)–(12)**: the game decomposes into $m$ phases; phase $i$ on $[t_i, t_{i+1}]$ involves $A_i,\dots,A_m, D$: $J^{(i)} = \Phi^{(i)}(x_{i:m}(t_i))$, $\forall i \in [m]$ (11), with $\Phi^{(m)}$ as in (6) and $\Phi^{(i)}(x_{i:m}) = \theta_i h(x_{A_i}) + V^{(i+1)}(x_{i+1:m})$ (12), $V^{(i+1)}$ the Value of phase $i+1$. All $\Phi^{(i)}$ are terminal-type, so again by [29, Theorem 8.2] the equilibrium trajectory in each phase is a **straight line** — the key geometric property.
- **Hierarchical problem (13, p. 235)**: attacker $A_i$'s individual capture-point problem, given predecessors' decisions $p_1,\dots,p_{i-1}$: $\min_{p_i} \theta_i h(p_i) + \tilde{V}^{(i+1)}(p_i) \;\text{s.t.}\; \frac{\|p_i - x_{A_i}\|}{\nu_i} - \sum_{j=1}^{i} \|p_j - p_{j-1}\| \le 0$, where $\tilde{V}^{(i+1)}(p_i)$ is the optimal value of the same problem with $i \to i+1$ (a function of $p_i$), and $p_0 = x_D$. The constraint measures the difference between the minimum times for $A_i$ and for D to reach $p_i$ when D must first traverse waypoints $p_1,\dots,p_{i-1}$; the feasible set is the **time-extended safe-reachable region** of $A_i$ (similar notions in [23], [25]).
- **Single-level collapse (P), (14), p. 235**: $\min_{p_{i:m}} f_\theta^{(i)}(p_{i:m}) = \sum_{j=i}^m \theta_j h(p_j) \;\text{s.t.}\; g_j^{(i)}(p_{i:m}, x_{i:m}) \le 0,\; j \in [m]\setminus[i-1]$, where $g_j^{(i)}(p_{i:m}, x_{i:m}) = \frac{\|p_j - x_{A_j}\|}{\nu_j} - \sum_{k=i}^{j} \|p_k - p_{k-1}\|$ (14), again with $p_{i-1} = x_D$ (notational abuse). Solving (P) once yields the solutions of all hierarchical problems at phase $i$.
- **Candidate equilibrium policies (15a)–(15b), p. 235** (the paper's guarding/assignment structure): $\gamma^{(i)\star}_{A_j}(x_{i:m}) = \frac{p_j^\star(x_{i:m}) - x_{A_j}}{\|p_j^\star(x_{i:m}) - x_{A_j}\|}$ for $j \in [m]\setminus[i-1]$, and $\gamma^{(i)\star}_D(x_{i:m}) = \frac{p_i^\star(x_{i:m}) - x_D}{\|p_i^\star(x_{i:m}) - x_D\|}$, where $p_i^\star,\dots,p_m^\star$ solve (P). Note the **defender always heads to the next capture point $p_i^\star$ of the first not-yet-captured attacker**; the capture order itself is *not* optimized (exogenously given by indexing).
- **Remark 1 (p. 235)**: "As to be shown in Theorem 2, solutions for (P) will remain unchanged in all subsequent phases along the equilibrium trajectory" — hence the whole analysis fixes $i = 1$ and suppresses phase superscripts ($f_\theta$, $g_i$, $\mathcal{A}$, $V$, $S$, $\Phi$). *Proof gap (minor)*: Theorem 2 establishes the Isaacs equation for the first phase only; the asserted time-consistency (that re-solving (P) along the equilibrium trajectory reproduces the same capture points) is claimed by reference rather than proved.
- **Definitions (16)–(19), p. 235**: feasible set $\mathcal{A}(x) = \{p \in \mathbb{R}^{mn} : g_i(p,x) \le 0, i \in [m]\}$ (16) — "the Cartesian product of the safe-reachable region of $A_1$ and the time-extended safe-reachable regions of $A_2,\dots,A_m$"; candidate Value $V(x) = \min_p \{f_\theta(p) : p \in \mathcal{A}(x)\}$ (17); solution set $\mathcal{S}(x) = \{p \in \mathcal{A}(x) : f_\theta(p) = V(x)\}$ (18); working state region $\mathcal{X} = \{x : h(p_i) > 0, \forall p \in \mathcal{S}(x), i \in [m]\}$ (19) — states for which **no** optimal capture point (of any global minimizer) lies within $\mathcal{T}$.
- **Remark 2 (p. 235)**: $\mathcal{S}(x)$ is always nonempty and compact. Sketch: $\mathcal{A}(x)$ is compact because if $\|p\| \to \infty$ then some $g_i(p,x) \to \infty$ (at least one attacker has a bounded (time-extended) safe-reachable region — plausible since $1/\nu_i > 1$ while the waypoint chain sum grows like $\|p_i\|$); $h$ continuous; Weierstrass' Theorem [30, Proposition A.8]. Sketch-level: the claim that *some* $g_i \to \infty$ along every unbounded sequence is asserted, not proven in detail.
- **Remark 3 (p. 235–236)**: for $m \ge 2$, $\mathcal{A}(x)$ is no longer guaranteed convex: the constraint functions $g_i$ (except $i=1$) are **concave in $p_j$ for all $j \in [i-1]$** (each term $-\|p_k - p_{k-1}\|$ is concave in the earlier capture points). Consequence: computing/identifying global minimizers of (P) is challenging; "it is often observed that this problem does have more than one local minimizer" (→ Remark 6).
- **Remark 4 (p. 236)**: if $x \in \mathcal{X}$, any local minimizer of (P) is attained **on the boundary** of $\mathcal{A}(x)$, and at such a point the constraint gradients are all linearly independent, i.e., $p_1 \ne \dots \ne p_m$ and $p_i \ne x_{A_i}$ for all $i \in [m]$; moreover "removing any constraint will strictly decrease the (global) optimal value of the problem" (correct direction: enlarging the feasible set of a minimization problem lowers the optimum). *Proof gap (minor)*: the implication "distinctness ⇒ LICQ" and the strict-decrease property are stated with a sketch only; the strict-decrease property is later used (implicitly) to justify strict complementarity, but the paper simply asserts that "the first three conditions in Theorem 1 automatically hold at any local minimizer" (p. 236).

### 5.4 Isaacs framework and parametric sensitivity (Section IV-B, pp. 236–237)
- **Hamiltonian (20, p. 236)** (over the regular region): $H(\nabla U, u, v) = (\nabla_{x_D} U)^\top v + \sum_{i=1}^m (\nabla_{x_{A_i}} U)^\top u_i$, with $U$ the Value of the first phase.
- **Isaacs equation (21a)–(21b), p. 236** [29]: $\bar{H}(\nabla U) = 0$ for $x \in \mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{C}_1$; $U(x) = \Phi(x)$ for $x \in \partial\mathcal{C}_1$, where $\bar{H} = \min_u \max_v H = \max_v \min_u H$ and
  $\bar{H}(\nabla U) = \|\nabla_{x_D} U\| - \sum_{i=1}^m \nu_i \|\nabla_{x_{A_i}} U\|$ (22),
  with the minimizing/maximizing controls $u_i^*(t) = -\frac{\nabla_{x_{A_i}} U}{\|\nabla_{x_{A_i}} U\|}$, $v^*(t) = \frac{\nabla_{x_D} U}{\|\nabla_{x_D} U\|}$ (23). (Decisive structure: the defender's gradient block couples to *all* attackers through the shared waypoint chain, so $\nabla_{x_D} V$ collapses to $(\sum_i \lambda_i^\star)(p_1^\star - x_D)/\|p_1^\star - x_D\|$.)
- **Parametric-optimization apparatus**: active index set $\mathcal{I}(p,x) = \{i \in [m] : g_i(p,x) = 0\}$ (24); Lagrangian $\mathcal{L}(p,\lambda,x) = f_\theta(p) + \sum_{i=1}^m \lambda_i g_i(p,x)$ (25); KKT conditions (26a)–(26c) [30]: $\nabla_{(p,\lambda)}\mathcal{L}(p,\lambda,x) = 0$, $\lambda_i \ge 0\ \forall i$, $\sum_{i=1}^m \lambda_i g_i(p,\lambda,x) = 0$ — note (26c) as printed carries the argument list "$(p,\lambda,x)$" inside $g_i$, an apparent typo for $g_i(p,x)$ (verified against the rendered page).
- **Theorem 1 (quoted from [31, Theorems 3.2.2, 3.4.1]; p. 236)**: Let $\bar{x} \in \mathcal{X}$ and let $p^*$ be a local minimizer of (P) at $\bar{x}$. If there exists $\lambda^*$ such that $(p^*,\lambda^*,\bar{x})$ satisfies: (1) $f_\theta$ and all $g_i$ twice continuously differentiable in $(p,x)$ near $(p^*,\bar{x})$; (2) **LICQ**: $\{\nabla_p g_i(p^*,\bar{x})\}_{i \in \mathcal{I}(p^*,\bar{x})}$ linearly independent; (3) **SCS**: $\lambda_i^* > 0$ for all $i \in \mathcal{I}(p^*,\bar{x})$; (4) **SOSC**: $(p^*,\lambda^*)$ satisfies KKT (26) and $y^\top \nabla^2_{pp}\mathcal{L}(p^*,\lambda^*,\bar{x})\, y > 0$ for all $y \in \mathcal{F}(p^*,\bar{x}) = \{y \ne 0 : \nabla_p g_i(p^*,\bar{x})^\top y = 0,\ i \in \mathcal{I}(p^*,\bar{x})\}$ — then there exist a neighborhood $\mathcal{B}(\bar{x},\epsilon) = \{x : \|x-\bar{x}\| < \epsilon\}$, $\epsilon > 0$, and continuously differentiable functions $p^\star: \mathcal{B} \to \mathbb{R}^{mn}$, $\lambda^\star: \mathcal{B} \to [0,\infty)^m$ such that at any $x \in \mathcal{B}$: (i) $p^\star(x) = p^*$, $\lambda^\star(x) = \lambda^*$, and $p^\star(x)$ is a strict local minimizer of (P); (ii) the (local) optimal value function $\mathcal{W}(x) = \mathcal{L}(p^\star(x),\lambda^\star(x),x)$ is $C^1$ with gradient $\nabla\mathcal{W}(x) = \nabla_x \mathcal{L}(p^\star(x),\lambda^\star(x),x)$ (27). Role: converts the *absence of a closed-form* $V$ into $C^1$ sensitivity w.r.t. the state, which is exactly what is needed to check (21)–(22) pointwise.
- **Remark 5 (pp. 236–237), uniqueness argument**: if SOSC holds at $(p^*,\lambda^*,\bar{x})$, then from (26a), $\nabla_{(p,\lambda)}\mathcal{L}(p^*,\lambda^*,\bar{x}) = [\nabla_p\mathcal{L};\, \nabla_\lambda\mathcal{L}] = 0$ (28), and by the matrix property in [32, Example 4.5], $\det\!\big(\nabla^2_{(p,\lambda)(p,\lambda)}\mathcal{L}(p^*,\lambda^*,\bar{x})\big) \ne 0$ (29) (nonsingular KKT matrix from LICQ + SOSC); by the implicit function theorem [31], $(p^*,\lambda^*)$ is the **unique** solution of $\nabla_{(p,\lambda)}\mathcal{L}(p,\lambda,x) = 0$ (30) on $\mathcal{B}(\bar{x},\epsilon)$. Since any local minimizer must satisfy (30) (with some multiplier, by Remark 4 and necessary conditions), SOSC is a **sufficient condition for (P) to have a unique local minimizer near $\bar{x}$**, which "can immediately [be] deduce[d] from Remark 2 to be also global" (compactness of $\mathcal{A}(x)$). This is the paper's answer to the nonconvexity obstacle: *local* second-order analysis certifies *global* uniqueness.

### 5.5 Theorem 2 (main result; p. 237)
- **Theorem 2 (p. 237)**: At any $x \in \mathcal{X}$ for which there exists a local minimizer of problem (P) satisfying (SOSC): (1) the optimal value function $V$ is continuously differentiable and satisfies the Isaacs equation (21) **in the classical sense**, i.e., it computes the Value of the game; (2) the control policies (15) are the corresponding equilibrium strategies of the game. (Analytic result; local in state — holds on $\mathcal{X}$ wherever SOSC holds — but global in the decision variable via Remark 5.)
- **Proof, step 1 (gradient of $V$, eq. (31), p. 237)**: By Remark 5, $\mathcal{S}(x) = \{p^\star(x)\}$, so $V(x) = \mathcal{L}(p^\star(x),\lambda^\star(x),x)$; by Theorem 1, $V \in C^1$ and $\nabla V(x) = \nabla_x \mathcal{L}(p^\star(x),\lambda^\star(x),x) = \underbrace{\nabla_x f_\theta(p^\star(x))}_{=\,0} + \sum_{i=1}^m \lambda_i^\star(x)\nabla_x g_i(p^\star(x),x)$, i.e., the stacked vector
  $\Big[-\tfrac{\lambda_1^\star(x)}{\nu_1}\tfrac{p_1^\star(x)-x_{A_1}}{\|p_1^\star(x)-x_{A_1}\|};\ \dots;\ -\tfrac{\lambda_m^\star(x)}{\nu_m}\tfrac{p_m^\star(x)-x_{A_m}}{\|p_m^\star(x)-x_{A_m}\|};\ \big(\sum_{i=1}^m \lambda_i^\star(x)\big)\tfrac{p_1^\star(x)-x_D}{\|p_1^\star(x)-x_D\|}\Big]$ (31).
  ($\nabla_x f_\theta = 0$ since $f_\theta$ does not depend on $x$; the $x_D$-block collects the $-\|p_1 - x_D\|$ terms of all constraints, hence the multiplier sum.) Note (31) holds *at the single point* $x$ where SOSC holds; $C^1$ extension to the neighborhood is what Theorem 1 buys.
- **Proof, step 2 (decisive computation, eq. (32), p. 237)**: substituting (31) into (22),
  $\bar{H}(\nabla V(x)) = \Big\|\big(\sum_{i=1}^m \lambda_i^\star(x)\big)\tfrac{p_1^\star(x)-x_D}{\|p_1^\star(x)-x_D\|}\Big\| - \sum_{i=1}^m \nu_i\Big\|\big(-\tfrac{\lambda_i^\star(x)}{\nu_i}\big)\tfrac{p_i^\star(x)-x_{A_i}}{\|p_i^\star(x)-x_{A_i}\|}\Big\| = \big|\sum_{i=1}^m \lambda_i^\star(x)\big| - \sum_{i=1}^m |\lambda_i^\star(x)| \overset{(26b)}{=} 0$ (32),
  because (26b) gives $\lambda_i^\star \ge 0$. Hence (21a) holds in the classical sense. Substituting (31) into (23) retrieves exactly the candidate policies (15) — i.e., $u_{A_i}^\star = (p_i^\star - x_{A_i})/\|p_i^\star - x_{A_i}\|$ and $v^\star = (p_1^\star - x_D)/\|p_1^\star - x_D\|$. This computation is the heart of the paper: the multipliers of the (P) KKT system become the gradient weights of the game's Value function.
- **Proof, step 3 (boundary condition (21b))**: if $x \in \partial\mathcal{C}_1$ then $x_{A_1} = x_D$; the safe-reachable region of $A_1$ degenerates to the singleton $\{x_{A_1}\}$ and (P) reduces to $\min_{p_{2:m}} \theta_1 h(x_{A_1}) + \sum_{i=2}^m \theta_i h(p_i)$ s.t. $\frac{\|p_i - x_{A_i}\|}{\nu_i} - \sum_{j=2}^{m} \|p_j - p_{j-1}\| \le 0$, $i \ge 2$ (as printed, p. 237), "whose optimal value is by definition equivalent to $\Phi(x)$ (see (12) and (13))". *Proof gap (typo, minor)*: the printed inner sum upper limit $m$ is inconsistent with (13)–(14); for attacker $i$ the defender's waypoint chain should end at $p_i$, so the sum should read $\sum_{j=2}^{i}\|p_j - p_{j-1}\|$ — with the printed form all attackers $i \ge 2$ would share one identical constraint. The conclusion (optimal value $= \Phi(x)$, matching the boundary data $U = \Phi$ on $\partial\mathcal{C}_1$) is nonetheless the intended and correct reading. $\square$
- **Physical meaning / extensibility**: the theorem certifies that the *geometric* sequential-pursuit policy (chase the first capture point; each attacker runs toward its own assigned capture point, all computed from one nonconvex program) is a verified saddle-point equilibrium wherever the second-order certification holds — the first such verification for heterogeneous *cooperative* attacker teams over general convex targets in $\mathbb{R}^n$. The template (convex $h$, simple motion, unit-speed defender) could be extended to other proximity functions and dimensions, but the certification is inherently local-in-state and conditional on SOSC.
- **Remark 6 (p. 237), singular surfaces**: (P) often admits more than one (but finitely many) strict global minimizers, in which case Theorem 2 no longer applies: A may choose among several equilibrium strategies with the same Value while D faces "a momentary dilemma" about A's choice — the authors **infer the existence of a dispersal surface of attackers** and leave its identification to future work, referring to [24] (Deng–Shi–Zhong, TAES 2024) for singular surfaces in the two-homogeneous-attacker game.

### 5.6 Noncooperative baseline (33, p. 237)
For comparison, in [25] a team of *noncooperative* attackers sequentially solve $\min_{p_i} h(p_i) \;\text{s.t.}\; \frac{\|p_i - x_{A_i}\|}{\nu_i} - \sum_{j=1}^{i}\|p_j - p_{j-1}\| \le 0$ (33) — like (13) but hierarchical *only through the constraints* (each attacker indifferent to the others' objective values) and with **no weights $\theta_i$**.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
- **Author-stated**: (i) identification of the attacker dispersal surface (multiple strict global minimizers of (P), Remark 6, p. 237) is left for future work — outside it, Theorem 2 does not apply; (ii) future problem variant: "the defender has no prior (or incorrect) information about the parameters of the attackers" (Section VI, p. 237).
- **Capture order is exogenous** (p. 234: "Under the assumption that the indexing of A corresponds to the order in which they will be captured"): neither team optimizes the capture order; the "assignment" (order) is input, not output — a significant gap vs. [26], where capture-order optimization is the objective.
- **Partial answer to Problem 1**: Theorem 2 "(partially) answers" it (p. 236); the analyzed region is $\mathcal{X}$ (19) with SOSC holding locally — the capturable set $\mathcal{W}$ is never characterized, and the attackers' winning region (optimal capture points inside $\mathcal{T}$) and the game's barrier are not constructed; the analysis is classical (pointwise $C^1$ Value), not viscosity.
- **SOSC is a per-state sufficient condition**: existence of a local minimizer satisfying SOSC must be checked instance-by-instance (done numerically in Section V); no structural parameter conditions on $(\nu_i, \theta_i, x^0)$ guaranteeing SOSC are given, so the certificate's scope is not known a priori.
- **Sketch-level lemmas**: Remark 2 (compactness: "some $g_i \to \infty$"), Remark 4 (distinctness ⇒ LICQ; strict decrease upon constraint removal — the latter implicitly underpins strict complementarity, which the paper declares "automatic", p. 236) are stated with sketches; Remark 1's time-consistency claim is asserted by reference to Theorem 2, which treats only the first phase.
- **Printed typos (verified against rendered pages)**: (26c) reads $\sum_{i=1}^m \lambda_i g_i(p,\lambda,x) = 0$ — argument list should be $(p,x)$ (p. 236); the boundary-case constraint in the proof of Theorem 2 reads $\sum_{j=2}^{m}\|p_j - p_{j-1}\|$ where $\sum_{j=2}^{i}$ is intended (p. 237); "the two norm" (p. 234) for 2-norm.
- **Modeling restrictions**: simple motion for all agents; unit-speed defender strictly faster than every attacker ($\nu_i < 1$); point capture ($\|x_{A_i} - x_D\| = 0$) strictly outside $\mathcal{T}$; perfect state feedback for both teams (no sensing/communication constraints); cooperation is imposed through the joint weighted cost — individual attacker preferences (e.g., why a low-weight attacker accepts sacrifice) are outside the model.
- **Critical observation on the numerical evidence**: the emergent-cooperation conclusion rests on one parameter set with $10^3$ random initial states; the mechanism (multipliers $\lambda_i^\star$ from (31) weighting each attacker's contribution to $\nabla_{x_D}V$) is consistent with the observation, but no ablation over $\theta_i$ or $\nu_i$ magnitudes is provided.

## 8. Citation Value
**Role in a paper**: cite P048 as the reference for **one-defender-vs-many target guarding against a heterogeneous, cooperative attacker team with importance weights**, and as a methodological exemplar of *certifying geometric reach-avoid strategies via parametric nonlinear-programming sensitivity* (Fiacco-type SOSC ⇒ classical Isaacs-equation verification) rather than HJI/viscosity computation. It sits between the noncooperative 1-vs-many line ([25], [26]) and the two-cooperative-attacker singular-surface analysis of [24], and extends the authors' own convex-target guarding series [9], [10], [13] to attacker heterogeneity and team cooperation; the time-extended safe-reachable region with waypoint chains (13), (14) is a reusable device for sequential-capture constraints, and Remark 6's dispersal-surface conjecture is a natural hook for singular-surface work. Suggested citation sentences: "Equilibrium policies for guarding a convex target from multiple sequentially captured attackers have been certified via parametric optimization sensitivity analysis, with heterogeneity in attacker speeds and importance weights shown to induce emergent sacrificial cooperation [Lee et al., ACC 2024, P048]"; or "In [P048], the single-level program over stacked capture points is generally nonconvex, and a saddle-point equilibrium is established only where its KKT system satisfies the second-order sufficient condition."
