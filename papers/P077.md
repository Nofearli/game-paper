## 2. Research Background

Multiplayer pursuit–evasion games (PEGs) with terminal constraints — reach-avoid games — are generically hard because the exact solution requires solving a high-dimensional HJI equation over the joint state of all players (the paper cites the full-dimensional HJI numerical approach of Fisac et al. [21] and the approximate pairwise-outcome and open-loop alternatives of Chen et al. [16] and Zhou et al. [17]). A practical route is to reduce the game to lower-dimensional quantities. This paper pursues that route with a scalar **risk metric** built purely from **time-related** quantities (time-to-target, time-to-intercept, min–max time-to-capture) rather than purely geometric ones, motivated by the authors' statement that time metrics "can be the basis for an evasion framework which is as independent of the specific nature of the players as possible" (p. 697). The adversarial model is deliberately restricted: the pursuer team plays the **semi-cooperative relay pursuit** strategy of Bakolas–Tsiotras [1] (also used in [16]), in which at every instant only the pursuer that could capture the evader fastest (the *active* pursuer) engages, while all others stay at their posts — a model relevant to patrol/surveillance agents that prefer to stay near their initial positions. The target location is known only to the evader, so a pursuer computing intercept times must assume the evader does not maneuver toward an unknown goal; this asymmetry is exactly what makes the "minimum intercept time" φ_s differ from the "min–max capture time" φ_c and what the entire safe/risky/failing classification exploits. The work extends the authors' own semi-analytical evader designs (dynamic roadmap and static-game approximation, [23], [24]) to a fully analytical, state-feedback construction.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap

**Problem (Problem 1, p. 697).** In R² (claimed to extend "naturally" to R³), N pursuers P_i (i ∈ I = {1,…,N}) and one evader E have single-integrator dynamics ẋ_i = v_{pi} u_i, ẋ_e = v_e u_e, u ∈ U = {u ∈ R² : ‖u‖ ≤ 1} (eq. (1a)–(1b)). Given initial states x̄_i, x̄_e, target x_T, capture radius ℓ and target tolerance ϵ (typically ℓ > ϵ), find u_e(·) steering the evader so that ‖x_e(t_f) − x_T‖ ≤ ϵ while ‖x_e(t) − x_i(t)‖ > ℓ for all i and all t ∈ [0, t_f). Termination: first instant at which either tolerance is met; if both occur simultaneously the evader is declared successful (p. 697). Perfect information about all player states is assumed at all times; the target location is private to the evader. Pursuers commit to relay pursuit (eq. (2)): u*_j = r_j/‖r_j‖ if j = i_c (active pursuer, smallest capture time = "relay metric"), else 0, with r_j = x_e − x_j.

- **[authors' claim]** Prior multi-pursuer reach-avoid results are either numerical HJI solutions [21], approximations via pairwise outcomes [16] or open-loop formulations [17], two-defender analytical results [22], or the authors' semi-analytical roadmap/static-game schemes [23], [24]; none gives an *analytical, closed-form feedback* solution for reaching a **fixed target point** against relay pursuers (p. 697, Sec. I-B).
- **[verified from cited-text descriptions in the paper]** The literature review itself (pp. 696–697) describes [1], [10] as dynamic-Voronoi sequential pursuer selection, [8] as Voronoi-cell-shrinking switching pursuit, [12] as greedy grid pursuit, [21] as full HJI, [16], [17] as approximations, [22] as an analytical three-player (two defenders, one attacker) solution, [18] as priority-based sequential path planning, and [19] as a Nash quadratic game — consistent with the claimed gap for a *point-target, N+1-player, feedback* solution, provided one accepts the two model restrictions the gap statement does not foreground: (i) pursuers are confined to relay pursuit (only one active pursuer at any time), and (ii) the evader's strategy is *feasible* (guaranteed against the prescribed pursuer model), not a saddle-point strategy of a fully adversarial N+1-player game.
- **[authors' claim]** "In contrast to the above-mentioned reach-avoid problems, in this paper, we present an analytical solution to the problem of reaching a fixed target point. Further, our partition of the state-space reflects the fact that the evader must frequently follow curved paths to successfully reach the target" (p. 697). The first claim is verified for the two-player game (Props. 1–7 are fully analytical); for N > 1 the solution is a pointwise convex program (Prop. 8) whose success is validated only numerically — so "analytical solution" holds strictly only for N = 1. The "curved paths" remark contrasts with [22]-style partitions in which straight-line motion suffices in each cell.

## 4. Core Contributions

1. **(Theory/metric)** A novel scalar **log-risk metric** β_i = log(φ_T/φ_s) (eq. (9), p. 700) for the two-player target-seeking evasion game, built from the minimum time-to-target φ_T = ‖x_T − x_e‖/v_e and the minimum **intercept** time φ_s of an evader that runs straight for the target (both closed-form via a quadratic equation, eq. (4)).
2. **(Theory/partition)** A partition of the augmented state space X = {z ∈ R⁴ : z = [x_e^T x_i^T]^T} into **safe (S)**, **failing (F)** and **risky (R)** open sets (Prop. 1, p. 699) determined by comparisons of φ_s, φ_c, φ_T; with closed-form circular boundaries of the evader-position projections S_e(x_i), F_e(x_i) (Prop. 2, p. 700) — the reach-avoid analogue of Apollonius-circle partitions, with circles centered at the target and at the pursuer.
3. **(Theory/control, two-player)** A **quickest descent** nonlinear feedback: inside S, pure target seeking (Prop. 3, eq. (10)); outside S, the control −χ_i/‖χ_i‖ that pointwise minimizes the time derivative of the log-risk (Prop. 5, eqs. (11)–(15)); switched by the region (eq. (19)). Conditional safe-reach guarantee from R (Prop. 4), invariance of S (Corollary after Prop. 3), a no-capture guarantee for equal speeds via an inflated capture radius ℓ̂ (Prop. 6), and continuity of the switched law at ∂S (Prop. 7).
4. **(Algorithm/extension)** Extension to N pursuers: S = ∩_i S_i, F = ∪_i F_i, R = int(X \ (S̄ ∪ F̄)); a **weighted least-squares / QCQP** over the unit ball that simultaneously minimizes all risk derivatives, with relevance weights w_i = 1 + e^{−φ_s(x_e,x_i)} normalized to w̄_i (eqs. (20)–(26), Prop. 8, p. 704–705), and the switched law (27).
5. **(Experiment)** Monte-Carlo validation (∼10⁵ runs for N = 1; random initial conditions in R, random targets) showing 0.00% capture for N = 1, 26.88% for N = 2, 41.66% for N = 4 (Table I, p. 706), plus trajectory and risk-contour illustrations (Figs. 4, 7).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results

### 5.0 Dynamics, information structure, cost, termination

- **Dynamics (eq. (1a)–(1b), p. 697)**: ẋ_i = v_{pi} u_i, x_i(0) = x̄_i; ẋ_e = v_e u_e, x_e(0) = x̄_e; u_i, u_e ∈ U = {u ∈ R² : ‖u‖ ≤ 1}; zero input used only after termination. Positions only (no velocity states).
- **Pursuer model (eq. (2), p. 698)**: relay pursuit — u*_j(x_e, x_j) = r_j/‖r_j‖ if j = i_c, else 0; r_j := x_e − x_j. The active index i_c is the argmin of the relay metric (least capture time over the team).
- **Range dynamics (eq. (3), p. 698)**: d‖r_i‖/dt = r_i^T(v_e u_e − v_{pi} u_i)/‖r_i‖, r_i(0) = x̄_e − x̄_i.
- **Min–max time-to-capture (eq. (4), p. 698)**: with optimal pursuit u_i = r_i/‖r_i‖ and optimal evasion u_e = r_i/‖r_i‖, φ_c(x_e, x_i) is the smallest positive real root of
  (v_e² − v_{pi}²)φ² + 2(v_e r_i^T u_e − ℓ v_{pi})φ + ‖r_i‖² − ℓ² = 0.  (4)
  Substituting u_e = r_i/‖r_i‖ gives the explicit form (eq. (6), p. 699): (v_e² − v_{pi}²)φ² + 2(v_e‖r_i‖ − ℓ v_{pi})φ + ‖r_i‖² − ℓ² = 0. If v_e < v_{pi} this quadratic always has a positive real root → capture is inevitable (φ_c well defined, value of the two-player zero-sum game). If v_e ≥ v_{pi} and ‖r̄_i‖ > ℓ, optimal evasion precludes capture: φ_c is **not well defined** (Fig. 2, p. 699), although φ_s can still be finite.
- **Minimum intercept time**: φ_s(x_e, x_i, x_T) = smallest positive real root of (4) with u_e = (x_T − x_e)/‖x_T − x_e‖ (single quadratic equation, solved via [25]); it is the value of the minimum-time problem of intercepting a non-maneuvering (target-bound) evader, and serves as a **lower bound for φ_c under non-optimal evader play** (p. 698). Also φ_T(x_e, x_T) = ‖x_T − x_e‖/v_e.
- **Evader cost (eq. (5), p. 698)** — a terminal-time ratio (verified from the rendered equation):
  P(u_e(·)) := [ φ_T(x_e, x_T) − ϵ/v_e ] / [ min_i φ_s(x_i, x_e, x_T) ] |_{t=t_f}.
  P = 0 on success (target reached uncaptured); P → ∞ when capture before target reach is inevitable (denominator → 0 at capture, e.g., slower evader). Goal: minimize P, i.e., drive it to 0 if possible.
- **Termination/winning**: evader wins iff ∃ t_f with ‖x_e(t_f) − x_T‖ ≤ ϵ and ‖x_e(t) − x_i(t)‖ > ℓ ∀i, t ∈ [0, t_f); simultaneous capture-and-arrival counts for the evader; capture before arrival wins for the pursuers.

### 5.1 Proposition 1 (p. 699) — safe/failing/risky partition

**Statement.** For fixed x_T, the open sets
- S := {z ∈ X : φ_s(x_e, x_i, x_T) > φ_T(x_e, x_T)} ("safe": straight run to target cannot be interrupted),
- F := {z ∈ X : φ_s(x_e, x_i, x_T) < φ_T(x_e, x_T) and φ_c(x_e, x_i) < φ_T(x_e, x_T)} ("failing": even the max available time φ_c is below the time-to-target),
- R := {z ∈ X : φ_s(x_e, x_i, x_T) < φ_T(x_e, x_T) and φ_c(x_e, x_i) > φ_T(x_e, x_T)} ("risky": straight run is interceptable, but the max available time exceeds φ_T)

satisfy (1) X = S̄ ∪ F̄ ∪ R̄ and (2) S ∩ F = ∅, F ∩ R = ∅, S ∩ R = ∅.

**Assumptions**: φ_c, φ_s, φ_T uniquely determined by z (via (4) and ‖x_T − x_e‖); φ_c meaningful as a finite quantity for the F/R dichotomy (for v_e ≥ v_{pi} the classification is repaired separately, see Prop. 2/Remark 3).
**Proof approach** (3 steps): (i) given (x_e, x_i), the three times are unique functions of z, so every z satisfies the (closed) membership condition of at least one of S̄, F̄, R̄ → X ⊆ S̄ ∪ F̄ ∪ R̄; (ii) S, F, R ⊆ X and X is closed ⇒ S̄ ∪ F̄ ∪ R̄ ⊆ X; (iii) disjointness of the open sets is immediate from the strict inequalities defining them (e.g., φ_s > φ_T excludes both F and R).
**Decisive fact**: uniqueness of φ_c, φ_s, φ_T per state + trichotomy of comparisons with φ_T. Local/global: global in X (with x_T as a fixed parameter; the augmented space X folds the target in as a coordinate z = [x_e^T x_i^T]^T after WLOG x_T = 0, p. 700).
**Physical meaning**: S = guaranteed win by running straight; F = doomed; R = win depends on maneuvering (capture time of a *maneuvering* evader exceeds the intercept time of a straight-running one because pursuers do not know the evader's intent).
**Extensibility/caveats**: "partition" is in the sense of pairwise-disjoint open sets whose closures cover X — boundary states (∂S, ∂F, ∂R) belong to several closures (the paper acknowledges "the closures of S, F, and R may intersect", Remark 1, p. 699–700). For v_e ≥ v_{pi}, φ_c is undefined; the paper then treats F geometrically ( Remark 3: for v_e = v_{pi}, F is exactly the capture ball of radius ℓ; for v_e > v_{pi}, Prop. 2's radius (1 − v_e/v_{pi})‖x_i‖ + v_eℓ/v_{pi} becomes negative for ‖x_i‖ > ℓ v_{pi}/(v_{pi} − v_e)... i.e., F is empty beyond a bounded region). This edge handling is stated but not formalized inside Prop. 1 itself — **minor proof gap in rigor** (the proposition implicitly presumes φ_c exists).

**Remark 1 (p. 699–700)**: qualitative reading of S/F/R as safe/unsafe/undecided; **Remark 2 (p. 700)**: from R, two possibilities — a control exists that transfers the state into S at some t_s, or none does; membership in R is precisely the "can possibly reach S" situation.

### 5.2 Proposition 2 (p. 700) — closed-form circular boundaries (Apollonius-type cells)

**Statement.** Pursuer at x_i, target at origin. (1) ∂S_e(x_i) (projection of ∂S on the evader's position plane) is the **circle centered at the target** with radius (∥x_i∥ − ℓ) v_e/v_{pi}; (2) ∂F_e(x_i) is the **circle centered at the pursuer** x_i with radius (1 − v_e/v_{pi})‖x_i‖ + v_e ℓ/v_{pi}.

**Assumptions**: x_T = 0 (WLOG); the boundary equalities φ_s = φ_T (for S) and φ_c = φ_T (for F).
**Proof approach** (algebraic, 4 steps): (i) rearrange (4) into the intercept-sphere form (eq. (7), p. 700): ‖r_i + v_e u_e φ_s‖ = ℓ + v_{pi} φ_s; (ii) on ∂S, φ_s = φ_T = ‖x_e‖/v_e and u_e = −x_e/‖x_e‖, so u_e φ_s = −x_e/v_e; substituting, ‖r_i − x_e‖ = ‖−x_i‖ = ℓ + v_{pi}‖x_e‖/v_e, whence ‖x_e‖ = (∥x_i∥ − ℓ)v_e/v_{pi} — **decisive intermediate equality**; (iii) for F, with u_e = r_i/‖r_i‖, (7) yields eq. (8), p. 700: ‖r_i‖ = ℓ + (v_{pi} − v_e) φ_c; (iv) on ∂F ∩ {r_i = αx_e, α < 0} (pursuer between evader and target, where intercept = capture), φ_c = φ_T(x̂_e) = φ_s(x̂_e, x_i) and using (8) plus the ∂S value gives ‖r_i‖ = ℓ + (v_{pi}/v_e − 1)(∥x_i∥ − ℓ)v_e/v_{pi}, i.e., ‖x_e − x_i‖ = (1 − v_e/v_{pi})‖x_i‖ + v_eℓ/v_{pi} — second decisive equality.
**Analytic, global** (in the evader's position plane, for fixed x_i). **Physical meaning**: the evader is safe iff it is inside the target-centered circle (∂S_e); it is doomed iff it is inside the pursuer-centered circle (∂F_e); between the two circles lies the risky annular region where evasion maneuvering matters. **Relation to classical objects**: these circles play the role Apollonius circles play in plain pursuit games; here they are "target-intercept" circles — for v_e = v_{pi} the F-circle collapses to the capture ball (radius ℓ, Remark 3, p. 700), and the S-circle shrinks proportionally to v_e/v_{pi}. **Extensibility**: gives an O(1) per-pursuer membership test; the multi-pursuer sets are Boolean combinations of these circles (Sec. IV), so the whole partition is computable in closed form for any N.
**Remark 3 (p. 700)**: degeneracy at v_e = v_{pi} (F = ball of radius ℓ); S shrinks as v_e/v_{pi} ↓.

### 5.3 Proposition 3 (p. 700, eq. (10)) + unnumbered Corollary (p. 701) — optimal play and invariance in S

**Statement (Prop. 3).** If [x_e^T x_i^T]^T ∈ S at time t, the control minimizing the performance index (5) is the pure target-seeking feedback
u_eS(x_e) = −x_e/‖x_e‖ if ‖x_e‖ > ϵ, else 0.  (10)
**Proof** (3 steps): in S, φ_s > φ_T, so constant-speed straight motion reaches the target before any intercept is possible; the resulting P is exactly 0, the global minimum of (5); hence (10) is optimal. Analytic; global on S.
**Corollary (p. 701).** Under (10) in S, the trajectory remains in S for all τ ∈ [t, t_f]. **Proof**: along the straight optimal trajectory, (d/dt)φ_s(x_e, x_i) = (d/dt)φ_T(x_e) = −1 (both clocks tick down at unit rate), so the strict inequality φ_s > φ_T is preserved — **decisive intermediate equation**; a one-line transport/invariance argument (no Nagumo formalism needed because both functions decrease at the same known rate).
**Remark 4 (p. 701)**: the guarantee is stringently tied to (10) — deviating from it inside S can still lead to capture before reaching the target. **Remark 5 (p. 701)**: on ∂S, (10) is still applied but arrival coincides with capture; if v_e = v_{pi} there exists a state in ∂S ∩ ∂F with φ_s = φ_T = φ_c, i.e., the evader is "already captured" at t = 0 (degenerate, no game ensues).
**Physical meaning**: S is a forward-invariant win set under greedy target seeking. **Extensibility**: the same unit-rate argument underlies the N-pursuer safe set S = ∩_i S_i.

### 5.4 Proposition 4 (p. 701) — conditional safe-reach from R

**Statement.** If the initial augmented state is in R and there exists an evader control signal u_e(·) that drives the log-risk β_i(x_e, x_i) to zero at some t_s > 0, then the evader can reach the target safely: ∃ t_f > t_s : ‖x_e(t_f)‖ < ϵ and ‖r_i(t)‖ > ℓ for all t ∈ [t_s, t_f].
**Assumptions**: initial state in R (φ_T/φ_s > 1 but φ_T < φ_c); **existence of a risk-driving control** — this is a hypothesis, not established for all of R.
**Proof approach** (2 steps): reaching β_i = 0 means φ_T/φ_s = 1, i.e., the state has been transferred to ∂S without capture during the transition; from t_s onward invoke Prop. 3/Corollary (invariance + arrival). Analytic but **conditional**; global over the (uncharacterized) subset of R from which the transition exists.
**Physical meaning**: R is the "maneuver first, then sprint" region; the two-phase structure (drive risk down, then go straight) is the paper's central operating principle. **Remark 6 (p. 701)**: states in R for which no control history achieves φ_T/φ_s = 1, and all states in F, end in capture. **Proof gap (acknowledged)**: Remark 7 (p. 702) concedes that "the subset of states for which such a transition to S is possible is determined by the relative speeds of the two players" but no closed-form characterization of that subset is given.

### 5.5 Problem 2 (eq. (11), p. 701) + Proposition 5 (p. 701–702, eqs. (12)–(15)) — quickest descent control law

**Problem 2.** On [0, t_s] (t_s = first time the augmented state reaches ∂S), choose u_e(t) = û minimizing the pointwise cost J_t(u) := β̇_i(u; x_e(t), x_i(t)) — the time derivative of the log-risk along the trajectories. (For states starting in F, where no transition exists, the same pointwise minimization is applied "until capture occurs", p. 701.)

**Statement (Prop. 5).** The solution is the nonlinear state feedback, for t ∈ [0, t_s],
u_eR(x_e, x_i) = −χ_i/‖χ_i‖ if ‖χ_i‖ ≠ 0; −x_e/‖x_e‖ if ‖χ_i‖ = 0 and ‖x_e‖ > ϵ; 0 otherwise,  (12)
where (eq. (13), p. 701–702)
χ_i(x_e, x_i) := x_e/(φ_T ‖x_e‖) − ν/μ,
with (verified against the rendered page 702)
ν(x_e, x_i) := v_e r_i − (v_e² φ_s r_i)/‖x_e‖ + v_e² φ_s (x_e^T r_i) x_e/‖x_e‖³ − v_e² φ_s x_e/‖x_e‖,
μ(x_e, x_i) := ‖r_i‖² − ℓ² − φ_s ( v_{p} ℓ + v_e (r_i^T x_e)/‖x_e‖ ).

**Assumptions**: pursuer plays relay/pure pursuit u_i = r_i/‖r_i‖; φ_s obtained from the quadratic (4) (arguments dropped per the paper's footnote 1); μ ≠ 0 along the analysis (implicit differentiation of (4) requires the root to be simple); U is the unit ball.
**Proof approach** (4 steps): (i) differentiate the definition β_i = log(φ_T/φ_s): β̇_i = φ̇_T/φ_T − φ̇_s/φ_s; (ii) obtain φ̇_s and φ̇_T by **implicit differentiation of the quadratic (4)** and of φ_T = ‖x_e‖/v_e with respect to time (the quadratic's coefficients depend on (x_e, x_i, φ_s)); (iii) after grouping terms, the **decisive structural equation** (eq. (14), p. 702): β̇_i = χ_i^T u_e + γ_i, where γ_i is the u_e-free scalar (eq. (15), p. 702): γ_i(x_e, x_i) := (1/μ)( −v_e φ_s v_p (r_i^T x_e)/(‖r_i‖ ‖x_e‖) + v_p ‖r_i‖ ); (iv) since β̇_i is affine in u_e, the minimizer over the unit ball is the negative normalized gradient direction û_1 = −χ_i/‖χ_i‖ (Cauchy–Schwarz: min_{‖u‖≤1} χ^T u + γ = −‖χ‖ + γ); if χ_i = 0 the risk derivative carries no information and the fallback û_2 = −x_e/‖x_e‖ (target seeking) is used.
**Nature**: analytic, pointwise-in-time (greedy), global where χ_i, μ are well defined; the resulting φ_s in (13) makes the law genuinely nonlinear in the state.
**Physical meaning**: χ_i is the "risk descent direction": Remark 9 (p. 703) shows via the closed form (18) that for v_e = v_{pi} the control is a **convex-type blend of pure evasion (−r_i/‖r_i‖) and pure target seeking (−x_e/‖x_e‖)** with weights governed by e^{−β̂_i} — the riskier the situation, the more the law weights evasion; as risk vanishes it converges to pure target seeking. **Extensibility**: the affine-in-u structure (14) is exactly what makes the N-pursuer extension a least-squares problem (Sec. IV); the same derivation would go through for any kinematics where φ_s admits implicit differentiation.

### 5.6 Range dynamics and Proposition 6 (p. 702, eqs. (16)–(18)) — no-capture guarantee for equal speeds

**Range dynamics under relay pursuit (eq. (16), p. 702)**: d‖r_i‖/dt = (v_e/‖r_i‖) r_i^T u_e − v_{pi}. Since max‖u_e‖ = 1, d‖r_i‖/dt < 0 whenever v_e < v_{pi} — relative distance decreases monotonically regardless of evasion (consistent with inevitable capture for the slower evader). For v_e = v_{pi} the sign depends on u_e, which is what Prop. 6 exploits.

**Statement (Prop. 6).** Let v_e = v_{pi}. If ‖r_i(0)‖ > ℓ, then for every inflated radius ℓ̂ ∈ (ℓ, ‖r_i(0)‖], applying the quickest descent law (12) **with ℓ replaced by ℓ̂** yields ‖r_i‖ ≥ ℓ̂ > ℓ for all t ∈ [0, t_f]; hence the evader avoids capture for all t > 0.

**Assumptions**: WLOG v_e = v_{pi} = 1; initial separation exceeds ℓ; the modified (conservative) risk uses ℓ̂ in (13), (15) and in φ_s's quadratic.
**Proof approach** (5 steps): (i) define the modified log-risk, **decisive closed form** (eq. (17), p. 702): β̂_i(x_e, x_i) = log( 2(ℓ̂ ‖x_e‖ + r_i^T x_e) / (‖r_i‖² − ℓ̂²) ); (ii) the corresponding direction vector (eq. (18), p. 702): χ̂_i = (2/(‖r_i‖² − ℓ̂²)) [ −(1 − e^{−β̂_i}) r_i + e^{−β̂_i} (‖x_e‖ + ℓ̂) x_e/‖x_e‖ ]; (iii) as ‖r_i‖ → ℓ̂, β̂_i → ∞ so e^{−β̂_i} → 0, and χ̂_i/‖χ̂_i‖ → −r_i/‖r_i‖ (pure evasion) — shown both for small ‖x_e‖ directly and for large ‖x_e‖ = Λ by a scaling argument: with δ = 1/Λ at ‖r_i‖² − ℓ̂² = δ, e^{β̂_i} = 2(ℓ̂Λ + ‖r_i‖Λ cos θ_{re})/δ = O(Λ²) (Landau notation, footnote 2), so the target-seeking term is dominated; (iv) from (16) with u_e = −χ̂_i/‖χ̂_i‖ and v_e = v_{pi} = 1: d‖r_i‖/dt = −(r_i^T χ̂_i/(‖r_i‖ ‖χ̂_i‖)) − 1, and as the state approaches the set A_c := {z : ‖r_i‖ = ℓ̂} (the paper says convergence "induced by the Hausdorff metric"), d‖r_i‖/dt → 0; (v) therefore the distance decelerates to zero rate at the ℓ̂-shell and never crosses it: for any ‖r_i(0)‖ > ℓ one can pick ℓ̂ ∈ (ℓ, ‖r_i(0)‖] with ‖r_i‖ ≥ ℓ̂ > ℓ on [0, t_f]. Note "even as β̂_i → ∞, the actual log-risk β_i < ∞".
**Nature**: analytic but **asymptotic/barrier-flavored**; a local-in-the-limit argument on the ℓ̂-shell, global in the sense that the initial distance only needs to exceed ℓ.
**Physical meaning**: against an equal-speed pursuer, chasing risk reduction fastest also preserves a hard safety margin: the law automatically becomes pure evasion near the inflated capture radius, producing a "soft barrier" at ‖r_i‖ = ℓ̂ — this is the paper's formal capture-avoidance guarantee (the analogue of a barrier surface, obtained without HJI).
**Extensibility**: the inflation trick (optimize w.r.t. ℓ̂ > ℓ to buy strictness) is reusable in any risk-based design.
**Proof gap (critical observation)**: step (iv)–(v) establish lim d‖r_i‖/dt → 0 at the shell, but a rigorous forward-invariance statement (e.g., a Nagumo/Brežis condition on the set {‖r_i‖ ≥ ℓ̂}, or a comparison argument bounding d‖r_i‖/dt ≤ −g(‖r_i‖) with g > 0 outside the shell) is **not** provided; nor is χ̂_i ≠ 0 along trajectories verified (the fallback branch of (12) could in principle alter the argument). The Hausdorff-metric sentence is informal. The conclusion is nonetheless corroborated by the Monte-Carlo evidence (0.00% capture in ~10⁵ runs, Table I).

**Remark 8 (p. 703)**: from (16), for v_e = v_{pi} the distance on [0, t_f] is lower-bounded by ℓ and upper-bounded by its initial value; for v_e > v_{pi} it is not upper-bounded by the initial value; **in both cases the augmented state never transitions from R to F** under the quickest descent law (the risky set is "safe to roam").
**Remark 9 (p. 703)**: the weighted-sum interpretation of (18) (see 5.5).
**Fig. 4 (p. 703)**: contours of β̇_i at t = 0, t_1, t_2 for a v_e = v_{pi} run: the evader moves from the β̇ > 0 region into the β̇ < 0 region and reaches the safe set (axes rotated so the pursuer sits on the positive x-axis relative to the target).

### 5.7 Proposition 7 (p. 703–704) — continuity of the switched law at ∂S

**Statement.** Let v_e = v_{pi}. If ℓ̂ ∈ (ℓ, ‖r_i(0)‖] is such that ‖x_i‖ > ℓ̂, then as [x_e^T x_i^T]^T → ∂S, ‖u_eR(x_e, x_i) − u_eS(x_e)‖ → 0.

**Assumptions**: v_e = v_{pi} = 1 WLOG; the ℓ̂-inflated law (12)/(17)/(18) is in use; pursuer outside the inflated capture ball (‖x_i‖ > ℓ̂).
**Proof approach** (3 steps): (i) the ℓ̂-safe set boundary is ∂Ŝ: ‖x_e‖ = ‖x_i‖ − ℓ̂ (target-centered circle, cf. Prop. 2); just outside it ‖x_e‖ = ‖x_i‖ − ℓ̂ + ρ with ρ → 0⁺, where β̂_i is a small positive number and β̂_i → 0⁺ as ρ → 0⁺; since β̂_i > β_i, also β_i → 0⁺ (consistency of the inflated and true risks) — intermediate fact; (ii) the **decisive limit computation** (p. 704, displayed fraction): χ̂_i/‖χ̂_i‖ = ‖−(1 − e^{−β̂_i}) r_i + e^{−β̂_i}(‖x_i‖ + ρ) x_e/(‖x_i‖ − ℓ̂ + ρ)‖-normalized ratio, whose numerator/denominator are written explicitly; as ρ → 0⁺, e^{−β̂_i} → 1 kills the evasion term (1 − e^{−β̂_i}) r_i → 0 and χ̂_i/‖χ̂_i‖ → x_e/‖x_e‖; (iii) matching the limits of (10) and (12) gives ‖u_eR − u_eS‖ → 0.
**Nature**: analytic, local at ∂S. **Physical meaning**: the switched law (19) has **no discontinuity/chattering at the S/R boundary** — the quickest descent law lands tangentially on the straight-run strategy, so the two-phase maneuver (maneuver, then sprint) glues continuously. **Remark 10 (p. 704)**: near ∂S, (d/dt)‖x_e‖ = (x_e^T u_e)/‖x_e‖ = −1, i.e., the optimal action in R near ∂S is already the straight line to the target.

### 5.8 The switched two-player strategy (eq. (19), p. 703)

u_X(x_e, x_i) = u_eS(x_e) if [x_e^T x_i^T]^T ∈ S, else u_eR(x_e, x_i). Combined with Prop. 3 (optimality in S), the Corollary (invariance), Prop. 4 (conditional reach), Prop. 6 (no capture at v_e = v_{pi}), and Prop. 7 (continuous switching), this is the paper's two-player "analytical solution". Fig. 5(b) (p. 703) depicts the resulting velocity field (blue arrows in R, black in S, for a fixed pursuer position).

### 5.9 Section IV (p. 704–705) — multi-pursuer extension

- **Partition**: S := ∩_{i=1}^N S_i, F := ∪_{i=1}^N F_i, R := int(X \ (S̄ ∪ F̄)) (p. 704). With unit speeds, projections of F are balls of radius ℓ around each pursuer (p. 705, via Remark 3). Fig. 6 (p. 704) shows S_e(x_1, x_2) as the intersection of the individual target-centered circles — the multi-pursuer analogue of an Apollonius-cell partition, with cells now Boolean combinations of circles.
- **Risk vector and its derivative** (eqs. (20)–(21), p. 704): b = [β_1, …, β_N]^T; ḃ(u_e) = [β̇_1, …, β̇_N]^T with β̇_i(u_e; x_e, x_i) = ∇_{x_e}β_i ẋ_e + ∇_{x_i}β_i ẋ_i = χ_i^T u_e + γ_i (same χ_i (13), γ_i (15) per pursuer). λ(u_e) := ḃ(u_e; x_e, x_1, …, x_N) (eq. (22)); note the paper prints λ : U → R^{N+1} — **typo**: b, λ ∈ R^N.
- **Ideal per-pursuer target** (eq. (23), p. 704): λ_des = [−‖χ_1‖, …, −‖χ_N‖]^T + [γ_1, …, γ_N]^T, i.e., each coordinate individually minimized as the controller (12) would do if that pursuer were alone.
- **Relevance weights** (eq. (24)–(25), p. 705): w_i(x_e, x_i) = 1 + e^{−φ_s(x_e, x_i)}; W := diag[w̄_1, …, w̄_N] with w̄_i = w_i/(Σ_{i=1}^N w_i). Since w_i ∈ (1, 2] is decreasing in φ_s, **more imminent pursuers (small φ_s) receive larger weight** — a soft, time-to-intercept-based priority that can effectively ignore irrelevant pursuers (the paper's N = 3 illustration, p. 704).
- **Problem 3 (eq. (26), p. 705)**: minimize J_w(u_e) := ‖λ_des − λ(u_e)‖²_W = (λ_des − λ(u_e))^T W (λ_des − λ(u_e)) subject to ‖u_e‖ ≤ 1.
- **Proposition 8 (p. 705).** The evader's control for all t ∈ [0, t_s] (t_s = first arrival at ∂S) is u*_e(t) = û_e, the solution of Problem 3. **Proof approach** (3 steps): W is positive definite by construction (w̄_i > 0, Σ w̄_i = 1); λ is affine in u_e (eq. (21)); composition of a positive-definite quadratic form with an affine map is convex, and the ball constraint is convex, so Problem 3 is a convex program — "in particular, a quadratically constrained quadratic program"; by construction its solution is the quadratically constrained least-squares solution, taken pointwise in time. **Nature**: analytic formulation, numerical solution (no closed form given); with a 2-D decision variable it is a trust-region-type problem, cheap per step. **Physical meaning**: a weighted least-squares compromise among N risk-descent objectives that reduces exactly to Prop. 5 when N = 1. **Caveats**: (i) convexity of a positive-definite quadratic form is fine, but the paper's phrase "quadratically constrained quadratic program" is loose — the only quadratic constraint is ‖u_e‖ ≤ 1, and the problem is a constrained least squares; (ii) **no theoretical guarantee** is provided that the aggregated law achieves the R → S transition for N > 1 (unlike Prop. 4's conditional statement for N = 1); success is validated only by simulation.
- **Multi-pursuer switched strategy (eq. (27), p. 705)**: u*_e(t) = u_eS(x_e) if [x_e^T x_1^T ⋯ x_N^T]^T ∈ S, else u_eR(x_e, x_1, …, x_N) := û_e. The analogue of (19).

### 5.10 Summary of formal results

Eight Propositions (1–8) and one unnumbered Corollary; three Problems (1–3) as formulations. Guarantee inventory: in S — optimality + invariance + arrival (Prop. 3, Cor.); in R (N = 1) — conditional safe reach (Prop. 4) and, at v_e = v_{pi}, unconditional no-capture with margin ℓ̂ (Prop. 6) and continuous switching (Prop. 7); in F — capture (Remark 6); N > 1 — feasibility-only (Prop. 8), empirically supported. The Voronoi-like capture logic enters through relay pursuit: the active pursuer is the argmin of capture time over the team (a min-time Voronoi selection, cf. [1]), which the evader's risk vector (20) implicitly accounts for by weighing all pursuers simultaneously.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues

**Author-stated:**
1. The safety requirement φ_s ≥ φ_T for all t ∈ [0, t_f] is explicitly **conservative** — "in reality, the evader can safely reach the target if its time-to-capture by the ith pursuer is greater than the time to reach the target", but φ_c is not computable in closed form under non-optimal play, so its lower bound φ_s is used instead (p. 701).
2. Performance degrades with N (Table I); future work: improved multi-pursuer quickest-descent law, different weighting functions for the cost (26), and **noisy position information** at the evader (p. 706).
3. The subset of R from which the R → S transition is possible is not characterized — it "is determined by the relative speeds of the two players" (Remark 7, p. 702); Prop. 4 is conditional on the existence of a risk-driving control.
4. Prop. 6/7 and the degeneracy analysis are carried out for v_e = v_{pi} (WLOG = 1); for v_e < v_{pi} capture is inevitable (eq. (6)) and the law only minimizes risk pointwise until capture (p. 701).
5. Exact multiagent PEG solutions are onerous due to dimensionality — the whole approach is a deliberate reduction (p. 696).

**Critical observations (with location):**
6. **Adversarial scope**: guarantees hold only against relay pursuit (eq. (2)) — a semi-cooperative model where at most one pursuer moves. Against simultaneous, fully cooperative pursuit none of the props applies; the evader law is a feasible strategy, not a saddle-point strategy of the N+1-player game (visible throughout Secs. II–IV; the abstract's "feasible control strategy" wording is accurate).
7. **Prop. 6 proof gap**: forward invariance of {‖r_i‖ ≥ ℓ̂} is argued by a limiting rate argument (d‖r_i‖/dt → 0 at the shell; e^{β̂_i} = O(Λ²) scaling, p. 702) and an informal Hausdorff-convergence sentence, without a Nagumo-type condition or a strict comparison inequality, and without verifying χ̂_i ≠ 0 en route (p. 702).
8. **Multi-pursuer extension is heuristic**: the weighted least-squares compromise (26) equalizes nothing provably; no R → S transition guarantee for N > 1, no capture-avoidance analogue of Prop. 6; empirical capture rates grow steeply with N (26.88% at N = 2, 41.66% at N = 4 — Table I), which the authors acknowledge (p. 706).
9. **Typo/minor errata**: λ : U → R^{N+1} on p. 704 should be R^N (b and λ are N-vectors); Problem 3 is called a "quadratically constrained quadratic program" though it is a weighted least-squares over a ball (trust-region-type, 2-D variable); Table I's header "Solution Method" sits over the last column "P at t_f" which is a metric, not a method; relay pursuit is cited as [16] in Sec. II-A-1 (p. 697) but as [1] in the Introduction.
10. **Model restrictions**: single-integrator kinematics with bounded speed but unconstrained turn dynamics; R³ extension claimed "natural" (p. 697) but never developed; perfect state information contradicted only in future work (noise); target privacy is modeled only through the φ_s/φ_c asymmetry (pursuers forecast a straight-running evader), not through an explicit partial-observability game.
11. **"Almost all initial conditions" (abstract)** is verified only for v_e = v_{pi} = 1, initial states in R, and modulo the collinear-standoff exceptions (p. 706); from F (the capture ball at equal speeds) the game ends in capture by design (Remark 6); no slower-evader (v_e < v_{pi}) Monte-Carlo is reported, although the framework is formulated for it (Fig. 3(a)).
12. **No baselines/ablations**: no comparison with [16], [22], [23], [24], or with non-risk-based evasion (e.g., pure evasion or APF-style laws); the independence claim w.r.t. initial distance and capture radius (p. 706) rests on aggregate random sampling rather than controlled variation.

## 8. Citation Value

**Paragraph role.** P077 is a reference point for *analytical, risk-metric-based feedback evasion with terminal constraints* in multi-pursuer reach-avoid settings: it supplies (i) the log-risk metric β = log(φ_T/φ_s) and the safe/failing/risky partition defined purely by comparisons of closed-form capture/intercept/target times (Props. 1–2, eqs. (4)–(9)) — a lightweight, Apollonius-circle-like alternative to HJI-based reachable-set partitions; (ii) the quickest-descent switching feedback (eqs. (12)–(19)) with a two-phase "maneuver-then-sprint" structure, a soft-barrier no-capture guarantee at equal speeds via radius inflation (Prop. 6, eqs. (16)–(18)), and a continuity result at the switching boundary (Prop. 7); and (iii) the standard formulation of target-seeking evasion against relay (semi-cooperative) pursuit, where the active pursuer is a min-time Voronoi-like selection. It is the natural citation when a paper needs a feasible (non-equilibrium) evader benchmark against relay pursuit, a time-based state-space partition for reach-avoid games, or a convex-combination (QCQP) extension pattern from two-player guarantees to N-pursuer heuristics; its Table I (0%/26.88%/41.66% capture at N = 1/2/4) is a usable empirical anchor for the difficulty scaling of single-evader multi-pursuer evasion.

**Suggested citation sentences.**
- "Selvakumar and Bakolas partition the augmented state space of a two-player target-seeking evasion game into safe, failing, and risky sets via closed-form comparisons of time-to-target, intercept, and min–max capture times, and synthesize a switching nonlinear feedback that minimizes the time derivative of a log-risk metric to steer the evader onto the boundary of the safe set [P077]."
- "Against relay pursuit, the quickest-descent evasion law of [P077] guarantees no capture for an equal-speed evader (via an inflated capture radius acting as a soft barrier) and, in Monte-Carlo studies, achieves a 0% capture rate with a single pursuer but only 58–73% target-reach rates with four pursuers, motivating learning- or optimization-based multi-pursuer evasion designs."
