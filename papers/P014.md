## 2. Research Background

In security operations, a high-value adversarial attacker (carrying intelligence or hazardous material) is often more valuable relocated than destroyed: defenders must lure it along a predefined path into a controlled goal area despite obstacles [Sec. I, p. 8467]. Prior herding work models the intruder as a passive "sheep" obeying repulsion/selfish-herding rules (artificial potential fields [6], self-propelled models [8], [9], outermost-push [10], circular-arc vector fields [11], m-waypoint bird herding [12]), which is invalid when the intruder is adversarial with unrestricted strategy. PE-game formulations of herding [13], [14] aim at capturing the attacker at minimal cost (and, per [15], leave the capture location uncontrollable), while computational game-theoretic herding (DGT [18] via GAMBIT [19]) suffers discretization-driven complexity. Analytic multiplayer reach-avoid (RA) barriers via HJI PDEs [21]–[24] scale exponentially with dimension [25], and backward integration of retrogressive path equations from asymmetric target sets is hampered by complex singular surfaces [28]; the decomposition alternative [29], [30] needs a symmetric target line (TL) in a convex domain, which obstacles destroy. This letter bridges these threads: an analytic differential-game solution for guiding an adversarial attacker through a non-convex, obstacle-laden domain along a freely specified route.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap

**Problem (CTR)**: N defenders must steer an adversarial attacker A (single integrators, defenders faster: αn = va/vn < 1) along a collision-free guide path (A* + Dubins smoothing, turning radius rw) to a circular target region T(center xt, radius ϵ), without letting A escape the formed "virtual defense channel" Ωch (the guide path ηS⁰ offset by ±ϵ) and without capture (‖xn − xa‖ ≤ ℓn); only relative distance and LOS angle are sensed [Secs. II-A, II-B, p. 8468]. The minimal configuration is two escorts (Der, Del) patrolling the channel boundary plus one herder Dh inside it (Definition 1) [p. 8469].

**Gap, as claimed by authors** [p. 8468, Sec. I]:
- [authors' claim] Unlike shepherding [5]–[12], no prediction/control model of the attacker is assumed. — Verified as a modeling stance: the attacker is treated as an unrestricted adversary inside a game-of-kind/barrier analysis; no evader model is used in the math.
- [authors' claim] Unlike PE herding [13], [14], the goal is relocation to a designated area with obstacles, not capture. — Consistent with [15] (Dorothy et al., Automatica 2024): in a PE game the attacker chooses the capture point, so the final position is uncontrollable; the CTR barrier formulation removes this by making the target line dynamic.
- [authors' claim] Unlike HJI/numerical barrier work [21]–[24] and DGT [18], the strategies are analytic and support real-time updates. — Verified in Sec. V: three barriers computed in ≈0.0986 s on a desktop CPU.
- [authors' claim] Unlike decomposition results [26], [27], [29], [30] that prevent entry into a fixed target line in a convex domain, here the TL is dynamic (channel walls move with the guides) and works in non-convex domains. — Verified: the "extended target set" T̊(L) is defined by the union of the target set and moving defense paths L = Lh ∪ Lel ∪ Ler (Definition 2), so the TL translates/curves with the Dubins path (straight segment Ll_i and arc segment Lc_i cases are both solved).
- [authors' claim] First use of differential game theory with a real ground-robot group to relocate an unrestricted-motion attacker along a specified route. — Plausible within the surveyed literature (not independently verified); the hardware demonstration (Sec. VI) supports the "real robots" half.

**Residual gap the paper leaves**: the formal guarantees are invariance (A cannot leave T̊(L)); convergence of A to T is argued behaviorally, not proven (see Sec. 7).

## 4. Core Contributions

Stated at [p. 8468, end of Sec. I] and verified against the technical sections:

1. **Modeling**: Formulation of the attacker-relocation (CTR) problem as a multiplayer RA game and its rigorous decomposition into two types of one-on-one subgames — the path defense game (PDG, herder vs. attacker on the inter-escort path Lh) and the path blockade game (PBG, escort vs. attacker on the channel walls Ler/Lel), with a Game of Kind on the extended target set T̊(L) [Sec. III, p. 8469].
2. **Theory (barriers / Game of Kind)**: Complete partition of the game state space into capture and escape regions via analytic barrier expressions for three TL geometries — straight inter-escort line (2), straight channel wall (3), and curved channel wall (4) — with a nesting result (7) yielding the convex-hull safe set R*_c; all computable online with negligible burden.
3. **Algorithm (collaborative feedback control)**: Scalar risk metrics μ(ρ,β) (A.3) and fi(Δβ, θw) (App. C) driving nonlinear state-feedback strategies for herder and escorts built on prescribed performance control (PPC), so transient tracking errors obey exponential performance functions (8), (13) — giving robustness to control errors [Sec. IV, p. 8470–8471].
4. **Extension results**: Capture-radius ℓi > 0 handled exactly via a minimum-circumscribed-circle reduction to the ℓ = 0 Apollonius barrier (Lemma 1, eq. (10)); extension to higher-order player dynamics argued via PPC inner loops (Remark 2).
5. **Experiment**: Simulation comparisons against three herding/pursuit baselines under three attacker policies (Table II) and a four-robot hardware demonstration in an obstacle field [Secs. V–VI, p. 8471–8473].

## 5. Key Mathematical Results (exhaustive)

### 5.1 Setup: dynamics, information, winning conditions

- **Dynamics (p. 8468, eq. (1))**: ẋa = ua, ẋn = un (single integrators), x(·) ∈ Ωfr ⊂ R², Ω = Ωfr ∪ Ωobs; ‖ua‖ ≤ va, ‖un‖ ≤ vn, speed ratio αn = va/vn < 1 (every defender faster than the attacker). Capture: ∃n: ‖xn − xa‖ ≤ ℓn; typically ϵ > ℓn; T is known only to defenders; both players avoid Ωobs. Terminal time tf = instant A is captured or enters T. **Information structure**: no absolute positions — only relative distance and LOS angle per pair (onboard camera/UWB-style sensing), consistent with the barriers being written in (ρ, β) polar coordinates.
- **Guide geometry (p. 8468–8469)**: A* path from x_a⁰ to xt, Dubins-smoothed with turning radius rw into ηS⁰ (mixed straight/arc); offsets ±ϵ give ηS^r, ηS^l bounding the virtual channel Ωch with tangent endpoints xtr, xtl. Defense path set L = Lh ∪ Lel ∪ Ler, where Lh is the shortest path between the two escorts and Ler/Lel are the escort-to-endpoint segments of the channel walls.
- **Definition 2 (Extended target set, p. 8469)**: L̄ = T ∪ L divides Ωfr; the region enclosed by L̄ excluding itself is T̊(L). The attacker starts inside: x_a⁰ ∈ T̊(L).
- **Game of Kind (p. 8469)**: A 'wins' if it escapes T̊(L) (with ρi,a ≠ ℓi, ρi,a = ‖xa − xi‖, i ∈ {er, el, h}); defenders 'win' if they drive ρi,a → ℓi while A is still in T̊(L). Since termination occurs at T, A has exactly three escape routes: crossing Lh, Lel, or Ler — giving the PDG and the two PBGs.
- Notation (Table I, p. 8468): ψi ∈ (−π, π] path-tangential angle of Li at xi; θ ∈ (−π, π] LOS angle from xer to xel; τ(·) ∈ {−1, 0, 1} directional sign (right turn / straight / left turn); R_{c,i}^{c±}, R_{c,i}^l CRs in the PBG for τi = 1, −1, 0; xB = (xB, yB) intersection point of R_{c,i}^{c+} and R_{c,i}^{c−}; R*_c convex hull formed by xB, xel, xer; μ(·), f_er(·), f_el(·) three risk metric functions; la, lh ≥ 0 distances from xa and xh to Lh.

### 5.2 Barrier of the PDG — eq. (2), p. 8469

**Barrier (2)**: Bh = {(ρh,a, βh,a) ∈ R² | ρh,a (sin(θ − βh,a) − αh) = lh (1 − αh²)}.
- Interpretation: cost = distance of Dh to A when A reaches the TL Lh; constructed "using a similar analytical approach as in the line of survival game (where A and Dh are on the same side of the TL [30])" — i.e., the analytic-barrier machinery of Yan et al. (T-RO 2020) applied to a same-side target line. The barrier is a surface in the relative (ρh,a, βh,a) plane parameterized by lh and θ.
- **Remark 1 (p. 8469)**: since Lh's anchor points are the escorts, A will not escape through Lh's endpoints; for lh > 0 and αh < 1, (2) holds iff sin(θ − βh,a) ≥ αh; at sin(θ − βh,a) = αh one gets lh ≡ 0 (Dh on Lh) and the PDG degenerates into a PBG. If A is confined to the CR described by (2), it cannot win regardless of strategy. Local/analytic; physical meaning: the herder's dominance boundary relative to the moving "goal line" Lh.

### 5.3 Barriers of the PBG — eqs. (3)–(5), p. 8469–8470

- **Straight-TL barrier (B-SL), eq. (3)**: B_i^l = {(ρi,a, βi,a) ∈ R² | βi,a = ψi ± arcsin αi}, '+' for i = er, '−' for i = el. Derivation: a PBG with lh ≡ 0 and θ = ψi or ψi + π is an instance of the PDG (Remark 1), so the B-SL is the degenerate case of (2) — two LOS rays at ± arcsin αi about the wall tangent direction; A between escort and wall (within these rays) cannot cross without capture. Analytic, local to the wall.
- **Arc-TL barrier (B-AL), eq. (4)**: B_i^c = {(θai, ra) ∈ R² | arcsin αi − arcsin(−αi ri/ra) + √(1/αi² − 1) − √((ra/(αi ri))² − 1) = ±τi θai}, where ri = rw ± ϵτi is the radius of the arc segment Lc_i (τi = +1 left turn, −1 right turn), θai is A's angular position w.r.t. Di about the arc center, and ra is A's distance to that center. Derivation "similar to the circular target defense [26]" (Von Moll et al., TAC 2023): the escort guards a circular-arc target line, and the classical circular-target-barrier computation is transplanted to the offset Dubins arc. **Existence condition (p. 8470)**: (4) exists iff αi(rw + ϵ) ≥ rw − ϵ, i.e. **rw ≥ (1 + αi)/(1 − αi) · ϵ** — a design rule linking the guide-path turning radius to speed ratio and channel half-width: higher speed ratio demands a larger turning radius.
- **Reparameterization, eq. (5)**: ra = (ri² − 2αi ri ρi,a + ρi,a²)^{1/2}, s.t. 0 < ρi,a ≤ αi ri — expresses the arc barrier in escort-centric polar coordinates.
- **Convexity computation, eq. (6)**: with fB(ρi,a) = ri θai − ρi,a cos ai, ai = arcsin αi,
  ∂fB/∂ρi,a = (αi ri − ρi,a)/(αi ra²) · √(ra² − (αi ri)²) − cos ai,
  ∂²fB/∂²ρi,a = −(1/(αi ra²)) √(ra² − (αi ri)²) (1 − (αi ri − ρi,a)²/ra²) − (αi ri²/ra⁴) (αi ri − ρi,a)²/√(ra² − (αi ri)²).
  Since ra ∈ (αi ri, ri), ∂²fB/∂²ρi,a < 0; and ∂fB/∂ρi,a = 0 at ρi,a = 0, hence fB(ρi,a) < fB(0) = 0; the same holds for τi = −1. This concavity/monotonicity underpins the region-nesting claim below.

### 5.4 Region nesting and the safe set R*_c — eq. (7), p. 8470

**Nesting (7)**: R_{c,er}^{c+} ⊂ R_{c,er}^{l} ⊂ R_{c,er}^{c−} ⟹ R_{c,el}^{c−} ⊂ R_{c,el}^{l} ⊂ R_{c,el}^{c+}.
- Meaning: capture regions for right-turning, straight, and left-turning wall types are nested, so if A lies in the **enveloping region (ERG)** formed by R_{c,er}^{c+} and R_{c,el}^{c−} (the innermost pair), it cannot escape the TL Li regardless of wall type. Defining the **convex hull R*_c** formed by the vertices of the ERG boundary (the intersection points xB of the arc barriers, plus xel, xer per Table I), the paper asserts, from the convexity of the function f_{αr}(ra), that **R*_c ⊂ (R_{c,er}^{c+} ∩ R_{c,el}^{c−})**. This set is the certified safe-initialization set used in Theorems 1–2 and in the simulations (x_a⁰ ∈ R*_c ∩ Rc,h). Proof approach: geometric set nesting + a convexity argument (eq. (6)); analytic; note the containment claim is asserted in one sentence ("we can assert") — see Sec. 7.

### 5.5 Herder feedback law and Theorem 1 — eqs. (8), (9), (10), pp. 8470

- **PPC constraint (8)**: −γj(t) < ej(t) < γj(t) ∀t ≥ 0, j = ρ, β, with performance function γj(t) = (γj⁰ − γj^∞) e^{−kj t} + γj^∞, γj⁰ > |ej(0)|; error transformation ej = γj tanh(σj), σj = atanh(zj), zj = ej/γj (standard PPC funnel).
- **Desired signals (9)**: βd = θ − π/2; ρd = ρ − (l0/(1 − αh²)) (1 − αh/sin(θ − β)) + la/sin(θ − β); with l0 = (1 − αh²) γρ⁰/(cos γβ⁰ − αh) for lh > 0 and l0 = ρ for lh = 0; la ≥ 0 is the distance from A to Lh. Interpretation: the herder tracks the LOS angle bisector-like direction θ − π/2 and a distance schedule that keeps the Apollonius barrier strictly on the far side of Lh.
- **Theorem 1 (p. 8470)**: Given x_h⁰, x_a⁰ with x_a⁰ ∈ Rc,h ∩ T̊(L), ℓh = 0, and the desired signals (9), design a control law achieving prescribed-performance tracking of β and ρ per (8). If γβ⁰ < arccos αh, then the attacker, unless captured, is unable to cross or reach Lh.
  - **Proof (Appendix A, p. 8473)**: (i) from (8), |β − βd| < γβ⁰ and |ρ − ρd| < γρ⁰; (ii) with βd = θ − π/2 and 0 < γβ⁰ < arccos αh, sin(θ − β) > cos γβ⁰ > αh, so by Remark 1 barrier (2) exists; (iii) for t ∈ [0, tf), eq. (A.1): |ρ − ρd| = |lh − ld|/sin(θ − β), ld := ρ − (l0/(1 − αh²))(sin(θ − β) − αh), hence |lh − ld| < γρ⁰; (iv) the barrier radius ρB = lh(1 − αh²)/(sin(θ − β) − αh) satisfies (A.2): ρB < ρ − l0 + (1 − αh²) γρ⁰/(cos γβ⁰ − αh); (v) substituting l0 = (1 − αh²) γρ⁰/(cos γβ⁰ − αh) gives **ρB < ρ** — the decisive inequality; (vi) define the risk metric (A.3): μ(ρ, β) = ((ρ − ρB)/(1 − αh²)) (sin(θ − β) − αh), the distance from the Apollonius circle Ae,h to Lh, with μ(ρB, β) ≡ 0; (vii) μ(ρ, β) > 0 for ρ > ρB and sin(β − θ) > αh, i.e., Ae,h lies to the right of Lh, so A cannot preempt Dh at Lh. Tools: PPC funnel inequalities + Apollonius-circle geometry of the line-of-survival barrier; analytic; global within the sector sin(θ − β) ≥ αh.
- **Lemma 1 (p. 8470)**: For radius ℓh > 0 and ρ > ℓh, the minimum circumscribed circle (MCC) of the boundary of R'e,h (the ER with nonzero capture radius) is Ae,c′, the Apollonius circle associated with the capture point xc′ = xh + ℓh [cos β; sin β] on the LOS and xa.
  - **Proof (Appendix B, p. 8473)**: (i) by [31] (Garcia, Automatica 2021) the ER R'e,h is delimited by the Cartesian Oval condition ρ1 = ρ2/αh + ℓh (ρ1 = Dh-to-oval-point distance, ρ2 = A's travel distance to the same point); (ii) convexity of R'e,h from Lemma 2 of [32] (Hu & Liu, Automatica 2023); (iii) law of cosines ρ2² + ρ² − 2ρ2ρ cos ς = ρ1² solved for ρ2, eq. (B.1): ρ2 = (αh/(αh² − 1)) (ρ cos ς + ℓh − √((αh ρ cos ς + ℓh)² − (αh² − 1)(ρ² − ℓh²))), valid branch for 0 < αh < 1; (iv) the oval is symmetric about ς = 0, giving extreme points x0, xπ along the LOS (B.2); (v) for a convex set the MCC is determined by these endpoints, eq. (B.3): oc = (x0 + xπ)/2, rc = ‖x0 − xπ‖/2; (vi) substitution yields (B.4): oc = xa/(1 − αh²) − (αh²/(1 − αh²)) xc′, rc = (αh/(1 − αh²)) (ρ − ℓh), which by Apollonius-circle properties is exactly Ae,c′. Decisive equation: (B.4). Consequence (p. 8470, eq. (10)): the ℓh = 0 analysis remains valid with ρ → ρ + ℓh; goal μ(ρ + ℓh, β) > μ(ρB, β) simplifies to ρ > ρB − ℓh, and (A.2) becomes (10): ρB − ℓh ≤ ρ − l0′ + (1 − αh²) γρ⁰/(cos γβ⁰ − αh), with l0′ = l0 + ℓh; hence the design constant relaxes to l0 = (1 − αh²) γρ⁰/(cos γβ⁰ − αh) − ℓh for lh > 0.

### 5.6 Escort feedback laws and Theorem 2 — eqs. (11)–(13), pp. 8470–8471

- **Escort dual task**: position task — track a reference point xd,i on the TL Li (parametrized by ϖi ∈ R), error ep,i = ‖xi − xd,i‖; velocity task — keep {xa, xi} inside Di's CR by manipulating Di's arc length si (Di's speed is ṡi). In the Serret–Frenet (SF) frame of ηS⁰, sa and lya are A's arc length and normal offset.
- **Adaptive arc-length offset (11)**: Δs = di cot(ca θw), where di = min{ ρ^d_{i,a} |sin(β^d_{i,a} − ψi)|, −ρ^d_{i,a} |sin(β^d_{i,a} − ψi)| + 2ϵ }, with ρ^d_{i,a}, β^d_{i,a} the distance/LOS from xd,i to xa, and design parameters ca > 1, θw > 0. Δs is the arc-length offset of A along the path obtained by shifting ηS⁰ by lya, mapped back onto ηS⁰ as Δsa.
- **Desired escort arc length (12)**: s_i^d = s_a^d ± ϵ ∫₀ᵗ (τ_{d,a} κ_{d,a} ṡ_a^d) dt, with s_a^d = sa − Δsa and τ_{d,a}, κ_{d,a} the directional sign and curvature of ηS⁰ determined by s_a^d — i.e., the escort rides the wall at the lateral offset ±ϵ with speed matched to the guided attacker.
- **Path-following Problem 2 (p. 8471)**: ensure xi → xd,i and si → s_i^d subject to PPC constraints (13): −γj(t) < e_{j,i}(t) < γj(t), j = p, s.
- **Theorem 2 (p. 8471)**: For x_a⁰ ∈ R*_c and ℓi > 0, by solving Problem 2, the attacker, unless captured, is unable to cross or reach Li if Σj γj⁰ ≤ ℓi. That is, there always exists xa ∈ T̊(L).
  - **Proof (Appendix C, p. 8474)**: (i) for ep,i = es,i = 0 and ρi,a > ℓi, Theorem 1 blocks Lh; any escape must cross Ler or Lel; (ii) take i = er: since R*_c ⊂ (R_{c,er}^{c+} ∩ R_{c,el}^{c−}), A must cross the convex hull (CH); at the first touch of the BCH, βi,a = βi,B and the distance from A to Lh is Δla = ρi,a cos(βi,B − ψi); (iii) "since the shortest distance between two points is a straight line", eq. (C.1): Δla ≤ Δsi ≤ ρi,a sin(βi,B − ψi) cot(ca θw); (iv) define the risk metric fi(Δβ, θw) = Δβ − θw with Δβ = max{βi,a, ψi} − min{βi,a, ψi}; choosing θw = Δβ gives fi = 0 at the BCH touch and fi > 0 while A is inside the CH; (v) but substituting θw = Δβ into (C.1) demands sin(Δβ − ca θw) ≥ 0 while ca > 1 forces Δβ − ca θw < 0, i.e., sin(Δβ − ca θw) < 0 — contradiction, so A cannot escape the CH; (vi) extreme case: if fi → 0 as A approaches the reference point and A could still escape uncaptured (‖xa(tf) − xi(tf)‖ > ℓi), then since ‖ej,i‖ < Σj γj,i⁰ ≤ ℓi, as ‖xa − xd,i‖ → 0 one gets ‖xa − xi‖ < Σ‖ei‖ < ℓi — A is captured, contradiction. Hence even with nonzero errors, Σj γj,i⁰ ≤ ℓi confines A to T̊(L). Tools: contradiction argument + PPC funnel bounds + straight-line distance bound; analytic; the decisive inequality is (C.1) combined with ca > 1.
- **Closing argument (p. 8471, informal)**: because A's objective is to evade capture as soon as possible and it cannot exit through Lh, Ler, Lel, an attacker avoiding prolonged stays progresses along Ωch; the extended target set T̊(L) "will consistently narrow until the attacker enters the target set T". This attractivity claim is heuristic, not a theorem (see Sec. 7).
- **Remark 2 (p. 8471)**: single-integrator (1) doubles as the kinematic layer of higher-order systems (e.g., quadcopters); designing inner loops to satisfy (8) and (13) extends the method — an architecture claim, not a proof.

**Proof-gap notes** (with location):
1. Eq. (C.1) (p. 8474): the chain Δla ≤ Δsi is justified only by "the shortest distance between two points is a straight line"; the geometric configuration guaranteeing that A's distance to Lh is bounded by the escort's arc-length offset is not derived (it implicitly uses the specific placement of xd,i and the channel offset ±ϵ). The subsequent contradiction argument inherits this.
2. R*_c ⊂ (R_{c,er}^{c+} ∩ R_{c,el}^{c−}) (p. 8470) rests on one sentence about "the convex nature of the function f_{αr}(ra)"; no formal convex-hull argument is given.
3. Theorem 2's statement quantifies over "by solving Problem 2" — the result is conditional on the PPC path-following problem being solved, whose solvability (input saturation vs. funnel compatibility) is assumed, not proven.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues

**Author-stated**:
- Future work: CTR with larger numbers of attackers and defenders [Conclusion, p. 8473] — the formalism is for one attacker, and the minimum viable team is exactly two escorts + one herder.
- Extension to higher-order dynamics is asserted via Remark 2 (meet (8)/(13) with inner loops) without analysis of the coupled stability/saturation problem [p. 8471].
- l0 = ρ for lh = 0 in (9): the herder's desired-distance schedule degenerates when the inter-escort line collapses onto the herder — the lh > 0/lh = 0 switch is not analyzed for chattering [p. 8470].

**Critical observations (with page/eq.)**:
1. **Invariance vs. convergence**: Theorems 1–2 prove only that A cannot cross Lh/Ler/Lel while PPC holds; arrival at T is argued behaviorally ("an attacker, who avoids prolonged stays in one location, tends to progress along the virtual channel", p. 8471). A purely evasive attacker that loiters inside T̊(L) indefinitely is not excluded by the theory; the narrowing of T̊(L) is a consequence of assumed attacker preference, not a proven attractivity property.
2. **Proof gap in (C.1)** (p. 8474): the bound Δla ≤ Δsi is asserted from the straight-line-distance principle without specifying the configuration under which the escort's arc-length offset dominates A's distance to Lh; Theorem 2's contradiction argument relies on it.
3. **R*_c containment asserted, not proven** (p. 8470, after eq. (7)): "Given the convex nature of the function f_{αr}(ra), we can assert that R*_c ⊂ (R_{c,er}^{c+} ∩ R_{c,el}^{c−})" — a one-line justification for the certified safe set used by both theorems and the simulations.
4. **Barrier sector condition**: (2) exists only where sin(θ − βh,a) ≥ αh (Remark 1); the win-region guarantees are local to this sector, and the global claim assembles three such local certificates — the patching across segments (straight/arc, left/right) is handled by nesting (7) but the seam behavior (A at segment junctions of L) is not analyzed.
5. **Theorem 2's error bound is conservative**: Σj γj⁰ ≤ ℓi ties the funnel size directly to the capture radius; with the experimental choice "capture radius = robot length" (p. 8472) for physical safety, the effective ℓi may be smaller than the geometric one, tightening the feasible funnels (not discussed).
6. **Comparison fairness**: PE [16] optimizes capture, SNH [17] optimizes herding of noncooperative but model-bound "sheep", DGT [18] uses a 5-action discretization; Table II's MTD/MGT gaps partly reflect objective mismatch rather than pure algorithmic superiority (the authors partially acknowledge this on p. 8472).
7. **Single-integrator idealization**: (1) omits actuator dynamics and sensing noise; robustness enters only through the PPC funnels, whose admissible magnitude vs. control authority trade-off is unquantified.
8. Minor: "Core i3-8100U" (p. 8471) is an unusual model designation (Intel's lineup has i3-8100 and i3-8130U); quoted as printed.

## 8. Citation Value

**Role in the literature**: This is the bridge paper between analytic multiplayer reach-avoid barriers (Isaacs-style barriers, Apollonius circles, explicit-policy decomposition à la Yan et al. [30] and Shishika et al. [29]) and adversarial herding/relocation: it converts the fixed target line required by convex-domain RA decompositions into a *moving* target line realized by a virtual defense channel around a Dubins guide path, thereby extending analytic barrier methods to non-convex domains, and pairs the Game-of-Kind analysis with executable PPC feedback laws and a real-robot demonstration. It is also a useful instance of importing the circular-target-defense barrier [26] and Cartesian-oval capture-radius machinery [31] into a new geometric setting. Cite it when: (a) motivating dynamic/asymmetric target sets or moving target lines in reach-avoid games; (b) surveying herding of *adversarial* (model-free) intruders versus shepherding of passive agents; (c) needing a template for turning Game-of-Kind barriers into funnel-constrained feedback control robust to capture radii and control errors; or (d) contrasting analytic real-time barriers against HJI/numerical or learning-based herding baselines.
Suggested citation sentences: "Zhao et al. decompose adversarial herding into path-defense and path-blockade subgames whose analytic barriers are evaluated on dynamically relocated target lines defined by a virtual defense channel, yielding real-time strategies validated on ground robots [P014]." Or: "Analytic RA barriers have been extended from fixed target lines in convex domains [P030-analogue of [30]] to moving target lines that herd an unrestricted attacker through a non-convex workspace via prescribed-performance feedback [P014]."

**Cross-references within this knowledge base**: builds on [30] (Yan et al. T-RO 2020, analytical barriers in convex domains), [26] (Von Moll et al. TAC 2023, circular target defense), [31] (Garcia, Automatica 2021, Cartesian oval / cooperative target protection), [32] (Hu & Liu, Automatica 2023, HJI justification of geometric target-defense solution), [15] (Dorothy et al., Automatica 2024, One Apollonius Circle); contrasted with shepherding [6], [10], [11], [17] and computational herding [18].
