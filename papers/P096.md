## 2. Research Background

Autonomous surface vehicles increasingly perform large-scale maritime missions (cooperative rescue, detection, investigation), but mission objectives between rival ASV groups conflict, so the fully-cooperative cooperative-control toolkit (path following [5]–[8], target tracking [9]–[12], encircling [13]–[16], formation [17]–[20]) does not apply. Prior non-cooperative ASV work cited by the authors — polynomial sliding-fitting pursuit [21], threat-potential-field attack–defense [22], backstepping interception with disturbances [23], neural-dynamics domain protection [24], prescribed-time capture [25] (Automatica), and multiagent PPO pursuit [26] — designs strategies *unilaterally*, and the authors argue this "may be unable to sufficiently consider the strategic interactions and mutual influences among the agents" (p. 2). Differential games (Isaacs [30]; multi-pursuer–multi-evader [31]; HJI justification of geometric target-defense solutions [32]) and reach-avoid games in particular ([38] two-evader–one-pursuer, [39] matching-based 3-D heterogeneous multiplayer capture (Automatica), [40] 3-D multiplayer reach-avoid of aerial robots guarding a static point) provide the natural two-sided framework, but existing reach-avoid results assume single-integrator or fully-actuated players, ignore inter-player and obstacle collisions, and (in [40]) protect only a static point. This paper transports the reach-avoid differential game to a marine setting: two teams of underactuated ASVs with sideslip, velocity and yaw-rate constraints, a protected circular area, and static/dynamic obstacles, with safety enforced by CBF-based QPs.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap

**Problem.** $N$ attacking ASVs $A_k$, $k=1,\dots,N$ versus $2N$ defending ASVs $D_i$, $i=1,\dots,2N$, in a domain with $S$ static and $E$ dynamic obstacles plus one protected circular target area $\Omega_T=\{\mathbf p_T\in\mathbb R^2:\|\mathbf p_T-\mathbf p_T^C\|\le r_T\}$ (p. 3). Every two defenders are (exogenously) assigned to one attacker; the game state of the $k$-th "reach-avoid pair" is $\mathbf p_k=(\mathbf p^D_i,\mathbf p^D_j,\mathbf p^A_k)\in\mathbb R^6$. All players have **the same resultant velocity**. Defenders win by capturing ($\|\mathbf p^D_i-\mathbf p^A_k\|<\mu$, eq. (6)) before the attacker reaches the area; the attacker wins by reaching $\Omega_T$. Safety (mutual and obstacle collision avoidance, eq. (14)) and velocity limits (eq. (15)) must hold throughout.

**Gap [authors' claims, p. 2, contributions 1)–3)]**:
1. *vs [21]–[26]*: conflict between two ASV groups was handled by unilateral strategy design; introducing differential-game two-sidedness plus an online cooperation decision ("orthogonal-line-related condition") is new for ASVs. **[Verified from cited titles]**: [21]–[26] are indeed single-sided guidance/learning designs (e.g., [24] neurodynamics attack–defense guidance, [25] prescribed-time estimator capture with switching); none solves a two-sided zero-sum reach-avoid game. The gap statement is consistent.
2. *vs [33]–[40]*: differential-game reach-avoid works used single-integrator or fully-actuated models and a static-point target ([40]); here an underactuated ASV with velocity constraints and an extended target *area* is treated, via an auxiliary-variable/guidance-point technique and an equivalent closest-point optimization (eq. (16)). **[Verified]**: [40] (Garcia, Casbeer, Pachter, RA-L 2020) indeed uses simple-motion players and a static target point; the claimed model gap is real.
3. *vs [38]–[40]*: potential collisions among players and with obstacles were ignored; a CBF-QP layer is added here. **[Verified]**: none of [38]–[40] models collision avoidance; correct.
- **Caveat**: the "gap" is a *composition* (equal-speed planar reach-avoid geometry + guidance-point underactuation trick + standard CBF-QP safety filter); each ingredient is established in the literature ([19]-style geometric solutions, Fossen [45] guidance-point, Ames et al. [42]–[44] CBFs); the novelty is their integration and the online 1v1/2v1 capture-type switch, not new game theory.

## 4. Core Contributions

1. **Theory (Game of Kind/Degree for equal-speed pairs)**: For each defender-pair-vs-attacker game, an equal-speed orthogonal-line barrier $\mathcal B(\mathbf p_k)$ (eqs. (18), (23)) partitions $\mathbb R^6$ into winning regions $\mathcal R_D,\mathcal R_A$ (eq. (8)); a closed-form optimal interception point $\mathbf I^*_{k1}$ (1v1, eq. (21)) and $\mathbf I^*_{k2}$ (2v1 cooperative, eqs. (25)–(26)) with unit-speed saddle-point strategies (eqs. (22), (27)); HJI verification of both value functions (eqs. (37)–(41), (42)–(47), Theorem 1).
2. **Modeling**: Underactuated ASV kinematics with sideslip (eq. (4)) converted to a fully-actuated 2-D point-mass game on an *off-center guidance point* $\mathbf p^D_i=\mathbf p_i+\iota_i\varrho_i$ via the invertible mapping $R_{\iota_i}(\psi_{iw})$ (eq. (5), $\det R_{\iota_i}=\iota_i>0$); protected target is an **area**, handled by the equivalent optimization (eq. (16)) that tracks the attacker-closest point $\mathbf p^*_T\in\Omega_T$.
3. **Algorithm**: (a) an **online cooperation decision** for each defender pair using the signed side-of-line tests $\Phi_1,\Phi_2$ (eq. (28)): simultaneous 2v1 capture, individual 1v1 capture by $D_i$ or $D_j$, or best individual point — chosen by comparing which side of each orthogonal line the rival's 1v1 capture point lies on; (b) a **CBF-QP safety filter** (eq. (35)) over three CBF families (static obstacles eq. (29)–(30), dynamic obstacles eq. (31)–(32), intra-group neighbors eq. (33)–(34)) with hard body-frame velocity box constraints; (c) the **optimal safe guidance law** (eq. (36)) mapping the QP output back to $(U_i, r_i)$ commands for the underactuated ASV.
4. **Experiment**: One 5-vs-10 MATLAB-style simulation with 2 static + 1 dynamic obstacles (Section V, Figs. 4–8) validating capture, constraint satisfaction and safety distances. No hardware experiment, no baseline comparison.

## 5. Key Mathematical Results

### 5.0 Model, information structure, cost, termination (Section II-C, pp. 3–4, eqs. (4)–(15))

- **Underactuated kinematics** (eq. (4), citing Fossen [45]): $\dot x_i = U_i\cos\psi_{iw}$, $\dot y_i = U_i\sin\psi_{iw}$, $\dot\psi_{iw} = r_i + \dot\beta_i$, where $U_i=\sqrt{u_i^2+v_i^2}>0$ is the resultant of surge $u_i$ and sway $v_i$; $\psi_{iw}=\psi_i+\beta_i$ is the actual course; $\beta_i=\arctan(v_i/u_i)$ is the sideslip angle; $r_i$ the body-frame yaw rate. The ASV is underactuated: it controls $(U_i, r_i)$ but its planar velocity direction is the course $\psi_{iw}$, not a freely chosen heading.
- **Guidance-point (auxiliary variable) reduction** (p. 3, eq. (5)): with a constant positive offset $\iota_i>0$ and rotation vector $\varrho_i=[\cos\psi_{iw},\sin\psi_{iw}]^T$, define the shifted point $\mathbf p^D_i=\mathbf p_i+\iota_i\varrho_i$. Then $\dot{\mathbf p}^D_i=[u^x_i,u^y_i]^T=R_{\iota_i}(\psi_{iw})[U_i,\, r_i+\dot\beta_i]^T$ with $R_{\iota_i}(\psi_{iw})=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\psi_{iw} & -\iota_i\sin\psi_{iw}\\ \sin\psi_{iw} & \iota_i\cos\psi_{iw}\end{bmatrix}$. Since $\det R_{\iota_i}=\iota_i>0$, *any* planar velocity $\mathbf u^D_i\in\mathbb R^2$ of the guidance point is realizable by a unique $(U_i,r_i)$: $[U_i,r_i]^T = R^{-1}_{\iota_i}(\psi_{iw})\mathbf u^D_i - [0,\dot\beta_i]^T$ (used in eqs. (35)–(36)). This is the paper's device for making the underactuated vehicle "fully actuated" at the game level (the game is solved on $\mathbf p^D,\mathbf p^A$ point masses; underactuation re-enters only through the velocity box in the QP).
- **Information structure**: perfect, memoryless **state feedback**; each player recomputes its strategy from current $\mathbf p^A_k,\mathbf p^D_i,\mathbf p^D_j$ (p. 6, Section III-C); no delay, measurement noise, or partial observability is modeled. The defender pairing $(D_i,D_j)\leftrightarrow A_k$ is fixed exogenously ("every two defenders against one attacker", p. 3).
- **Capture / termination** (eqs. (6)–(7)): capture when $\|\mathbf p^D_i-\mathbf p^A_k\|<\mu$, $\mu>0$ — as printed, eq. (6) reads "$\|\mathbf p^D_i-\mathbf p^A_k\|<\mu, i = 2k = 1\ldots 2N$", an obvious **typo** for the index set. Termination set $\mathcal T=\mathcal T_D\cup\mathcal T_A$ with $\mathcal T_D=\{\mathbf p_k \mid \|\mathbf p^D_i-\mathbf p^A_k\|\le\mu \ \vee\ \|\mathbf p^D_j-\mathbf p^A_k\|\le\mu\}$ and $\mathcal T_A=\{\mathbf p_k\mid\|\mathbf p^A_k-\mathbf p_T\|=0\}$; $\mathbf p_{kf}=\mathbf p_k(t_f)$.
- **Game of Kind** (eqs. (8)–(9)): $\mathcal R_D=\{\mathbf p_k\mid\mathcal B(\mathbf p_k)>0\}$, $\mathcal R_A=\{\mathbf p_k\mid\mathcal B(\mathbf p_k)<0\}$, barrier $\mathcal B=\{\mathbf p_k\mid\mathcal B(\mathbf p_k)=0\}$; $\mathcal B$ is explicitly constructed in Section III (eqs. (18)/(23)).
- **Game of Degree payoffs**: for $\mathbf p_k\in\mathcal R_D$ (eqs. (10)–(11)): $J_k=\Phi_D(\mathbf p_k(t_f))=\|\mathbf p^A_{kf}-\mathbf p_T\|$, value $V(\mathbf p_{k0})=\min_{\mathbf u^A_k(\cdot)}\max_{\mathbf u^D_i(\cdot),\mathbf u^D_j(\cdot)}\Phi_D$ — the **attacker minimizes** its terminal distance to the target point while **defenders maximize** it (capture as far from the area as possible). For $\mathbf p_k\in\mathcal R_A$ (eqs. (12)–(13)): $J_k=\Phi_A=\min(\|\mathbf p^D_{if}-\mathbf p^A_{kf}\|,\|\mathbf p^D_{jf}-\mathbf p^A_{kf}\|)$, $V=\min_{\mathbf u^D_i,\mathbf u^D_j}\max_{\mathbf u^A_k}\Phi_A$ — defenders minimize the terminal miss distance. Note the reversed min–max roles between the two regions, and that **no solution is derived for the $\mathcal R_A$ degree game** (only the remark that the attacker should head straight for the area, p. 4).
- **Safety & input objectives** (eqs. (14)–(15)): pairwise distances of every game member $\mathbf p^A_\varpi$ (with $[\mathcal A,\varpi]=[D,i]$ or $[A,k]$ — note the superscript is printed $A$ even for defenders) from static obstacles $\mathbf p_s$, dynamic obstacles $\mathbf p_d$, and neighbors $\mathbf p^A_l$ must stay at least $\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3\in\mathbb R^+$; **eq. (14) prints "≤"**, which is the opposite of the stated safety objective and inconsistent with the CBFs (29)–(34) enforcing $h\ge0$; the intended sense is $\ge$ (typo). Velocity limits $U_{\min}\le U_i\le U_{\max}$, $r_{\min}\le r_i\le r_{\max}$ (eq. (15)).

### 5.1 Preliminaries adopted (Section II-B, pp. 2–3, no new proof)

- **Definition 1** [42]: feasible set $\mathcal D=\{x\in\mathcal S\subset\mathbb R^n: h(x)\ge0\}$ as 0-superlevel set; forward invariance for $x(t_0)\in\mathcal D$.
- **Definition 2** [43] (CBF condition, eq. (3)): $\sup_{u\in\mathbb R^m}\{L_f h(x)+L_g h(x)u+\kappa(h(x))\}\ge0$ for an extended class-$\mathcal K_\infty$ function $\kappa$.
- **Lemma 1** [44]: if $h$ is a CBF for (1) on $\mathcal D$, any Lipschitz continuous control satisfying the CBF constraint renders $\mathcal D$ safe. (Quoted from [42]–[44]; used as the sole safety certificate in Theorem 1.)

### 5.2 Reach-avoid strategy module (Section III-A, pp. 4–5, eqs. (16)–(28)) — stated geometrically, not numbered as theorems

- **Closest point on the area** (eq. (16)): $\mathbf p^*_T=\min_{\mathbf p_T\in\mathbb R^2}\|\mathbf p^A_k-\mathbf p_T\|^2$ s.t. $\mathbf p_T\in\Omega_T$ — as printed, "min" should be "argmin"; for $\mathbf p^A_k\notin\Omega_T$ the solution is the radial projection of $\mathbf p^A_k$ onto the circle $\|\mathbf p_T-\mathbf p^C_T\|=r_T$, updated continuously under play ("constantly updated under its game strategy", p. 4).
- **1v1 quantities** (eq. (17)): $W^A_k=\sqrt{(x^A_k-x^*_T)^2+(y^A_k-y^*_T)^2}$, $W^D_i=\sqrt{(x^D_i-x^*_T)^2+(y^D_i-y^*_T)^2}$ — distances of attacker/defender to $\mathbf p^*_T$.
- **Barrier** (eq. (18)): $\mathcal B(\mathbf p_k)=(W^A_k)^2-(W^D_i)^2$. $\mathcal B>0$ ⟺ the attacker is farther from the (closest point of) the area than the defender — equal-speed Apollonius-type reasoning (p. 4: "their winning area in the plane space is separated by a straight line orthogonal to the line segment $\overline{D_iA_k}$", Fig. 3a).
- **Orthogonal line** (eq. (19)): $(x^A_k-x^D_i)(x-x^*_T)+(y^A_k-y^D_i)(y-y^*_T)=\mathcal B(\mathbf p_k)/2$ — a line with normal $\mathbf p^A_k-\mathbf p^D_i$ (hence orthogonal to $\overline{D_iA_k}$), offset from $\mathbf p^*_T$ by $\mathcal B/2$ along the normal; for $\mathcal B=0$ it is the line through $\mathbf p^*_T$ orthogonal to $\overline{D_iA_k}$.
- **Optimal interception point 1v1** (eqs. (20)–(21)): intersect (19) with the perpendicular through $\mathbf p^*_T$ (eq. (20)): $x^*_{k1}=\frac{\mathcal B(\mathbf p_k)}{2\Theta^2}(x^A_k-x^D_i)+x^*_T$, $y^*_{k1}=\frac{\mathcal B(\mathbf p_k)}{2\Theta^2}(y^A_k-y^D_i)+y^*_T$, where $\Theta=\sqrt{(x^A_k-x^D_i)^2+(y^A_k-y^D_i)^2}=\|\mathbf p^A_k-\mathbf p^D_i\|$ (foot of the perpendicular from $\mathbf p^*_T$ to line (19), i.e., $\mathbf I^*_{k1}=\mathbf p^*_T+\frac{\mathcal B(\mathbf p_k)}{2\Theta^2}(\mathbf p^A_k-\mathbf p^D_i)$). **Key structural property (used in the proof, p. 7): $\mathbf I^*_{k1}$ is equidistant from $A_k$ and $D_i$**, i.e., $\|\mathbf I^*_{k1}-\mathbf p^A_k\|=\|\mathbf I^*_{k1}-\mathbf p^D_i\|$ — verifiable in one line: substituting $\mathcal B=(\mathbf p^A-\mathbf p^D)\cdot(\mathbf p^A+\mathbf p^D-2\mathbf p^*_T)$ into $\|\mathbf I-\mathbf p^A\|^2-\|\mathbf I-\mathbf p^D\|^2$ gives 0. Hence $\Theta_i=\Theta_k$ below.
- **1v1 saddle-point strategies** (eq. (22)), for $\mathbf p_k\in\mathcal R_D$: $\mathbf u^{A*}_k=\frac{1}{\Theta_k}[x^*_{k1}-x^A_k,\; y^*_{k1}-y^A_k]^T$, $\mathbf u^{D*}_i=\frac{1}{\Theta_i}[x^*_{k1}-x^D_i,\; y^*_{k1}-y^D_i]^T$, with $\Theta_k=\|\mathbf I^*_{k1}-\mathbf p^A_k\|$, $\Theta_i=\|\mathbf I^*_{k1}-\mathbf p^D_i\|$ — both players drive at **unit speed** straight at the interception point (pure pursuit of a moving point; feedback form since $\mathbf I^*_{k1}$ is recomputed from current states).
- **2v1 barrier** (eq. (23)): $\mathcal B(\mathbf p_k)=\max\{\mathcal B_1(\mathbf p_k),\mathcal B_2(\mathbf p_k)\}$ with $\mathcal B_1=(W^A_k)^2-(W^D_i)^2$, $\mathcal B_2=(W^A_k)^2-(W^D_j)^2$. (Cross-reference typo: p. 5 says the winning region and barrier "are given by equation (13) and equation (14)", which should be (8) and (9).)
- **Cooperative interception point** (eqs. (24)–(26)): $\mathbf I^*_{k2}$ = intersection of the two orthogonal lines (19) and (24) ($(x^A_k-x^D_j)(x-x^*_T)+(y^A_k-y^D_j)(y-y^*_T)=\mathcal B_2/2$): $x^*_{k2}=x_C+x^*_T$, $y^*_{k2}=y_C+y^*_T$ with $x_C=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{(y^A_k-y^D_i)\mathcal B_2(\mathbf p_k)-(y^A_k-y^D_j)\mathcal B_1(\mathbf p_k)}{\Gamma}$, $y_C=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{(x^A_k-x^D_j)\mathcal B_1(\mathbf p_k)-(x^A_k-x^D_i)\mathcal B_2(\mathbf p_k)}{\Gamma}$, $\Gamma=(x^A_k-x^D_j)(y^A_k-y^D_i)-(x^A_k-x^D_i)(y^A_k-y^D_j)$ (Cramer's rule solution of the 2×2 line system; **degenerate iff $\Gamma=0$**, i.e., the two orthogonal lines are parallel — the paper never states or handles this case; proof gap).
- **2v1 strategies** (eq. (27)), $\mathbf p_k\in\mathcal R_{DC}\subset\mathcal R_D$ (simultaneous-capture subregion): $\mathbf u^{A*}_k=\frac{1}{\Theta_k}[x^*_{k2}-x^A_k, y^*_{k2}-y^A_k]^T$ and likewise $\mathbf u^{D*}_i,\mathbf u^{D*}_j$ toward $\mathbf I^*_{k2}$ (unit speed).
- **Online capture-type decision ("orthogonal-line-related condition")** (eq. (28)): $\Phi_1=(x^A_k-x^D_i)(x^*_j-x^*_T)-\tfrac{\mathcal B_1(\mathbf p_k)}{2}+(y^A_k-y^D_i)(y^*_j-y^*_T)$ and $\Phi_2=(x^A_k-x^D_j)(x^*_i-x^*_T)-\tfrac{\mathcal B_2(\mathbf p_k)}{2}+(y^A_k-y^D_j)(y^*_i-y^*_T)$, where $\mathbf I^*_i,\mathbf I^*_j$ are the two 1v1 capture points from eq. (21). $\Phi_2$ is the signed side test of $\mathbf I^*_i$ w.r.t. line (24) ($>0$: on the attacker's side; $<0$: on $D_j$'s side), $\Phi_1$ likewise for $\mathbf I^*_j$ w.r.t. (19). Decision rule (p. 5): $\Phi_1<0\wedge\Phi_2<0$ ⇒ cooperative simultaneous capture at $\mathbf I^*_{k2}$; $\Phi_1<0\wedge\Phi_2>0$ ⇒ $D_i$ captures alone at $\mathbf I^*_i$; $\Phi_1>0\wedge\Phi_2<0$ ⇒ $D_j$ captures alone at $\mathbf I^*_j$; $\Phi_1>0\wedge\Phi_2>0$ ⇒ the capture point among $\mathbf I^*_i,\mathbf I^*_j$ that gives $A_k$ the minimum cost ("brings $A_k$ closest to the area"). **No formal proof is given that this rule selects the team-optimal capture type**; it is argued by geometric dominance of the orthogonal lines (the justification is the same equal-speed win-region logic as the Game of Kind, asserted not proven).

### 5.3 Safety module: CBFs and QP (Section III-B, pp. 5–6, eqs. (29)–(35))

- **Static-obstacle CBF** (eqs. (29)–(30)): $h_{\varpi s}(\mathbf p^A_\varpi,\mathbf p_s)=\|\mathbf p^A_\varpi-\mathbf p_s\|^2-\alpha_1^2$, requirement $\dot h_{\varpi s}=2(\mathbf p^A_\varpi-\mathbf p_s)^T\mathbf u^A_\varpi\ge-\gamma_{\varpi s}h_{\varpi s}$, $\gamma_{\varpi s}\in\mathbb R^+$.
- **Dynamic-obstacle CBF** (eqs. (31)–(32)): $h_{\varpi d}=\|\mathbf p^A_\varpi-\mathbf p_d\|^2-\alpha_2^2$, $\dot h_{\varpi d}=2(\mathbf p^A_\varpi-\mathbf p_d)^T(\mathbf u^A_\varpi-\dot{\mathbf p}_d)\ge-\gamma_{\varpi d}h_{\varpi d}$ — requires knowledge of the obstacle velocity $\dot{\mathbf p}_d$ (source unmodeled).
- **Neighbor CBF** (eqs. (33)–(34)): $h_{\varpi l}=\|\mathbf p^A_\varpi-\mathbf p^A_l\|^2-\alpha_3^2$, $\dot h_{\varpi l}=2(\mathbf p^A_\varpi-\mathbf p^A_l)^T(\mathbf u^A_\varpi-\mathbf u^A_l)\ge-\gamma_{\varpi l}h_{\varpi l}$ — requires the neighbor's current input $\mathbf u^A_l$ (standard CBF-QP assumption, unremarked).
- **Safety QP** (eq. (35)), solved per ASV at each instant: $\mathbf u^{A*}_{\varpi c}=\arg\min_{\mathbf u^A_\varpi\in\mathbb R^2}\|\mathbf u^A_\varpi-\mathbf u^{A*}_\varpi\|^2$ (minimal deviation from the nominal *game* strategy of eq. (22)/(27)) subject to $\mathcal Q^A_{\varpi s}\mathbf u^A_\varpi\le\gamma_{\varpi s}h_{\varpi s}$, $\mathcal Q^A_{\varpi d}\mathbf u^A_\varpi\le\gamma_{\varpi d}h_{\varpi d}-2(\mathbf p^A_\varpi-\mathbf p_d)\dot{\mathbf p}_d$, $\mathcal Q^A_{\varpi l}\mathbf u^A_\varpi\le\gamma_{\varpi l}h_{\varpi l}-2(\mathbf p^A_\varpi-\mathbf p^A_l)\mathbf u^A_l$, and the **body-frame velocity box** $[U_{\min};r_{\min}]\le R^{-1}_{\iota_\varpi}(\psi_{\varpi w})\mathbf u^A_\varpi-[0;\dot\beta_\varpi]\le[U_{\max};r_{\max}]$, where $\mathcal Q^A_{\varpi s}=-2(\mathbf p^A_\varpi-\mathbf p_s)^T$, $\mathcal Q^A_{\varpi d}=-2(\mathbf p^A_\varpi-\mathbf p_d)^T$, $\mathcal Q^A_{\varpi l}=-2(\mathbf p^A_\varpi-\mathbf p^A_l)^T$ (row vectors; the minus signs make $\mathcal Q\mathbf u\le\gamma h$ exactly equivalent to $\dot h\ge-\gamma h$). Feasibility of the QP (in particular compatibility of the CBF inequalities with the velocity box) is **not analyzed** — no feasible-fallback is provided.
- **Guidance mapping** (eq. (36)): $[U^{A*}_{kc},r^{A*}_{kc}]^T=R^{-1}_{\iota_k}(\psi_{kw})\mathbf u^{A*}_{kc}-[0,\dot\beta_k]^T$ and $[U^{D*}_{ic},r^{D*}_{ic}]^T=R^{-1}_{\iota_i}(\psi_{iw})\mathbf u^{D*}_{ic}-[0,\dot\beta_i]^T$ — inverts eq. (5) so the underactuated ASV realizes the QP-safe planar velocity. Both teams run the same filter, so the guidance is "multilateral" (each side optimizes against the other, Section III-C).

### 5.4 Theorem 1 (p. 6, Section IV; proof pp. 6–7, eqs. (37)–(47)) — the only theorem

**Statement (as printed, condensed)**: Consider the ASV model (5), the optimal capture positions (21) for $\mathbf p_k\in\mathcal R_D$ and (25) for $\mathbf p_k\in\mathcal R_{DC}$, the game strategies (22) for $\mathbf p_k\in\mathcal R_D$ and (27) for $\mathbf p_k\in\mathcal R_{DC}$, and the optimal velocity signal (35). Then the safe game guidance signals (36) of the two teams are *optimal*, and the multi-ASV system is *safe* (control objectives (14)–(15) are satisfied).

**Proof approach, step by step**:

1. **1v1 value function** (eq. (37)): $V(\mathbf p_k)=(x^*_{k1}-x^*_T)^2+(y^*_{k1}-y^*_T)^2=\frac{1}{4}\frac{\mathcal B^2(\mathbf p_k)}{\Theta^2}$ — the squared distance of the capture point from the closest area point; from eq. (21), $\|\mathbf I^*_{k1}-\mathbf p^*_T\|=|\mathcal B|/(2\Theta)$. $V\to0$ as the barrier is approached, so the attacker's degree-game cost is the capture-point distance to the area. Assumption printed: $\Theta\neq0$ ("otherwise, the attacker will be captured by the defender, resulting in the end of the game", p. 6).
2. **Gradient computation** (eqs. (38)–(39)): chain-rule differentiation of $V=\mathcal B^2/(4\Theta^2)$ with $\partial\mathcal B/\partial\mathbf p^A_k=2(\mathbf p^A_k-\mathbf p^*_T)$, $\partial\Theta^2/\partial\mathbf p^A_k=2(\mathbf p^A_k-\mathbf p^D_i)$; regrouped into the decisive structural form (39): $\partial V/\partial\mathbf p^A_k=-c\,[\,x^*_{k1}-x^A_k,\;y^*_{k1}-y^A_k\,]$, $\partial V/\partial\mathbf p^D_i=+c\,[\,x^*_{k1}-x^D_i,\;y^*_{k1}-y^D_i\,]$ with the *same* scalar $c$ (printed as $\mathcal B^2(\mathbf p_k)/\Theta^2$).
3. **HJI verification** (eqs. (40)–(41)): with the printed HJI form $\frac{\partial V}{\partial\mathbf p_k}\cdot\mathbf f(\mathbf p_k,\mathbf u^{A*}_k,\mathbf u^{D*}_i)+\mathbf g(t,\mathbf p_k,\mathbf u^{A*}_k,\mathbf u^{D*}_i)-\frac{\partial V}{\partial t}=0$ and the specializations $\partial V/\partial t=0$, $\mathbf g(\cdot)=0$, insert $\dot{\mathbf p}^A_k=\mathbf u^{A*}_k$, $\dot{\mathbf p}^D_i=\mathbf u^{D*}_i$ (unit-speed strategies): $\nabla V\cdot\mathbf f=\frac{\mathcal B^2(\mathbf p_k)}{\Theta^2}\Big[\frac{(x^*_{k1}-x^D_i)^2+(y^*_{k1}-y^D_i)^2}{\Theta_i}-\frac{(x^*_{k1}-x^A_k)^2+(y^*_{k1}-y^A_k)^2}{\Theta_k}\Big]=\frac{\mathcal B^2(\mathbf p_k)}{\Theta^2}(\Theta_i-\Theta_k)=0$ (eq. (41)), **because $\mathbf I^*_{k1}$ is equidistant from $A_k$ and $D_i$** (see 5.2) so $\Theta_i=\Theta_k$. Conclusion: (37) solves the HJI equation along optimal play (the barrier/value is stationary under saddle-point strategies), hence (22) are the 1v1 optimal strategies.
4. **2v1 value function** (eq. (42)): $V(\mathbf p_k)=(x^*_{k2}-x^*_T)^2+(y^*_{k2}-y^*_T)^2=x_C^2+y_C^2$.
5. **2v1 gradient algebra** (eqs. (43)–(46)): differentiate $V=x_C^2+y_C^2$ w.r.t. $x^A_k$ using the Cramer expressions (26) ($x_C,y_C$ depend on $\mathcal B_1,\mathcal B_2$ and positions; $\Gamma$ depends on positions only); the printed intermediate (43) is reorganized by **adding and subtracting** $\frac{2(y^D_i-y^D_j)}{\Gamma}(x^*_{k2}-x^*_T)^2$ and $\frac{2(x^D_j-x^D_i)}{\Gamma}(x^*_{k2}-x^*_T)(y^*_{k2}-y^*_T)$ to reach (44); the "last item" of (44) is then collapsed via the **decisive identity (45)**: $\frac{1}{2}\big((W^D_i)^2-(W^D_j)^2\big)+\frac{(x^D_j-x^D_i)}{\Gamma}(x^*_{k2}-x^*_T)=(y^D_i-y^D_j)\cdot\Big[\frac{x^A_k((W^D_j)^2-(W^D_i)^2)}{2\Gamma}+\frac{x^D_j((W^D_i)^2-(W^A_k)^2)}{2\Gamma}+\frac{x^D_i((W^A_k)^2-(W^D_j)^2)}{2\Gamma}\Big]=(y^D_i-y^D_j)\,y_C$ (the bracket equals $y_C$ by the same Cramer rule that defines $y_C$ in eq. (26)). Substituting (45) into (44) and using $x^*_{k2}=x_C+x^*_T$ yields the factorized form (46): $\partial V/\partial x^A_k=\frac{2}{\Gamma}\big[(y^D_i-y^D_j)(x^*_{k2}-x^*_T)+(x^D_j-x^D_i)(y^*_{k2}-y^*_T)\big](x^*_{k2}-x^A_k)$. "According to similar steps (43)–(46)" the remaining five partials are obtained, giving the compact gradients (unnumbered, p. 7): $\partial V/\partial\mathbf p^A_k=\frac{2}{\Gamma}[\,x^*_{k2}-x^A_k,\,y^*_{k2}-y^A_k\,]\,S$, $\partial V/\partial\mathbf p^D_i=\frac{2}{\Gamma}[\,x^*_{k2}-x^D_i,\,y^*_{k2}-y^D_i\,]\,S_i$, $\partial V/\partial\mathbf p^D_j=\frac{2}{\Gamma}[\,x^*_{k2}-x^D_j,\,y^*_{k2}-y^D_j\,]\,S_j$ with $S=(y^D_i-y^D_j)(x^*_{k2}-x^*_T)+(x^D_j-x^D_i)(y^*_{k2}-y^*_T)$, $S_i=(y^D_j-y^A_k)(x^*_{k2}-x^*_T)+(x^A_k-x^D_j)(y^*_{k2}-y^*_T)$, $S_j=(y^A_k-y^D_i)(x^*_{k2}-x^*_T)+(x^D_i-x^A_k)(y^*_{k2}-y^*_T)$.
6. **2v1 HJI verification** (eq. (47)): since $\mathbf I^*_{k2}$ is equidistant from all three players ($\Theta_i=\Theta_j=\Theta_k$, p. 7), $\nabla V\cdot\mathbf f=\frac{2\Theta_k}{\Gamma}\big[S+S_i+S_j\big]=0$, because the coefficients of $(x^*_{k2}-x^*_T)$ — $(y^D_i-y^D_j)+(y^D_j-y^A_k)+(y^A_k-y^D_i)$ — and of $(y^*_{k2}-y^*_T)$ — $(x^D_j-x^D_i)+(x^A_k-x^D_j)+(x^D_i-x^A_k)$ — cancel pairwise (elementary but decisive telescoping). Hence (42) solves the HJI equation and (27) are the 2v1 optimal strategies.
7. **Safety part**: "By applying Lemma 1, it can be proved that the proposed optimal guidance strategy (37) satisfies the constraint conditions in formula (36)" (p. 7 — cross-reference typo; the intended chain is: QP (35) enforces $\dot h\ge-\gamma h$ for all three CBF families and the velocity box, so by Lemma 1 [44] each $h_{\varpi s},h_{\varpi d},h_{\varpi l}\ge0$ is forward invariant ⇒ distances never fall below $\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3$ ⇒ objective (14) (in the ≥ sense) and (15) hold). "This completes the proof of Theorem 1."

**Scope, character, and gaps of Theorem 1**:

- **Local vs global / analytic vs numerical**: fully analytic and global on each winning region (closed-form feedback strategies); the Game of Kind partition is asserted by the equal-speed orthogonal-line argument (standard for $\|\mathbf u^A\|=\|\mathbf u^D\|$ simple motion) but **never proven** (no barrier-crossing/viscosity argument, no boundary-condition check of the HJI PDE beyond stationarity along optimal play).
- **Proof gap 1 (coefficient typo/inconsistency)**: differentiating $V=\mathcal B^2/(4\Theta^2)$ (eq. (37)) gives $\partial V/\partial x^A_k=\frac{\mathcal B(\mathbf p_k)}{\Theta^2}(x^A_k-x^*_T)-\frac{2(x^A_k-x^D_i)}{\Theta^2}V$ (and analogs), i.e., the first-term coefficient is $\mathcal B(\mathbf p_k)/\Theta^2$, whereas eqs. (38)–(39) and (40)–(41) print $\mathcal B^2(\mathbf p_k)/\Theta^2$. As printed, (38) and (39) are mutually inconsistent (matching them would require $\mathcal B=1$). The final conclusion (41) is unaffected because the argument only needs the *same* proportionality constant $c$ on $\pm(\mathbf I^*_{k1}-\mathbf p)$ — but the derivation as printed is not correct line-by-line.
- **Proof gap 2 (model mismatch in the HJI)**: the HJI is verified for the *reduced point-mass dynamics* $\dot{\mathbf p}=\mathbf u$ of the shifted points with unit-speed controls (eqs. (22)/(27)); the underactuated model (4)–(5) never enters the optimality argument. Optimality of the QP-filtered signals (36) is claimed ("The optimality of safe game guidance signals (36) is further proved", p. 7) but **not shown**: when a CBF or velocity constraint is active, the QP output $\mathbf u^*_{\varpi c}\ne\mathbf u^*_\varpi$ and the HJI stationarity argument no longer applies; no result quantifies the value loss induced by the safety filter.
- **Proof gap 3 ($\mathcal R_A$ game)**: the min–max value (13) in the attacker-winning region is never solved; only the attacker's heuristic (head straight to $\mathbf p^*_T$, p. 4) and the defenders' intent (approach $A_k$) are described.
- **Proof gap 4 (degeneracies)**: $\Gamma=0$ (parallel orthogonal lines, eq. (26)) and the $\Phi_1>0\wedge\Phi_2>0$ tie-breaking (eq. (28)) are not analyzed; capture-type optimality is argued geometrically.
- **Minor errata as printed** (all verified at 300 dpi): eq. (6) "i = 2k = 1...2N"; eq. (14) "≤" for safety distances; p. 5 "equation (13) and equation (14)" for (8)/(9); eq. (16) "min" for argmin; eq. (44) "$(y^*_{k2}-x^*_T)$" for $(y^*_{k2}-y^*_T)$; Theorem-1 proof citing "strategy (37)" and "formula (36)" for the QP (35); "Sensior Member" byline; numerous grammar slips ("a area", "donated as").
- **Physical meaning**: $V(\mathbf p_k)=\frac{\mathcal B^2}{4\Theta^2}$ is the squared closest-to-area distance at which the attacker will be captured under optimal play — a *guaranteed protection margin*; the barrier $\mathcal B=0$ is the exact win/loss boundary; the cooperative point $\mathbf I^*_{k2}$ pushes the capture location farther from the area than either 1v1 point, which is the payoff of defender cooperation (p. 5: "the attacker can then be captured further away from the target area").
- **Extensibility**: the three-layer architecture (point-mass game on a guidance point → geometric capture point → CBF-QP + input inversion) is model-generic: any vehicle admitting a shifted fully-actuated point (Fossen-type marine craft, fixed-wing UAVs with sideslip) can reuse it; the equal-speed assumption and the circular static area are the binding restrictions; the QP can absorb any number of CBF pairs.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues

**Author-stated**: none explicitly (the conclusion, Section VI, merely restates the contribution); the equal-resultant-velocity assumption is stated as a model premise (p. 3) but its restrictiveness is not discussed.

**Critical observations (with page/eq.)**:
1. **Equal speeds for all 15 ASVs** (p. 3, "all players have the same resultant velocity") — the entire barrier/orthogonal-line geometry (eqs. (18)–(28)) and the HJI stationarity (eqs. (41), (47)) collapse for heterogeneous speeds; the Apollonius machinery of [39] would be needed, and is not imported. The QP's velocity box $[0,1]$ m/s effectively *imposes* equality by construction in simulation.
2. **Optimality–safety composition is unproven** (p. 7, proof of Theorem 1): HJI optimality holds for the nominal strategies (22)/(27); the executed signals are the QP outputs (35)–(36), which differ whenever a safety constraint is active (visible in Figs. 6–7 saturation transients). No bound on the induced value loss, no proof of capture under the closed loop, and no Game-of-Kind preservation proof (a CBF deviation could in principle push a state across $\mathcal B$).
3. **HJI treatment is informal** (p. 6): the "HJI equation" is written with an undefined $\mathbf g(t,\mathbf p_k,\cdot)$ term that is set to zero and $\partial V/\partial t=0$; only stationarity along the saddle-point trajectories is checked — no viscosity/verification argument, no terminal-condition check on $\mathcal T$, and the Game of Kind partition is asserted from Fig. 3 geometry.
4. **Attacker-winning region unsolved** (eqs. (12)–(13), p. 4): no defender strategy or value is derived for $\mathbf p_k\in\mathcal R_A$; symmetric treatment is missing.
5. **Degeneracies unhandled**: $\Gamma=0$ in eq. (26) (parallel orthogonal lines, e.g., collinear $D_i, D_j, A_k$); the $\Phi_1>0\wedge\Phi_2>0$ tie case of eq. (28); $\Theta\to0$ at the endgame (p. 6 admits the game "ends" but the strategies (22)/(27) are then singular — the division by $\Theta_k,\Theta_i$ in (22) blows up exactly at capture).
6. **Information assumptions are strong and unmodeled**: perfect state feedback of all rival positions (Section III-C); dynamic-obstacle velocity $\dot{\mathbf p}_d$ (eq. (32)) and neighbor inputs $\mathbf u^A_l$ (eq. (34)) required at each QP; no delays, noise, or communication topology — a notable omission for a marine multi-ASV setting.
7. **QP feasibility** (eq. (35)) is not analyzed: CBF inequalities + body-frame velocity box may be infeasible jointly; no fallback (e.g., prioritized slack) is given.
8. **Printed-equation defects** (all listed in 5.4, e.g., eq. (6) index typo, eq. (14) reversed inequality, $\mathcal B^2$ vs $\mathcal B$ in (38)–(41), eq. (44) $x^*_T$ typo, wrong cross-references on p. 5 and in the proof): signal a lightly-edited manuscript; readers reproducing from the printed equations must be aware.
9. **Simulation-only validation** (Section V): one 5-vs-10 scenario, no experiment, no baseline, no statistical metrics; robustness to disturbances/uncertainties (contrasted with [23]) untouched; obstacle model (size of the red disks vs $\alpha$) not tied to physical dimensions.

## 8. Citation Value

**Paragraph role**: a *systems/application-tier* reach-avoid paper that bridges the geometric equal-speed reach-avoid game literature ([38]–[40], Garcia–Casbeer–Pachter; Yan–Shi–Zhong–Bullo) and the marine cooperative-control community (T-IV/TIE/Ocean Engineering USV attack–defense line [21]–[26]), using the standard CBF-QP safety-filter stack ([42]–[44], Ames et al.) and Fossen's guidance-point trick [45] to make simple-motion game strategies executable by underactuated, sideslip-affected ASVs. Its distinctive reusable artifacts are: (i) the closed-form cooperative interception point $\mathbf I^*_{k2}$ (eqs. (25)–(26)) for a 2-vs-1 equal-speed triple with an *extended circular target*, with HJI stationarity verification of $V=\mathcal B^2/(4\Theta^2)$ (1v1) and $V=x_C^2+y_C^2$ (2v1); and (ii) the $\Phi_1/\Phi_2$ side-of-line test (eq. (28)) as an online 1v1-vs-2v1 mode switch for defender pairs. Cite it as the ASV/instantiation reference for "reach-avoid differential game + CBF safety on underactuated vehicles", as an example of layered game-then-QP designs and their unproven optimality-safety composition, or when surveying equal-speed orthogonal-line/barrier constructions extended from point targets to target areas — not as a source for new game-theoretic machinery (its game layer is a direct adaptation of [38]–[40] geometry to $\mathbb R^2$ with an area target). Example citation sentence: "Extending the equal-speed orthogonal-line reach-avoid construction to an area-target, multi-pair setting, [P096] derives closed-form 1v1/2v1 capture points with an online cooperation switch, and enforces inter-ASV and obstacle safety through CBF-based QPs mapped onto underactuated ASV guidance signals via an off-center point and input inversion; optimality is verified via HJI stationarity for the nominal (unfiltered) strategies only."
