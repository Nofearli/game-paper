## 2. Research Background
Pursuit-evasion (PE) games model adversarial scenarios from predator–prey interactions to aerial combat and missile interception, and since Isaacs' seminal monograph [3] they have spawned variants such as target-attacker-defender (TAD), perimeter defense, and multiplayer reach-avoid games. Two solution families dominate: geometric methods built on isochrons (the set of points a player reaches at equal time), whose intersections give the perpendicular bisector for equal speeds and the Apollonius circle for different speeds, and differential-game/HJI or numerical (reach-avoid, reinforcement learning) methods, which guarantee optimality but suffer from the curse of dimensionality and poor accuracy/long computation times (p. 1299). The authors observe that "most of existing works about PE problems ... assume that all the players move in a 2D plane", and that the small 3D literature ([11] Yan et al., CDC 2019; [19] Garcia et al., RA-L 2020) treats only equal constant speeds (p. 1299). Yet many natural scenarios pair a faster pursuer moving in 3D space with a slower evader confined to a plane: eagles capturing hares or rats, UAVs or missiles attacking tanks (p. 1299). The paper therefore studies PE between a faster 3D pursuer and a slower 2D-plane evader, in two variants depending on whether the evader has a target (hole of the hare / safe region of the tank): a pure PE game and a TAD game.

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Geometry (modeling)**: Extension of the Apollonius circle to the 3D-pursuer/2D-evader setting — the intersection of the two players' isochrones is a circle in the evader's plane with closed-form center (eq. (5)) and radius (eq. (6)) depending on the pursuer's altitude $z_P$; Remark 1 interprets it as the intersection of a 3D "Apollonius sphere" with the evader's plane (pp. 1300–1301).
2. **Theory, PE game (Problem 1)**: Lemma 1 (max-speed, constant, straight-line optimal play via PMP) + closed-form capture point $q^\ast$ (farthest circle point for E), value $t_E(\boldsymbol{x})$ (eq. (9)) and feedback strategies (eq. (10)); Theorem 1 asserts Nash-optimality of these feedback strategies (pp. 1301–1302).
3. **Theory, TAD game (Problem 2)**: (a) *game of kind* — exact partition of the state space by the quadratic barrier $B(\boldsymbol{x}) = \delta^2(x_E^2+y_E^2) - (x_P^2+y_P^2+z_P^2)$ into winning sets $\mathcal{W}_P$ ($B>0$, P wins) and $\mathcal{W}_E$ ($B<0$, E wins) (p. 1302); (b) *game of degree* — for $\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{W}_P$, evader's optimal goal is the circle point $g^\ast$ nearest the target, value $L_f(\boldsymbol{x})$ (eq. (12)), feedback strategies (13)–(14); Theorem 2 asserts the value and Nash optimality (pp. 1302–1303). Counterintuitive finding: the losing evader should not beeline to the target.
4. **Verification (experiment)**: Numerical simulations for both games comparing the proposed strategies $(\boldsymbol{u}^\ast,\boldsymbol{v}^\ast)$ against pure-pursuit, pure-evade and task-oriented baselines; Tables I–II exhibit the Nash cross-payment structure and consistency of the simulated cost with the analytic values (9) and (12) (pp. 1303–1304).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results (MOST IMPORTANT)

**Dynamics, information structure, termination (Section II, pp. 1300–1301).** State $\boldsymbol{x} = (x_P, y_P, z_P, x_E, y_E)^\top \in \mathbb{R}^5$; coordinates chosen with $x,y$ axes in the evader's plane. Dynamics are single integrators in each player's own space, eq. (1):
$$\dot{x}_P = u_x,\ \dot{y}_P = u_y,\ \dot{z}_P = u_z;\qquad \dot{x}_E = v_x,\ \dot{y}_E = v_y,$$
with $\boldsymbol{u}=(u_x,u_y,u_z)^\top$, $\boldsymbol{v}=(v_x,v_y)^\top$. **Assumption 1** (eq. (2)): $\exists\, u,v \in (0,+\infty)$ with $\sqrt{u_x^2+u_y^2+u_z^2} \le u$, $\sqrt{v_x^2+v_y^2} \le v$. **Assumption 2** (eq. (3)): $u/v \triangleq \delta > 1$ (pursuer strictly faster). Information: perfect state feedback; the Apollonius circle is "determined by the instantaneous positions" and is recomputed at each state, so all strategies below are feedback strategies (p. 1301, Remarks 2–3). The generic zero-sum cost is $J = \Phi(\boldsymbol{x}(t_f),\boldsymbol{u}(t_f),\boldsymbol{v}(t_f),t_f) + \int_0^{t_f} h(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v},t)\,dt$, value $V(\boldsymbol{x},t) = \min_{\boldsymbol{u}}\max_{\boldsymbol{v}} J$, satisfying the HJI equation (eq. (7)):
$$-\frac{\partial V}{\partial t} = \frac{\partial V}{\partial \boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{u}^\ast,\boldsymbol{v}^\ast)^\top + h(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{u}^\ast,\boldsymbol{v}^\ast,t).$$

**Apollonius circle in the plane (Section II-B, pp. 1300–1301, eqs. (4)–(6)).** Points $(x,y,0)$ of the evader's plane simultaneously reachable by both players at maximum speed satisfy
$$\frac{\sqrt{(x-x_P)^2 + (y-y_P)^2 + z_P^2}}{\sqrt{(x-x_E)^2 + (y-y_E)^2}} = \delta,$$
which rearranges to the circle, eq. (4):
$$\Big(x - \tfrac{\delta^2 x_E - x_P}{\delta^2-1}\Big)^2 + \Big(y - \tfrac{\delta^2 y_E - y_P}{\delta^2-1}\Big)^2 = \frac{z_P^2}{\delta^2-1} + \frac{\delta^2\left[(x_P-x_E)^2 + (y_P-y_E)^2\right]}{(\delta^2-1)^2},$$
with center, eq. (5): $\boldsymbol{c} = \big(\tfrac{\delta^2 x_E - x_P}{\delta^2-1}, \tfrac{\delta^2 y_E - y_P}{\delta^2-1}, 0\big)^\top$ and radius, eq. (6):
$$r = \sqrt{\frac{z_P^2}{\delta^2-1} + \frac{\delta^2\left[(x_P-x_E)^2 + (y_P-y_E)^2\right]}{(\delta^2-1)^2}}.$$
Interior of the circle = evader's dominant region (E reaches it first); exterior = pursuer's dominant region (p. 1300). I independently re-derived (4)–(6) by squaring and completing the square in $\| (x,y,0)-P\|^2 = \delta^2 \|(x,y,0)-E\|^2$; both center and radius are exactly correct, and $\|\boldsymbol{c}\|^2 - r^2 = B(\boldsymbol{x})/(\delta^2-1)$ identically — the identity underlying the barrier below. **Remark 1** (pp. 1300–1301): if the evader could also move in 3D, the capture point would live in 3D and the isochron intersection would be a sphere — the "Apollonius sphere"; the circle (4) used in this paper is precisely the intersection of that sphere with the evader's plane. **Extensibility**: the formulas are pointwise algebraic in $(P, E, \delta)$, hence directly usable as a feedback map and transferable to any fixed evader plane after a rigid coordinate change (the WLOG placement of the plane as $z=0$).

**Degenerate configuration $\mathcal{R}_0$ (Section III, p. 1301).** $\mathcal{R}_0 = \{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^5 \mid (x_E-x_P)^2 + (y_E-y_P)^2 \neq 0\}$. When $\boldsymbol{x}\notin\mathcal{R}_0$ (P exactly above E), the circle center $\boldsymbol{c}$ coincides with E; all E strategies are equivalent and P should shadow E while descending to the plane — no optimal strategy needs to be computed. All results below assume $\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{R}_0$.

**Lemma 1 (p. 1301, unnumbered).** *Statement*: for differential game (1)(2)(3)(8) with $\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{R}_0$, the optimal strategies of P and E are constant, at maximum speed, with straight-line trajectories. *Assumptions*: dynamics (1), speed bounds (2), cost (8) $J = \int_0^{t_f} dt$ (pure running cost, $h \equiv 1$, no terminal cost), capture time $t_f$ defined by the terminal manifold $x_P = x_E,\ y_P = y_E,\ z_P = 0$ (pursuer must reach the evader *in the plane*). *Proof approach*: Pontryagin's maximum principle — Hamiltonian $H = 1 + \lambda_x u_x + \lambda_y u_y + \lambda_z u_z + \mu_x v_x + \mu_y v_y$; since $\dot{\boldsymbol{\lambda}} = \partial H/\partial \boldsymbol{x} = \boldsymbol{0}$ (integrator dynamics), all costates are constant; P's $\boldsymbol{u}^\ast = \arg\min_{\boldsymbol{u}} H$ s.t. $\|\boldsymbol{u}\| \le u$ is a linear program over a convex ball, so the optimum lies on the boundary, $\|\boldsymbol{u}^\ast\| = u$; identically $\|\boldsymbol{v}^\ast\| = v$; constant costates then force constant optimal directions, i.e., straight lines. *Decisive step*: constancy of costates from $\partial H/\partial x \equiv 0$. *Scope*: local-in-structure but global in validity for this game; analytic. *Physical meaning*: justifies computing the isochron intersection at maximum speeds ("which is in accordance with the optimal strategies", p. 1301). *Proof gap (minor)*: the argument shows only that *any* optimal play is max-speed and straight; it does not identify *which* straight lines are optimal (that is Theorem 1's content, deferred to [20]). Also note the TAD case reuses this lemma by analogy ("Since the running term (integral term) in (11) is zero, similar to Lemma 1...", p. 1302) without restating the PMP argument for the purely terminal-cost game, where the Hamiltonian has no $+1$ term.

**PE game solution (Section III, pp. 1301–1302).** With capture point $q^\ast$ chosen as the point of the Apollonius circle farthest from E,
$$q^\ast = \Big(\tfrac{\delta^2 x_E - x_P}{\delta^2-1} + r\cos\theta,\ \tfrac{\delta^2 y_E - y_P}{\delta^2-1} + r\sin\theta,\ 0\Big)^\top,\quad \cos\theta = \tfrac{x_E - x_P}{d},\ \sin\theta = \tfrac{y_E - y_P}{d},$$
where $d = \sqrt{(x_E-x_P)^2 + (y_E-y_P)^2}$ is the planar P–E distance. Note $q^\ast = \boldsymbol{c} + r\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}$ with $\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}$ the unit vector from P's ground projection toward E; since $\boldsymbol{c} - E = \tfrac{d}{\delta^2-1}\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}$, $q^\ast$ is simultaneously the farthest circle point from E *and* the farthest from P's projection — a key structural coincidence of this geometry (my derivation). **Value function, eq. (9)**:
$$t_E(\boldsymbol{x}) = \frac{\|q^\ast - E\|}{v} = \frac{1}{v}\Big(r + \|\boldsymbol{c}-E\|\Big) = \frac{1}{v}\Big(r + \frac{\sqrt{(x_E-x_P)^2 + (y_E-y_P)^2}}{\delta^2-1}\Big).$$
**Strategies, eq. (10)**: $u_x^\ast = u\cos\phi\cos\theta,\ u_y^\ast = u\cos\phi\sin\theta,\ u_z^\ast = -u\sin\phi$; $v_x^\ast = v\cos\theta,\ v_y^\ast = v\sin\theta$, with
$$\cos\phi = \frac{\|P_{proj} - q^\ast\|}{\|P - q^\ast\|} = \frac{\delta^2 d + \sqrt{(\delta^2-1)z_P^2 + \delta^2 d^2}}{\delta\left(d + \sqrt{(\delta^2-1)z_P^2 + \delta^2 d^2}\right)},\qquad \sin\phi = \frac{z_P}{\|P-q^\ast\|} = \frac{(\delta^2-1)z_P}{\delta\left(d + \sqrt{(\delta^2-1)z_P^2 + \delta^2 d^2}\right)}.$$
*Verification (mine, numeric)*: with $\delta=2$, $P=(8.00,1.42,9.41)$, $E=(7.92,9.60,0)$: $r = 7.698$, $d = 8.180$, $t_E = (r + d/3)/v = 10.42/v$ — matches Table I's 10.43; and $\|P-q^\ast\|/u = 20.849/2 = 10.425 = t_E$, i.e., both players reach $q^\ast$ simultaneously, so capture indeed occurs on the circle. I also confirmed the identity $\|P-q^\ast\| = \delta(d + \sqrt{(\delta^2-1)z_P^2 + \delta^2 d^2})/(\delta^2-1)$ used implicitly in $\cos\phi, \sin\phi$. *Physical meaning*: the faster 3D pursuer must convert its altitude advantage into intercept geometry; capture time grows with altitude $z_P$ through $r \sim z_P/\sqrt{\delta^2-1}$ and shrinks with $\delta$. *Extensibility*: for $z_P = 0$ the formulas degenerate gracefully to the classical 2D Apollonius pursuit.

**Theorem 1 (PE game) (p. 1302).** *Statement*: for differential game (1)(2)(3)(8), the optimal strategies are given by (10) and the corresponding value function is $V(\boldsymbol{x}) = t_E(\boldsymbol{x})$ with $t_E$ from (9). *Assumptions*: (1)–(3), $\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{R}_0$, cost (8). *Proof*: "See the extended version of this paper [20]" — not contained in this paper. *Claimed properties* (Remark 2, p. 1302): (10) are exactly feedback strategies and are optimal in the sense of Nash equilibrium — unilateral deviation by either player increases its own cost; verified numerically in Section V. *Local/global*: global over $\mathcal{R}_0$ (claim); analytic. *HJI note*: the abstract and conclusion assert the value functions "are further proved to be the solution of Hamilton-Jacobi-Isaacs equation", but that proof lives in [20]; within the ACC paper the only in-text optimality evidence is Lemma 1 plus Table I.

**Game of kind, TAD (Section IV-A, p. 1302).** Static target $T = O = (0,0,0)^\top$ (WLOG). Terminal sets: $\mathcal{S}_P = \{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^5 \mid x_P = x_E,\ y_P = y_E,\ z_P = 0\}$ (capture) and $\mathcal{S}_E = \{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^5 \mid x_E = 0,\ y_E = 0\}$ (E reaches target). Winning sets are read off the Apollonius circle: $\mathcal{W}_P = \{\boldsymbol{x} \mid O \text{ outside the circle}\}$, $\mathcal{W}_E = \{\boldsymbol{x} \mid O \text{ inside the circle}\}$, which equal
$$\mathcal{W}_P = \{\boldsymbol{x} \mid \delta^2(x_E^2+y_E^2) - (x_P^2+y_P^2+z_P^2) > 0\},\qquad \mathcal{W}_E = \{\boldsymbol{x} \mid \delta^2(x_E^2+y_E^2) - (x_P^2+y_P^2+z_P^2) < 0\}.$$
**Barrier function**: $B(\boldsymbol{x}) = \delta^2(x_E^2 + y_E^2) - (x_P^2 + y_P^2 + z_P^2) = \delta^2\|E\|^2 - \|P\|^2$ (sign of $\|\boldsymbol{c}\|^2 - r^2$, up to the positive factor $\delta^2-1$; my identity above). *Winning condition, precisely*: E wins (reaches $O$ first) iff $\delta\|E\| > \|P\|$, i.e., iff $\|P\|/u < \|E\|/v$ with $u = \delta v$ — P can reach the target strictly before E and can guard it; P wins iff $\delta\|E\| < \|P\|$. For simple motion this is the exact (not merely sufficient) partition, matching the classical state-feedback target-defense construction of [21], although the paper argues it only geometrically ("According to Apollonius circle, it is easy to determine these two sets", p. 1302) without a formal backward-reachability/HJI barrier proof. *(Textual slip: the sentence introducing the two sets names them "$\mathcal{W}_P$ and $\mathcal{W}_S$" before using $\mathcal{W}_E$.)*

**Game of degree, losing case $\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{W}_E$ (Section IV-B, p. 1302).** Claimed optimal strategies: both players move to the target directly. Printed cost for P: $J_P = \sqrt{(x_P-x_E)^2 + (y_P-y_E)^2}\,\big|_{t_f}$ (terminal P–E separation); printed value function:
$$V(\boldsymbol{x}) = (x_P^2 + y_P^2 + z_P^2) - \delta^2(x_E^2 + y_E^2) = -B(\boldsymbol{x}) = \|P\|^2 - \delta^2\|E\|^2.$$
Verification is explicitly waived: "Due to space limits, we omit the simple verification of this case." *Proof gap (the main technical soft spot of the paper)*: (i) under direct-to-target play, E arrives at $t_f = \|E\|/v$ with E at $O$ and P at radial distance $\|P\| - \delta\|E\|$, so the natural terminal-distance value is $\|P\| - \delta\|E\|$, not $\|P\|^2 - \delta^2\|E\|^2 = (\|P\|-\delta\|E\|)(\|P\|+\delta\|E\|)$ — the printed $V$ is a monotone rescaling of the terminal separation, not the separation itself; (ii) the stationary HJI residual for $V=-B$ with these strategies is $\frac{\partial V}{\partial \boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{u}^\ast,\boldsymbol{v}^\ast)^\top = -2u\|P\| + 2\delta^2 v\|E\| = 2\delta v(\delta\|E\| - \|P\|)$, which vanishes only on the barrier $\|P\| = \delta\|E\|$ — so $V=-B$ is not shown (and appears unable) to satisfy (7) off the barrier. This case is thus heuristic; fortunately it is not the paper's central result.

**Game of degree, winning case $\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{W}_P$ (Section IV-B, pp. 1302–1303).** Cost, eq. (11): $J = \Phi(t_f) = \|E - O\|\big|_{t_f} = \sqrt{x_E^2 + y_E^2}\big|_{t_f}$; P maximizes (captures E as far from the target as possible), E minimizes. Since the running term is zero, constant max-speed straight-line strategies are argued as in Lemma 1. Because the isochron intersection is a circle, E's optimal play is **not** to head to the target directly; E should move to the circle point nearest the target:
$$g^\ast = \Big(\tfrac{\delta^2 x_E - x_P}{\delta^2-1} - r\cos\psi,\ \tfrac{\delta^2 y_E - y_P}{\delta^2-1} - r\sin\psi,\ 0\Big)^\top,\qquad \cos\psi = \tfrac{\delta^2 x_E - x_P}{d_\delta},\ \sin\psi = \tfrac{\delta^2 y_E - y_P}{d_\delta},$$
with $d_\delta = \sqrt{(\delta^2 x_E - x_P)^2 + (\delta^2 y_E - y_P)^2} \neq 0$ for $\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{W}_P$ (if $d_\delta=0$ then $\boldsymbol{c}=O$ and $O$ would be inside the circle, i.e., $\mathcal{W}_E$ — the implication is sound, my check). **Value, eq. (12)**:
$$L_f(\boldsymbol{x}) = \|g^\ast\| = \|\boldsymbol{c}\| - r = \frac{d_\delta}{\delta^2-1} - r,$$
the final E–target distance when both meet at $g^\ast$. **E's strategy, eq. (13)**: $v_x^\ast = v\cos\alpha,\ v_y^\ast = v\sin\alpha$ with
$$\cos\alpha = \frac{x_E - x_P - r(\delta^2-1)\cos\psi}{\sqrt{d^2 + (\delta^2-1)^2 r^2 - 2r(\delta^2-1)f}},\quad \sin\alpha = \frac{y_E - y_P - r(\delta^2-1)\sin\psi}{\sqrt{d^2 + (\delta^2-1)^2 r^2 - 2r(\delta^2-1)f}},$$
and $f = (x_E-x_P)\cos\psi + (y_E-y_P)\sin\psi$. **P's strategy, eq. (14)**: $u_x^\ast = u\cos\beta\cos\gamma,\ u_y^\ast = u\sin\beta\cos\gamma,\ u_z^\ast = -u\sin\gamma$ with
$$\sin\gamma = \frac{z_P}{\|g^\ast - P\|} = \frac{(\delta^2-1)z_P}{\sqrt{\delta^4 d^2 + (\delta^2-1)^2 r^2 - 2r\delta^2(\delta^2-1)f + (\delta^2-1)^2 z_P^2}},$$
$$\cos\gamma = \frac{\sqrt{\delta^4 d^2 + (\delta^2-1)^2 r^2 - 2r\delta^2(\delta^2-1)f}}{\sqrt{\delta^4 d^2 + (\delta^2-1)^2 r^2 - 2r\delta^2(\delta^2-1)f + (\delta^2-1)^2 z_P^2}},$$
$$\cos\beta = \frac{\delta^2(x_E - x_P) - r(\delta^2-1)\cos\psi}{\sqrt{\delta^4 d^2 + (\delta^2-1)^2 r^2 - 2r\delta^2(\delta^2-1)f}},\quad \sin\beta = \frac{\delta^2(y_E - y_P) - r(\delta^2-1)\sin\psi}{\sqrt{\delta^4 d^2 + (\delta^2-1)^2 r^2 - 2r\delta^2(\delta^2-1)f}}.$$
I verified at 600-dpi rendering that the printed $\sin\gamma$ has **no** stray factor before the square root, and confirmed all denominators by expanding $\|g^\ast - E\|$ and $\|g^\ast - P\|$ (the radicals equal $(\delta^2-1)\|g^\ast-E\|$ and $(\delta^2-1)\|g^\ast-P\|$ respectively). *Numeric check (mine)*: with $\delta=2$, $P=(6.97,5.83,9.15)$, $E=(9.80,0.33,0)$: $\boldsymbol{c}=(10.743,-1.503)$, $r=6.702$, $\|\boldsymbol{c}\| = 10.848$, $L_f = 4.146$ vs Table II's 4.1481 (agreement within initial-condition rounding); $\|P-g^\ast\|/u = 11.530/2 = 5.765 = \|g^\ast-E\|/v$ — simultaneous arrival at $g^\ast$; $B(\boldsymbol{x}) = 218.3 > 0$ confirming $\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{W}_P$; and $\|\boldsymbol{c}\|^2 - r^2 = 72.77 = B/3$. *Physical meaning*: the doomed evader trades target proximity against capture geometry — heading straight for $O$ lets P intercept *earlier and farther out*, whereas steering to $g^\ast$ forces capture exactly at the circle point closest to $O$; this formalizes the "hare's hole" intuition. *Extensibility*: same construction works for any static target location after translation; Remark 3 stresses $g^\ast$ and the strategies are recomputed from current positions, hence feedback.

**Theorem 2 (TAD game) (p. 1303).** *Statement*: consider differential game (1)(2)(3)(11) with $\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{W}_P$; the value function of the game is $V(\boldsymbol{x}) = L_f(\boldsymbol{x})$ (eq. (12)) and the optimal strategies are given by (14) (P) and (13) (E). *Assumptions*: (1)–(3), $\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{W}_P$, terminal cost (11). *Proof*: "See the extended version of this paper [20]" — not in this paper. *Scope*: global over $\mathcal{W}_P$ (claim); analytic; Nash sense per Remark 3. After Theorem 2 the authors state Problem 2 is solved for both cases.

**How different dimensions / different speeds are handled (summary).** No projection or surrogate planar pursuer is introduced: the 3D pursuer position enters the planar isochron intersection directly through the altitude term $z_P^2/(\delta^2-1)$ in (4)–(6), which inflates the circle; the Apollonius-circle machinery of [13], [14] is thereby generalized rather than the perpendicular-bisector machinery of equal-speed works [10], [11]. The speed asymmetry enters everywhere through $\delta$ (only $\delta^2-1$ denominators and $\delta^2$-scaled coordinates); every formula reduces to a classical 2D result as $z_P \to 0$. Winning conditions: PE game — capture always occurs (P faster) at time $t_E(\boldsymbol{x})$ (9); TAD — E wins iff $B(\boldsymbol{x}) < 0$ ($\delta\|E\| > \|P\|$), P wins iff $B(\boldsymbol{x}) > 0$, with degree values $-B(\boldsymbol{x})$ (unverified, $\mathcal{W}_E$) and $L_f(\boldsymbol{x})$ (12) ($\mathcal{W}_P$).

## 6. Simulation and Experiments
All experiments are numerical simulations of the two differential games in Section V (pp. 1303–1304); no hardware experiments.

Baselines: **pure-pursuit** (P heads to E directly), **pure-evade** (E moves opposite to the pursuer's direction in the plane), and the proposed pair (10). Parameter table as printed:

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
**Author-stated** (p. 1304, Conclusions): future work will extend the results to multiple players in mixed 3D/2D spaces, and to an *active* (moving) target, which "is also interesting". Also implicit from Section V: only single-initial-condition demonstrations are given.

**Critical observations (with page/eq. references)**:
1. **Proofs deferred**: Theorem 1 and Theorem 2 proofs are "See the extended version of this paper [20]" (pp. 1302, 1303); the HJI-satisfaction claim of the abstract and conclusion is likewise not demonstrated inside this paper. The ACC paper's only in-text proofs are Lemma 1's PMP argument and the simulation tables.
2. **Unverified $\mathcal{W}_E$-case value function (p. 1302)**: $V = -B(\boldsymbol{x})$ is inconsistent with the printed cost $J_P$ (terminal P–E separation would be $\|P\| - \delta\|E\|$ under the claimed direct-to-target play, not $\|P\|^2 - \delta^2\|E\|^2$), and its stationary HJI residual $2\delta v(\delta\|E\| - \|P\|)$ vanishes only on the barrier — see Section 5. This is the weakest analytic element of the paper.
3. **Game-of-kind exactness asserted, not proved** (p. 1302): the partition via "O outside/inside Apollonius circle" is argued geometrically without a backward reachable set / HJI barrier construction; exactness for simple motion is nonetheless plausible via the standard target-guarding argument (cf. Ref. [21]).
4. **Idealized models**: single integrators, constant speed bounds, perfectly known state, static point target fixed at the origin (WLOG but excludes moving targets), capture defined by exact coincidence ($z_P = 0$, $x_P = x_E$, $y_P = y_E$, p. 1301) with no capture radius — numerically this requires an unreported tolerance.
5. **Strategy structure**: the feedback laws require the evader to remain in a plane and the pursuer to have unbounded turn rate (velocity-controlled); no input lag, gravity, or feasibility constraints for UAV/missile dynamics are modeled despite the motivating scenarios (p. 1299).
6. **Equilibrium subtlety**: because the Apollonius circle is recomputed instantaneously, the "Nash" verification in Tables I–II is a one-shot, one-state check per game; stochastic or robust equilibrium notions and multi-sample validation are absent.
7. **Editorial slips** (indicative of haste, none affecting the math): "Noth that ... deternimined" (p. 1301); "divided into two sets, $\mathcal{W}_P$ and $\mathcal{W}_S$" for $\mathcal{W}_E$ (p. 1302); "in Section IV and Section III" reversed order sentence (p. 1300); "Hoping to taking the chance to avoid the evader" (p. 1302). I verified at high resolution that the otherwise-suspicious $\sin\gamma$ denominator (p. 1303) contains no typographical factor and matches $\sin\gamma = z_P/\|g^\ast-P\|$ exactly (my numeric check: 0.7934 vs 0.7936).
8. **Extensibility caveats**: the barrier $B(\boldsymbol{x})$ is specific to a *static* target; an active target or multiple evaders changes the isochron intersection topology (authors' own future work). The "Apollonius sphere" of Remark 1 (pp. 1300–1301) is sketched but not developed into a 3D-vs-3D solution.

## 8. Citation Value
This is a short but clean conference paper whose lasting role in the knowledge base is as the *canonical reference for dimension-heterogeneous pursuit-evasion geometry*: it shows exactly how the Apollonius circle deforms when the pursuer's altitude $z_P$ enters the isochron intersection (eqs. (4)–(6)), gives the closed-form feedback laws (9), (10), (12)–(14) for both the time-optimal-capture (PE) and target-defense (TAD) degrees of play, and supplies the compact quadratic barrier $B(\boldsymbol{x}) = \delta^2\|E\|^2 - \|P\|^2$ that exactly separates winning configurations for a faster 3D pursuer against a planar evader heading to a static target. Cite it when motivating 3D-vs-2D reach-avoid/TAD formulations (UAV vs ground vehicle, raptor vs terrestrial prey, missile vs tank), when needing a closed-form benchmark or baseline strategy for heterogeneous-space PE games, or when tracing the lineage from planar Apollonius methods [13], [14] toward dimension-generalized isochron geometry; note that formal optimality proofs and the HJI verification reside in its extended version arXiv:2202.13522, so pair the citation with that preprint when the claim is optimality. Suggested citation sentences: "Li, Wang and Xie extended the Apollonius circle to pursuit-evasion games in which a faster pursuer moves in 3D space while a slower evader is confined to a plane, deriving feedback value functions for both pure pursuit and target-defense variants [P052]"; or "For a faster 3D pursuer against a planar evader, the game of kind is exactly partitioned by the barrier $\delta^2\|E\|^2 - \|P\|^2$, and the doomed evader's optimal response is to head for the Apollonius-circle point nearest the target rather than the target itself [P052]."
