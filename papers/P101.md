## 2. Research Background
The paper studies multiplayer pursuit-evasion differential games with simple motion in the plane, in the lineage of Isaacs' lifeline game and modern reach-avoid games. In multi-player PE games a single pursuer may face several evaders, and cooperative pursuit (many-vs-one) has been analysed extensively (Chikrii & Kalashnikova 1987; Zhou et al. 2016; Kothari et al. 2017; Hayoun & Shima 2017), while the *evader-side* cooperation problem — how several slower evaders can jointly maximize the number of survivors against one faster pursuer guarding a goal region — had received much less analytic treatment. HJI reachability solves low-dimensional reach-avoid games exactly but suffers the curse of dimensionality (gridding), and remedies such as linear programming or system decomposition remain structure-specific and inexact; geometric tools (Voronoi diagrams, Apollonius circles, Euclidean shortest paths) instead yield explicit strategy instructions for special game setups. The authors' own prior line (Yan et al., TCYB 2019, TCYB 2020a, TRO 2020b) solved two-pursuer-one-evader reach-avoid games via pairwise barriers; this paper is the mirror problem with the cooperation on the evader side. Stated applications: robot competitions where two slower robots guard a goal against a faster one, collision avoidance where two vehicles reach a target line past a moving obstacle, and maritime border defense of a coastline against two invaders (pp. 1–2).

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
**Problem** (Section 2, pp. 3–4): two evaders $E_1, E_2$ with constant speeds $v_{E1}, v_{E2} > 0$ and one faster pursuer $P$ with speed $v_P > 0$, speed ratios $\alpha_i = v_{Ei}/v_P < 1$, simple motion in $\mathbb{R}^2$ (eq. (1), p. 3). The plane is split by the line $L = \{(x,y)\mid y=0\}$ into play region $\Omega_1 = \{y > 0\}$ and goal region $\Omega_2 = \{y \le 0\}$. Both evaders start in $\Omega_1$ and cooperate to reach $\Omega_2$; $P$ starts anywhere in $\mathbb{R}^2$ (possibly already in $\Omega_2$, cf. simulation Case 2) and wins by point capture (Euclidean distance zero). Once an evader reaches $\Omega_2$ it can no longer be captured; the game ends when no evader remains in $\Omega_1$, with three possible results: both captured, one reaches/one captured, both reach (p. 3). Information structure: nonanticipative (Elliott–Kalton style) — the pursuer chooses its current input knowing all current states *plus the two evaders' current control inputs*; the evaders know all current states and, because $P$ must declare its strategy before they choose inputs, can predict $P$'s response; the authors call the formulation "an instantiation of the Stackelberg game" (p. 3). Objectives: (a) game of kind — determine, for any initial configuration, *how many* evaders can guarantee arrival in $\Omega_2$; (b) game of degree — team goal (maximize survivors) is prioritized over individual goal (if capture is unavoidable, be captured as close to $\Omega_2$ as possible).

**Gap as claimed by the authors** (pp. 2–3): (i) the lifeline-game variations of Dutkevich & Petrosyan (1972), Chen & Sastry (2006) and Kawęcki et al. (2009) treat only one-evader-one-pursuer cases; (ii) Garcia et al. (2019) let two attackers cooperate against a coastline defender but minimize the *combined distance to the border when both are captured* (a degree objective), not the number of survivors; (iii) Shishika et al. (2020) is a perimeter-defense variant with defenders on the boundary; (iv) Yan et al. (2019 TCYB) and Yan et al. (2020a) treat two pursuers and one evader, i.e., cooperation on the *pursuer* side. Hence "the two-evader-one-pursuer scenario considered here makes the analysis more interesting": one must decide when the pursuer can capture both, and design evader cooperation that maximizes survivors, including self-sacrifice as observed between prey animals.

**Verified from cited text**: the cited one-evader lifeline works are indeed 1-vs-1 variants (titles as printed in the reference list confirm: "Games with a 'Life-line'. The case of l-capture"; "Pursuit controller performance guarantees for a lifeline pursuit-evasion game over a wireless sensor network"; "Guarding a line segment"); Yan et al. 2019/2020a are indeed "two defenders and one attacker" titles; Garcia et al. 2019 is "Strategies for defending a coastline against multiple attackers" (CDC 2019). The gap claim is consistent with the citation record as printed. Two further authors' claims deserve flags: (a) "this is the first time in current works to formally introduce the concept of δ-Apollonius circle" (p. 7) — a priority claim plausible for the *formal definition plus explicit polar-form computation*, but not independently verifiable here (time-advantage-shifted Apollonius constructions exist implicitly in earlier lifeline-type analyses); (b) the barrier of Lemma 3.3 is explicitly credited to their own Yan et al. (2020b, TRO), with only "a simpler proof" contributed here — i.e., the single-evader no-time-difference barrier is *not* claimed as new.

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Pursuit strategy (theory)**: the evader dominance region compression (EDRC) strategy — Lemma 3.1 proves that if $P$ always runs toward the intersection of the current Apollonius circle $\mathcal{A}_i$ with the ray from $E_i$ along $E_i$'s current heading, the new Apollonius circle is internally tangent to the old one, so $E_i$'s dominance region strictly shrinks until capture (p. 4–5, eqs. (4)–(10)); Theorem 3.2 gives the *unique* closed-form feedback realization (p. 5, eq. (11)).
2. **Time-shifted barrier machinery (theory)**: Definition 4.1 introduces the δ-Apollonius circle $\mathcal{A}_i^\delta$ (locus reachable by $P$ exactly $\delta$ earlier than $E_i$), Lemma 4.2 computes it explicitly in polar coordinates (p. 7, eqs. (18)–(23)); Theorem 4.3 constructs the barrier and the two winning regions of a 1-vs-1 reach-avoid game with evasion head start $\delta>0$ in parametric form (p. 8, eqs. (24)–(26)) — the tool that handles the *second-pursued* evader.
3. **Game of kind for 2-vs-1 (theory)**: classification of how many evaders reach $\Omega_2$ from the one-vs-one barriers (p. 9, three-case list), and Theorem 5.4: when both evaders are capturable, $E_2$ (second-pursued) escapes iff $\mathbf{x}_2 \in \mathcal{Q}_E$, a union over $E_1$'s possible capture points on $\mathcal{A}_1$ of time-shifted evader winning regions (pp. 10–11, eqs. (38)–(39)).
4. **Cooperative aiding strategy (modeling + theory)**: payoff $J_2 = h_2(t_{f1})$ (the second evader's Apollonius-circle clearance to $L$ at the instant $E_1$ is captured), Lemma 5.3 (straight-line optimal trajectories), and Theorem 5.5: explicit optimal aiding strategies $(\mathbf{u}_1^*, \mathbf{u}_2^*)$ characterized by three coupled trigonometric equations (42a)–(42c) (pp. 12–13) — the formal "one evader sacrifices itself for the teammate" mechanism.
5. **Algorithm (extension)**: Algorithm 1 (p. 15) lifts the two-evader results to $N_e$ evaders vs. one pursuer (win/lose sorting, aiding pairing, individual-goal fallback).
6. **Experiments**: four initial configurations validating each regime of the classification (pp. 14–16, Figures 8–10).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results (MOST IMPORTANT — exhaustive)
Throughout, bold $\mathbf{x}$ denotes position vectors; $\|\cdot\|_2$ the Euclidean norm; $\alpha_i = v_{Ei}/v_P \in (0,1)$; $\mathbf{u}_i = (\cos\psi_i, \sin\psi_i)^\top$, $\mathbf{u}_P = (\cos\phi, \sin\phi)^\top$.

### 5.1 Dynamics, information structure, termination (p. 3, eq. (1))
- **Dynamics (eq. (1))**: simple motion for all three players, $\dot x_1 = v_{E1}\cos\psi_1,\ \dot y_1 = v_{E1}\sin\psi_1$; $\dot x_2 = v_{E2}\cos\psi_2,\ \dot y_2 = v_{E2}\sin\psi_2$; $\dot x_P = v_P\cos\phi,\ \dot y_P = v_P\sin\phi$, with initial states $(x_{10}, y_{10})$, $(x_{20}, y_{20})$, $(x_{P0}, y_{P0})$ and control constraint $U = \{u \mid u \in [0, 2\pi)\}$ (instantaneous headings). The two evaders choose $(\psi_1, \psi_2)$ *jointly* (team).
- **Winning/termination conditions** (pp. 3–4): capture = zero Euclidean distance (point capture; non-zero radius deferred to Yan, Duan et al. 2019 / Yan, Shi et al. 2019); an evader wins locally by reaching $\Omega_2$ (crossing $L$); game terminates when no evader remains in $\Omega_1$; evader in $\Omega_2$ is immune. Assumption: $P$ faster, $\alpha_i < 1$ for $i = 1, 2$.
- **Information structure** (p. 3): nonanticipative — $P$'s current input may depend on all current states **and the evaders' current inputs**; evaders know all current states and the declared strategy; Assumption 5.2 (p. 9) additionally lets the evaders know *which* of them is currently pursued. Information flow (p. 9–10): evaders learn the pursuit target → evaders compute inputs → pursuer applies EDRC. This asymmetry is what makes the sequential (chase-order) analysis well-posed.

### 5.2 Apollonius circle and maps (p. 4, eqs. (2)–(3))
- Maps $\eta_i(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}) = \dfrac{\mathbf{x} - \alpha_i^2 \mathbf{y}}{1-\alpha_i^2}$ (center) and $r_i(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}) = \dfrac{\alpha_i\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|_2}{1-\alpha_i^2}$ (radius), unnumbered, p. 4.
- **Apollonius circle** (eq. (2)–(3)): $\mathcal{A}_i = \{\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^2 \mid \|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_i\|_2 = \alpha_i \|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_P\|_2\} = \{\mathbf{x} \mid \|\mathbf{x} - \eta_i(\mathbf{x}_i,\mathbf{x}_P)\|_2 = r_i(\mathbf{x}_i,\mathbf{x}_P)\}$ — the set of points reachable by $E_i$ and $P$ in equal minimal time. Interior = $E_i$'s dominance region, exterior = $P$'s (Isaacs 1967). Verified by completing the square (printed derivation between (2) and (3), p. 4).

### 5.3 Lemma 3.1 (EDRC pursuit strategy) (p. 4–5, eqs. (4)–(10))
- **Statement**: if $P$ moves toward $\mathbf{x}_i^*$, the intersection of $\mathcal{A}_i$ with the ray from $\mathbf{x}_i$ along $E_i$'s current heading, then $E_i$'s dominance region strictly decreases until capture.
- **Proof approach**: direct kinematics over an infinitesimal $\Delta t$. New positions (eq. (4)–(5)); new center (eq. (6)) $\eta_i(\mathbf{x}_i',\mathbf{x}_P') = \eta_i(\mathbf{x}_i,\mathbf{x}_P) + \frac{v_P \Delta t\,[(1-\alpha_i^2)\mathbf{x}_i^* - \mathbf{x}_i + \alpha_i^2\mathbf{x}_P]}{(1-\alpha_i^2)\|\mathbf{x}_i^*-\mathbf{x}_P\|_2}$; new radius (eq. (7)) $r_i(\mathbf{x}_i',\mathbf{x}_P') = r_i(\mathbf{x}_i,\mathbf{x}_P) - \frac{v_P \Delta t\, \alpha_i \|\mathbf{x}_i-\mathbf{x}_P\|_2}{(1-\alpha_i^2)\|\mathbf{x}_i^*-\mathbf{x}_P\|_2}$.
- **Decisive equation (eq. (10))**: using the tangency condition (8) and the norm identity (9), $\|\eta_i(\mathbf{x}_i',\mathbf{x}_P') - \eta_i(\mathbf{x}_i,\mathbf{x}_P)\|_2 = r_i(\mathbf{x}_i,\mathbf{x}_P) - r_i(\mathbf{x}_i',\mathbf{x}_P')$, i.e., center displacement equals the radius loss; moreover $\|\mathbf{x}_i^* - \mathbf{x}_i'\|_2 = \alpha_i\|\mathbf{x}_i^* - \mathbf{x}_P'\|_2$ so $\mathbf{x}_i^* \in \mathcal{A}_i'$. Hence $\mathcal{A}_i'$ is an **inscribed circle of $\mathcal{A}_i$ tangent at $\mathbf{x}_i^*$** (Fig. 2, p. 4). Analytic, exact, global in the plane; radius decay rate $\propto \alpha_i\|\mathbf{x}_i-\mathbf{x}_P\|_2 / \|\mathbf{x}_i^*-\mathbf{x}_P\|_2$ depends on the evader's chosen heading.
- **Physical meaning**: the pursuer "shadows" the evader's intended Apollonius point, so the point set the evader can still defend against capture shrinks monotonically — the pursuit analogue of a shrinking safe region, and the mechanism that makes the *second-phase* game (Theorem 5.4) a clean 1-vs-1 subgame starting at a predictable capture point.
- **Extensibility**: works for any $\alpha_i<1$ and any evader heading; the same identity underlies the TRO 2020b barrier analysis.

### 5.4 Theorem 3.2 (unique feedback pursuit strategy) (p. 5, eq. (11))
- **Statement**: the feedback strategy for $P$ guaranteeing strict shrinkage of $E_i$'s dominance region regardless of $E_i$'s input is unique and equals $\mathbf{u}_P = \frac{\mathbf{x}_i^* - \mathbf{x}_P}{\|\mathbf{x}_i^*-\mathbf{x}_P\|_2}$ with
$$\mathbf{x}_i^* = \mathbf{x}_i + \frac{\alpha_i^2 (\mathbf{x}_i - \mathbf{x}_P)^\top \mathbf{u}_i}{1-\alpha_i^2}\,\mathbf{u}_i + \frac{\alpha_i\sqrt{\alpha_i^2\left((\mathbf{x}_i-\mathbf{x}_P)^\top\mathbf{u}_i\right)^2 + (1-\alpha_i^2)\|\mathbf{x}_i-\mathbf{x}_P\|_2^2}}{1-\alpha_i^2}\,\mathbf{u}_i. \tag{11}$$
- **Assumptions**: simple motion, $\alpha_i<1$, current states and $E_i$'s current heading $\mathbf{u}_i$ measurable (given by the information structure).
- **Proof approach**: uniqueness is argued game-theoretically — if $P$ picked any point of $\mathcal{A}_i$ other than $\mathbf{x}_i^*$, $E_i$ could reach $\mathbf{x}_i^*$ first and $\mathbf{x}_i^*$ would remain in $E_i$'s dominance region (p. 5). The formula follows from the intersection conditions (12)–(13): $\|\mathbf{x}_i^* - \eta_i(\mathbf{x}_i,\mathbf{x}_P)\|_2 = r_i$ and $(\mathbf{x}_i^*-\mathbf{x}_i)/\|\mathbf{x}_i^*-\mathbf{x}_i\|_2 = \mathbf{u}_i$, reduced through (14)–(15) to a quadratic in $\|\mathbf{x}_i^*-\mathbf{x}_i\|_2$ whose positive root gives (11). Pure state feedback; no memory.
- **Physical meaning**: an explicit Apollonius-point-shadowing law — a drop-in replacement for greedy/line-of-sight pursuit that provably compresses the evader's safe set.
- **proof gap (minor, informal uniqueness)**: the uniqueness argument presumes "after a small time interval, $E_i$ can reach $\mathbf{x}_i^*$ before $P$, regardless of $P$'s following strategy" (p. 5); this infinitesimalreachability claim is asserted, not formalized (no value-function argument that EDRC is payoff-optimal, only that it is the unique strategy preventing dominance-region growth). Sufficient for the paper's use but not a rigorous optimality proof.

### 5.5 Remark 3.1 (confinement) (p. 6)
By EDRC, $P$ captures $E_i$ **inside the initial** $\mathcal{A}_i$; consequently, if at any moment $\mathcal{A}_i \cap \Omega_2 = \varnothing$, capture before reaching $\Omega_2$ is guaranteed. This is the bridge from a dominance-set statement to the reach-avoid outcome.

### 5.6 Lemma 3.3 (barrier without time difference) (p. 6, eqs. (16)–(17)) — credited to Yan et al. (2020b, TRO); new simpler proof here
- **Statement**: for fixed pursuer position $\mathbf{x}_P$, with both starting simultaneously, the barrier is
$$\mathcal{B}_i(\mathbf{x}_P) = \Big\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^2 \ \Big|\ (1/\alpha_i^2 - 1)y^2 - (x - x_P)^2 - (1-\alpha_i^2)y_P^2 = 0,\ y > 0 \Big\}. \tag{16}$$
- **Assumptions**: 1-vs-1, $\alpha_i<1$, point capture, straight-line boundary $L$; analysis restricted to the 2-D slice for the given $\mathbf{x}_P$ (p. 6, "two-dimensional slices of the winning regions").
- **Proof approach**: on the barrier, under optimal play $E_i$ is captured exactly on $L$; then $\mathcal{A}_i$ must be **tangent to $L$** (Fig. 3(a)), i.e., the circle's lowest point touches $y=0$: eq. (17) $\frac{y_i - \alpha_i^2 y_P}{1-\alpha_i^2} = \frac{\alpha_i\|\mathbf{x}_i-\mathbf{x}_P\|_2}{1-\alpha_i^2} \Rightarrow (1/\alpha_i^2-1)y_i^2 - (x_i-x_P)^2 - (1-\alpha_i^2)y_P^2 = 0$ (squaring verified to reproduce (16) exactly). $y_i>0$ because $E_i$ stays in $\Omega_1$ until termination.
- **Physical meaning**: (16) is the upper branch of a hyperbola (since $1/\alpha_i^2 - 1 > 0$); Remark 3.2 + Fig. 3(b): $\mathcal{W}_{E,i}(\mathbf{x}_P)$ (evader winning region) lies between $\mathcal{B}_i$ and $L$, $\mathcal{W}_{P,i}(\mathbf{x}_P)$ (pursuer winning region) above it; on $\mathcal{B}_i$ capture occurs exactly at $L$ under optimal play. Within $\mathcal{W}_{P,i}$ the EDRC strategy (Theorem 3.2 + Remark 3.1) certifies capture; inside $\mathcal{W}_{E,i}$, $E_i$ runs straight to a point of $\mathcal{A}_i \cap L$.
- **proof gap (mild)**: the proof establishes *necessity* (positions on the barrier ⟹ tangency) and cites Isaacs' qualitative maxim for the semipermeable "no player can win" property, rather than constructing the pair of strategies proving that the tangency set is exactly the separatrix (the $E_i$-side argument via Remark 3.1 and the $P$-side via EDRC do cover both regions, so the conclusion is sound, but the boundary case itself is argued, not proven).

### 5.7 Definition 4.1 + Lemma 4.2 (δ-Apollonius circle) (p. 7, eqs. (18)–(23))
- **Definition (eq. (18))**: $\mathcal{A}_i^\delta = \{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^2 \mid \frac{\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_i\|_2}{v_{Ei}} = \frac{\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_P\|_2}{v_P} + \delta\} = \{\mathbf{x} \mid \|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_i\|_2 = \alpha_i\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_P\|_2 + v_{Ei}\delta\}$ — points that $P$ reaches exactly $\delta>0$ sooner than $E_i$; $\mathcal{A}_i = \mathcal{A}_i^0$. Practical reading: $P$ starts pursuing $E_i$ only after $E_i$ has evaded for time $\delta$ (the head start created by the teammate's sacrifice). Authors' priority claim: first formal introduction of this object.
- **Lemma 4.2 (explicit polar form, eq. (19))**: with origin at $\mathbf{x}_i$, $\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{x}_i + \rho\mathbf{e}$, $\mathbf{e} = (\cos\theta,\sin\theta)^\top$,
$$\rho = \frac{c + v_{Ei}\delta}{1-\alpha_i^2} + \frac{\sqrt{(c+v_{Ei}\delta)^2 - (1-\alpha_i^2)\left(v_{Ei}^2\delta^2 - \alpha_i^2\|\mathbf{x}_i-\mathbf{x}_P\|_2^2\right)}}{1-\alpha_i^2},\qquad c = \alpha_i^2(\mathbf{x}_i-\mathbf{x}_P)^\top\mathbf{e}.$$
- **Proof approach**: substitute into (18) to get (20): $(\rho - v_{Ei}\delta)^2 = \alpha_i^2\|\mathbf{x}_i-\mathbf{x}_P + \rho\mathbf{e}\|_2^2,\ \rho \ge v_{Ei}\delta$; solve the quadratic (21) and exclude the minus root via (22)–(23): the constraint $\rho - v_{Ei}\delta \ge 0$ forces $-\alpha_i(c + \alpha_i^2 v_{Ei}\delta) \le +\sqrt{(c+\alpha_i^2 v_{Ei}\delta)^2 + (1-\alpha_i^2)(\alpha_i^4\|\mathbf{x}_i-\mathbf{x}_P\|_2^2 - c^2)}$, which **fails for the negative sign by Cauchy–Schwarz** ($\alpha_i^4\|\mathbf{x}_i-\mathbf{x}_P\|_2^2 \ge c^2$) — the decisive inequality. Analytic, global.
- **Physical meaning**: the head start inflates the circle by $v_{Ei}\delta$ in the metric of (18); the circle is no longer centered on segment $\mathbf{x}_i\mathbf{x}_P$'s Apollonius center, and its shape/feasible directions depend on $\delta$ — the geometric accounting of "time bought by the teammate".

### 5.8 Theorem 4.3 (barrier and winning regions with time difference) (p. 8–9, eqs. (24)–(35)) — core new machinery
- **Statement**: suppose $E_i$ evades before $P$ pursues it with time difference $\delta > 0$. Given $\mathbf{x}_P$, the barrier is the parametric curve
$$\mathcal{B}_i^\delta(\mathbf{x}_P) = \Big\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^2 \mid x = x^* - ab,\ y = a\sqrt{1-b^2},\ x^*\in\mathcal{P}\Big\}, \tag{24}$$
$$a = \alpha_i\|\mathbf{x}^* - \mathbf{x}_P\|_2 + v_{Ei}\delta,\qquad b = \frac{\alpha_i(x^*-x_P)}{\|\mathbf{x}^*-\mathbf{x}_P\|_2},\qquad \mathbf{x}^* = (x^*,0)^\top, \tag{25}$$
with feasible set $\mathcal{P} \subseteq \mathbb{R}$: if $\delta \le \frac{(1-\alpha_i^2)|y_P|}{\alpha_i v_{Ei}}$ then $\mathcal{P} = \mathbb{R}$; if $\delta > \frac{(1-\alpha_i^2)|y_P|}{\alpha_i v_{Ei}}$ then
$$\mathcal{P} = \Big\{x \in \mathbb{R} \ \Big|\ |x - x_P| \ge \sqrt{\Big(\frac{\alpha_i v_{Ei}\delta}{1-\alpha_i^2}\Big)^2 - y_P^2} \Big\}. \tag{26}$$
The corresponding winning regions $\mathcal{W}_{E,i}^\delta, \mathcal{W}_{P,i}^\delta$ are separated by $\mathcal{B}_i^\delta$ (Fig. 4, p. 8; for large $\delta$ the barrier splits into two symmetric branches and a vertical window above $P$ opens through which $E_i$ escapes even directly "over" the pursuer).
- **Assumptions**: 1-vs-1 reach-avoid, simple motion, $\alpha_i<1$, point capture, straight goal line; $E_i$ starts at $\mathbf{x}_i \in \Omega_1$ at time $t$, $P$ starts at $\mathbf{x}_P$ at $t+\delta$.
- **Proof approach** (Isaacs' method + static reduction): terminal payoff at the first entry time $t_1 = \min\{t \mid \mathbf{x}_i(t)\in L\}$: $J_1 = \|\mathbf{x}_i(t_1) - \mathbf{x}_P(t_1)\|_2$ (eq. (27)); value $V_1 = \max_{\mathbf{u}_i}\min_{\mathbf{u}_P} J_1$ (evader maximizes terminal clearance, pursuer minimizes). Hamiltonian (eq. (28)); terminal-type payoff + simple motion ⟹ constant optimal headings, straight-line trajectories (Isaacs 1967). Since both run toward the same goal point $\mathbf{x} = (x,0)$ on $L$, $J_1 = F(x) = \|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_P\|_2 - v_P\left(\frac{\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_i\|_2}{v_{Ei}} - \delta\right)$ (eq. (29)). **Decisive equations**: the barrier condition $F(x^*) = 0$, i.e. $\|\mathbf{x}^*-\mathbf{x}_i\|_2 = \alpha_i\|\mathbf{x}^*-\mathbf{x}_P\|_2 + v_{Ei}\delta$ (eq. (31)), combined with the stationarity condition $\frac{\mathrm{d}F}{\mathrm{d}x}(x^*) = 0 \Rightarrow \frac{\mathbf{x}^*-\mathbf{x}_P}{\|\mathbf{x}^*-\mathbf{x}_P\|_2} = \frac{\mathbf{x}^*-\mathbf{x}_i}{\alpha_i\|\mathbf{x}^*-\mathbf{x}_i\|_2}$ (eq. (30)), yields the parametrization $\mathbf{x}_i = \mathbf{x}^* - ab$, $y_i = a\sqrt{1-b^2}$ (eqs. (32)–(33), from $a^2b^2 + y_i^2 = a^2$). The feasible set follows from symmetry of $\mathcal{B}_i^\delta$ about $x = x_P$ and the inequality (34)–(35): $(1-\alpha_i^2)\|\mathbf{x}^*-\mathbf{x}_P\|_2 \ge \alpha_i v_{Ei}\delta$, which for $\delta$ above the threshold $\frac{(1-\alpha_i^2)|y_P|}{\alpha_i v_{Ei}}$ excludes goal points too close to $x_P$ (giving (26)).
- **Local/global, analytic/numerical**: analytic, global in the slice, exact; evaluation of $\mathcal{Q}_E$ later requires a one-dimensional parameter sweep (Remark 5.2), the only quasi-numerical step.
- **Physical meaning**: the head start $\delta$ bends the barrier away from $L$ — every unit of $\delta$ converts directly into extra escape geometry; the two-regime threshold (26) says that if the head start is large relative to the pursuer's depth $|y_P|$, no goal point near the pursuer's abscissa can be defended.
- **Extensibility**: plug-and-play for any 1-vs-1 subgame with a known start-time offset; used verbatim in Theorem 5.4 with $\mathbf{x}_P$ replaced by the teammate's sacrifice point.

### 5.9 Game-of-kind classification and Section-5 machinery (pp. 9–10)
- **Classification list (p. 9, unnumbered, from Lemma 3.3 + Remark 3.2)**: with $m$ = number of evaders guaranteed to reach $\Omega_2$ *irrespective of* $P$'s strategy: (i) $m = 2$ iff $\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathcal{W}_{E,i}(\mathbf{x}_P)$ for $i = 1, 2$; (ii) if $\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathcal{W}_{P,i}(\mathbf{x}_P)\cup\mathcal{B}_i(\mathbf{x}_P)$ and $\mathbf{x}_j \in \mathcal{W}_{E,j}(\mathbf{x}_P)$, then $m = 1$ ($E_i$ captured in $\Omega_1$ or at $L$, $E_j$ reaches $\Omega_2$); (iii) if $\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathcal{W}_{P,i}(\mathbf{x}_P)\cup\mathcal{B}_i(\mathbf{x}_P)$ for $i = 1,2$, then $m \le 1$ — the hard case resolved by Theorem 5.4. Remark 5.1 assigns strategies per case: (i) both head to points of $\mathcal{A}_i\cap L$; (ii) $E_j$ goes to $\Omega_2$, $E_i$ plays the *individual goal* — aim at the point of $\mathcal{A}_i$ closest to $\Omega_2$; (iii) aiding strategy needed.
- **Assumption 5.1 (pursuit strategy, pp. 9–10)**: $P$ always uses EDRC; chase order: (i) both evaders in own winning regions → random order; (ii) exactly one capturable → pursue it first; (iii) both capturable → random order. $P$ is assumed rational (pursues an evader it is *sure* to capture first, p. 9).
- **Assumption 5.2 (p. 9)**: evaders know who is currently pursued (the fairness-compensating communication assumption).
- **Clearance variable (eq. (36), p. 10)**: minimal $y$-coordinate of points on $\mathcal{A}_i$: $h_i = y_i - \frac{\alpha_i^2 y_P}{1-\alpha_i^2} - \frac{\alpha_i\|\mathbf{x}_i-\mathbf{x}_P\|_2}{1-\alpha_i^2}$ (= center height minus radius). In the both-capturable case $h_i \ge 0$ for $i=1,2$ (circles do not dip into $\Omega_2$).
- **Aiding-game payoff (eq. (37), p. 10)**: terminal set $\mathcal{T} = \{(\mathbf{x}_1,\mathbf{x}_2,\mathbf{x}_P)\mid \mathbf{x}_1 = \mathbf{x}_P\}$, terminal time $t_{f1}$ (capture instant of $E_1$), payoff $J_2 = h_2(t_{f1}) = \bar y_2 - \frac{\alpha_2^2 \bar y_P}{1-\alpha_2^2} - \frac{\alpha_2\|\bar{\mathbf{x}}_2 - \bar{\mathbf{x}}_P\|_2}{1-\alpha_2^2}$, value $V_2 = \min_{\mathbf{u}_1,\mathbf{u}_2}\max_{\mathbf{u}_P} J_2$; the evader part $(\mathbf{u}_1^*,\mathbf{u}_2^*)$ of an equilibrium is the **optimal aiding strategy**. Interpretation: $E_1$ sacrifices itself to minimize how deep $P$'s threat reaches toward $L$ against $E_2$ at the capture instant; $h_2(t_{f1}) < 0 \Rightarrow E_2$ escapes, $h_2(t_{f1}) \ge 0 \Rightarrow E_2$ captured (in $\Omega_1$ or at $L$).
- **Lemma 5.3 (p. 10)**: in the game (1)+(37) the optimal headings of all three players are constant and optimal trajectories are straight lines (Isaacs' method; terminal-type payoff + simple motion). One-line proof, by citation.
- **Cooperation mechanism (synthesis)**: the two-evader cooperation is *not* a dominance-region partition between the evaders; it is a **time-credit transfer with geometric steering**: (a) $E_1$ selects its own capture point $\mathbf{x}_1^* \in \mathcal{A}_1$ (thereby its sacrifice duration $t_E = \|\mathbf{x}_1^*-\mathbf{x}_1\|_2/v_{E1}$), (b) $E_2$ meanwhile runs to the best reachable position, so that at $t_{f1}$ the pair $(\mathbf{x}_2, \mathbf{x}_1^*)$ defines a δ-Apollonius geometry with $\delta = t_E$ for the second phase. The team-level classification then reduces to the single-evader δ-barrier of Theorem 4.3, unioned over all sacrifice points.

### 5.10 Theorem 5.4 (game of kind for two capturable evaders) (pp. 10–11, eqs. (38)–(39))
- **Statement**: assume $\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathcal{W}_{P,i}(\mathbf{x}_P)\cup\mathcal{B}_i(\mathbf{x}_P)$ for $i=1,2$ and (wlog) $P$ pursues $E_1$ first. Then $E_2$ can reach $\Omega_2$ without capture **iff** $\mathbf{x}_2 \in \mathcal{Q}_E$; $P$ can guarantee capturing both before they reach the *interior* of $\Omega_2$ **iff** $\mathbf{x}_2 \in \mathcal{Q}_P$, where
$$\mathcal{Q}_E = \Big\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^2 \mid x = x_2^* - ab,\ 0 < y < a\sqrt{1-b^2},\ a = \alpha_2\|\mathbf{x}_2^*-\mathbf{x}_1^*\|_2 + \|\mathbf{x}_1-\mathbf{x}_1^*\|_2,\ b = \frac{\alpha_2(x_2^*-x_1^*)}{\|\mathbf{x}_2^*-\mathbf{x}_1^*\|_2},\ \mathbf{x}_1^* = (x_1^*,y_1^*)\in\mathcal{A}_1,\ \mathbf{x}_2^* = (x_2^*,0),\ x_2^*\in\mathcal{P}\Big\},\qquad \mathcal{Q}_P = \Omega_1\setminus\mathcal{Q}_E, \tag{38}$$
with feasible set for $x_2^*$: if $\|\mathbf{x}_1-\mathbf{x}_1^*\|_2 \le \frac{(1-\alpha_2^2)v_{E1}|y_1^*|}{\alpha_2 v_{E2}}$ then $\mathcal{P} = \mathbb{R}$, else $\mathcal{P} = \{x\in\mathbb{R} \mid |x - x_1^*| \ge \sqrt{(\frac{\alpha_2 v_{E2}\|\mathbf{x}_1-\mathbf{x}_1^*\|_2}{(1-\alpha_2^2)v_{E1}})^2 - y_1^{*2}}\}$. \tag{39}
- **Assumptions**: both capturable; chase order $E_1 \to E_2$; $P$ plays EDRC (Assumption 5.1); the classification is order-dependent (Fig. 6(a) vs (b) draw the two orders).
- **Proof approach**: (1) $E_1$ is captured at its chosen goal point $\mathbf{x}_1^* \in \mathcal{A}_1(t)$ ($\bar{\mathbf{x}}_1 = \bar{\mathbf{x}}_P = \mathbf{x}_1^*$); (2) at the critical case $h_2(t_{f1}) = 0$, $\mathcal{A}_2(t_{f1})$ is tangent to $L$ at $\mathbf{x}_2^* = (x_2^*,0)$ and both $E_2$ and $P$ must run straight to $\mathbf{x}_2^*$ on $(t_{f1}, t_{f2}]$ — deviating lets the other side win (straightness of $E_2$'s whole trajectory $[t, t_{f2}]$ is proven by contradiction: aiming at $\mathbf{x}_2^*$ earlier gives $\|\mathbf{x}_2' - \mathbf{x}_2^*\|_2 < \|\bar{\mathbf{x}}_2 - \mathbf{x}_2^*\|_2 \Rightarrow h_2(t_{f1}) < 0$, contradicting optimality at $t_{f1}$); (3) regard $E_2$ as evading from $\mathbf{x}_2$ against a pursuer starting at $\mathbf{x}_1^*$ with **time difference $\delta = \|\mathbf{x}_1-\mathbf{x}_1^*\|_2/v_{E1}$** — "the evasion time that $E_1$ creates for $E_2$ by sacrificing itself" (p. 11); (4) by Theorem 4.3, $\mathbf{x}_2 \in \mathcal{B}_2^\delta(\mathbf{x}_1^*)$ on the critical surface, and $E_2$ wins iff $\mathbf{x}_2 \in \mathcal{W}_{E,2}^\delta(\mathbf{x}_1^*)$; (5) since the team may choose any $\mathbf{x}_1^* \in \mathcal{A}_1$, $\mathcal{Q}_E = \bigcup_{\mathbf{x}_1^*\in\mathcal{A}_1}\mathcal{W}_{E,2}^\delta(\mathbf{x}_1^*)$, which evaluates to (38)–(39) by (24)–(26).
- **Remark 5.2 (p. 11)**: constructive sweep to plot $\mathcal{Q}_E$: for each $\mathbf{x}_1^*\in\mathcal{A}_1$ compute $\mathcal{P}$, sweep $x_2^*\in\mathcal{P}$, plot $(x, y)$ from (38) to get a subregion of $\Omega_1$; the union over $\mathbf{x}_1^*$ is $\mathcal{Q}_E$ (Fig. 6, orange splitting curve).
- **Analytic/global**: exact and global on the 2-D slice given $(\mathbf{x}_1, \mathbf{x}_P)$ and the chase order; the union makes $\mathcal{Q}_E$ a 1-parameter family of curves thickened into a region.
- **Physical meaning**: $\mathcal{Q}_E$ is the set of teammate positions that the *sacrificing* evader can rescue: $E_1$'s sacrifice buys both the time head start $\delta$ *and* drags $P$'s second-phase start point $\mathbf{x}_1^*$ along $\mathcal{A}_1$; the team optimizes over both.
- **proof gap (equation inconsistency, eq. (38) vs (39)/(24))**: applying Theorem 4.3 with $i=2$ and $\delta = \|\mathbf{x}_1-\mathbf{x}_1^*\|_2/v_{E1}$ gives $a = \alpha_2\|\mathbf{x}_2^*-\mathbf{x}_1^*\|_2 + v_{E2}\delta = \alpha_2\|\mathbf{x}_2^*-\mathbf{x}_1^*\|_2 + \frac{v_{E2}}{v_{E1}}\|\mathbf{x}_1-\mathbf{x}_1^*\|_2$, but printed (38) has $a = \alpha_2\|\mathbf{x}_2^*-\mathbf{x}_1^*\|_2 + \|\mathbf{x}_1-\mathbf{x}_1^*\|_2$ — the factor $v_{E2}/v_{E1}$ is missing, while the threshold in (39) *does* retain the ratio (it is exactly the $\delta$-threshold of Theorem 4.3 rewritten). (38) and (39) are thus mutually consistent only for $v_{E1} = v_{E2}$ (which happens to hold in all simulations, $\alpha_1 = \alpha_2 = 0.6$). Treat (38) as correct up to the factor $v_{E2}/v_{E1}$ on the second term when speeds differ.

### 5.11 Theorem 5.5 (optimal aiding strategies) (pp. 12–13, eqs. (40)–(43))
- **Statement**: under the premises of Theorem 5.4 (both capturable, $P$ pursues $E_1$ first), the optimal aiding strategy and $P$'s optimal pursuit are
$$\mathbf{u}_i^* = \frac{\mathbf{x}_i^* - \mathbf{x}_i}{\|\mathbf{x}_i^*-\mathbf{x}_i\|_2},\ i = 1,2;\qquad \mathbf{u}_P^* = \frac{\mathbf{x}^*-\mathbf{x}_P}{\|\mathbf{x}^*-\mathbf{x}_P\|_2}, \tag{40}$$
where $\mathbf{x}^*$ is the EDRC target (11) with $i=1$ (so $P$'s input is its EDRC response to $E_1$'s heading), and
$$\mathbf{x}_1^* = \frac{\mathbf{x}_1 - \alpha_1^2\mathbf{x}_P}{1-\alpha_1^2} + \frac{\alpha_1\|\mathbf{x}_1-\mathbf{x}_P\|_2}{1-\alpha_1^2}(\cos\theta_1, \sin\theta_1)^\top,\qquad \mathbf{x}_2^* = \mathbf{x}_2 + (\cos\psi_2, \sin\psi_2)^\top, \tag{41}$$
i.e., $\mathbf{x}_1^*$ is the point of $\mathcal{A}_1$ at polar angle $\theta_1$ from its center $\mathbf{x}_C = \eta_1(\mathbf{x}_1,\mathbf{x}_P)$ (radius $l = r_1(\mathbf{x}_1,\mathbf{x}_P)$), and only $E_2$'s direction $\psi_2 \in (\pi, 2\pi)$ (downward, toward $\Omega_2$) matters. The pair $(\theta_1, \psi_2)$ is a pair of real solutions of three coupled equations in $(\theta_1, \psi_2, \omega)$, where $\omega$ is the polar angle of $\mathbf{x}_2^*-\mathbf{x}_1^*$:
$$\cos\psi_2 = \alpha_2\sin(\omega - \psi_2), \tag{42a}$$
$$|\cos\psi_2 + \alpha_2 k\sin\theta_1| = |\alpha_2\cos\theta_1|\sqrt{1-k^2}, \tag{42b}$$
$$s_2\sin(\omega-\gamma_2) + l\sin(\theta_1-\omega) + m\cos\psi_2 = 0, \tag{42c}$$
with (eq. (43)) $k = \frac{s_P\sin(\theta_1-\gamma_1)}{m}$, $s_2 = \|\mathbf{x}_2-\mathbf{x}_C\|_2$, $m = s_P\sqrt{1+\alpha_1^2 - 2\alpha_1\cos(\gamma_1-\theta_1)}$, $s_P = \|\mathbf{x}_P-\mathbf{x}_C\|_2$; $\gamma_1, \gamma_2$ are the polar angles of $\mathbf{x}_1-\mathbf{x}_C$ and $\mathbf{x}_2-\mathbf{x}_C$.
- **Assumptions**: same as Theorem 5.4; all quantities depend only on the three current positions (pure feedback).
- **Proof approach (reduction to 2-D static optimization)**: (1) by Lemma 5.3 all trajectories are straight; $E_1$ is captured at $\mathbf{x}_1^* \in \mathcal{A}_1$ because $P$ plays EDRC; the evasion time created is $t_E = \|\mathbf{x}_1^*-\mathbf{x}_1\|_2/v_{E1}$, so $E_2$'s reachable set before $t_{f1}$ is the disk $\mathcal{R}_2 = \{\mathbf{x} \mid \|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_2\|_2 \le v_{E2}t_E\}$ (eq. (44)); $J_2$ minimal forces $\mathbf{x}_2^*$ onto the *boundary*: $v_{E1}\|\mathbf{x}_2^*-\mathbf{x}_2\|_2 = v_{E2}\|\mathbf{x}_1^*-\mathbf{x}_1\|_2$ (p. 13). (2) Geometry: $s_P = l/\alpha_1$ (eq. (46)) and law of cosines in triangle $(\mathbf{x}_C, \mathbf{x}_1^*, \mathbf{x}_P)$: $m = \|\mathbf{x}_1^*-\mathbf{x}_P\|_2 = s_P\sqrt{1+\alpha_1^2-2\alpha_1\cos(\gamma_1-\theta_1)}$ (eq. (47)); note $\|\mathbf{x}_1^*-\mathbf{x}_1\|_2 = \alpha_1 m$ and $E_2$'s travel distance $\|\mathbf{x}_2^*-\mathbf{x}_2\|_2 = \alpha_2 m$ — both arrive at $t_{f1}$ simultaneously. (3) Payoff rewritten (eq. (48)) $J_2 = y_2^* - \frac{\alpha_2^2 y_1^*}{1-\alpha_2^2} - \frac{\alpha_2\|\mathbf{x}_2^*-\mathbf{x}_1^*\|_2}{1-\alpha_2^2}$ subject to the parametrization (49) ($\mathbf{x}_1^* = \mathbf{x}_C + l(\cos\theta_1,\sin\theta_1)$, $\mathbf{x}_2^* = \mathbf{x}_2 + \alpha_2 m(\cos\psi_2,\sin\psi_2)$), so $V_2 = \min_{\theta_1,\psi_2} J_2$. (4) **Decisive equations**: first-order conditions $\partial J_2/\partial\theta_1 = \partial J_2/\partial\psi_2 = 0$ (eq. (50)) with the derivatives (51)–(52) and $k = s_P\sin(\theta_1-\gamma_1)/m$: the $\psi_2$-condition collapses via $\cos\omega, \sin\omega$ definitions (45) to (54) $\cos\psi_2 = \alpha_2\sin(\omega-\psi_2)$, whence $|\cos\psi_2| \le \alpha_2$ and (42a); Case 1 ($\cos\omega \ne 0$): eliminating $\|\mathbf{x}_2^*-\mathbf{x}_1^*\|_2$ between (53) and (55) and using $\tan\omega$ from (56) gives (58)–(59), i.e., (42b); the geometric closure identity (60) $s_2\sin(\omega-\gamma_2) + l\sin(\theta_1-\omega) + \alpha_2 m\sin(\omega-\psi_2) = 0$ combined with (42a) gives (42c). Case 2 ($\cos\omega = 0$, i.e., $\mathbf{x}_1^* = \mathbf{x}_2^*$): forces $\psi_2 = 3\pi/2$ and $|\cos\theta_1| = |k|$ (eq. (61)), and (42) still holds.
- **Remark 5.3 (p. 14, authors' own caveat)**: (42) is only a **necessary** condition for optimality; the procedure is: enumerate all real solutions $(\theta_1, \psi_2, \omega)$ of (42), substitute each into (48)–(49), and keep the minimizer of $J_2$. Global optimality therefore rests on complete enumeration of roots of a nonlinear trigonometric system.
- **Analytic/global**: closed-form necessary conditions + finite enumeration; no convexity or solution-count theory is given, so uniqueness/existence of the optimizer is open (see §7).
- **Physical meaning**: $E_1$'s sacrifice direction (choice of $\theta_1$ on $\mathcal{A}_1$) and $E_2$'s heading $\psi_2$ are *jointly* optimized: $E_1$ trades its own survival margin against the time credit and the drag on $P$'s second-phase start point, while $E_2$ runs along the boundary of its reachable disk to place itself as close to $\Omega_2$ as possible at the capture instant. If $\mathbf{x}_2\in\mathcal{Q}_E$ this yields $h_2(t_{f1}) < 0$ (rescue succeeds); if $\mathbf{x}_2\in\mathcal{Q}_P$ it still minimizes the capture-depth of $E_2$ (individual goal, degree of the game) — p. 12 explains why the aiding strategy is used in *both* cases.

### 5.12 Algorithm 1 (multi-evader cooperative evasion, p. 15)
Partition $\mathcal{E}$ into $\mathcal{E}_{win} = \{E_i \mid \mathbf{x}_{Ei}\in\mathcal{W}_{E,i}(\mathbf{x}_P)\}$ and $\mathcal{E}_{lose}$; winners head to any point of $L$ inside their current $\mathcal{A}_i$; each pursued loser checks whether some teammate $E_j \in \mathcal{E}_{lose}$ can reach $\Omega_2$ under its help — if yes, the pair adopts the aiding strategy (team goal), otherwise the evader heads to the point of $\mathcal{A}_i$ closest to $\Omega_2$ (individual goal); unpursued non-aiding evaders do the same. Heuristic — no guarantee statement is given for $N_e > 2$ (complexity of multi-way aiding pairings unresolved).

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
**Author-stated**: point capture only, non-zero radius deferred to companion papers (p. 3); 2-D plane with a single straight boundary line; two evaders treated "as a start" for multi-evader cooperation, with Algorithm 1 a heuristic bridge and "future works will involve multiple evaders and pursuers" (pp. 4, 17); barrier/winning-region analysis given on 2-D slices for fixed pursuer position (p. 6), not the full 6-D state space.

**Critical observations (with page/eq.)**:
1. **Eq. (38) inconsistency** (p. 11): $a$ as printed omits the factor $v_{E2}/v_{E1}$ required by the proof's own $\delta = \|\mathbf{x}_1-\mathbf{x}_1^*\|_2/v_{E1}$ plus Theorem 4.3's (25), while the threshold (39) keeps the ratio — mutually consistent only when $v_{E1} = v_{E2}$ (all simulations satisfy this). See §5.10.
2. **Optimality is conditional on Assumptions 5.1–5.2**: $P$'s EDRC is *assumed* (Assumption 5.1), and the min–max reduction $V_2 = \min_{\theta_1,\psi_2} J_2$ (p. 13) silently fixes $P$'s payoff-maximizing response to the EDRC capture-at-goal-point behavior; no proof is given that EDRC maximizes $J_2$ against arbitrary evader teams, nor that the evader pair's strategy is a Nash equilibrium of the full two-evader game (only Stackelberg-style reasoning under the declared information protocol, p. 3).
3. **Informal uniqueness proof** in Theorem 3.2 (p. 5): the "evader can reach $\mathbf{x}_i^*$ within $\Delta t$ regardless of $P$" step is an infinitesimal assertion, not a formal argument (§5.4).
4. **Barrier semipermeability asserted, not constructed** in Lemma 3.3 (p. 6): tangency is shown necessary; the "no player can win on $\mathcal{B}_i$" property is cited from Isaacs rather than proven by exhibiting the crossing-preventing strategy pair on the boundary itself (both adjacent regions *are* covered via Remark 3.1/EDRC, so the conclusion stands).
5. **Theorem 5.5 gives necessary conditions only** (Remark 5.3, p. 14): optimality requires enumerating all real solutions of the trigonometric system (42a)–(42c); existence, finiteness, and multiplicity of solutions are not characterized, and no bound on the enumeration cost is given.
6. **Chase-order dependence**: $\mathcal{Q}_E/\mathcal{Q}_P$ are defined for a *fixed* order (Theorem 5.4 is wlog $E_1$ first); in classification case (iii) Assumption 5.1(iii) lets $P$ randomize the order, so the evader team's guarantee depends on an outcome it does not control; order-robust evader strategies are not analysed (Fig. 6(a) vs (b) merely illustrate both).
7. **Information assumptions are strong**: the pursuer observes the evaders' *current control inputs* (nonanticipative), and the evaders are told who is pursued (Assumption 5.2) — an explicit communication channel whose robustness to delayed/noisy target-identity information is unexplored.
8. **Experiments are illustrative only**: single deterministic runs, no solver/integration details, no quantitative metrics, no comparison to HJI or other baselines (§6); the outcome claims are therefore verified only qualitatively.
9. **Typos in the original**: Assumption 5.1(ii) writes $\mathbf{x}_j \in \mathcal{W}_{E,i}(\mathbf{x}_P)$ where $\mathcal{W}_{E,j}$ is meant (p. 10); Case 1 prints $\mathbf{x}_{P0} = (5.17.2)^\top$ with a missing comma (p. 14); "evsaion time" (p. 11) and "the fact thatNx2" (p. 11, for "$\mathbf{x}_2^*$") — none affect the mathematics.
10. **Scope of the classification**: case (ii) declares $m = 1$ "irrespective of $P$'s strategy" using the *current-position* barriers; the argument implicitly uses that pursuing the doomed $E_i$ first cannot hurt $P$ against $E_j$ (since $E_j$'s region already certifies escape against a direct rush) — sound, but the symmetric subtlety that $P$ might prefer capturing nobody's-guaranteed evader first to split the team is excluded by the rationality assumption (p. 9).

## 8. Citation Value
This is the canonical analytic reference for *evader-side cooperation* in reach-avoid differential games with simple motion: it complements the authors' two-pursuer-one-evader series (Yan et al., TCYB 2019; TCYB 2020a; TRO 2020b) by moving the cooperation to the losing side, and it supplies three reusable primitives — the EDRC feedback pursuit law (eq. (11)), the δ-Apollonius circle and time-shifted barrier (eqs. (18)–(26)), and the sacrifice/aiding equilibrium construction with the team-vs-individual goal hierarchy (eqs. (37)–(43)) — that later works on multi-evader reach-avoid, target defense with multiple intruders, or cooperative perimeter/lifeline games can import wholesale when a faster defender faces several slower intruders and the "how many intruders get through" (game of kind) question is primary. The honest framing to carry into a related-work section: results are exact but conditional on EDRC pursuit, straight-line boundaries, point capture, and full target-identity information, and the multi-evader extension is heuristic. Useful citation sentences: "Yan et al. [P101] showed that in a two-evader-one-pursuer half-plane reach-avoid game the first-pursued evader can sacrifice itself to buy a time advantage $\delta$ for its teammate — captured by the δ-Apollonius circle — and derived the exact region $\mathcal{Q}_E$ of teammate positions that such an aiding strategy rescues." Or, as a gap pointer: "extending the two-evader aiding framework of Yan et al. [P101] to $N$ evaders requires resolving both the chase-order dependence of the $\mathcal{Q}_E/\mathcal{Q}_P$ classification and the enumeration burden of the trigonometric optimality system (42)."
