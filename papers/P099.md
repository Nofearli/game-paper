## 2. Research Background
Multiplayer reach-avoid games — in which an attacking agent tries to reach a target set while pursuers try to capture it — are central to robot soccer interception, harbor/coastline defense, collision avoidance with multiple obstacles, and military confrontation scenarios [intro, p. 1035]. The standard analysis tool, the Hamilton–Jacobi–Isaacs (HJI) formulation [19], is "ideal when the game is low-dimensional, but high dimension makes this approach numerically intractable" and can hardly yield accurate analytical solutions for the proposed game [p. 1035]. Existing alternatives for multiplayer reach-avoid games (path defense + maximum matching [11], fishing-game barrier [21], harbor defense with finite strategy sets [22], HJI-based sequential path planning [23]) are mostly numerical, conservative for the defenders, or preform defenses one by one without defender cooperation [pp. 1035–1036]. Barrier construction (the boundary of the reach-avoid set separating winning regions) has been derived for specific two- and three-player games — differential-drive pursuit [34], fishing game [21], suicidal pedestrian / reversed Homicidal Chauffeur [35], pursuit under flow fields [36], visibility games [37], [38], lion-and-man with obstacle [39] — but not for a two-defender–one-attacker game guarding a line segment in a bounded rectangular domain. This paper fills that gap with a purely analytical construction intended as a benchmark "small block" for decomposing general multiplayer reach-avoid games [pp. 1036, 1044].

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
**Problem** [Sec. II, pp. 1036–1038]: three players — defenders $P_{D1}, P_{D2}$ (equal speed $v_D$) and attacker $P_A$ (speed $v_A$) — move in a closed rectangle $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ with vertices $O=(0,0)$, $m=(0,h)$, $n=(l,h)$; the top edge $\mathcal{T} \subset \partial\Omega$ between $m$ and $n$ is the attack target line (ATL). The attacker wins by reaching $\mathcal{T}$ before capture; the defenders win by intercepting (position coincidence of $P_A$ with at least one defender). Three questions: (1) who wins from a given initial configuration (game of kind); (2) if defenders win, what are saddle-point strategies for payoff (2); (3) if attacker wins, what are strategies for payoff (3) [p. 1038].

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Theory (game of kind)**: closed-form barrier $\mathcal{B}^1$ (one defender: two/three parts, Lemmas 1–2) and $\mathcal{B}^2$ (two defenders: three/five parts, Theorem 2) for speed ratio $\alpha = v_A/v_D \le 1$, built via a new **attack region (AR) method** — the AR $R^i_A$ (points the attacker reaches uncaught) computed from Voronoi diagrams ($\alpha = 1$) and Apollonius circles ($\alpha < 1$), with the barrier obtained by "utilizing the semipermeability of barrier" [p. 1038]. Winning regions follow as upper/lower regions of the barrier (Remark 1, Def. 1).
2. **Theory (which defenders matter)**: necessary and sufficient condition for the barrier to depend on only one defender — $x^0_{D2} \in \mathcal{W}^1_D(x^0_{D1},1) \cup \mathcal{B}^1(x^0_{D1},1)$ (Theorem 1); conversely $x^0_{D2} \in \mathcal{W}^1_A(x^0_{D1},1)$ forces both defenders into the barrier with the cooperative-capture arc $\mathcal{B}^2_3$ through the equidistant point $p_c$ (Theorem 2, eq. (12)). Comparing Theorem 2's barrier with Lemmas 1–2 identifies initial attacker positions capturable **only** by cooperating defenders [Sec. V-B, p. 1044].
3. **Theory (games of degree)**: optimal strategies inside each winning region for practical payoffs — DDR: capture point = closest point of the BAR to $\mathcal{T}$ (Lemma 5, eq. (17)); ADR: safe-distance-on-arrival payoff (3), with the ADR partitioned into fixed-target-point regions (FTPRs) and unfixed-target-point regions (UTPRs) separated by explicit straight-line **strategy change lines (SCLs)** (Theorems 3–4, eq. (18)); the OTP in UTPRs is the unique admissible root of a quartic (FNC (6)), solvable by Ferrari's method [41] (Remark 7).
4. **Modeling/algorithm**: all constructions are analytic with "essentially no computation burden", applicable to real-time/receding-horizon updates (piecewise open-loop ≈ feedback information structure, Sec. V-A), and usable as pairwise-outcome blocks (1v1 and 2v1) inside the maximum-matching framework of [11] to strengthen defender cooperation (Sec. V-B).
5. **Experiment**: none — the paper contains no simulation or hardware experiments; all ten figures are geometric constructions validating the analytic claims.

## 5. Key Mathematical Results (exhaustive)

### 5.0 Setup, dynamics, information, payoffs, termination
- **Dynamics (eq. (1), p. 1036)**: simple motion, $\dot{x}_{Di,1} = v_{Di}\cos\phi_{Di}$, $\dot{x}_{Di,2} = v_{Di}\sin\phi_{Di}$ ($i=1,2$); $\dot{x}_{A,1} = v_A\cos\phi_A$, $\dot{x}_{A,2} = v_A\sin\phi_A$. Controls $\phi_{Di}, \phi_A \in [-\pi,\pi)$; no constraints beyond constant speed; instantaneous heading changes. $v_{D1} = v_{D2} = v_D$; $\alpha = v_A/v_D$; **only $\alpha \le 1$ considered**: "Since the attacker always wins from any given initial positions of the players for $\alpha > 1$, we only consider the case $\alpha \le 1$" [p. 1037] — asserted without proof (see §7).
- **Information structure**: full-state — all players know $\Omega$, $\mathcal{T}$, and exact positions/speeds of everyone at all times [p. 1037]; no knowledge of the opponent's current strategy; strategies are saddle-point (zero-sum Nash) under open-loop info, refreshed in practice as piecewise open-loop with small refresh interval ≈ feedback [Sec. V-A, p. 1044]. In reachability terms the "initial positions" are the current positions of a practical scenario, re-analyzed on-line.
- **Payoffs**: DDR game of degree, eq. (2) p. 1037: $J_T = \min_{p\in\mathcal{T}}\|x_A(t_2)-p\|_2$ ($t_2$ = capture time) — attacker minimizes distance of the capture point to $\mathcal{T}$, defenders maximize it. ADR game of degree, eq. (3) p. 1037: $J_d = \min_{i=1,2}\|x_{Di}(t_1)-x_A(t_1)\|_2$ ($t_1$ = arrival time) — "safe distance on arrival"; attacker maximizes, defenders minimize. Note the paper stresses each player may pick its **own** payoff (not necessarily the opponent's), so strategies need not be mutually consistent [p. 1037].
- **Termination**: attacker reaches $\mathcal{T}$ safely, or capture = coincidence of $P_A$ with at least one defender [p. 1036].
- **AR/BAR objects** (Sec. III-A, p. 1038): $R^1_A(x^0_A,x^0_D,\alpha)$ — attacker's reachable-without-capture set vs. one defender; for $\alpha=1$ it is the Voronoi cell of $P_A$ and $\mathrm{bar}^1$ the bisector line [40]; for $\alpha<1$, $\mathrm{bar}^1$ is the Apollonius circle (Isaacs [19]) and $R^1_A$ its interior. For two defenders: $R^2_A = R^1_A(x^0_A,x^0_{D1},\alpha)\cap R^1_A(x^0_A,x^0_{D2},\alpha)$ for $\alpha<1$; Voronoi construction for $\alpha = 1$ (Fig. 2); $\mathrm{bar}^2 = \partial R^2_A$.
- **Dominance regions from the barrier** (Def. 1, Remark 1, p. 1038): for a curve $\mathcal{L}\subset\Omega$ with $|\mathcal{L}\cap\mathcal{T}|\le 1$ running from the right to the left side of $\Omega$, $\mathcal{R}_u(\mathcal{L})$ (containing $m$ and/or $n$) is the upper region, $\mathcal{R}_l(\mathcal{L})$ the lower; then $\mathcal{W}^i_A = \mathcal{R}_u(\mathcal{B}^i)$ (attacker dominance region, ADR) and $\mathcal{W}^i_D = \mathcal{R}_l(\mathcal{B}^i)$ (defender dominance region, DDR).

### 5.1 Proposition 1 (p. 1038) — straight-line property of the BAR
**Statement**: for any $p \in \mathrm{bar}^1(x^0_A,x^0_D,\alpha)$ or $p\in\mathrm{bar}^2(x^0_A,x^0_{D1},x^0_{D2},\alpha)$, the attacker can reach $p$ uncaught **iff** it travels along the straight line $\mathcal{P}(x^0_A, p)$ joining its initial position to $p$.
**Proof approach** (2 steps, definitional): (i) by definition of BAR, on the line the attacker arrives at $p$ no later than any defender going straight (extreme case: simultaneous arrival); (ii) any non-straight path takes strictly longer, so defenders heading straight to $p$ win. Tools: definition of AR/BAR + semipermeability reasoning. Meaning: the BAR is exactly the semipermeable locus where attacker and best defender times to $p$ coincide: $\mathrm{dist}(x^0_A,p) = \alpha\,\mathrm{dist}(x^0_D,p)$ (cf. eq. (8)). Local/analytic; used everywhere downstream.

### 5.2 Lemma 1 (pp. 1038–1039, eq. (4)) — barrier for $\alpha = 1$, one defender
**Statement**: for any $x^0_D\in\Omega$, $\mathcal{B}^1(x^0_D,1) = \bigcup_{i=1}^2 \mathcal{B}^1_i(x^0_D,1)$ with
$$\mathcal{B}^1_1(x^0_D,1) = \{x\in\Omega \mid \mathrm{dist}(x,m) = \mathrm{dist}(x^0_D,m),\ x_1\in[0, x^0_{D,1}]\},\qquad \mathcal{B}^1_2(x^0_D,1) = \{x\in\Omega \mid \mathrm{dist}(x,n) = \mathrm{dist}(x^0_D,n),\ x_1\in(x^0_{D,1}, l]\}.$$
I.e., two circular arcs centered at the ATL endpoints $m,n$, each passing through the defender, joined at $x_1 = x^0_{D,1}$ (Fig. 3(a)).
**Proof approach** (3 steps): (i) if $\mathrm{dist}(x^0_A,m) < \mathrm{dist}(x^0_D,m)$ then $R^1_A(x^0_A,x^0_D,\alpha)\cap\mathcal{T}$ is infinite ⇒ successful attack; if $>$, the intersection is empty ⇒ failed attack; (ii) equality defines the barrier part near $m$; analogous at $n$; (iii) degenerate cases $x^0_D = m$ or $n$ give the same result. Decisive relation: $\mathrm{dist}(x,m) = \mathrm{dist}(x^0_D,m)$ (Voronoi bisector). Global, analytic, exact. Physical meaning: attacker wins iff it is closer than the defender to some ATL endpoint (can slip to the top edge at a point the defender cannot contest).

### 5.3 First-order necessary condition — FNC (eqs. (5)–(6), p. 1039)
For $\alpha<1$ and $p=(p_1,h)\in\mathcal{T}$ define (eq. (5)) $F(p_1) = \mathrm{dist}(x^0_D,p) - \frac{1}{\alpha}\mathrm{dist}(x^0_A,p)$ — the defender–attacker separation when both run straight to $p$. If both play optimally, $p^*_1$ is an extreme point of $F$, hence (eq. (6), the **FNC**):
$$F'(p^*_1)=0 \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{p^*_1 - x^0_{D,1}}{\mathrm{dist}(x^0_D,p^*)} = \frac{p^*_1 - x^0_{A,1}}{\alpha\,\mathrm{dist}(x^0_A,p^*)}.$$
Geometric reading: cosines of the subtended angles at $p^*$ balance scaled by $\alpha$. The FNC is a **quartic** equation in $p^*_1$ (Remark 7, p. 1043) with at most four permissible roots; Lemma 6 guarantees exactly one root with $p^*_1 \ge x^0_{A,1}$, extractable in radicals by Ferrari's method [41].

### 5.4 Lemma 2 (p. 1039, eqs. (7)–(10)) — barrier for $\alpha < 1$, one defender
**Statement**: $\mathcal{B}^1(x^0_D,\alpha) = \bigcup_{i=1}^3 \mathcal{B}^1_i(x^0_D,\alpha)$ (three disjoint parts, Fig. 3(b)):
$$\mathcal{B}^1_1 = \{x\in\Omega \mid \mathrm{dist}(x,m) = \alpha\,\mathrm{dist}(x^0_D,m),\ x_1\in[0,k_1]\}$$
$$\mathcal{B}^1_2 = \{x\in\Omega \mid \mathrm{dist}(x,n) = \alpha\,\mathrm{dist}(x^0_D,n),\ x_1\in[k_2,l]\}$$
$$\mathcal{B}^1_3 = \Big\{x\in\Omega \;\Big|\; (x_1 - x^0_{D,1})^2 - \big(\tfrac{1}{\alpha^2}-1\big)(x_2-h)^2 + (1-\alpha^2)(x^0_{D,2}-h)^2 = 0,\ x_1\in(k_1,k_2)\Big\}$$
with division parameters $k_1 = \alpha^2 x^0_{D,1}$, $k_2 = (1-\alpha^2)l + \alpha^2 x^0_{D,1}$ (eq. (7)). **Verified against rendered p. 1039.**
**Proof approach** (5 steps): (i) take $x^0_A\in\mathcal{B}^1$; then $\mathrm{bar}^1(x^0_A,x^0_D,\alpha)\cap\mathcal{T}$ is a single point — the OTP $p^*$; (ii) Proposition 1 gives eq. (8) $\mathrm{dist}(x^0_A,p^*) = \alpha\,\mathrm{dist}(x^0_D,p^*)$; (iii) combining with FNC (6) yields eq. (9): $\alpha^2(p^*_1 - x^0_{D,1}) = p^*_1 - x^0_{A,1} \Rightarrow p^*_1 = \frac{x^0_{A,1} - \alpha^2 x^0_{D,1}}{1-\alpha^2}$ — the decisive intermediate equation; (iv) substituting (9) into (8) gives eq. (10), the locus of attacker positions on the barrier: $(x^0_{A,1}-x^0_{D,1})^2 - (\tfrac{1}{\alpha^2}-1)(x^0_{A,2}-h)^2 + (1-\alpha^2)(x^0_{D,2}-h)^2 = 0$ (a hyperbola branch in the attacker's plane); since $p^*_1\in[0,l]$, (9) forces $x^0_{A,1}\in[k_1,k_2]$, proving the middle part $\mathcal{B}^1_3$; (v) for $x^0_{A,1}\in[0,k_1)$, assuming an interior OTP $p^*_1\in(0,k_1)$ plus (9) gives the contradiction $p^*_1 < 0$, so $m$ is the OTP and $\mathcal{B}^1_1$ is the Apollonius circle w.r.t. $m$; symmetrically for $\mathcal{B}^1_2$. Analytic, global, exact. **Meaning**: the DDR boundary consists of two Apollonius arcs anchored at the ATL endpoints plus a central hyperbola whose apex sits at $x_1 = x^0_{D,1}$, $x_2 = h - \alpha(h - x^0_{D,2})$.
- **Remark 2** ($\alpha=1$, p. 1039–1040): on $\mathcal{B}^1_1$/$\mathcal{B}^1_2$ the optimal strategies are straight lines $\mathcal{P}(x^0_A,m)$, $\mathcal{P}(x^0_D,m)$ (resp. to $n$).
- **Remark 3** ($\alpha<1$, p. 1040): same for $\mathcal{B}^1_1,\mathcal{B}^1_2$; on $\mathcal{B}^1_3$ the OTPs for $P_A$ and $P_D$ are given by (9).

### 5.5 Lemma 3 (p. 1040) — distance ordering on the ATL
**Statement**: if $x^0_{D2}\in\mathcal{W}^1_D(x^0_{D1},1)\cup\mathcal{B}^1(x^0_{D1},1)$, then $\mathrm{dist}(q,x^0_{D2}) \ge \mathrm{dist}(q,x^0_{D1})$ for every $q\in\mathcal{T}$.
**Proof approach** (3 steps, Fig. 4): normalize $x = (\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$, $\theta\in[0,\pi]$, $y = (y_1,0)$, $y_1\in[0,1]$; take $q = y$, consider circles centered at $n$ or $m$ with radii $\mathrm{dist}(x^0_{D1},n)$ or $\mathrm{dist}(x^0_{D1},m)$; verify $H(\theta) = \mathrm{dist}(x,y)$ is monotonically increasing in $\theta$ on $[0,\pi]$ (Fig. 4(a), $r=1$). Decisive tool: monotonicity of chord distance along a circle. Local-to-ATL, analytic.

### 5.6 Theorem 1 (p. 1040) — when the barrier depends on only one defender
**Statement**: for two defenders and one attacker, the barrier depends only on $P_{D1}$ **iff** $x^0_{D2}\in\mathcal{W}^1_D(x^0_{D1},1)\cup\mathcal{B}^1(x^0_{D1},1)$ (i.e., in the $\alpha=1$ game $P_{D2}$ lies in $P_{D1}$'s DDR or on its barrier). This is the paper's **cooperative-defense dichotomy condition**: in the complement ($x^0_{D2}\in\mathcal{W}^1_A(x^0_{D1},1)$) both defenders shape the barrier.
**Proof approach** (4 steps): (i) by Remark 1, $\mathcal{B}^2 = \mathcal{B}^1(x^0_{D1},\alpha)$ ⟺ $\mathcal{W}^2_A(x^0_{D1},x^0_{D2},\alpha) = \mathcal{W}^1_A(x^0_{D1},\alpha)$; the inclusion $\mathcal{W}^2_A\subset\mathcal{W}^1_A$ is immediate from $R^2_A = R^1_A\cap R^1_A$; (ii) for the converse, if $x^0_{D2}\in\mathcal{W}^1_D\cup\mathcal{B}^1$ and $p\in\mathcal{W}^1_A(x^0_{D1},\alpha)$, there is $q\in\mathcal{T}$ with $\mathrm{dist}(q,p) < \alpha\,\mathrm{dist}(q,x_{D1})$; Lemma 3 upgrades this to eq. (11): $\mathrm{dist}(q,p) < \min\{\alpha\,\mathrm{dist}(q,x^0_{D1}),\,\alpha\,\mathrm{dist}(q,x^0_{D2})\}$ — the **decisive inequality** — so $p\in\mathcal{W}^2_A$, giving $\mathcal{W}^1_A\subset\mathcal{W}^2_A$; (iii) contrapositive: if $x^0_{D2}\in\mathcal{W}^1_A(x^0_{D1},1)$, "by brief verification" $\mathcal{W}^1_A(x^0_{D1},\alpha)\not\subset\mathcal{W}^2_A$ (sketched — see §7); (iv) $\alpha=1$ case read directly off Fig. 3(a) via the attacker's on-barrier optimal strategy. Analytic, global. **Meaning**: a second defender helps only if it is outside the first defender's ($\alpha=1$) dominance region — the precise condition under which cooperation is non-redundant.

### 5.7 Lemma 4 (p. 1040, eq. (12)) — the equidistant ATL point
**Statement**: if $x^0_{D2}\in\mathcal{W}^1_A(x^0_{D1},1)$, there is a unique $p\in\mathcal{T}$ with $\mathrm{dist}(p,x^0_{D1}) = \mathrm{dist}(p,x^0_{D2})$; writing $p_c = (p_{c,1},h)$ (WLOG $x^0_{D1,2}\ge x^0_{D2,2}$, so $x^0_{D1,1}\ne x^0_{D2,1}$):
$$p_{c,1} = \frac{\mathrm{dist}^2(x^0_{D1},m) - \mathrm{dist}^2(x^0_{D2},m)}{2(x^0_{D1,1} - x^0_{D2,1})}\quad (\text{eq. (12)}).$$
**Proof approach** (3 steps): (i) if no such $p$ existed, then (D2 not closer to $\mathcal{T}$) $\mathrm{dist}(p,x^0_{D1}) < \mathrm{dist}(p,x^0_{D2})$ on all of $\mathcal{T}$; (ii) but $x^0_{D2}\in\mathcal{W}^1_A(x^0_{D1},1)$ implies $\mathrm{dist}(m,x^0_{D2}) < \mathrm{dist}(m,x^0_{D1})$ or $\mathrm{dist}(n,x^0_{D2}) < \mathrm{dist}(n,x^0_{D1})$ — contradiction ⇒ existence; (iii) the perpendicular bisector of $\mathcal{P}(x^0_{D1},x^0_{D2})$ meets the line $\mathcal{T}$ at most once ⇒ uniqueness; (12) is the closed-form intersection. Analytic.

### 5.8 Theorem 2 (p. 1041, eqs. (13)–(16)) — five-part cooperative barrier
**Statement**: suppose the barrier is determined by both defenders, i.e. $x^0_{D2}\in\mathcal{W}^1_A(x^0_{D1},1)$, and $x^0_{D2,1} > x^0_{D1,1}$. Then $\mathcal{B}^2(x^0_{D1},x^0_{D2},\alpha) = \bigcup_{i=1}^5 \mathcal{B}^2_i$ (Fig. 5(a) for $\alpha<1$; Fig. 5(b) for $\alpha=1$):
$$\mathcal{B}^2_1 = \{x \mid \mathrm{dist}(x,m) = \alpha\,\mathrm{dist}(x^0_{D1},m),\ x_1\in[0,k_3]\}$$
$$\mathcal{B}^2_2 = \{x \mid (x_1 - x^0_{D1,1})^2 - (\tfrac{1}{\alpha^2}-1)(x_2-h)^2 + (1-\alpha^2)(x^0_{D1,2}-h)^2 = 0,\ x_1\in(k_3,k_4)\}$$
$$\mathcal{B}^2_3 = \{x \mid \mathrm{dist}(x,p_c) = \alpha\,\mathrm{dist}(x^0_{D1},p_c),\ x_1\in[k_4,k_5]\}$$
$$\mathcal{B}^2_4 = \{x \mid (x_1 - x^0_{D2,1})^2 - (\tfrac{1}{\alpha^2}-1)(x_2-h)^2 + (1-\alpha^2)(x^0_{D2,2}-h)^2 = 0,\ x_1\in(k_5,k_6)\}$$
$$\mathcal{B}^2_5 = \{x \mid \mathrm{dist}(x,n) = \alpha\,\mathrm{dist}(x^0_{D2},n),\ x_1\in[k_6,l]\}$$
with (eq. (14)) $k_3 = \alpha^2 x^0_{D1,1}$, $k_4 = (1-\alpha^2)p_{c,1} + \alpha^2 x^0_{D1,1}$, $k_5 = (1-\alpha^2)p_{c,1} + \alpha^2 x^0_{D2,1}$, $k_6 = (1-\alpha^2)l + \alpha^2 x^0_{D2,1}$. **Verified against rendered p. 1041.**
**Proof approach** (4 steps): (i) central part first: on $\mathcal{B}^2_3$ both defenders capture $P_A$ exactly when it reaches $\mathcal{T}$, giving the decisive simultaneous-arrival equation (15): $\mathrm{dist}(x^0_A,p^*) = \alpha\,\mathrm{dist}(x^0_{D1},p^*) = \alpha\,\mathrm{dist}(x^0_{D2},p^*)$, hence $p^* = p_c$ and $\mathrm{dist}(x^0_A,p_c) = \alpha\,\mathrm{dist}(x^0_{D1},p_c)$ (the $p_c$-centered Apollonius circle — note $\mathrm{dist}(x^0_{D1},p_c) = \mathrm{dist}(x^0_{D2},p_c)$ by construction); (ii) division points = intersections of $\mathcal{B}^2_3$ with $\mathcal{B}^1(x^0_{D1},\alpha)$ or $\mathcal{B}^1(x^0_{D2},\alpha)$: inserting $p^* = p_c$ into (9) yields eq. (16) $x^0_{A,1} = (1-\alpha^2)p_{c,1} + \alpha^2 x^0_{D1,1}$ (and symmetrically with $D2$), matching $k_4, k_5$; for $\alpha=1$, (4)+(15) give $x^0_{A,1} = x^0_{D1,1}$, consistent with (16); (iii) outer parts depend on one defender only: $\mathcal{B}^2_1 = \mathcal{B}^1_1(x^0_{D1},\alpha)$, and $\mathcal{B}^2_2$ is the left piece of $\mathcal{B}^1_3(x^0_{D1},\alpha)$ truncated at $x_1 = k_4$; mirror for $\mathcal{B}^2_4, \mathcal{B}^2_5$; (iv) the case $x^0_{D2,1} < x^0_{D1,1}$ is "by similar analysis" (not written out). Corollary observation (p. 1041): at $\alpha = 1$, $k_3 = k_4$, $k_5 = k_6$, $\mathcal{B}^2_2 = \mathcal{B}^2_4 = \emptyset$ — the barrier collapses to the three-part $\{\mathcal{B}^2_1,\mathcal{B}^2_3,\mathcal{B}^2_5\}$. Global, analytic, exact. **Meaning**: the cooperative arc $\mathcal{B}^2_3$ around $p_c$ is where both defenders are simultaneously critical; attacker positions on it are caught only through genuine two-defender coordination.
- **Remark 4 (p. 1042)**: OTPs on $\mathcal{B}^2_1,\mathcal{B}^2_3,\mathcal{B}^2_5$ are $m, p_c, n$ respectively; on $\mathcal{B}^2_2$ the OTPs for $P_A$ and $P_{D1}$ are given by (9) while $P_{D2}$ may do anything (and symmetrically on $\mathcal{B}^2_4$ w.r.t. $P_{D2}$).

### 5.9 Lemma 5 (p. 1042, eq. (17)) — DDR game of degree
**Statement**: if $x^0_A\in\mathcal{W}^2_D(x^0_{D1},x^0_{D2},\alpha)$ and the payoff is (2), then the capture point $p^*\in\Omega$ is the point of $\mathrm{bar}^2(x^0_A,x^0_{D1},x^0_{D2},\alpha)$ **closest to $\mathcal{T}$**, and the attacker's unique optimal strategy is the straight line $\mathcal{P}(x^0_A,p^*)$; with (17) $J_T = \min_{p\in\mathcal{T}}\mathrm{dist}(p,p^*)$.
**Justification** (2 steps): the attacker's OTP lies on the BAR (AR = reachable-uncaught set), and Proposition 1 forces straight-line motion; minimizing (2) ⟺ picking the BAR point nearest the ATL. **Remarks 5–6** (p. 1042) make it algorithmic: for $\alpha=1$ the BAR is a polygon, so only the (≤ 3) intersection points of $\mathrm{bar}^1(x^0_A,x^0_{D1},\alpha)$, $\mathrm{bar}^1(x^0_A,x^0_{D2},\alpha)$ and $\partial\Omega$ need checking (if $\mathrm{bar}^2\parallel\mathcal{T}$, that selection remains optimal); for $\alpha<1$ the BAR is made of circular arcs, so the peaks (apices) of the two Apollonius circles must also be tested (Fig. 7(b)). Defenders' strategies: if $p^*\in\mathrm{bar}^1\cap\mathrm{bar}^1$, both run straight to $p^*$; if $p^*$ lies on only one defender's Apollonius circle, that defender runs to $p^*$ and the other is arbitrary (it lies in $R^1_A(x^0_A,x^0_{D2},\alpha)$ and "makes no contribution to the payoff function (2)"); for unity the paper assumes both run to $p^*$ [p. 1042].

### 5.10 Lemma 6 (pp. 1042–1043 + Appendix pp. 1044–1045) — OTP uniqueness/monotonicity in the ADR
**Statement**: suppose $x^0_A\in\mathcal{W}^2_A(x_{D1},x_{D2},\alpha)$ and $x^0_{D2}\in\mathcal{W}^1_D(x_{D1},1)\cup\mathcal{B}^1(x_{D1},1)$. If $x^0_{A,1} > x^0_{D1,1}$ and $l$ is long enough, the OTP $p^* = (p^*_1,h)\in\mathcal{T}$ is **unique** with $p^*_1 > x^0_{A,1}$; moreover $F$ (eq. (5)) is strictly increasing on $[x^0_{A,1}, p^*_1]$ and strictly decreasing on $[p^*_1, l]$.
**Proof approach** (Appendix, ~7 steps; auxiliary geometry of Fig. 8 with points $b,c,e,r$ and lengths $l_1 = \mathrm{dist}(x^0_A,c)$, $l_2 = \mathrm{dist}(x^0_{D1},b)$, $l_3 = \mathrm{dist}(b,c)$): (i) apply the FNC re-expressed through $b,c$ — eq. (19): $\frac{\mathrm{dist}(b,p^*)}{\mathrm{dist}(x^0_{D1},p^*)} = \frac{\mathrm{dist}(c,p^*)}{\alpha\,\mathrm{dist}(x^0_A,p^*)}$; (ii) parallelism $\mathcal{P}(e,c)\parallel\mathcal{P}(x^0_{D1},b)$ (intercept theorem) gives eq. (20): $\frac{\mathrm{dist}(b,p^*)}{\mathrm{dist}(x^0_{D1},p^*)} = \frac{\mathrm{dist}(c,p^*)}{\mathrm{dist}(e,p^*)}$; (iii) substitute (19) into (20) — decisive reduction — eq. (21): $\mathrm{dist}(e,p^*)/\mathrm{dist}(x^0_A,p^*) = \alpha$; (iv) **case $x^0_{A,2} > x^0_{D1,2}$** (Fig. 8(a)): boundary cases $p^* = c \Leftrightarrow \alpha = 0$, $p^* = r \Leftrightarrow \alpha = 1$, $p^*$ right of $r \Leftrightarrow \alpha > 1$, hence $p^*\in\mathcal{P}(c,r)$ and $p^*_1 > x^0_{A,1}$; with $l_4 = \mathrm{dist}(c,r) = l_1l_3/(l_2-l_1)$, $\mathrm{dist}(c,p) = k\,l_4$, $k\in[0,1]$, uniqueness reduces to strict monotonicity of $G(k) = \mathrm{dist}(e,p)/\mathrm{dist}(x^0_A,p)$, eq. (22): $G(k) = \sqrt{\frac{(l_3+l_4)^2\left((l_3+k l_4)^2 + l_2^2\right)k^2}{(l_3+k l_4)^2\left((l_3+l_4)^2 k^2 + l_2^2\right)}}$; (v) $G$ shares monotonicity with $G_1(k) = \frac{((l_3+k l_4)^2+l_2^2)k^2}{(l_3+k l_4)^2\left((l_3+l_4)^2k^2+l_2^2\right)}$ (eq. (23)), whose derivative sign reduces (eq. (24)) to $G_2(k) = l_2^2 + l_3^2 + 3l_3l_4k + 3l_4^2k^2 - (l_3l_4 + 2l_4^2)k^3$ with $G_2'(k) = 3l_3l_4 + 6l_4^2 k - 3(l_3l_4+2l_4^2)k^2$; (vi) $G_2'(k) > 0$ on $(0,1)$, so $G_2(k) > G_2(0) = l_2^2 + l_3^2 > 0 \Rightarrow G_1'(k) > 0$ (eq. (25)) ⇒ unique $p^*$; sign of $F'$: $G(k) < \alpha$ on $[x^0_{A,1}, p^*_1)$ gives $F'(p_1) > 0$, and $F'(p_1) < 0$ on $(p^*_1, l]$, via (5), (19), (20); (vii) **case $x^0_{A,2}\le x^0_{D1,2}$** (Fig. 8(b); see typo note §7): $p^* = c \Leftrightarrow \alpha = 0$, $p^*_1\to\infty \Leftrightarrow \alpha\to 1$, so a solution of (21) exists; with $\mathrm{dist}(c,p) = k\in(0,\infty)$, $G(k) = \sqrt{\frac{((k+l_3)^2+l_2^2)k^2}{(k+l_3)^2(k^2+l_1^2)}}$ (eq. (26)), and $G_1'(k)$ reduces to $G_2(k) = l_1^2(l_3^3 + l_3 l_2^2 + 3l_3^2 k + 3l_3 k^2 + k^3) - l_2^2 k^3$, $G_2'(k) = 3l_1^2(l_3+k)^2 - 3l_2^2 k^2$ (eq. (27)); $x^0_{A,2}\le x^0_{D1,2}$ [printed "$\ge$"] implies $l_1 \ge l_2$, and $l_1 = l_2$ with $l_3 = 0$ cannot hold simultaneously, so $G_2'(k) > 0$ on $(0,\infty)$ and $G_2(k) > G_2(0) = l_1^2 l_3(l_3^2 + l_2^2) > 0 \Rightarrow G_1'(k) > 0$ (eq. (28)). Tools: Euclidean similarity (parallel lines), reduction of a transcendental ratio to a cubic-polynomial monotonicity argument, no numerical steps. Analytic, local to the stated geometry. **Meaning**: the attacker's best target is unique and strictly to its right; $F$ is unimodal, so FNC's stationary point is the global OTP — this is what legitimates solving the quartic (6) by Ferrari's formula.

### 5.11 Eq. (18) (p. 1043) — straight-line locus of constant-OTP attacker positions
Squaring FNC (6) and rearranging (verified consistent: $\alpha^2 s^2(u^2+v^2) = u^2(s^2+w^2)$ with $s = p^*_1 - x^0_{D1,1}$, $w = h - x^0_{D1,2}$, $u = p^*_1 - x^0_{A,1}$, $v = h - x^0_{A,2}$) gives
$$\sqrt{(1-\alpha^2)(x^0_{D1,1}-p^*_1)^2 + (x^0_{D1,2}-h)^2}\;\big(p^*_1 - x^0_{A,1}\big) = \alpha\big(p^*_1 - x^0_{D1,1}\big)\big(h - x^0_{A,2}\big)\quad(\text{eq. (18)}),$$
which is **linear** in $(x^0_{A,1}, x^0_{A,2})$: "the initial positions of the attacker whose OTP is $p^*$ form a straight line" [p. 1043]. This is the mechanism behind the SCLs below.

### 5.12 Theorem 3 (p. 1043) — ADR partition, one effective defender
**Statement**: suppose $x^0_{D2}\in\mathcal{W}^1_D(x_{D1},1)\cup\mathcal{B}^1(x_{D1},1)$. Then the ADR is divided into three disjoint parts $\mathcal{R}^1_{F1}, \mathcal{R}^1_{F2}, \mathcal{R}^1_{UF1}$ by two SCLs $\mathcal{P}(s_1,m)$ and $\mathcal{P}(s_2,n)$, where $s_1 = (k_1, s_1)$, $s_2 = (k_2, s_2)\in\mathcal{B}^1(x^0_{D1},\alpha)$ and $k_1,k_2$ are from (7) (Fig. 9). $\mathcal{R}^1_{F1},\mathcal{R}^1_{F2}$ are FTPRs with fixed OTPs $m$ and $n$; $\mathcal{R}^1_{UF1}$ is a UTPR whose OTP is given by FNC (6).
**Proof approach** (3 steps): (i) by Lemma 2 the OTP of $s_1$ is $m$; by (18) every attacker position on $\mathcal{P}(s_1,m)$ also has OTP $m$; (ii) by Lemma 6, points of $\mathcal{R}^1_{F1}$ (below/left of that line, within the ADR) inherit OTP $m$ ⇒ FTPR, and neighboring UTPR points use (6) ⇒ $\mathcal{P}(s_1,m)$ is the SCL; (iii) mirror for $\mathcal{R}^1_{F2}$, $\mathcal{P}(s_2,n)$; for $\alpha = 1$, $k_1 = k_2$ and $s_1 = s_2 = x^0_{D1}$. Analytic. **Meaning**: the attackers' optimal target is piecewise constant — the ATL endpoints — except in a central wedge where it varies continuously per the quartic FNC.
- **Remark 7 (p. 1043)**: FNC (6) is a quartic in $p^*_1$ (≤ 4 admissible roots); Lemma 6 ⇒ exactly one root satisfies $p^*_1\ge x^0_{A,1}$; "by Ferrari method [41], the OTP can be obtained accurately."

### 5.13 Theorem 4 (p. 1043) — ADR partition, cooperative case
**Statement**: if $x^0_{D2}\in\mathcal{W}^1_A(x^0_{D1},1)$, the ADR is divided into five disjoint parts $\mathcal{R}^2_{F1},\mathcal{R}^2_{F2},\mathcal{R}^2_{F3},\mathcal{R}^2_{UF1},\mathcal{R}^2_{UF2}$ by four SCLs $\mathcal{P}(s_3,m)$, $\mathcal{P}(s_4,p_c)$, $\mathcal{P}(s_5,p_c)$, $\mathcal{P}(s_6,n)$, with truncation points $s_3=(k_3,s_3),\dots,s_6=(k_6,s_6)$ on $\mathcal{B}^2$ using (14); for $\alpha=1$, $s_3 = s_4 = x^0_{D1}$ and $s_5 = s_6 = x^0_{D2}$ (Fig. 10). $\mathcal{R}^2_{F1},\mathcal{R}^2_{F2},\mathcal{R}^2_{F3}$ are FTPRs with fixed OTPs $m, p_c, n$; $\mathcal{R}^2_{UF1},\mathcal{R}^2_{UF2}$ are UTPRs with OTP by FNC (6).
**Proof approach** (2 steps): from Remark 4, Lemma 6, and (18), every $p\in\mathcal{R}^2_{F2}$ has OTP $p_c$, and $\mathcal{P}(s_4,p_c)$, $\mathcal{P}(s_5,p_c)$ are SCLs; within the ADR, the left side of $\mathcal{P}(s_4,p_c)$ depends only on $P_{D1}$ and the right side of $\mathcal{P}(s_5,p_c)$ only on $P_{D2}$, so Theorem 3 completes the partition. Analytic.

### 5.14 Section V results (non-theorem but substantive)
- **Information patterns** (pp. 1043–1044): reachability analysis is re-run at each current configuration; if the attacker is currently on the barrier, players use the on-barrier optimal strategies (Remarks 2/3/4); off the barrier, the advantaged team wins provided the opponent plays optimally — otherwise the opponent's deviation is exploitable. Open-loop solution coincides with closed-loop here; practical play is piecewise open-loop with small refresh intervals (≈ feedback). Strategies are saddle-point equilibria of the chosen zero-sum payoff under open-loop information.
- **Extension to multiplayer games** (p. 1044): embedding the 2v1 block into the pairwise-outcome/maximum-matching framework of [11] (M. Chen, Zhou, Tomlin, TAC 2017) yields matchings containing 1v1 **and** 2v1 pairs, expanding the defense team's strategy set; comparing the barrier of Lemmas 1–2 with Theorem 2 identifies attacker positions "captured only by two defenders cooperatively while any one of the two defenders cannot guarantee the capture individually"; also applicable to region-defense games by taking $\mathcal{T}$ as the splitting line; analytic cost ⇒ more efficient than [11]. Barrier construction for ≥ 3 defenders vs. 1 attacker is declared intractable analytically "due to the high dimensionality of the relative positions between multiple defenders" [p. 1044].

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
**Author-stated**:
- Only simple (point-mass, constant-speed, unbounded-turn) dynamics; "Future work involves games with more players and more practical dynamic models" [p. 1044, Conclusion].
- Only $\alpha \le 1$; extension to more defenders vs. one attacker "is intractable due to the high dimensionality of the relative positions between multiple defenders" [p. 1044]; the roadmap is to solve small blocks (2v2, 3v1, Nv1) first.
- Theorem 2 stated for $x^0_{D2,1} > x^0_{D1,1}$; the opposite ordering is "by similar analysis" [p. 1041].
- Lemma 6 requires "$l$ long enough" [p. 1042]; Theorems 3–4 inherit this qualification, so the SCL partition is guaranteed only for sufficiently long ATLs.

**Critical observations (this archive, with page/eq. traces)**:
1. **Unproven $\alpha>1$ claim**: "the attacker always wins from any given initial positions... for $\alpha > 1$" [p. 1037] is stated without proof or citation; in a bounded rectangle with a finite ATL it is plausible but not trivial (the attacker must also avoid walls) — a latent assumption of the whole $\alpha\le 1$ framework.
2. **Typo in Appendix, case 2**: the header prints "Then, we consider $x^0_{A,2} \ge x^0_{D1,2}$, as Fig. 8(b) shows" [p. 1045], but Fig. 8(b)'s caption and Lemma 6 use $x^0_{A,2}\le x^0_{D1,2}$; the subsequent step "$x^0_{A,2}\ge x^0_{D1,2}$ implies $l_1\ge l_2$" is only geometrically correct under $\le$ (attacker lower ⇒ longer rise to $c$), confirming the intended sign is $\le$.
3. **Domain-dimension typo**: "let $h, l$ be the horizontal and vertical lengths of $\Omega$" [p. 1036] is swapped relative to $m = (0,h)$, $n = (l,h)$ and Fig. 1 ($h$ = vertical height, $l$ = horizontal width).
4. **Sketched proofs**: Theorem 1's only-if direction ("by brief verification", p. 1040) and Theorem 2's mirrored configuration (p. 1041) are not fully written; the barrier-uniqueness of the OTP on $\mathcal{B}^1$ (that $\mathrm{bar}^1\cap\mathcal{T}$ is a single point for $x^0_A\in\mathcal{B}^1$) is asserted rather than proven in Lemma 2's step (i).
5. **Payoff decoupling caveat**: the paper itself warns that if the two teams optimize different payoff functions, "the defenders are likely to get closer to the attacker than the case where the attacker also employs the optimal strategy based on (3)" [p. 1037] — i.e., the quoted equilibria are per-player best responses under possibly mismatched objectives, not a single jointly-specified zero-sum game; saddle-point terminology (p. 1044) is therefore loose.
6. **Barrier is conditional on fixed defender positions**: $\mathcal{W}^2_A,\mathcal{W}^2_D$ are regions in the attacker's 2-D plane with defenders' positions as parameters; the results certify the game outcome under straight-line defender play toward the OTP, and the on-line procedure re-solves the analysis at every refresh — a receding-horizon scheme whose closed-loop guarantee (beyond "open-loop = closed-loop here", p. 1044) is argued informally.
7. **No empirical validation** of the real-time claim (see §6); the Ferrari closed form for the quartic FNC (6) is exact but algebraically heavy, and the paper does not exhibit the resolvent.
8. **UTPR strategies within $\mathcal{R}_{UF}$ depend on continuous FNC evaluation**, so "fixed vs. unfixed" strategy regions is the finest structural description given; robustness to measurement noise or adversary model mismatch is not treated.

## 8. Citation Value
**Role**: this is the canonical analytical benchmark for the smallest genuinely *cooperative* reach-avoid block — two defenders guarding one line segment against one attacker — and the natural companion/corrective to Chen–Zhou–Tomlin's pairwise-outcome maximum-matching framework [11] (whose 1v1 pairs ignore intra-team cooperation). It supplies: (i) closed-form barriers combining Voronoi bisectors, Apollonius circles, and a hyperbola arc in a bounded rectangular domain; (ii) an if-and-only-if condition (Theorem 1) deciding when a second defender is non-redundant, plus the $p_c$-centered cooperative Apollonius arc (Theorem 2) marking states capturable only by joint action; and (iii) the FTPR/UTPR/SCL decomposition with the quartic FNC (6) and its Ferrari solution, which together turn the game of kind into computable games of degree. Use it as the analytic anchor for barrier/winning-region papers on line- or perimeter-defense with multiple defenders, as the cooperative 2v1 building block in decomposition schemes for multiplayer reach-avoid games, and as the standard foil against which numerical HJI or learning-based reach-avoid methods are validated.
**Suggested citation sentences**: "Analytical barriers for the two-defender–one-attacker reach-avoid game on a rectangular domain were constructed in closed form via Voronoi diagrams and Apollonius circles, with a necessary-and-sufficient condition determining which defenders shape the barrier [P099]." Or: "The 2v1 block of [P099] — barrier parts $\mathcal{B}^2_1$–$\mathcal{B}^2_5$ and the FTPR/UTPR partition by strategy change lines — provides the cooperative pairwise outcome that 1v1-based maximum matching cannot capture."
