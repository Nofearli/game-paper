## 2. Research Background
Multi-robot systems with adversarial goals can threaten safety-critical infrastructure (airports, military facilities), motivating games in which multiple Dubins-car pursuers guard a planar convex region against malicious simple-motion evaders (p. 1). The classical Homicidal Chauffeur game (Isaacs 1965) took until Merz (1971) to be completely solved because of the nonlinearity of Dubins-car dynamics, and its reach-avoid variants inherit this difficulty (p. 1). Reach-avoid differential games (a.k.a. two-target games, perimeter-defense games, target-guarding problems) are usually treated either with complex dynamics and numerical methods (HJI level sets, viable up to about five states) or with simple-motion players and analytic/geometric methods (Apollonius circles, Voronoi partitions, dominance regions) (pp. 1-2). The authors' companion paper (Yan et al., IEEE TAC 69(4):2367-2382, 2024) solved the one-pursuer-one-evader Homicidal Chauffeur reach-avoid game with an infinite-area protected region with linear boundary via Apollonius-circle-based guaranteed winning strategies (p. 2). This paper extends that line to the genuinely multiplayer case — many pursuers, many evaders, an arbitrary compact convex goal region — while keeping all guarantees analytic and cheap to evaluate online.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
**Problem**: $N_p$ Dubins-car pursuers $\mathcal P$ cooperatively protect a compact convex goal region $\Omega_{\text{goal}}=\{y: g(y)\le 0\}$ (boundary $T$, play region $\Omega_{\text{play}}=\{g>0\}$, $g$ twice differentiable) against $N_e$ simple-motion evaders $\mathcal E$ starting in $\Omega_{\text{play}}$; the evasion team maximizes the number of evaders reaching $\Omega_{\text{goal}}$, the pursuit team maximizes captured evaders (p. 2, Fig. 1).

**Gap** [authors' claim, p. 2]: (i) "there are limited works on Homicidal Chauffeur reach-avoid differential games which integrate the Homicidal Chauffeur dynamics with reach-avoid competition goals"; the literature splits into complex dynamics + numerical methods vs. simple dynamics + analytical methods; (ii) HJI reachability was applied only to one-pursuer-one-evader cases (Chen et al. 2019) and "would suffer from high computational burden for multiplayer games" [consistent with the cited Chen et al. 2018 decomposition paper and the standard 5-state practical limit of level-set methods, which the authors invoke on p. 3]; (iii) the most relevant work, Yan et al. (2024, TAC), "mainly considers one pursuer against one evader for a protected region with infinite area and a linear boundary, and constructs pursuit strategies based on the Apollonius circle" [verified: that companion paper is cited as IEEE TAC 69(4):2367-2382 and described exactly this way on p. 2]; (iv) multiplayer reach-avoid games are solved suboptimally by decomposition into subgames plus task allocation [verified: Chen et al. 2017 pairwise outcomes; Shishika & Kumar 2018/2020; Yan et al. 2020b, 2022 — all cited works do follow this pattern]. The technical gap the paper fills: a *forward* (no backward HJI, no terminal-surface retrogression) sufficient winning condition for subgames with car-like pursuers, with a coalition-size reduction and an allocation layer that lifts subgame outcomes to a team strategy.

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Theory (winning concept)**: Pursuit enclosure functions (PEFs) and a new *enclosure region pursuit* (ERP) winning condition: a dynamically updated compact strictly convex region containing the evader must keep positive distance from $\Omega_{\text{goal}}$ at all times. ERP winning and its strategies are computed by **forward analysis** instead of backward reachability/HJI (pp. 2, 4; Definitions 1-3).
2. **Theory (complexity reduction)**: A coalition-reduction theorem: under ERP winning with positive safe distance, at most **two** pursuers of a coalition matter (support-constraint counting à la Calafiore–Campi), so only 1-vs-1 and 2-vs-1 subgames need be solved (Theorem 1, p. 5).
3. **Theory (state generation)**: A **steer-to-ERP** approach: an optimization problem (7) whose optimal value $V$ bounds the worst-case recession speed of the safe distance, certifying that any state whose safe distance exceeds $\max\{-VT,0\}$ and that can be steered into a known ERP-winning set within horizon $T$ is itself ERP-winning (Theorem 2, p. 5).
4. **Modeling + constructive conditions**: A *positional* PEF (12) built only on players' positions (generalizing the 3-D potential function of Yan et al. 2022 to the plane); explicit parameters, initial ERP-winning sets, and feedback strategies guaranteeing ERP winning for one pursuer (Theorem 3, Lemma 3) and two pursuers (Theorem 4) against one evader.
5. **Algorithm**: A binary integer program (60) over subgame outcomes that maximizes captured (ERP-defended) evaders with secondary lexicographic objectives on the sum of safe distances (Theorem 5: NP-hard), embedded in a multiplayer receding-horizon strategy (Algorithm 1, p. 11) with monotonically increasing number of guaranteed defeated evaders.
6. **Experiment**: Numerical case studies for 1v1, 2v1, and 4-vs-3 scenarios validating the three layers (Section 7, Fig. 5, p. 11-12).

## 5. Key Mathematical Results

### 5.1 Dynamics, information structure, winning conditions
- **Pursuer $P_i$ (Dubins car), eq. (1), p. 2**: $\dot x_{P_i}=v_{P_i}\cos\theta_{P_i}$, $\dot y_{P_i}=v_{P_i}\sin\theta_{P_i}$, $\dot\theta_{P_i}=v_{P_i}u_{P_i}/\kappa_i$, with $u_{P_i}\in\mathcal U_{\mathcal P}=\{u:[0,\infty)\to\mathcal S_1 \mid u \text{ piecewise smooth}\}$ (so $u_{P_i}\in[-1,1]$ is a normalized turning rate); $v_{P_i}>0$ max speed, $\kappa_i>0$ min turning radius.
- **Evader $E_j$ (simple motion), eq. (2), p. 2**: $\dot x_{E_j}=v_{E_j}u^x_{E_j}$, $\dot y_{E_j}=v_{E_j}u^y_{E_j}$, $u_{E_j}\in\mathcal U_{\mathcal E}=\{u:[0,\infty)\to\mathcal S_2\mid u\text{ piecewise smooth}\}$.
- **Speed ratio** $\alpha_{ij}=v_{P_i}/v_{E_j}>1$ (faster pursuers assumed). **Capture radius** $r_i>0$; capture occurs when the pursuer "is pursuing the former and their Euclidean distance is less than or equal to the pursuer's capture radius" — deliberately a **closed** capture condition, unlike Merz's strict inequality, because the forward analysis never needs the usable part of the terminal surface (p. 2-3).
- **Goal region** (p. 2): $\Omega_{\text{goal}}=\{y: g(y)\le0\}$ nonempty, compact, convex; $T=\{g=0\}$; $g\in C^2$; Hessian $H(x_G)$ and unit gradient $e_{IG}=g_y(x_G)/\|g_y(x_G)\|_2$ enter the strategy law.
- **Information structure (p. 2-3, same as Yan et al. 2024)**: pursuers decide with knowledge of all players' current positions **plus the evaders' current control inputs (speed and heading)**; evaders know only current positions; max speeds and $\Omega_{\text{goal}},T,\Omega_{\text{play}}$ known to both. Practical access to $\dot x_{E_j}$ justified via bearings-only estimation (Battistini & Shima 2014; Fonod & Shima 2018), assumed-access works (Exarchos et al. 2015), or phased-array radar velocity measurement (Pihl et al. 2012; Soumekh 1997). A pursuer strategy is $u_{P_i}:\mathcal S_{cj}\times\mathcal S_2\to\mathcal S_1$ — it uses $E_j$'s current control, *not* its strategy (p. 4).
- **Winning (p. 3)**: subgame $\mathcal P_c$ vs. $E_j$: $\mathcal P_c$ wins if $E_j$ can never reach $\Omega_{\text{goal}}$ before capture; two admissible modes: capture in $\Omega_{\text{play}}$ **or infinitely delaying entry**. A complete qualitative solution would need the induced HJI equation, but the subgame has $3|c|+2$ states vs. the $\approx 5$-state practical HJI limit (Chen et al. 2018).

### 5.2 Definitions (PEF machinery, pp. 4)
- **Definition 1 (Pursuit Enclosure Function)**: $f:\mathbb R^2\times\mathcal S_{ij}\to\mathbb R$ with $\mathcal S_{ij}=\{X_{ij}=(x_{P_i},\theta_{P_i},x_{E_j}):\|x_{P_i}-x_{E_j}\|_2>r_i\}$ is a PEF if for each $X_{ij}\in\mathcal S_{ij}$: (1) $\mathbb E(X_{ij};f)=\{x: f(x,X_{ij})\ge0\}$ is compact and strictly convex (the *enclosure region*); (2) $f$ is differentiable in both $x$ and $X_{ij}$ wherever $f(x,X_{ij})=0$; (3) $f(x_{E_j},X_{ij})\ge0$ (evader enclosed). For coalition $\mathcal P_c$ with PEF set $f_c=\{f_i\}_{i\in c}$: $\mathbb E(X_{cj};f_c)=\bigcap_{i\in c}\mathbb E(X_{ij};f_i)$, nonempty, compact, strictly convex.
- **Definition 2 (Safe distance)**: $\varrho(X_{cj};f_c)\triangleq d(\mathbb E(X_{cj};f_c),\Omega_{\text{goal}})$; since $x_{E_j}\in\mathbb E$, $d(x_{E_j},\Omega_{\text{goal}})\ge\varrho$, so $\varrho>0$ implies the evader is outside $\Omega_{\text{goal}}$.
- **Definition 3 (ERP winning state/strategy)**: $X_{cj}\in\mathcal S_{cj}$ is ERP winning if there exists $U_c$ with $\varrho(X^t_{cj};f_c)>0$ for **all** $t\ge0$ regardless of $u_{E_j}$; such $U_c$ is an ERP winning strategy. This is the "dynamic region stays outside the goal" sufficient winning condition, whose core reasoning was implicitly used with Apollonius circles for simple-motion players in Lee & Bakolas (2022a,b) and Yan et al. (2022) [authors' attribution, p. 3].
- **Definition 4 (Safe distance computation), eq. (3)**: convex program $\mathcal P(X_{cj})$: $\min_{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^2} d(x,y)$ s.t. $f_i(x,X_{ij})\ge0,\ g(y)\le0,\ \forall i\in c$; then $\varrho=d(x_I,x_G)$. Strict convexity of $\mathbb E$ + convexity of $\Omega_{\text{goal}}$ make this a convex program with unique solution when $\varrho>0$; the alternating projection algorithm of Lee & Bakolas (2022b) can solve it with slight modification [authors' claim, p. 4].
- **Definition 5 (Support constraint)**: for $\mathcal P$: $\min \phi(x)$ s.t. $x\in\bigcap_{i=1}^m X_i$ with removed-constraint programs $\mathcal P_k$, constraint $k$ is a *support constraint* iff $J^*_k<J^*$.
- **Lemma 1 (Calafiore & Campi 2006, recalled, p. 4)**: if $\phi(x)=c^\top x$ and $\mathcal P$ and all $\mathcal P_k$ admit a unique optimal solution, the number of support constraints is at most $n$ (the dimension) [verified: this is the standard support-constraint bound from the scenario-approach paper Calafiore & Campi, IEEE TAC 51(5):742-753, 2006].

### 5.3 Theorem 1 (Constraint Reduction, p. 5, eqs. (5)-(6))
**Statement**: Let $J^*_{cj}$ be the optimal value of $\mathcal P(X_{cj})$ in (3). If $J^*_{cj}>0$, there exists a subcoalition $\bar c\subseteq c$ with $|\bar c|\le 2$ and $J^*_{cj}=J^*_{\bar c j}$, where $J^*_{\bar c j}$ is the optimal value of program (5) (same as (3) restricted to $i\in\bar c$). Game meaning: when the safe distance is positive, at most two pursuers' enclosure regions determine the closest point $x_I$; only 1-vs-1 and 2-vs-1 subgames matter.
**Proof** (3 steps): (1) positivity gives unique optimal pair $(x_I,x_G)$ (closed strictly convex vs. closed convex sets); (2) recast (3) as the 3-dimensional program (6): $\min_{(x,\gamma)}\gamma$ s.t. $f_i(x,X_{ij})\ge0\ \forall i$, $d(x,x_G)-\gamma\le0$ — a linear objective in $(x,\gamma)$ with $|c|+1$ convex constraints and unique optimum $(x_I,\gamma^*)$, $\gamma^*=d(x_I,x_G)$; each $f_i$-removed program also has a unique optimum (same argument, only $|c|\ge3$ needed); (3) $d(x,x_G)-\gamma\le0$ is itself a support constraint, so by Lemma 1 at most $3$ support constraints total, hence **at most two** among the $f_i$; deleting non-support constraints leaves $J^*$ unchanged. Decisive step: support-constraint counting in dimension 3. Analytic, global. Note the theorem concerns the *program*, i.e., it applies whenever $\varrho>0$, exactly the regime where ERP winning is at stake.

### 5.4 Theorem 2 (Steer-to-ERP Approach, p. 5, eq. (7))
**Statement**: Given $f_c$ and $X_{cj}\in\mathcal S_{cj}$, if there exist a pursuit strategy $U^1_c$ and horizon $T\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}$ such that $X^{t^\star}_{cj}$ is an ERP winning state for some $t^\star\in[0,T]$ regardless of $u_{E_j}$, and
$$\varrho(X_{cj};f_c)>\max\{-VT,\,0\},$$
where $V$ is the optimal value of the nonlinear program (7):
$$\min \sum_{i\in c} v_{P_i}\lambda_i\Big(f_{i,P}^\top e_{\theta_{P_i}}+\tfrac{|f_{i,\theta}|}{\kappa_i}+\tfrac{\|f_{i,E}\|_2}{\alpha_{ij}}\Big)$$
over variables $x_I\in\mathbb R^2,x_G\in\mathbb R^2,X'_{cj}\in\mathcal S_{cj},\lambda_i,\lambda_g$ subject to the KKT system of $\mathcal P(X'_{cj})$: $0=\lambda_g g_y(x_G)+\sum_i\lambda_i f_{i,x}(x_I,X'_{ij})$; $0=d_y(x_I,x_G)+\lambda_g g_y(x_G)$; $f_i(x_I,X'_{ij})\ge0,\ \lambda_i\le0,\ \lambda_i f_i(x_I,X'_{ij})=0$; $g(x_G)=0,\ \lambda_g\ge0$ — then $X_{cj}$ is an ERP winning state; moreover if $U^2_c$ is an ERP winning strategy for $X^{t^\star}_{cj}$, the concatenation ($U^1_c$ on $[0,t^\star]$, $U^2_c$ after) is an ERP winning strategy for $X_{cj}$.
**Proof** (5 steps): (1) KKT conditions (8a)-(8d) for (3); complementary slackness (8c) splits $c$ into active indices $c^{=0}$ ($f_i(x_I)=0,\lambda_i\le0$) and inactive $c^{>0}$ ($\lambda_i=0$), eq. (9); (2) the safe-distance speed is $\frac{d}{dt}\varrho=\frac{d}{dt}d(x_I,x_G)=-\sum_{i\in c}\lambda_i f_{i,x}^\top\dot x_I$ (eq. (10); the $x_G$ term vanishes because $g_y^\top(x_G)\dot x_G=0$ as $x_G$ stays on $\partial\Omega_{\text{goal}}$); (3) for active $i$, $f_i(x_I)\equiv0$ gives eq. (11): $f_{i,x}^\top\dot x_I=-v_{P_i}f_{i,P}^\top e_{\theta_{P_i}}-f_{i,\theta}v_{P_i}u_{P_i}/\kappa_i-v_{E_j}f_{i,E}^\top u_{E_j}$; (4) minimizing over $u_{P_i}\in\mathcal S_1,u_{E_j}\in\mathcal S_2$ (using $\lambda_i\le0$) yields the min recession speed $V(X'_{cj})=\sum_i\lambda_i(v_{P_i}f_{i,P}^\top e_{\theta_{P_i}}+|f_{i,\theta}|v_{P_i}/\kappa_i+v_{E_j}\|f_{i,E}\|_2)$, and minimizing over all $X'_{cj}\in\mathcal S_{cj}$ (expressed via the KKT system, with $d_x=-d_y$) gives exactly program (7); (5) if $\varrho(X_{cj})>\max\{-VT,0\}$ the safe distance cannot hit zero before some $t^\star\in[0,T]$. Decisive equation: (10)+(11) chain turning the differential game bound into a **static** optimization over KKT-consistent states. $V\le0$ by construction (each $\lambda_i\le0$), so $\max\{-VT,0\}$ means: either the initial margin absorbs the worst decay over the steering horizon, or $V=0$. Analytic, global over $\mathcal S_{cj}$. Remark 1 (p. 6): (7) simplifies greatly when PEF derivatives are closed-form. *Observation*: the hypothesis "there exists $U^1_c$ steering into the ERP-winning set within $T$ regardless of $u_{E_j}$" is itself a reachability statement — nonconstructive in general; Theorems 3-4 make it constructive via explicit heading-chasing strategies.

### 5.5 Lemma 2 (Positional PEF, p. 6, eqs. (12)-(13)) and the initial winning set
**Statement**: $f^{ps}_i(x,X_{ij})=\|x-x_{P_i}\|_2-\alpha_{ij}\|x-x_{E_j}\|_2-r_i$ (eq. 12) is a PEF; its zero locus is a Cartesian (Apollonius) oval (Wasz et al. 2019). Note the region $\{f^{ps}_i\ge0\}$ contains the evader and is bounded because $\alpha_{ij}>1$.
**Proof** (4 steps): (1) condition (2): $f^{ps}_i=0$ forces $x\ne x_{P_i},x\ne x_{E_j}$ (values there are $-\alpha_{ij}\|x_{P_i}-x_{E_j}\|-r_i<0$ and $\|x_{E_j}-x_{P_i}\|-r_i>0$), hence smoothness; (2) condition (3): $f^{ps}_i(x_{E_j})=\|x_{E_j}-x_{P_i}\|-r_i>0$ on $\mathcal S_{ij}$; (3) condition (1), boundedness: polar coordinates at $x_{E_j}$, $x=x_{E_j}+\rho e$, $e=(\cos(\psi+\psi_0),\sin(\psi+\psi_0))$; the boundary solves $\|x_{E_j}+\rho e-x_{P_i}\|-\alpha_{ij}\rho-r_i=0$, giving $\rho=\frac{1}{\alpha_{ij}^2-1}(h_1(\psi)+h_2(\psi))$ with $h_1=(x_{E_j}-x_{P_i})^\top e-\alpha_{ij}r_i$ and $h_2=\sqrt{h_1^2+(\alpha_{ij}^2-1)(\|x_{E_j}-x_{P_i}\|_2^2-r_i^2)}$ (eq. 13; $h_2>0$ real since $\alpha_{ij}>1$ and $\|x_{E_j}-x_{P_i}\|>r_i$) — bounded, positive $\rho$; (4) strict convexity: $\rho^2+2(\frac{d\rho}{d\psi})^2-\rho\frac{d^2\rho}{d\psi^2}>0$ via the same argument as Yan et al. (2022, Lemma 3.1), then strict convexity of $\{f\ge0\}$ follows from Yan et al. (2022, Lemma 2.1). **Tool reuse**: the PEF class extends the 3-D potential function of Yan et al. (2022) to the plane (p. 6).
**Initial ERP-winning set, eqs. (14)-(15)**: with $f^{ps}_c=\{f^{ps}_i\}_{i\in c}$,
$$\mathcal X^{ps}_{cj}=\{X_{cj}\in\mathcal S_{cj}\mid \varrho(X_{cj};f^{ps}_c)>0,\ \theta_{P_i}=\sigma_i(X_{cj}),\ i\in c\},$$
where the **heading function** $\sigma_i$ points each pursuer at the closest enclosure point: $[\cos\sigma_i,\sin\sigma_i]^\top=\frac{x_I-x_{P_i}}{\|x_I-x_{P_i}\|_2}$, with $(x_I,x_G)$ from the positional convex program $\mathcal P^{ps}(X_{cj})$ (eq. 15, i.e., (3) with $f^{ps}_i$).

### 5.6 Theorem 3 (ERP Winning Parameters, State and Strategy — one pursuer, pp. 6-8, eqs. (16)-(20))
**Statement**: For $\mathcal P_c=\{P_i\}$ vs. $E_j$, if (1) **parameters** satisfy
$$\alpha_{ij}>3,\qquad r_i/\kappa_i>C_{M1}(\alpha_{ij}),\qquad C_{M1}(\alpha_{ij})=1+\frac{3\alpha_{ij}^2+4\alpha_{ij}-3}{(\alpha_{ij}-1)^2(\alpha_{ij}-3)}\quad(16)(17)$$
and (2) the **safe distance** satisfies
$$\varrho(X_{ij};f^{ps}_i)>\frac{2\pi r_i/(\alpha_{ij}-1)}{r_i/\kappa_i-C_{M1}(\alpha_{ij})}\qquad(18)$$
then $X_{ij}$ is an ERP winning state and the feedback strategy (19)
$$u_{P_i}=\begin{cases}-\frac{\kappa_i}{v_{P_i}}\frac{e_{IP_i}^{\circ\top}\dot x_I}{d_{IP_i}}, & \theta_{P_i}=\sigma_i(X_{ij}),\\ \operatorname{sgn}(\sin(\sigma_i(X_{ij})-\theta_{P_i})), & \text{otherwise},\end{cases}$$
(with $(x_I,x_G)$ from (15) and $\dot x_I$ given by (20): $\dot x_I=\frac{(\alpha_{ij}a_1^\circ e_{IE_j}^\top+A)\dot x_{E_j}-v_{P_i}a_1^\circ}{d_f\,a_1^\top e_{IG}^\circ}$, $d_f=\|e_{IP_i}-\alpha_{ij}e_{IE_j}\|_2$, $a_1=\frac{e_{IP_i}^\circ e_{IP_i}^\top e_{IG}}{d_{IP_i}}-\frac{\alpha_{ij}e_{IE_j}^\circ e_{IE_j}^\top e_{IG}}{d_{IE_j}}-\frac{d_fa_2e_{IG}^\circ}{1+d_{IG}a_2}$, $A=\frac{\alpha_{ij}d_fe_{IG}^\circ e_{IG}^\top e_{IE_j}^\circ e_{IE_j}^\top}{d_{IE_j}}$, $a_2=\frac{e_{IG}^{\circ\top}H(x_G)e_{IG}^\circ}{\|g_y(x_G)\|_2}$ [all as printed; $x^\circ=[y,-x]^\top$ is clockwise $\pi/2$ rotation; $a_2$ is a boundary-curvature term]) is an ERP winning strategy.
**Proof** (7 steps; subscript-free notation): (1) *heading invariance*: under (19), $\dot x_P\equiv v_Pe_{IP}$ (eq. 21) is preserved, since differentiating gives the identity (22) $=0$ using (1) and (19); (2) *invariance of $\mathcal X^{ps}$*: by (10)-(11) and (12), $\frac{d}{dt}\varrho(X;f^{ps})=\lambda(-v_P+v_E\alpha)=0$ (exact cancellation because $\alpha v_E=v_P$ and the pursuer aims at $x_I$) — the safe distance is conserved, so $X^t\in\mathcal X^{ps}\ \forall t$; (3) *control feasibility* $|u_P|\le1$: compute $\dot x_I$ from the persistent KKT system — parallelism $g_y(x_G)\parallel(x_I-x_G)$ gives (23); $g(x_G)\equiv0$ gives (24); solve for $\dot x_G$ (25); $f^{ps}_x\parallel g_y$ gives (26) with $c_1,\dots,c_4$; $f^{ps}(x_I,X)\equiv0$ gives (27); combining into (28) yields $\dot x_I=k_1(c_1+c_5)^\circ-k_2d_fe_{IG}^\circ$ over $k_3$ (29) with $|k_1|\le2v_P$, $|k_2|\le v_P/d_{IP}+v_P/d_{IE}$ (30); (4) *lower bound on the denominator*: $k_3=\frac{d_f}{d_{IP}}(\gamma_{PG}^2-\frac{\alpha d_{IP}\gamma_{EG}^2}{d_{IE}})-d_f\|c_5\|_2$ (31), $d_f=\gamma_{PG}-\alpha\gamma_{EG}$ (32), and the decisive inequality (33):
$$\gamma_{PG}^2-\frac{\alpha d_{IP}\gamma_{EG}^2}{d_{IE}}=\frac{-r\gamma_{PG}^2+2d_f(\alpha d_{IE}+r)\gamma_{PG}-(\alpha d_{IE}+r)d_f^2}{\alpha d_{IE}}\le\frac{-r(d_f-1)^2+\alpha d_fd_{IE}(d_f-2)}{\alpha d_{IE}}:=-(rk_4/d_{IE}+k_5),$$
with $k_4=(d_f-1)^2/\alpha$, $k_5=d_f(d_f-2)$ (34); the bound is attained at $\gamma_{PG}=1$ and **requires $\alpha>3$** (so $k_4,k_5>0$), giving $|k_3|\ge(rk_4/d_{IE}+k_5)d_f/d_{IP}+d_f\|c_5\|_2$ (35); (5) *$\dot x_I$ bound*: chaining (29), (30), (35) through (36)-(37) with $d_f\in[\alpha-1,\alpha+1]$, $k_6=-2d_f^2+(\alpha+4)d_f+2\alpha^2-2\ge\alpha(\alpha+1)$, $k_7=-2d_f^2+(\alpha+5)d_f+2\alpha^2+2$, and the rational maximizations $\frac{k_6}{\alpha d_fk_4}\le\frac{\alpha^3+12\alpha^2-17\alpha+8}{(\alpha^2-1)(\alpha-2)^2}$, $\frac{k_7}{d_fk_5}\le\frac{\alpha^2+12\alpha-9}{(\alpha-1)^2(\alpha-3)}$ (the latter larger), obtaining $\|\dot x_I\|_2\le\frac{3\alpha^2+4\alpha-3}{(\alpha-1)^2(\alpha-3)}v_P$ (38); then $|u_P|\le\frac{\kappa\|\dot x_I\|_2}{v_Pr}\le\frac{\kappa(3\alpha^2+4\alpha-3)}{r(\alpha-1)^2(\alpha-3)}\le1$ iff (16) (using $d_{IP}\ge r$); (6) *steering phase*: while $\theta_P\ne\sigma(X)$, max-rate turn gives the angle-chasing lower bound (40) $|\dot\theta_P|-|\dot\sigma|\ge\frac{v_P}{r}\big(\frac r\kappa-1-\frac{3\alpha^2+4\alpha-3}{(\alpha-1)^2(\alpha-3)}\big)>0$ using $|\dot\sigma|\le\frac{\|\dot x_I\|_2+v_P}{r}$ (39), so the heading error (at most $\pi$) vanishes within $T:=\pi/(|\dot\theta_P|-|\dot\sigma|)$; meanwhile $\frac{d}{dt}\varrho\ge-|\lambda|(v_P|f_P^\top e_{\theta_P}|+v_E\|f_E\|_2)\ge-\frac{v_P+v_E\alpha}{d_f}\ge\frac{2v_P}{1-\alpha}$ (41), since $|\lambda|=1/d_f$ by (8a) and $d_f\ge\alpha-1$; (7) *synthesis*: requiring $\varrho>\frac{2\pi r}{(\alpha-1)(r/\kappa-C_{M1}(\alpha))}$ (42, equivalent to (18)) makes $\varrho+T\min_{t'}\frac{d}{dt}\varrho>0$, so some $X^{t^\star}\in\mathcal X^{ps}$ is reached with positive margin; conclude via Theorem 2. All steps analytic and global (for all $u_{E_j}$, all $X_{ij}\in\mathcal S_{ij}$ meeting (18)). **Physical meaning**: a sufficiently fast, sufficiently agile (large $r_i/\kappa_i$) pursuer that keeps its nose pointed at the closest point of its Apollonius-oval enclosure can defend the region from any initial state with enough margin; the margin threshold grows like $\frac{2\pi r/(\alpha-1)}{r/\kappa-C_{M1}}$ and blows up as $r/\kappa\downarrow C_{M1}$. *Magnitude check*: $\alpha=5\Rightarrow C_{M1}=1+92/32=3.875$, so (16) demands $r/\kappa>3.875$ (Case 1 uses $r/\kappa=4.873$).
- **Lemma 3 (Relaxed ERP Winning Parameters, p. 8, eq. (43))**: if $X_{ij}\in\mathcal X^{ps}_{ij}$ (heading already aligned) and $\alpha_{ij}>3$, $r_i/\kappa_i\ge\frac{5\alpha_{ij}^2-9\alpha_{ij}+8}{(\alpha_{ij}-1)(\alpha_{ij}-2)^2}$, then $X_{ij}$ is ERP winning with strategy (19). Proof: a sharper chain (44) exploiting $e_{IP}^{\circ\top}\dot x_P=0$ and monotonicity in $d_f$ gives $|u_P|\le\frac{\kappa}{r}\big(\frac{2}{d_f}+\frac{\alpha(2\alpha+d_f)}{d_f(d_f-1)^2}\big)\le\frac{\kappa(5\alpha^2-9\alpha+8)}{r(\alpha-1)(\alpha-2)^2}\le1$. Note (43) is weaker than (16) (e.g., $\alpha=4$: bound $= (80-36+8)/(3\cdot4)=52/12\approx4.33$ vs. $C_{M1}(4)=1+61/9\approx7.78$), but only applies on $\mathcal X^{ps}$; (16) is needed to *reach* $\mathcal X^{ps}$. Visualized in Fig. 3 (p. 9).
- **Remark 2 (p. 8)**: the law (19)/(20) needs the evader's current $\dot x_{E_j}$; with estimation errors one must relax $\theta_{P_i}=\sigma_i(X_{ij})$ and obtain robust conditions — "left for future work".

### 5.7 Theorem 4 (Two pursuers, pp. 8-10, eqs. (45)-(46))
**Statement** ($c=\{1,2\}$): two scenarios. (1) If $\mathcal P^{ps}(X_{cj})$ has a **unique** support constraint (say $P_1$), reduce to the one-pursuer case of Theorem 3: verify $P_1$ first; if it fails, verify $P_2$ (either may win even if the other fails). (2) If $\mathcal P^{ps}(X_{cj})$ has **two** support constraints and the parameters (16) hold for both pursuers separately and
$$\varrho(X_{cj};f^{ps}_c)>\min_{i'\in c}\max_{i\in c}\frac{2\pi r_iv_{P_{i'}}/(\alpha_{i'j}v_{P_i}-v_{P_i})}{r_i/\kappa_i-C_{M2}(\alpha_{1j},\alpha_{2j})},\qquad C_{M2}(\alpha_{1j},\alpha_{2j})=1+\min_{i''\in c}\frac{3\alpha_{i''j}^2+4\alpha_{i''j}-3}{(\alpha_{i''j}-1)^2(\alpha_{i''j}-3)}\quad(45)$$
then $X_{cj}$ is an ERP winning state with cooperative strategy (46) (same structure as (19) for each $i\in c$), with $\dot x_I=\frac{a_1^\circ(\alpha_{2j}e_{IE_j}^\top\dot x_{E_j}-v_{P_2})-a_2^\circ(\alpha_{1j}e_{IE_j}^\top\dot x_{E_j}-v_{P_1})}{a_1^\top a_2^\circ}$, $a_i=e_{IP_i}-\alpha_{ij}e_{IE_j}$.
**Proof** (6 steps): (1) heading invariance (47) as in Theorem 3; (2) invariance: $\frac{d}{dt}\varrho=\sum_{i\in c}\lambda_i(v_E\alpha_ie_{IE}^\top u_E-v_{P_i})\ge\sum_i\lambda_i(v_E\alpha_i-v_{P_i})=0$; (3) $\dot x_I$ from the two active constraints: $\begin{bmatrix}a_1^\top\\a_2^\top\end{bmatrix}\dot x_I+\begin{bmatrix}\alpha_1e_{IE}^\top\dot x_E-e_{IP_1}^\top\dot x_{P_1}\\ \alpha_2e_{IE}^\top\dot x_E-e_{IP_2}^\top\dot x_{P_2}\end{bmatrix}=0$, solved in (48), simplified under (47) to (49); (4) *$\dot x_I$ bound via enclosure geometry*: writing $a_1^\top a_2=d_1d_2\cos\gamma$, $\gamma\in(0,\pi)$, $\|\dot x_I\|_2^2=\frac{k_2^2d_1^2+k_1^2d_2^2-2k_1k_2d_1d_2\cos\gamma}{d_1^2d_2^2\sin^2\gamma}$, and $\frac{d}{d\gamma}\|\dot x_I\|_2^2=\frac{2(k_1d_2\cos\gamma-k_2d_1)(k_2d_1\cos\gamma-k_1d_2)}{d_1^2d_2^2\sin^3\gamma}$ (50); as $\gamma\to0$ (ovals nested) it degenerates to the one-pursuer case bounded by (38); as $\gamma\to\pi$ (ovals externally tangent) $x_I\to x_E$ and $\|\dot x_I\|_2=v_E$; hence $\|\dot x_I\|_2\le\min_{i\in c}\frac{3\alpha_i^2+4\alpha_i-3}{(\alpha_i-1)^2(\alpha_i-3)}v_{P_i}$ (51) and $|u_{P_i}|<1$ under (16) (52); (5) angle chasing: $|\dot\theta_{P_i}|-|\dot\sigma_i|\ge\frac{v_{P_i}}{r_i}\big(\frac{r_i}{\kappa_i}-1-\min_{i'\in c}\frac{v_{P_{i'}}(3\alpha_{i'}^2+4\alpha_{i'}-3)}{v_{P_i}(\alpha_{i'}-1)^2(\alpha_{i'}-3)}\big)>0$, alignment of both headings within $T:=\max_i\pi/(|\dot\theta_{P_i}|-|\dot\sigma_i|)$; (6) safe-distance decay: $\frac{d}{dt}\varrho\ge-(2v_{P_1}|\lambda_1|+2v_{P_2}|\lambda_2|)=2v_{P_1}\lambda_1+2v_{P_2}\lambda_2$ (53); minimizing $\sum 2v_{P_i}\lambda_i$ over the KKT constraint $\lambda_1a_1+\lambda_2a_2+e_{IG}=0$ (54) via its own KKT system (55) gives $2v_{P_1}\lambda_1+2v_{P_2}\lambda_2=z^\top e_{IG}\ge-\|z\|_2$ (56) with $z=(2v_{P_2}a_1^\circ-2v_{P_1}a_2^\circ)/(a_1^\top a_2^\circ)$ (57) — same structure as $\dot x_I$ in (49), so the $\gamma$-argument (58) yields $\frac{d}{dt}\varrho\ge\max_i\frac{2v_{P_i}}{1-\alpha_i}$ (59); combining with $T$ gives (45). Global, analytic, valid for all $u_{E_j}$. **Physical meaning**: two slower-to-turn pursuers can still win jointly; the worst-case recession speed of the joint enclosure is governed by the *better* pursuer's speed margin, and tangency of the two ovals is the degenerate worst case. Visualized in Fig. 4 (p. 10).

### 5.8 Theorem 5 (Task Allocation, p. 11, eq. (60)) and Algorithm 1
**Setting**: bipartite graph $G=(\mathcal V_{\mathcal P}\cup\mathcal V_{\mathcal E},\mathcal E)$, $\mathcal V_{\mathcal P}$ = all nonempty coalitions of size $\le2$, edge $e_{cj}$ iff $\mathcal P_c$ ERP-wins against $E_j$; conflict graph $C=(\mathcal E,\bar{\mathcal E})$ joins edges sharing a pursuer. **BIP (60)**: $\max_{a_{cj}\in\{0,1\}}\sum_{e_{cj}\in\mathcal E}a_{cj}+z(a_{cj})$ s.t. $\sum_{\mathcal P_c}a_{cj}\le1\ \forall E_j$; $\sum_{E_j}a_{cj}\le1\ \forall\mathcal P_c$; $a_{cj}+a_{pq}\le1\ \forall(e_{cj},e_{pq})\in\bar{\mathcal E}$; with $L=1+\max_{\mathcal P_c,E_j}\varrho(X_{cj};f_c)$.
**Theorem 5**: (1) the BIP is **NP-hard** (polynomial reduction of 3-dimensional matching, Karp 1972 — "identical argument to the proof of Theorem 4.1 in Yan et al. (2022)"); (2) $z\equiv0$: solution matches the most defeated evaders; (3) $z(a_{cj})=a_{cj}\varrho(X_{cj};f_c)/(\min\{N_p,N_e\}L)$: most defeated evaders with **maximum** sum of safe distances, because $0\le\sum z<\frac{\sum a_{cj}L}{\min\{N_p,N_e\}L}\le1$ makes the $z$-term strictly dominated by the value of matching one more evader (lexicographic objective); (4) $z$ negated: minimum sum of safe distances. Solvers: Gurobi/Matlab; for large teams the Sequential Matching Algorithm (Yan et al. 2022) is a 1/2-approximation, exact when no 2-pursuer coalition is chosen.
**Algorithm 1 (Multiplayer ERP strategy, p. 11)**: build $\mathcal V_{\mathcal P}$ ($1\le|P_c|\le2$); repeat: test ERP_Winning($X_{cj},f_c$) for all pairs via the steer-to-ERP checks of Theorems 3/4 (parameters + safe distance with positional PEFs); solve BIP (60); matched coalitions run (19) or (46), unmatched players run any strategy; integrate with time step $\Delta$; remove captured/arrived evaders; until $\mathcal V_{\mathcal E}=\emptyset$. Claimed property: monotonically increasing number of guaranteed defeated evaders (p. 11). Coalition reduction cuts the task count from $(2^{N_p}-1)N_e$ to the printed "(N_p+1)/N_pN_e" (p. 11) — evidently a typographical rendering of $N_p(N_p+1)/2\cdot N_e$ coalitions of size $\le2$ (see Section 7).

### 5.9 Proof-gap notes
No outright errors found. Two soft points: (a) Theorem 2's steering hypothesis is existential; its constructive instantiations (Theorems 3-4) only cover the specific heading-chasing laws (19)/(46); (b) the global bound $V$ in (7) presumes KKT multipliers exist for all $X'_{cj}\in\mathcal S_{cj}$ (constraint qualification), which holds where $\varrho>0$ but is not discussed at $\varrho=0$ states; both are consistent with the paper's sufficient-condition framing. Also, formal winning includes the "infinitely delay entry" mode (p. 3): the theorems guarantee $\varrho>0$ for all $t$, not finite-time capture; capture is observed in simulation (Fig. 5) but no capture-time theorem is stated.

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
**Author-stated**: (i) strategy (19)/(20) requires the evader's current control input ($\dot x_{E_j}$); robustness to estimation errors (relaxing $\theta_{P_i}=\sigma_i(X_{ij})$) is "not straightforward" and left for future work (Remark 2, p. 8); (ii) future work: two-car (double-integrator-like) evader dynamics, distributed game versions, and heuristic pruning of coalitions to reduce the NP-hard allocation (Conclusion, p. 11-12); (iii) the allocation BIP is NP-hard (Theorem 5(1)).
**Critical observations**:
1. **Sufficient-condition conservatism**: ERP winning is sufficient, not necessary; the guaranteed-winning region is further shrunk by chained bounds — $\alpha_{ij}>3$ excludes moderate speed ratios, $C_{M1}$ forces $r_i/\kappa_i$ large (e.g., $>7.78$ at $\alpha=4$, $>3.875$ at $\alpha=5$), and (18)/(45) demand large initial margins (p. 6-10). No claim is made that the characterized region is maximal.
2. **Strong information asymmetry**: pursuers continuously need every evader's speed and heading (Section 2.2, p. 2-3; Remark 2), while evaders get positions only — the guarantee is against evaders under this information structure, not against better-informed evaders.
3. **Capture not proven in finite time**: Definition 3 + the invariance proofs give $\varrho>0\ \forall t$; the game's winning definition (p. 3) explicitly admits "infinitely delaying entry", and no capture-time bound is derived for (19)/(46) — capture appears only in the abstract's wording and in simulations.
4. **Printed typo, p. 11**: the task count after coalition reduction is printed as "(N_p + 1)/N_pN_e", which cannot be right as typeset; the count of size-≤2 coalitions is $N_p(N_p+1)/2$, so the intended expression is presumably $\frac{N_p(N_p+1)}{2}N_e$.
5. **Theorem 2's steering hypothesis is existential** (p. 5): outside the specific heading-chasing constructions of Theorems 3-4, verifying it is a reachability problem of the same nature the paper set out to avoid.
6. **Online convex programming**: the strategy needs $(x_I,x_G)$ from (15) and $\dot x_I$ from (20)/(49) at every instant; no computational cost, solver, or real-time timing is reported.
7. **Discontinuous strategy**: (19)/(46) switch between max-rate turning and the heading law at $\theta_{P_i}=\sigma_i$; chattering/implementation issues are not discussed.
8. **Geometry restrictions**: $g\in C^2$, $\Omega_{\text{goal}}$ compact convex; the boundary curvature enters through $a_2=e_{IG}^{\circ\top}H(x_G)e_{IG}^\circ/\|g_y\|_2^2$ in (20), so non-smooth or nonconvex protected regions are out of scope; capture is additionally qualified informally by "the latter is pursuing the former" (p. 2), never formalized.
9. **Evaluation scale**: three small numeric scenarios, no baselines, no repeated trials, key scenario-3 and platform parameters UNREPORTED (p. 11).

## 8. Citation Value
This paper is the multiplayer capstone of the Yan et al. Homicidal-Chauffeur reach-avoid line (companion one-vs-one TAC paper Yan et al. 2024): it introduces pursuit enclosure functions and the enclosure-region-pursuit (ERP) winning concept that converts backward HJI/terminal-surface analysis into a forward, convex-program-based sufficient condition, proves via support-constraint counting that at most two pursuers ever matter per evader, and lifts 1-vs-1/2-vs-1 subgame outcomes to a receding-horizon team strategy through an NP-hard but 1/2-approximable matching problem. It is the natural citation for: forward-analysis winning conditions for car-like pursuers in reach-avoid games; coalition-size reduction in target defense; enclosure/Apollonius-oval-based cooperative strategies with guaranteed winning under asymmetric information (pursuers know evader velocities); and task allocation with lexicographic secondary objectives. Ready-to-adapt citation sentences: "For multiplayer Homicidal Chauffeur reach-avoid games, Yan et al. (2024) proposed pursuit enclosure functions whose enclosure-region-pursuit winning condition is certified by forward analysis — avoiding backward HJI computations — and proved that a pursuit coalition never needs more than two of its pursuers to defend a convex region against one evader." Or: "Yan et al. (2024) synthesized cooperative Dubins-car pursuit strategies via positional enclosure functions, a steer-to-enclosure certification, and a binary-program task allocation solved in a receding-horizon fashion, guaranteeing a monotonically increasing number of defended evaders."
