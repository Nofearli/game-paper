## 2. Research Background
Learning coordination in large-scale multiagent systems (city-level traffic light control, fleet management, multi-robot systems, epidemics control — all cited, p. 1) faces the curse of dimensionality: joint learning fails on the exponentially growing joint state–action space, while independent learning drowns in the non-stationarity of concurrent policy updates (p. 1, citing Nguyen, Nguyen, and Nahavandi 2020). Mean-Field Reinforcement Learning (MFRL) — MF-Q/MF-AC (Yang et al. 2018; Subramanian and Mahajan 2019), model-free MF-RL (Carmona, Laurière, and Tan 2019), adversarial collaborative learning (Luo et al. 2020), multi-type MF (Subramanian et al. 2020) — reduces the population interaction to pairwise interactions between each *central agent* and the *average effect* of its neighbors, which makes deep MARL tractable for swarms (Hüttenrauch et al. 2017; 2019). The paper positions itself inside this lineage and attacks three residual weaknesses of the average-interaction ansatz (p. 1–2): uniform averaging discards per-neighbor influence; the mean-field Taylor expansion is inherently local, so agents beyond each other's neighborhoods never coordinate; and existing MF methods need the global state or neighbors' actions at execution time. The proposed remedy, Hierarchical Mean-Field (HMF) learning, decomposes the population into groups and runs two levels of coordinated learning — bottom-level intra-group coordination and top-level inter-group coordination over per-group mean-field functions — plus a dynamic-grouping mechanism (IPC) to keep the approximation errors of regrouping small.

## 3. Research Problem and Gap
- **Problem**: learn coordinated policies for $N$-agent stochastic games $\langle \mathcal{S}, \mathcal{A}^1,\dots,\mathcal{A}^N, r^1,\dots,r^N, p, \gamma\rangle$ (p. 2, Background: Stochastic Game) at population scales where per-agent neighbor-MF functions are still too costly and where agents hold only local observations; produce execution-time policies that depend on locally accessible information only, while still achieving system-level (population-wide) coordination.
- **[Authors' claim 1]** (p. 1–2): all existing MFRL methods approximate an agent's interactions "merely by their average", so individual neighbors' influences cannot be distinguished — problematic for heterogeneous roles (motivating example: in a prey–predator game each predator's importance varies with its position relative to the prey). **[Verified from printed equations]** eq. (1) (p. 2) averages pairwise Q-functions uniformly, $Q^k(s,a) = \frac{1}{\mathcal{N}^k}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}(k)} Q^k(s,a^k,a^j)$, and eq. (5) (p. 2, MFVFD, Zhang et al. 2021) uses the unweighted neighbor means $\mu_o(o^{-k}), \mu_a(a^{-k})$ — so the uniform-averaging claim is true of the printed baseline constructions; the claim about MAgent-scale behavior is an argument, not a proven fact.
- **[Authors' claim 2]** (p. 1): because the MF approximation "involves local Taylor expansion with respect to each central agent and ignores the high-order terms", it is in principle only valid for *local* interactions, leaving no coordination mechanism at the whole-population level. **[Partially verified]** the Taylor-remainder growth is demonstrated inside the paper itself (p. 4, "Error Reduction": $R_{2,k} = (\delta x^{\mu(x)}_k)^2 \nabla^2_{\mu(x)} \bar{Q}_k(\mu(x)) + o((\delta x^{\mu(x)}_k)^2)$, deferred for the "mild condition" to Yang et al. 2018); the *performance consequence* (population-level inferiority) is asserted and only supported empirically.
- **[Authors' claim 3]** (p. 1, citing Zhang et al. 2021): existing MFRL methods "require the availability of the global state or the actions of neighbors for action selection in the execution process", limiting them to perfect communication / global observability. **[Verified from printed equations]** the background MF-Q recurrence (eqs. (2)–(4), p. 2) is indexed on the global state $s$; MFVFD's eq. (5) needs neighbor observation/action means $(o^{-k}, a^{-k})$. Note the precision of the claim: it is *disjunctive* ("global state **or** actions of neighbors"), and MFVFD is itself the local-observation branch — the claim is fair as printed.
- **Gap filled**: (i) replace the per-agent neighbor MF function by one **Group MF (GMF)** function per group and add a **top level** that coordinates groups over their GMF functions (multi-scale coordination: individuals → groups → population); (ii) weight group members by an **attention mechanism** instead of a uniform mean; (iii) act at execution from the **local** Q-function only (partial observability / constrained communication); (iv) **dynamic grouping** with KL-based policy consistency (IPC) to bound the error injected by regrouping. No prior work cited combines group-level MF functions with inter-group coordination (Related Work, p. 7: "Unlike all these studies that the MF function approximates the interactions with the neighbors for each agent, HMF features a hierarchical learning scheme…").

## 4. Core Contributions
1. **Modeling (value factorization)**: individual joint Q-function split into a local part and an individual-MF (IMF) part, $Q^k(s,a) = Q^k_{\mathrm{LOC}}(x_k) + Q^k_{\mathrm{IMF}}(\mu(x), x_k)$, $x_k=(o_k,a_k)$ (eq. (6), p. 2) — with the IMF conditioned on the neighborhood average *including the agent itself* (p. 2–3), in contrast to MFVFD's neighbor-only mean of eq. (5).
2. **Theory (group mean-field collapse, derivation chain eqs. (7)–(11), p. 3)**: the influence-weighted sum of all individual joint Q-functions of a group (eq. (7)) is shown to reduce, under an additive virtual-reward decomposition and a first-order Taylor argument, to $\ Q_{\mathrm{GRP}}(x) = \sum_{k\in N}\lambda_k Q^k_{\mathrm{LOC}}(x_k) + 2\,Q_{\mathrm{GMF}}(\mu(x))$ (eq. (11)) — i.e., one Group MF function $\bar{Q}\equiv Q_{\mathrm{GMF}}(\mu(x))$ replaces the sum of per-agent IMF functions, with the group mean field entering with weight 2. This is the paper's central analytic identity; it is a heuristic derivation, **not** a numbered theorem, and it carries an implicit homogeneity assumption (see §5.2 and §7).
3. **Algorithm (HMF, Algorithm 1, p. 4)**: DQN-style value learning with (a) per-group TD losses $\mathcal{L}^{(i)}_{\mathrm{GRP}}$ (eq. (12)), (b) a population-level loss $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TOT}}$ over $Q_{\mathrm{TOT}} = \sum_{i\in m} Q^{(i)}_{\mathrm{GMF}}(\mu(x^{(i)}))$ (eqs. (13)–(14)), (c) a central-critic variant over the joint group means for competitive tasks (eqs. (15)–(16)), (d) attention-computed influence weights $\lambda_k$, (e) a Taylor-remainder regularization $\alpha\sum_{k\in\mathcal{N}}(x_k-\mu(x))^2$, and (f) execution purely from $Q^k_{\mathrm{LOC}}$ ($\epsilon$-greedy) — enabling partial observability.
4. **Mechanism (IPC dynamic grouping, p. 4–5)**: K-Means-style clustering of agents on observation–action pairs with a farthest-point re-seeding rule (eq. (17) and the $D_k=\arg\min\|x_k-\mu_r\|_2^2$ / $d(k,i)=|x_k-\bar{x}^{(i)}|_2^2$ assignments), plus minimization of $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_k\|\bar{\pi}^{(i)}_{\mathrm{GMF}})$ (eq. (18)) to pull new members' policies toward the group mean historical policy, reducing regrouping-induced approximation error.
5. **Experiments**: HMF and its IPC variant beat MADDPG/VDN/MFVFD on MPE (Spread, Pursuit Evasion), DQN/MFQ/MFVFD (plus MFAC/QMIX/VDN in the figure legends) on MAgent Battle (200 v 100), and are the only method approaching the 0.6 near-optimal average payoff in a 100-agent El Farol-style Bar game; scalability to $n\in\{200,600,1000,1400\}$ agents; ablations over group count, regularization, and top-level availability (pp. 5–7). No convergence guarantee of any kind is offered — all guarantees are empirical.

## 5. Key Mathematical Results (exhaustive)
The paper contains **no formal theorem/lemma/proposition/corollary environments and no convergence proofs**; its analytic content is a chain of derivation equations (6)–(11), the learning objectives (12)–(16), a heuristic error analysis, and the grouping/consistency constructions (17)–(18). All are recorded below with assumptions and gaps. *What is mean-fielded at which level*: (L1, individual) each agent's interaction with its neighborhood is averaged into $Q^k_{\mathrm{IMF}}(\mu(x),x_k)$, with $\mu(x)$ the **attention-weighted** mean of group members' observation–action pairs; (L2, group) the weighted sum of individual IMF functions collapses into **one** GMF function per group, $\bar{Q}(\mu(x))\equiv Q_{\mathrm{GMF}}(\mu(x))$ (this is the key contrast with MFVFD, which maintains one MF function per agent); (L3, population) groups coordinate through $Q_{\mathrm{TOT}}=\sum_i Q^{(i)}_{\mathrm{GMF}}$ or a central critic over the $m$ group means.

### 5.0 Setup and inherited MF machinery (p. 2, eqs. (1)–(5))
- **Stochastic game** (p. 2): $\langle \mathcal{S}, \mathcal{A}^1,\dots,\mathcal{A}^N, r^1,\dots,r^N, p,\gamma\rangle$, $\gamma\in[0,1)$; $r^k:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}^1\times\dots\times\mathcal{A}^N\to\mathbb{R}$; $p:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}^1\times\dots\times\mathcal{A}^N\to\Omega(\mathcal{S})$; joint policy $\pi\triangleq[\pi^1,\dots,\pi^N]$; value $v^k_\pi(s)=\sum_{t=0}^\infty\gamma^t\mathbb{E}_{\pi,p}[r^k_t\,|\,s_0=s]$; Bellman pair $Q^k_\pi(s,a)=r^k(s,a)+\gamma\mathbb{E}_{s'\sim p}[v^k_\pi(s')]$, $v^k_\pi(s)=\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi}[Q^k_\pi(s,a)]$. Local-information input converts this to Partially Observable Stochastic Games (citing Yang and Wang 2020) — the formal scaffold of everything that follows.
- **Pairwise decomposition (eq. (1))**: $Q^k(s,a)=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}^k}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}(k)}Q^k(s,a^k,a^j)$, $\mathcal{N}^k$ = number of neighbors, $\mathcal{N}(k)$ = neighbor index set (Yang et al. 2018 lineage).
- **MF Q-learning recurrence (eq. (2))**: $Q^k_{t+1}(s,a^k,\bar{a}^k)=(1-\alpha)Q^k_t(s,a^k,\bar{a}^k)+\alpha[r^k_t+\gamma v^k_t(s')]$ with mean action $\bar{a}^k=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}^k}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}(k)}a^j$; **value (eq. (3))**: $v^k_t(s')=\sum_{a^k}\pi^k_t(a^k|s',\bar{a}^k)\,\mathbb{E}_{a^{-k}\sim\pi^{-k}_t}[Q^k_t(s',a^k,\bar{a}^k)]$; **Boltzmann policy (eq. (4))**: $\pi^k_t(a^k|s,\bar{a}^k)=\frac{\exp(-\beta Q^k_t(s,a^k,\bar{a}^k))}{\sum_{a^{k'}\in\mathcal{A}^k}\exp(-\beta Q^k_t(s,a^{k'},\bar{a}^k))}$ — global-state MF-Q baseline that HMF's claim 3 targets.
- **MFVFD decomposition (eq. (5), Zhang et al. 2021)**: $Q^k(s,a)\approx Q^k_{\mathrm{LOC}}(o^k,a^k)+Q^k_{\mathrm{NB}}(\mu_o(o^{-k}),\mu_a(a^{-k}))$ — local + neighbor-MF; the direct predecessor that HMF generalizes (neighbor-only → group mean incl. self; per-agent MF → per-group MF).

### 5.1 Individual decomposition (p. 2, eq. (6))
$Q^k(s,a) = Q^k_{\mathrm{LOC}}(x_k) + Q^k_{\mathrm{IMF}}(\mu(x), x_k)$, where $x_k=(o_k,a_k)$ and $\mu(x)$ is "the group's observation–action distribution"; $Q^k_{\mathrm{LOC}}$ is conditioned on own information only, $Q^k_{\mathrm{IMF}}$ on the average information in agent $k$'s neighborhood *including its neighbors and itself* (p. 2–3). Interpretation: execution uses $Q^k_{\mathrm{LOC}}(x_k)$ only (partial observability), while the IMF term shapes learning.

### 5.2 Group mean-field collapse — the central derivation (p. 3, eqs. (7)–(11))
- **Group value (eq. (7))**: $Q_{\mathrm{GRP}}(x) = \sum_{k\in N}\lambda_k\left(Q^k_{\mathrm{LOC}}(x_k)+Q^k_{\mathrm{IMF}}(\mu(x),x_k)\right)$, with influence weights $\lambda_k\in[0,1]$, $\sum_{k\in N}\lambda_k=1$ (implemented via attention, Vaswani et al. 2017, p. 4).
- **Virtual-reward additive decomposition (eq. (8))**: let $r(\mu(x),x_k)$ be a virtual interaction reward whose expected discounted sum equals $Q^k_{\mathrm{IMF}}$; *assuming* $r(\mu(x_t),x_{k,t}) = r(\mu(x_t)) + r(x_{k,t})$ (neighborhood effect + self effect), then
  $$\sum_{k\in N}\lambda_k Q^k_{\mathrm{IMF}}(\mu(x),x_k)=\sum_{k\in N}\lambda_k\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{t=1}^\infty\gamma^{t-1}\big(r(\mu(x_t))+r(x_{k,t})\big)\Big] = \bar{Q}(\mu(x)) + \sum_{k\in N}\lambda_k\bar{Q}_k(x_k),$$
  with $\bar{Q}(\mu(x))=\mathbb{E}\big[\sum_{t=1}^\infty\gamma^{t-1}r(\mu(x_t))\big]$ and $\bar{Q}_k=\mathbb{E}\big[\sum_{t=1}^\infty\gamma^{t-1}r(x_{k,t})\big]$. As printed, the third line of (8) writes the first expectation as $\mathbb{E}[\sum_{t=1}^\infty\gamma^{t-1}r(\mu(x_{t=1}))]$ — the subscript "$x_{t=1}$" is a typographical oddity meaning $\mu$ evaluated along the trajectory from $t=1$ (proof gap: notation, p. 3).
- **Fluctuation representation (eq. (9))**: $x_k = \mu(x) + \delta\bar{x}_k$ **where** $\mu(x)=\sum_{k\in N}\lambda_k x_k$ — i.e., deviations are measured from the *weighted* mean.
- **Taylor collapse (eq. (10))** — decisive intermediate step: assuming sufficient differentiability of $\bar{Q}_k$,
  $$\bar{Q}(\mu(x))+\sum_{k\in N}\lambda_k\Big[\bar{Q}_k(\mu(x)) + \delta x^{\mu(x)}_k\nabla_{\mu(x)}\bar{Q}_k(\mu(x)) + R_{2,k}\Big] = \bar{Q}(\mu(x))+\sum_{k\in N}\big(\lambda_k\bar{Q}_k(\mu(x))+\lambda_k R_{2,k}\big) \approx \bar{Q}(\mu(x))+\sum_{k\in N}\lambda_k\bar{Q}_k(\mu(x)) = 2\bar{Q}(\mu(x)).$$
  Justifications given: (i) the first-order term vanishes because $\sum_{k\in N}\lambda_k\delta x^{\mu(x)}_k = 0$ by eq. (9) — *exact*, since it is the definition of the weighted mean; (ii) the remainder $R_{2,k}$ is "a small fluctuation under some mild condition (the proof refers to (Yang et al. 2018))" — *inherited, not re-derived*; (iii) the final equality $2\bar{Q}$ uses $\sum_k\lambda_k\bar{Q}_k(\mu(x))=\bar{Q}(\mu(x))$, i.e., **all agents' MF value functions coincide once conditioned on the group mean** — an implicit agent-homogeneity assumption nowhere stated or defended (proof gap: this identification is exactly what the paper's own heterogeneous-influence motivation, p. 1, calls into question; see §7).
- **Resulting group value (eq. (11))**: $Q_{\mathrm{GRP}}(x) = \sum_{k\in N}\lambda_k Q^k_{\mathrm{LOC}}(x_k) + 2Q_{\mathrm{GMF}}(\mu(x))$, where $\bar{Q}\equiv Q_{\mathrm{GMF}}$ is baptized the **Group MF (GMF) function**. The support argument for $\bar{Q}$ capturing group characteristics (p. 3): $\mu(x)=\sum_k\lambda_k x_k = N\times\lambda\times\bar{x}$ with $\bar{x}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_k x_k$ "can be seen as the empirical distribution of the population action-observation" — exact only for uniform $\lambda_k=1/N$; with attention weights $\mu(x)$ is a weighted mean, not the empirical mean (proof gap, p. 3, between eqs. (10) and (11)). *Physical meaning* of the factor 2: the group mean field is counted twice in $Q_{\mathrm{GRP}}$ — once as the (approximate) common part of the individual IMF values and once explicitly — so the population term carries double weight relative to the local terms in both the value and the TD targets below.
- **Extensibility**: the derivation needs only (i) additive virtual-reward decomposition, (ii) differentiability, (iii) mean-centered deviations, (iv) homogeneous conditional MF values, and (v) bounded $R_{2,k}$; it would carry over to any grouped partition with well-defined means, but the factor-2 weight and the remainder bound are specific to this heuristic argument.

### 5.3 Learning rules (p. 4, eqs. (12)–(14), Algorithm 1)
- **Group-level TD (eq. (12))**: $\mathcal{L}^{(i)}_{\mathrm{GRP}} = \mathbb{E}_{x^{(i)}_t,x^{(i)}_{t+1}}\big[Q^{(i)}_{\mathrm{GRP}}(x^{(i)}_t)-y^{(i)}_t\big]^2$ with TD target
  $$y^{(i)}_t = r^{(i)}_t + \gamma\sum_{k\in\mathcal{N}(i)}\lambda_{k,t+1}\hat{Q}^k_{\mathrm{LOC}}(x^{(i)}_{k,t+1}) + 2\hat{Q}^{(i)}_{\mathrm{GMF}}(\mu(x^{(i)}_{k,t+1)})),$$
  where $r^{(i)}_t$ is the **sum of the group members' rewards** at step $t$, $\hat{Q}_{\mathrm{LOC}},\hat{Q}_{\mathrm{GMF}}$ are target networks, and the weights $\lambda_{k,t+1}$ are time-indexed (recomputed by attention each step). As printed, the GMF target argument carries a stray member index "$\mu(x^{(i)}_{k,t+1)}$" — meaningless per-member index, a typo (p. 4).
- **Population level (eq. (13))**: $Q_{\mathrm{TOT}}(x^{(1)},\dots,x^{(m)}) = \sum_{i\in m}Q^{(i)}_{\mathrm{GMF}}(\mu(x^{(i)}))$, $m$ = number of groups; **total loss (eq. (14))**: $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TOT}} = \mathbb{E}_{\{x^{(j)}_t,x^{(j)}_{t+1}\}_{j=1}^m}\big(Q_{\mathrm{TOT}}(\{x^{(j)}_t\}_{j=1}^m)-y_t\big)^2$ with $y_t = r_t + \gamma\sum_{i\in m}\hat{Q}^{(i)}_{\mathrm{GMF}}(\mu(x^{(i)}_{t+1}))$ and $r_t$ = **sum of all agents' rewards** at step $t$. Note the double counting is inherited at the top level: the GMF networks are trained by both $\mathcal{L}^{(i)}_{\mathrm{GRP}}$ (through the $2\hat{Q}^{(i)}_{\mathrm{GMF}}$ term) and $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TOT}}$.
- **Algorithm 1 (p. 4)**: initialize $Q^k_{\mathrm{LOC},\theta},\hat{Q}^k_{\mathrm{LOC},\hat\theta}$ for all agents and $Q^{(i)}_{\mathrm{GMF},\phi},\hat{Q}^{(i)}_{\mathrm{GMF},\hat\phi}$ for all groups; per episode: every agent samples $a_k$ from $Q^k_{\mathrm{LOC},\theta}$ with $\epsilon$-greedy; store $\langle o,a,r,o'\rangle$ (joint quantities dimensioned $[o_k]^{N\times M}_{k=1}$ — $N\times M$ = agents × groups, so $N$ = group size here, colliding with $N$ = whole population in eq. (7) and $m$ = #groups in eq. (13); also Algorithm 1 uses $M$ where eq. (13) uses $m$) into replay buffer $\mathcal{D}$; sample mini-batch of $K$ experiences; get next actions $a'$ from the target local nets; **for each group $i$**: set $Q^{(i)}_{\mathrm{GRP}}$ per eq. (11) and minimize $\mathcal{L}^{(i)}_{\mathrm{GRP}}$; then minimize $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TOT}}$; soft-update targets $\hat\theta_k\leftarrow\tau\theta_k+(1-\tau)\hat\theta_k$, $\hat\phi^{(i)}\leftarrow\tau\phi^{(i)}+(1-\tau)\hat\phi^{(i)}$. Information structure: acting = local only; learning = per-group supervisor sees $\mu(x^{(i)})$ (the weighted group mean) and group reward sums; top level sees all group means. Termination/reward are environment-specific (see §6). **No step-size or convergence analysis is given for the coupled (per-group + total) updates** — the two losses share GMF parameters with no stated compatibility guarantee (proof gap, p. 4, lines 10–14).

### 5.4 Competitive variant — central critic over group means (p. 4, eqs. (15)–(16))
"To address the coordination problem in competitive tasks", the top level is expanded with a central critic evaluating the **joint mean observation–action of multiple groups**: $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TOT}} = \mathbb{E}_{\mu(x^{(i)}_t),\mu(x^{(i)}_{t+1})}\big[Q_{\mathrm{GMF}}(\mu(x^{(1)}_t),\dots,\mu(x^{(m)}_t)) - y_t\big]$ (eq. (15)) with $y_t = r_t + \gamma\hat{Q}_{\mathrm{GMF}}(\mu(x^{(1)}_{t+1}),\dots,\mu(x^{(m)}_{t+1}))$ (eq. (16)). Proof gaps as printed: (i) eq. (15) **lacks the square** of a squared-loss (compare eqs. (12), (14)) — evident typo; (ii) the symbol $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TOT}}$ is reused for two different objectives (eqs. (14) and (15)), and $Q_{\mathrm{GMF}}$ silently changes meaning from the per-group function of eq. (11) to a single $m$-input central critic; (iii) whether the per-group losses (12) are still applied in this mode is not stated (p. 4).

### 5.5 Error-reduction regularization (p. 4, "Error Reduction in the MF Approximation")
With sufficient differentiability, $R_{2,k} = \big(\delta x^{\mu(x)}_k\big)^2\nabla^2_{\mu(x)}\bar{Q}_k(\mu(x)) + o\big((\delta x^{\mu(x)}_k\big)^2\big)$, so the neglected error $\sum_k\lambda_k R_{2,k}$ "can be enormous especially in large-scale scenarios" since it grows quadratically in the deviation $\delta x^{\mu(x)}_k$ of an agent from the group mean. Remedy: append the regularization term $\alpha\sum_{k\in\mathcal{N}}(x_k-\mu(x))^2$ ("keeping the distance of agents to the center point within an acceptable range") to constrain learning. This is a heuristic control of the Taylor remainder, not a bound: no value of $\alpha$, no norm definition ($x_k$ mixes discrete actions and observations), and no resulting error constant are given (proof gap, p. 4). Notation collision: $\alpha$ is the MF learning rate in eq. (2) and the regularization coefficient here.

### 5.6 Dynamic grouping and IPC (p. 4–5, eqs. (17)–(18))
- **Grouping (K-Means style)**: at each episode start a randomly chosen agent seeds the first group's central node $\mu$; each agent's distance to the current central node is $D_k=\arg\min\|x_k-\mu_r\|_2^2$ ($r$ = index of the central node; the $\arg\min$ variable is implicit — over candidate centers $r$; printed without its subscript, p. 5 — minor gap); after each iteration the node with maximum distance in $D_k$ becomes a new central node (farthest-point re-seeding); the group mean is (eq. (17))
  $$\bar{x}^{(i)} = \left(\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}^{(i)}}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{N}(i)}(o_k),\ \frac{1}{\mathcal{N}^{(i)}}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{N}(i)}(a_k)\right),$$
  the pair (mean observation, mean action) of group $i$; agent $k$ is assigned to the group minimizing $d(k,i)=|x_k-\bar{x}^{(i)}|_2^2$, and centers are updated with the new members; "similar agents can be clustered in a group after several iterations" (p. 5).
- **Information and Policy Consistency (IPC)**: regrouping makes group policies diverse and "hard for the group MF policy to adapt to new added agents"; each group $i$ maintains a mean historical policy $\bar{\pi}^{(i)}_{\mathrm{GMF}}$ and each agent's policy update is regularized by (eq. (18))
  $$D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(\pi_k\,\|\,\bar{\pi}^{(i)}_{\mathrm{GMF}}\big) = \mathbb{E}\big[\log\pi_k - \log\bar{\pi}^{(i)}_{\mathrm{GMF}}\big],$$
  pulling individual policies toward the group mean to "lower the new agents' influence on the group policy" (p. 5). Proof gap: the weight of this KL term relative to the DQN losses, and how it interfaces with $Q$-learning at all (policies here are $\epsilon$-greedy over $Q^k_{\mathrm{LOC}}$), are not specified in the main text — the "overall IPC framework and the pseudo code" are deferred to the Appendix, which is not contained in this PDF (p. 5).

## 7. Limitations and Open Issues
- **Author-stated**: future evaluation "in other large-scale domains such as city-level light or traffic control" (p. 7). Implicitly: all evidence is empirical; no theory of when HMF converges or by how much its MF approximation errs.
- **No formal guarantees (critical)**: the central identity (eqs. (10)–(11)) rests on (i) the additive virtual-reward decomposition $r(\mu(x_t),x_{k,t})=r(\mu(x_t))+r(x_{k,t})$ — a strong separability assumption on interactions; (ii) the discarded Taylor remainder $R_{2,k}$, whose "mild condition" is inherited from Yang et al. 2018 without restatement; and (iii) the unstated homogeneity identification $\bar{Q}_k(\mu(x))=\bar{Q}(\mu(x))\ \forall k$ (p. 3). Point (iii) sits in tension with the paper's own headline motivation — that agents have *different* influences and roles — since heterogeneous influence is precisely what makes conditional MF value functions differ across agents. The factor-2 weighting of the GMF term is therefore heuristic; nothing bounds $Q_{\mathrm{GRP}}$'s approximation error against the true group value.
- **Untracked coupled optimization**: GMF networks receive gradients from both $\mathcal{L}^{(i)}_{\mathrm{GRP}}$ (weight-2 target term, eq. (12)) and $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TOT}}$ (eq. (14)); no analysis of the fixed point of this coupling, and $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TOT}}$'s target $y_t$ reuses the same GMF targets being trained (moving-target issue unaddressed).
- **Typographical/notation defects** (all verified against page renders): eq. (15) missing its square (p. 4); $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TOT}}$ reused for two objectives and $Q_{\mathrm{GMF}}$ changing type between eqs. (11) and (15) (p. 4); $\mu(x_{t=1})$ in eq. (8) (p. 3); stray member index in $\mu(x^{(i)}_{k,t+1})$ of the TD target (p. 4); $\mu(x)=N\times\lambda\times\bar{x}$ exact only for uniform weights (p. 3); $\delta\bar{x}_k$ vs. $\delta x^{\mu(x)}_k$ inconsistency between eqs. (9) and (10); $\alpha$ doubled as learning rate (eq. (2)) and regularization coefficient (p. 4); $N$/$\mathcal{N}$/$N\times M$/$m$/$M$ population-vs-group index collisions (eqs. (7), (12), (13), Alg. 1); "$D_k=\arg\min\|x_k-\mu_r\|_2^2$" without the minimized-over variable (p. 5); "HFM" for HMF and "prefect communication", "the the" (pp. 1, 4, 5).
- **Undocumented baselines**: MFAC and QMIX appear in every legend of Figs. 3–4 but neither is described in the text and QMIX is not in the references (pp. 6–7).
- **Reproducibility**: all hyper-parameters (learning rates, network sizes, $\gamma$, batch size, $\tau$, attention architecture, group sizes used in the main experiments, capture threshold in Pursuit Evasion, MAgent battle termination) live in an Appendix absent from this PDF; no seeds/std protocol for MAgent/Bar; no wall-clock or memory measurements, although scalability is the paper's selling point; the MFVFD failure in MAgent is explained only by an unquantified hypothesis (p. 6).
- **Conceptual scope**: the top level (eq. (13)) sums per-group GMF values with no inter-group influence model beyond reward sums; the competitive central critic (eq. (15)) evaluates only the $m$ group means, discarding within-group dispersion exactly where competitive heterogeneity would matter; the IPC policy-consistency term could suppress the very diversity the attention mechanism is meant to exploit (interplay unexamined); grouping runs at episode starts, so mid-episode regime shifts are not tracked (p. 5).
- **Reward-shaping dependence**: group/top-level targets use summed rewards $r^{(i)}_t, r_t$, which presumes a common team reward signal — precisely the condition under which VDN was excluded in MAgent (p. 6), so HMF's advantage over VDN in decomposable-reward settings remains untested.

## 8. Citation Value
This is the reference implementation of *hierarchical/group-structured mean-field MARL*: it is the natural citation when a large-population learning or pursuit problem (e.g., many-pursuer interception, swarm coverage, mass dynamics) requires coordination beyond a single neighbor-averaging layer, and when execution must rely on local observations only. It also anchors the "uniform averaging discards per-neighbor influence" argument (attention-weighted MF) and the El Farol/Bar-game evidence for MF methods in mixed cooperative-competitive populations. Suggested citation sentences: "Yu (AAAI 2023) factorizes each agent's value into a local term plus an attention-weighted group mean-field term and coordinates groups through their group mean-field functions, scaling cooperative and mixed competitive learning to 1400 agents." / "Group-level mean-field abstractions with inter-group coordination have been shown to outperform per-agent mean-field Q-learning in large-population benchmarks (Yu 2023)."
