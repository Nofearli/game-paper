# Visualization Guidelines for Game-Paper Figures

Recurring figure types in the 117-paper library, their conventions as actually drawn, what each must show to function as evidence, and exemplar papers. Implement with `scripts/simulation_plotting.py` (the style contract below is that script's, and it points back to this file). Traceability: PXXX (+page where the archive records it).

**The governing skill rule** (references/reviewer_checklist.md, item 11): figures–equations consistency — symbols in figures match symbols in the text; captions self-contained; **barriers/regions drawn with geometrically correct proportions (verify constants, not aesthetics)**. Concretely: equal axis aspect for all geometry plots, Apollonius circles/barriers plotted from their equations (never sketched), and player symbols placed at the exact coordinates used in the simulation. The archive repeatedly catches violations of the spirit of this rule — P012's Fig. 8 caption duplicating Fig. 5's, P091's ρ₃ = 1.6 typo for ρ₆, P011's Case 2 initial state sitting inside its own detection set — and a figure that contradicts the printed parameters is exactly the kind of flaw this rule prevents.

## 1. Trajectory plots (2-D planar and snapshot sequences)

**Conventions observed.** Color coding by role with both players visible per panel; evader blue / pursuers red is the most common assignment (P058 Sec. VI-A: "evaders blue, pursuers red"; P059 Fig. 9: red/black = planned defender/attacker, blue = actual, dotted = predicted); triangles for target points (P058: cyan/black triangles = evader's FS/RS targets); timestamps under each snapshot (P013 Fig. 5: t = 0, 8.5, 9.8, 12, 16.5 s; P027 Fig. 9: k = 0, 3, 6, 8 s; P084 Fig. 7: t = 18, 33, 142 s; P014 Fig. 4: t = 0, 22.7, 44.5, 68.2 s). Capture events are marked distinctly: red star at the capture point (P067 Fig. 8(c), P107: initiation star / termination diamond / penetration point black dot), distance dropping below the capture radius as the certification (P013 Fig. 6, P062 Fig. 8(d)).

**Companion distance/time curves are near-mandatory.** Nearly every trajectory figure in the library is paired with a separation-vs-time panel: pursuer–evader distances with the capture radius as a dashed horizontal line (P027 Fig. 3b Dis1/Dis2/Dis3; P107 Figs. 7b/8b with the 2r interception level dashed; P026 Figs. 8–13(b): attacker–target and attacker–defender separation vs. time step, capture radius dashed, dip-and-recover showing the near-miss); P062 Fig. 8(d) is the 3-D exemplar: dual-axis plot (left: min pursuer–evader distance; right: evader–target distance) with dashed lines at r_s = 5 m and r_c = 0.5 m and a vertical magenta marker at capture.

**Evidence requirements.** (i) Equal aspect ratio and metric axes — the geometry claims (capture radii, dominance regions) must be measurable off the plot. (ii) Initial positions printed in the caption or a table (the archive flags their absence as a reproducibility gap in P028's hardware scenarios, P033's Scenario B, P053's Scenario III). (iii) Markers at sample intervals, not dense polylines (the script's `plot_trajectory` dots every 20th point). (iv) Show at least one non-optimal-opponent run when claiming feedback robustness (P058 Sim 2; P050 S2/S3). (v) In 3-D, project or render consistently and state the projection: P019 explicitly annotates its Fig. 6 "apparent re-entry into D̄_0 is a projection artifact from R^6 to R^2" — a caption that pre-empts a reviewer objection. Exemplars: P013 (swarm phases), P033 (straight cooperative vs. curved individual trajectories — shape itself is the evidence), P107 (x_I jumps at boundary switches, Fig. 7c).

## 2. Dominance-region / barrier / Apollonius schematics

**Conventions observed.** Regions shaded at low alpha with solid black or colored boundary curves; dashed vs. solid distinguishes single-defender from cooperative or from predicted-vs-executed (P089 Fig. 5: blue dashed = single-defender optimal, red dashed = attacker vs. single defender, blue solid = cooperative two-defender, red solid = attacker vs. cooperation, with an enlarged inset of the capture points Q, Q1, Q2 — the inset is what proves the ordinate ordering claim); Apollonius circles drawn as full circles with foci marked (the script's `apollonius_circle` + `demo_apollonius`: circle lw 1.4, fill alpha 0.08, P marker "o", E marker "s", LOS dashed black, speed ratio in the title); barrier pieces labeled per equation (P073 Figs. 2–6: translucent bisector-plane pieces B¹, B², B³, barrier point/arc/spherical patch per theorem, coalition prism W_P; P099: barrier composed of two Apollonius arcs anchored at ATL endpoints plus a central hyperbola); tangent constructions annotated explicitly (P025 Fig. 5(a): magnified barrier with tangent circle; P053 Fig. 7: tangent geometry of Theorem 3).

**Evidence requirements.** (i) Circles/ovals/hyperbolas plotted from their exact equations with the printed parameters — a schematic that is not metrically correct is decoration, and reviewer_checklist item 11 forbids it. (ii) Label every curve/region with the same symbol used in the theorem (P012's Ω_DDR described as "the ADR related to the pair" in its Sec. 5.2 is the cautionary example of symbol-text contradiction). (iii) When a figure carries a legend citing a theorem, make the citation match the caption — P008's Fig. 8 legend says "Theorem 4" while the caption cites Theorem 5 (recorded erratum). (iv) Show parameter sensitivity as a small multiples row, not a single instance: P025 Fig. 3 sweeps φ_D ∈ {0.1π, 0.3π, 0.5π} × ν ∈ {1, 0.3}; P092 Fig. 9 overlays the Apollonius circle Apol(T,W), both limiting paths, observation disks, and the shaded "Candidate Optimal Headings" wedge. Exemplars: P053 (Figs. 1–5, the isochrone families and the 16-case grid Fig. 5(a)–(p)), P012 (composite barriers), P073 (3-D barrier construction), P023.

## 3. Value-function / winning-set 2-D maps

**Conventions observed.** Contour plots of the (viscosity) value with the zero-sublevel boundary emphasized in a single strong color (P050 Fig. 3(a): contour of V with the red zero-sublevel boundary; Fig. 3(b): the coalition partition {W_D_I}); nested sublevel sets drawn as concentric shaded regions (P021 Fig. 7: S_0 ⊂ S_1.55 ⊂ S_2.5 with the tangency point y_m and the KKT gradient arrow from Fig. 8's dotted −∂W/∂y); HJI-derived sets as unions of labeled subregions (P040 Fig. 2: defender-winning sets F_D, R_D, and the composed W_D sliced at a fixed attacker coordinate — the slice convention must be printed since a 4-D set is shown in 2-D); winning-region maps colored by owner (P058 Fig. 6(e)/(f): blue = evader winning region, computed numerically as initial positions whose I_R0 ∈ R, with r = 3 vs. r = 1 panels proving the radius sensitivity).

**Evidence requirements.** (i) State the slice/projection and the grid resolution — P040's own disclosure that the zero sublevel set underapproximates near F_D ∩ F (with the ±ϵ fix) is the honesty standard for all level-set maps. (ii) Overlay the initial conditions of the trajectory figures on the region map so the reader can verify region-membership consistency (P058 4b does this implicitly; P011's four cases would benefit from it). (iii) If areas are claimed, print them (P058 concedes area quantification "not straightforward" — an admission, not an excuse). Exemplars: P050, P040, P021, P058.

## 4. Parameter-regime diagrams

**Conventions observed.** Axes are the two most decision-relevant parameters (speed ratio vs. team size, speed vs. perimeter fraction); each algorithm gets a frontier curve; theoretical impossibility boundaries overlie the algorithmic frontiers in a distinct constant style (P008 Figs. 8–12: the green constant-v = 1 curve is the Theorem-5 impossibility boundary; Dec-Sweep/Dec-CaC/Coop-CaC frontiers linear in M; SNP curves piecewise constant with breakpoints exactly at the analytic thresholds — e.g., Dec-CaC beats Dec-SNP for ρ > 0.76 at r = 0.02 but ρ > 0.38 at r = 0.1, Figs. 10–11); outcome maps over parameter grids with capture probability encoded by shading (P114 Figs. 3–5: Δd_min surfaces and outcome maps over (r_p, r_e), (Δθ₀, Δd₀), (a_p, a_e), with numbered regions ①–⑥ interpreted in the text; P077 Table I plays the same role tabulated: 0%/26.88%/41.66% capture at N = 1/2/4).

**Evidence requirements.** (i) Every regime boundary drawn must correspond to a numbered equation or theorem; label it. (ii) State which claims the diagram cannot support: P008's frontiers are *sufficient* conditions, so the true achievable frontier could be better (archive obs.) — the caption should say so. (iii) When a swept parameter crosses a theoretical transition (α vs. α_c in P062; ρ_inter1/2 in P012), place at least one simulated case on each side — P062 failing to exercise the α < α_c branch is the flagged gap. Exemplars: P008 (the model), P114, P005–P009 competitive-ratio thread.

## 5. Metric comparisons: box plots, bars, and curve families

**Conventions observed.** Box plots for outcome distributions with n printed (P003 Fig. 2: capture-time boxes n = 94 vs. n = 24 with median ≈1.8 s vs. ≈5.0 s and outliers to ≈5.6 s; P079 Fig. 9: completion-rate boxplots from 100 training sessions per method across three tasks); stacked outcome bars (capture/goal/timeout counts, P003 Fig. 4); success-rate bars with a secondary time-series overlay (P107 Fig. 9: bars = penetration success, broken lines = average time, over four swept ratios — dual-axis, one panel per sweep); training curves with variance shading (P079 Fig. 6: ADE vs. epochs over 20 runs with ±1σ; P072 Figs. 12–16: reward/length/loss curves for proposed vs. ablations); the script's `demo_metric_curves` shows the canonical two-panel layout (box + curve, COL2 width).

**Evidence requirements.** (i) Print n and the repetition protocol in the caption (P051: 3 repetitions, mean ± error bars, is the library's cleanest; P084's Fig. 9 with 20 repeats but no numeric table is the flagged deficiency). (ii) Decompose outcomes rather than reporting one aggregate rate (P078's Table 3 rows that do not sum to 100% — the ≈9.6% inconclusive mass — are the paper's real finding; P026's Table 2 decomposes wins by barrier region R_c vs. R_e). (iii) Keep colors/linestyles consistent across all metric figures in the paper. Exemplars: P003, P079, P107, P051.

## 6. 3-D renders

**Conventions observed.** MATLAB-style surface/line renders with wireframes for sensing regions and role-coded colors; inactive players shown muted (P062 Fig. 8: pursuers gray when inactive, blue when active, wireframe sensing spheres, ϑ* marked "×", capture near target boundary in (c)); phased snapshots in 3-D (P013 Fig. 5(a)–(e); P033 Figs. 9–16 with straight-line cooperative trajectories); geometric constructions as translucent surfaces with projection lines dashed (P073 Figs. 2–6: BES planes, spherical barrier patch over triangle S, hexagonal prism W_P, dashed projection lines L_i,j; P025 Fig. 4: 3-D geodesic plus top-down infinitesimal-move diagram).

**Evidence requirements.** (i) Arbitrary axis ranges are acceptable for pure geometry (P073's axes ≈ [−1.5, 1.5] are fine because nothing quantitative is claimed), but any 3-D figure supporting a quantitative claim needs stated units (P033's Table I interception heights are the quantitative anchor its Figs. 9–12 visualize). (ii) State the viewpoint/projection when a 2-D projection can mislead (P019's artifact note). (iii) Mark termination events in 3-D as in 2-D (P062's capture marker; P013's distance curve pairing). Exemplars: P062, P033, P073, P013.

## 7. Experiment photos and hardware panels

**Conventions observed.** Timestamped photo sequences with the state annotated per stamp (P067 Fig. 7: stamps ≈ 1.54, 4.30, 7.67, 9.34 s; P064 Fig. 7(a)–(h) one stage per photo: P2 reaches deployment → P1 deploys → E2 detected → E2 captured → E1 detected → E1 captured → final state); platform close-ups with hardware modules labeled (P060 Figs. 15–16: GPS antenna, cabin, thrusters, image/data + 4G, battery, GNSS module, master control unit; P084's Crazyflie over the 0.5 m floor tiles — the tiles are the scale bar); sim-vs-experiment trajectory overlays (P058 Figs. 8–9; P064 Fig. 8; P067 Figs. 8–9, with detection/capture instants quoted in both, e.g., 7.65 s vs. 8.30 s) — the overlay, with "a similar pattern" as the fidelity criterion, is the library's standard hardware-evidence figure.

**Evidence requirements.** (i) Photos must be paired with the matching simulated trajectory and timestamps, and any timing offsets explained (P067 attributes them to "non-negligible regulation time", p. 8). (ii) Include at least one quantitative hardware panel if at all possible — P027's Table 6 tracking errors (≈0.02 m average / 0.05 m max) is the library's best; P064/P067's trial-count-free reporting is the flagged weakness. (iii) State exclusions in the caption (P064/P067's barrier-critical initial conditions). Exemplars: P067, P064, P084, P060.

## 8. Specialty panels worth reusing

- **Belief/weight evolution**: belief trajectories with regime annotations (P029 Fig. 4(e)/Fig. 9(d): level-0 belief falls within 1–10 s, level-2 converges by 20–30 s, with the *reason* printed in the caption — "only the predicted level-2 trajectory matches the attacker's motion trend", p. 16564).
- **Assignment/matching visualizations**: bipartite graphs and re-assignment snapshots (P012 Fig. 9; P084's target-switch jump at ~210 iterations in its Fig. 2 distance curves; P059 Fig. 9's coalition assignment vs. the baseline's failure to guard E3).
- **Competitive-ratio and complexity log-plots with trend lines**: P015 Fig. 9 (log-scale time vs. N_A with the N_A^5 guide line, and the MIS curve truncated at N_A = 25 with the reason printed); P078 Fig. 9 (flat 2×4 LP vs. growing N×(N+1) LP).
- **Log-scale histogram of pairwise differences** for Monte-Carlo margin comparisons (P060 Figs. 11–12) — better than overlaying 3000 trajectories.
- **Signal-saturation panels**: control inputs plotted against their printed bounds with dashed limit lines (P096 Figs. 6–7; P014 Fig. 9 speeds under dashed maxima) — direct visual certification of constraint satisfaction.

## 9. IEEE style requirements (implemented in `scripts/simulation_plotting.py`)

The script is the executable style contract; its docstring points to this file. Import `ieee_rcparams()` and use `new_fig`/`save`; the constants below are binding:

- **Column widths**: COL1 = 3.5 in (single column), COL2 = 7.16 in (double column/full width). Height via `aspect` (geometry plots ≈ 0.85).
- **Fonts**: serif family (Times New Roman → STIXGeneral → DejaVu Serif fallback), mathtext STIX; base/axis-label/tick font 8 pt, title 9 pt, legend 7 pt.
- **Lines and markers**: linewidth 1.2 pt default; marker size 4 pt (trajectory sample dots 2.5 pt via `plot_trajectory`, one per `marker_every=20` samples).
- **Grid and legend**: grid on, alpha 0.3, linewidth 0.4; legend framealpha 0.9, edgecolor "0.8". No chartjunk; every axis labeled with quantity + unit ("$x$ [m]", "capture time [s]").
- **Grayscale safety**: line styles carry meaning, not only color — the player palette is fixed (C_P = #1f77b4 blue pursuer/defender-side, C_E = #d62728 red evader/attacker-side, C_D = #2ca02c green, C_A = #9467bd purple, C_T = #7f7f7f gray target/neutral) and each series also gets a distinct linestyle (solid/dashed/dash-dot), mirroring the library's dashed-predicted vs. solid-actual convention (P028 Fig. 11 planned dotted vs. actual solid; P029 Fig. 9 planned solid vs. predicted dotted).
- **Geometry plots**: `set_aspect("equal")` always (demo_apollonius, demo_reachavoid_barrier); shading alpha 0.08–0.25 for regions; barriers/curves lw 1.4–1.5; annotations via `annotate` with offset points.
- **Output**: `savefig.dpi = 600`, `bbox_inches = "tight"`, constrained layout, and always both PDF and PNG (`save()` writes `name.pdf` + `name.png` to assets/).
- **Statistical panels**: box plots with black medians and 0.4-alpha filled boxes (`demo_metric_curves` left panel); curve panels with distinct linestyle+color pairs and a legend (`demo_metric_curves` right panel, ylim chosen to show separation).

## 10. Caption conventions observed in the library

- **Claim-linked, self-contained captions**: a reader should get the finding without the body text — P089's Fig. 5 caption carries the Q-ordinate ordering claim; P062's Fig. 8(d) caption names both invariants (sensing retention and capture-before-target); P029's Fig. 4 caption explains the belief-identification mechanism.
- **Parameter provenance in captions**: initial positions and parameter values belong in the caption or a referenced table (P033, P089, P058 all print them there); the archive flags their absence (P028 hardware, P053 Scenario III) as reproducibility defects.
- **Theorem cross-references**: captions routinely cite the theorem a figure validates (P008 "Theorem 5", P012 "validates Theorems 1/6/7"); keep legend and caption citations identical (P008's legend/caption mismatch is a recorded erratum).
- **Errata and artifact notes in captions**: P019's projection-artifact note and P053's typo-flagged condition captions show the norm — if a panel could be misread, annotate it in place.
- **Figure-to-claim mapping discipline**: every figure should be accountable to exactly one contribution (the archives maintain explicit "figure → claim" maps; P084's coverage summary and P040's mapping are models). A figure that validates nothing (P073's illustrative-only set) is acceptable only if the text says so and the evidence gap is acknowledged.
