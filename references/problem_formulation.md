## 0. The shared modeling spine

Nearly every analytic paper in the library instantiates the same skeleton (P104, Sec. 2.1, eq. (1), p. 2 gives the canonical statement):

- **Players**: pursuers/defenders $P_i$ ($i \in \mathcal{P}$ or $\mathcal{D}$) vs. evaders/attackers/intruders $E_j$ ($j \in \mathcal{E}$ or $\mathcal{A}$); a third role (target, flag, protected asset) is usually a *region*, not a player; active-player variants add a mobile Target $T$ (P022, P080, P082, P090, P111).
- **Dynamics**: "simple motion" (Isaacs mass points) $\dot{x}_{P_i} = v_{P_i} u_{P_i}$, controls are instantaneous headings $u \in \mathcal{U} = \{u \in \mathbb{R}^n : \|u\|_2 \le 1\}$ (P104 eq. (1); P100 eq. (1), p. 109; P103 eq. (1), p. 3; P058 eq. (1); P075 eq. (1)). Non-simple variants are catalogued in §2.13.
- **Capture**: $\|x_{P_i} - x_{E_j}\|_2 \le r_i$ with capture radius $r_i \ge 0$ (point capture iff $r_i = 0$) (P104 Assumption A3; P103 p. 3).
- **Regions**: play region $\Omega_{\text{play}}$, goal/target region $\Omega_{\text{goal}}$ (or $\Omega_{\text{tar}}$), splitting hypersurface $\mathcal{T}$ with $\Omega_{\text{goal}} = \{y \mid g(y) \le 0\}$, $\mathcal{T} = \{g = 0\}$ (P057 p. 2; P104 p. 3; P103 p. 3: plane $T = \{z = 0\}$).
- **Payoff**: zero-sum terminal (Mayer) $J = \Phi(x(t_f))$, or counting objective $J$ = number of evaders captured in the play region with saddle value $\max_{\Sigma_P} \min_{\Sigma_E} J = \min_{\Sigma_E} \max_{\Sigma_P} J$ (P104 eq. (2), p. 3) [asserted — the equality needs an equilibrium-existence caveat, cf. P083 Item 7].
- **Termination**: $t_f$ = first entry into the terminal set (capture ∪ breach); the review flags "non-unique terminal conditions (capture OR entry)" as the structural difficulty of backward HJI integration (P104 Sec. 1, p. 2).
- **Winning regions / barrier**: $\mathcal{W}_P, \mathcal{W}_E$, barrier $\mathcal{B} = \{x : \text{neither side can force a win}\}$ (P104 Sec. 2.3.1, p. 3; P022 p. 555; P015 Property 1, p. 2).
- **Information structures** (four canonical types, after Mitchell et al. 2005, catalogued in P104 Sec. 2.3.2, p. 3): open loop; state feedback; non-anticipative (Elliott–Kalton; P018 p. 2, P040 eqs. (2)–(6), P100 p. 110); anticipative. Conflict to track: P103 p. 3 *deliberately rejects* non-anticipative structures for multiplayer games ("too strong... the game may not necessarily admit a value", citing Cardaliaguet) and uses state feedback; P104's Assumption A2 is full-state information; P064 Sec. II-B explicitly rejects non-anticipative patterns under partial observability. Choose one and justify — reviewers of this literature check this.
- **Standard assumption set for simple-motion M-RA games** (P104 Assumptions 1–4, p. 2): A1 $v_{P_i} \ge v_{E_j}$; A2 full-state information; A3 radius/point capture; A4 constant player counts, pursuit until no evader remains in $\Omega_{\text{play}}$. Its fragility is itself citable: P104 Sec. 7 lists the five open axes (dynamics, sequential capture, spatio-temporal coupling, fast evaders, partial information); P083 Sect. 5 lists the same four for perimeter defense.

## 1. Problem-class modeling profiles

### 1.1 One-versus-one pursuit–evasion (point or radius capture)
- **Players/state**: $x = (x_P, x_E) \in \mathbb{R}^4$; simple motion, speeds $v_P \ge v_E$ (P023 p. 3: $\|v_E\| \le \nu$, $\|v_P\| \le 1$, $\nu < 1$; P041 eq. (1), p. 3: $\dot{x}_p = \alpha u_p$, $\dot{x}_e = u_e$).
- **Information**: P041 is the rigorous reference: non-anticipative (Elliott–Kalton) strategies, Def. 1 p. 4, eq. (3); P044 pp. 1054 (Thm 1, HJI verification template cited to Hespanha) uses state feedback with "no assumption on the opponent's controller" (P034 p. 4167 same).
- **Payoff/termination**: capture time $J = \int_0^{t_f} dt$ with terminal manifold $x_P = x_E$ (P052 Lemma 1, p. 1301); or terminal separation. P023: point capture (p. 3). P114: unicycle players, capture radius $\epsilon_2$, alert-turn protocol (Eq. (1)–(2), p. 2).
- **Key assumptions/guarantees**: straight-line optimal play via PMP with constant costates (P052 Lemma 1 [proven]; P044 Proposition 1/Theorem 2 [proven]); P023's containment results (Thm 1: capture time bound $t \le t_0 + 2(1+\nu^{-1}) R_C \ln(R_C/\delta)$ [proven]).
- **Exemplars**: P023 (Lyapunov containment), P041 (dominance regions under non-anticipativity; Thms. 4–7 [proven]), P044 (2P1E tutorial + verified 1v1 blocks), P052 (dimension-heterogeneous 3D-vs-2D), P053 (isochron framework, 16-case taxonomy, Table I p. 7), P002 (3D fuzzy actor–critic; kinematics typo flagged — $v_z = v\cos\theta > 0$ prevents descent, archive §7).
- **Proof-gap flag**: P053's Lemma 2 isochrone circles and P054's Lemma 1–2 are asserted without full PMP derivation (P054 §7(1)); P002 has no theorem environments at all.

### 1.2 Reach–avoid games (1v1, with/without time limits)
- **Geometry**: attacker $A$ starts in $\Omega_{\text{play}} = \{y > 0\}$, wants $\Omega_{\text{goal}} = \{y \le 0\}$; defender $D$ faster ($\alpha = v_A/v_D < 1$). Canonical in P011 eq. (1), p. 4; P012 pp. 193–195; P061 eqs. (1)–(4).
- **Payoff**: terminal distance to target center or min-distance payoff $J = \min_{t}\mathrm{dist}(x_I(t), \mathcal{T})$ (P049 eq. (10), p. 2).
- **Time limits**: P011 — only the attacker is energy-limited ($t_A$, range $R_A = v_A t_A$); "cannot leave detection range once detected" [proven, Apollonius monotone-separation argument, Theorem 1 p. 6]. P012 — time limits on **both** players; classification by $\Delta t = \bar t_A - \bar t_D$ and $m = \hat r_D/l^0$ into two stages and three circle-relation subgames (contribution 1, pp. 193–195); the $\Delta t > 0$ case produces the additional connecting arc $\mathcal{B}^{ADD}_\alpha$ and a discontinuous barrier (contribution 3). Barriers are closed-form polar expressions [proven].
- **Damped dynamics**: P061 — both players damped double integrators $\ddot{x} = -\mu \dot{x} + u$, $|u| \le u_m$; optimal play is max acceleration with constant heading (eqs. (7)–(14) via Maximum Principle [proven]); isochrones are circles (15); the **multiple reachable region** (MRR) with boundary curves $\mathcal{B}_I, \mathcal{B}_{II}$, cusp time $t_u$ (30)–(31), barrier time $t_{is}$ (25) [proven, Lemma 2 + Theorem 1]; circumscribing/inscribing isochrone times: at least one, at most three (Theorem 1).
- **Exemplars**: P011, P012, P061, P049 (two-phase reconnaissance as stacked reach–avoid), P047 (car defender via heading tracking; no value function computed for the car game — analytic machinery is reference generation only, archive Sec. "Winning/termination", p. 1657).

### 1.3 Target–Attacker–Defender (TAD / active defense)
- **Players/state**: $x = (x_T, x_A, x_D) \in \mathbb{R}^6$; speeds $V_A = V_D > V_T = \gamma V_A$ (P022 eq. (1), p. 554; P080 eq. (1), p. 2–3; P082 eq. (1), p. 2).
- **Termination** (P022 eq. (2), p. 554–555): $\mathcal{C} = \mathcal{C}_1 \cup \mathcal{C}_2$, $\mathcal{C}_1 = \{x_A = x_D\}$ (D intercepts A; T escapes), $\mathcal{C}_2 = \{x_A = x_T\}$ (A captures T); point capture; order-of-preference Definition 1 rules out nontermination ("Type P" preference; nontermination Υ is worst for A) [asserted as a modeling postulate]. P082 eqs. (2)–(4): **both** A and D have non-zero capture radii $R > 0$, $r > 0$ under equal speeds — first in the Garcia lineage (contribution 1). P080: asymmetric radii — only A has $r > 0$ (eqs. (3)–(4)).
- **Payoff**: $V(x_0) = \min_\beta \max_{\delta,\zeta} \|x_{A_f} - x_{T_f}\|$ (P082 eq. (5), p. 3); P055 uses $J = (x_{A_f} - x_T)^2 + (y_{A_f} - y_T)^2$ (eqs. (7)–(9), p. 1852); the aimpoint is the interception point $I^*$ on the orthogonal bisector of $\overline{AD}$, abscissa the real root of an explicit quartic (P022 eq. (15)) [proven with C¹ Value (17)/(74) verified against HJI].
- **Speed-varying variant** (P055): second-order plants with force saturation; 6-D SOTDDG decomposed into (i) force allocation solved by PMP (bang-bang full acceleration then coast) and (ii) a 1-D extremal search over the average speed ratio $\bar\alpha$ — no numerical HJI (Abstract; §IV-A pp. 1857–1858) [proven via Lemmas 1–2 (C¹ terminal value in the ratio) + Theorems 1–3; directionality of "Nash" inherits the usual reduction caveat]. Printed typo: where-line of termination eq. (6) "$y_{D_f} = x_D(t_f)$" should be $y_D(t_f)$ (archive p. 1852 note).
- **Dimension-heterogeneous variant** (P052 Problem 2): pursuer in 3D, evader/target in 2D plane; barrier $B$ compact quadratic form (eqs. (12)–(14)) [Theorem 1 asserts Nash-optimality, p. 1302 — archive notes the verification is substitutional, not a uniqueness argument].
- **Spacecraft TAD** (P111): CW equations in LVLH frame (eq. (1), p. 11406), impulsive maneuvers $\Delta v$ at fixed interval $\Delta t$ with STM discretization (eq. (2), STM blocks sin/cos of $n\Delta t$); no capturability/barrier analysis is derived — purely constructive/learning formulation (archive §5.10) [asserted].
- **Exemplars**: P022 (flagship closed form), P080, P082, P055, P052, P026 (MDP/RL attacker guidance, 9-D state, Eqs. (19)–(29)), P090 (nonzero-sum LQ multi-agent TAD with anomalous defenders; relative coordinates, binary-visibility adjacency (16)–(17), masking (20)).
- **Proof-gap flags**: P080's Theorem 1 keeps the two-sided-PMP machinery but the barrier tangency test (Theorem 2, eqs. (50)–(51), (56)) is new machinery — treat the "Attacker's ultimate interception curve" $y = \pm\sqrt{r^2 - x^2}(1 - x_A/x)$, $x \in [-r, 0)$ as [proven within the paper's construction] but check the equal-speed restriction; P055's average-speed-ratio reduction freezes $\bar\alpha$ at maximum — archive flags the ratio-freezing as an assumption-driven reduction, exact only under the stated coast assumptions.

### 1.4 Target guarding of convex regions (multi-defender, single attacker)
- **Model** (P050 eqs. (1a)–(1b), p. 6358): $\dot{x}_A = u_A$, $\dot{x}_{D_i} = \nu_i u_{D_i}$, $\nu_i > 1$, $\|u\| \le 1$, simple motion in $\mathbb{R}^n$; cost $J = d_{\mathcal{T}}(x_A(t_f))$ squared distance to convex target $\mathcal{T}$ (eqs. (2)–(3)); $t_f$ = first entry into capture set $C_c$ or breach set $C_b$.
- **Value** (P050 eq. (9)): $V$ = candidate geometric value, proved the **viscosity solution** of the HJI equation (Theorem 2) and $C^1$ exactly where LICQ holds (Theorem 1(3), eq. (28)) via parametric convex programming sensitivity [proven — this is the library's most complete verification for the MDSA class].
- **Geometric core**: dominance region (P031 eq. (2.5)); with capture radius $r > 0$ the Apollonius circle becomes a **Cartesian oval** (P031; P094 Lemma 3.1 eq. (7); P060's oval condition $d_{D_iC} = \beta_i r_A + \rho_{D_i}$, p. 4). P031 Theorem 2.1 justifies the geometric control law (2.7)/(2.8) as the HJI solution for arbitrary convex targets, $r > 0$, arbitrary $n$, under A1–A5 [proven]; flags: $v_D \ge v_I$ vs $a > 1$ inconsistency printed (A3, archive §7).
- **Cooperative attackers**: P048 — one defender vs. $m$ heterogeneous cooperative attackers, importance weights $\theta_i$, team cost $\sum_i \theta_i h(x_{A_i}(t_i))$ (eq. (3), p. 234); sequential capture with exogenous order; Theorem 2: at states where the stacked capture-point program (P) satisfies SOSC, $V$ is $C^1$ and satisfies the Isaacs equation classically [proven under SOSC]; feasible set nonconvex for $m \ge 2$ (Remarks 3–6, p. 235–237).
- **Compact convex target, general barrier** (P098): $B(\mathbf{x}; \Omega, \gamma) = \|\mathrm{Proj}_\Omega(C(\mathbf{x})) - C(\mathbf{x})\| - R(\mathbf{x})$ (eq. (19), Prop. 3) — the "generalized Apollonius" barrier via metric projection [proven within the preprint; several lemmas flagged as sketch-level in the archive's tier caveat].

### 1.5 Perimeter defense (line, circle, cone; turret variants)
- **Standard circular model** (P083 Item 1, p. 474): $N_A$ intruders vs $N_D$ defenders; defenders confined to the perimeter (A1); target region convex (A2); full state (A3); capture at zero distance (A4); $\nu \le 1$; payoff $Q$ = number of intruders that score. 1v1 payoff $J$ = defender–intruder distance at breaching (eq. (1), p. 475); barrier = level set $V = 0$; circular barrier first derived geometrically and "later verified with differential game approach" (Item 3, p. 475). Arbitrary convex perimeter: approach angle $\varphi = \cos^{-1}\nu$ (Item 4) and involute-based barrier at $\nu = 1$ (Item 5) [asserted, from the self-review's sources [25]–[28]].
- **Structural degeneracy [proven by argument, P083 p. 482]**: perimeter-confined defenders + point capture ⇒ intruders approaching simultaneously guarantee each defender captures at most one ⇒ game interesting only when $N_A < N_D$. This is the standard motivation for relaxing to mobile defenders or capture radii.
- **Competitive-analysis (worst-case) line** — a different information model entirely: intruders released adversarially (non-stochastic, non-anticipative releases), online algorithm sees only the revealed prefix; competitive ratio vs. offline optimum. P005 (line, 6 pp.): impossibility threshold $v > \frac{1-\rho}{2\rho}$ (Theorem III.1); regime $v \ge \frac{1-\rho}{1+\rho}$ forces $c \ge 2$ (Thm III.2); FCFS non-competitiveness (Lemma 2); extreme-speed (bang-bang) reduction (Lemma 1, p. 2) [proven]. P006 (cone $\mathcal{R}(\rho, \theta)$): static capture-all placement $r \ge \rho\tan\theta$ at $(\rho/\cos\theta, 0)$ (Lemma III.1), endpoint crossing time (Lemma III.2), no-finite-$c$ condition (Thm III.3), $c \ge 2$ condition (Thm III.4) [proven]. P008 (multi-vehicle cone, T-RO): parameters $(\theta, v, \rho, r, M)$; Theorem 5 (p. 1442) sharp finite-ratio boundary via stream-plus-burst adversary; Lemma 4 threshold $r \ge \rho \sin(\theta/M)$ [proven]. P009 (randomized line): Yao's minimax + oblivious adversary (Def. 1, p. 2); randomized lower bounds 2 and 4/3 (Thms. 1–2, p. 3) [proven]. Dynamics throughout: first-order integrators, unit speed, intruders straight-line radial at $v$ (P008 footnote 2; P009 p. 2).
- **Turret variants**: P007 — fixed-base turret (heading $\gamma_t$, slew rate $\dot\gamma_t = u$, $|u| \le \omega$, range $r \in [\rho, 1]$, startup time $\Delta$; lock-on + spool-up capture semantics; P1 offline ≡ TRP-TW (Theorem 3.1, p. 3352); 2-approximation (Prop. 3.2) [proven via reduction]). P086 — turret + mobile defender team: state $x = (x_D, y_D, x_A, y_A, \theta_T)$; capture sets as unions of geometric predicates (eq. (3)); turret dominance region bounded via transcendental equation $\nu\theta/\omega = r_A |\sin(\theta - \theta_A)|$ (Lemmas 3, 5; eqs. (19)–(26)) [asserted/proven-in-paper; archive flags cost $J = r_{A_f} - 1$ presupposes team capture — A-win unrepresented (§7)].
- **3D perimeter** (P025): defender on unit hemisphere, intruder on base plane; 3-state relative model $z = [\psi, \phi, r]$ (eq. (3), p. 2); payoff $p = \tau_D - \tau_A$ (eq. (4)); optimal breaching angle $\beta^*$ (Theorem 1, eq. (13)) [asserted — preprint, lemmas sketch-level per archive tier note].
- **Hereding/channel variant** (P014): attacker relocation via virtual defense channel; single integrators (eq. (1), p. 8468); barriers for straight/curved channel walls (eqs. (2)–(4), nesting (7)) [proven]; relative-distance + LOS-angle information only (polar barriers).

### 1.6 N-vs-1 and N-vs-N pursuit (multiplayer reach-avoid, simple motion)
- **MPME border-defense game** (P037, TAC 2021): state $\mathbb{R}^{2(N+M)}$, simple motion; binary assignment $\mu_{ij}$; terminal payoff $\sum_j y_{E_j}(t_f)$ = cumulative border penetration; dynamics frozen after each capture (regularization, p. 2346); value $V = \max_\iota y_{s_\iota}$ over feasible assignments, $C^1$ except dispersal surfaces [proven in the companion verification the archive records]. Flagged inconsistency: pursuers-first vs evaders-first state ordering between sections (archive §5).
- **3D N-vs-1 with plane goal** (P035, RAL 2020; P033, TCDS 2024): state $\mathbb{R}^9$ (P035) / $\mathbb{R}^{3(M+N)}$ (P033); barrier $B = \max\{B_1, B_2\}$, $B_i = R_E^2 - R_i^2$ (P035 eq. (21)); Values $V = \frac{1}{4}(R_E^2 - R_1^2)^2/\Gamma^2$ (P035 eq. (13), verified HJI [proven]); P033 Theorems 1–3: 1P1E/2P1E/3P1E 3D Apollonius-sphere values + Remark 4 "never need more than 3 pursuers in 3-D"; Assumption 1 p. 2087 (max-speed optimality under acceleration dynamics) is intuitive-not-proved [asserted].
- **3D barrier net** (P073): four equal-speed players; target plane $\mathcal{T} = \{K^T z = b\}$; barrier pieces by active-pursuer count: mirror points (1), circular arc (2, Thm 1 eqs. (9)–(10)), union of two 2-pursuer barriers (3 collinear, Thm 2), spherical patch over the projection triangle (3 noncollinear, Thm 3 eqs. (15)–(16)) [proven]. **Apollonius sphere** (P075; P013 Def. 2 eqs. (2)–(5)): the 3D dominance surface; P075 claims "first analytical 3D solution" (1-vs-1 eq. (13), 2-vs-1 Thm 2 eqs. (15)–(17)) — archive flags the HJI-differentiability claim in its contribution list as "not substantiated in the paper body".
- **Heterogeneous 3D with radii** (P103, Automatica 2022): controls on $\mathbb{S}^2$; capture sets $C_i = \{\|x - x_{P_i}\| \le r_i\}$; **evasion space (ES)** certificate $\bar{E}(s, j) = \{f_{ij} \ge 0, i \in s\}$ with $f_{ij}(x) = \|x - x_{P_i}\|_2 - \alpha_{ij}\|x - x_{E_j}\|_2 - r_i$ (function-based dominance, P104 Def. 3); coalition reduction: at most 3 pursuers matter (Cor. 1); HJI-to-convex-program reduction via KKT [proven]. Curse-of-dimensionality motivator: 12-state subgame ≈ $10^{12}$ grid points at 10 pts/dim (P103 Remark 3.2, p. 1).
- **Cooperative pursuit, Voronoi/area** (P113, Automatica 2016): equal unit speeds (WLOG normalization); Voronoi partition of $\Omega$; pursuer strategy $u_i^* = -(\partial A/\partial x_{p^i})/\|\partial A/\partial x_{p^i}\|$ (Eq. (6)); Theorem 2: head to the midpoint of the line of control [proven]; Theorem 3: energy $E = kA + \|x_e - x_p\|^2$ decreases at linear rate ⇒ finite-time capture [proven for $N=1$, convex Ω; the $N>1$ reduction is asserted; case-dichotomy typo "ż > c(β)" flagged in archive].
- **Relay pursuit** (P070): active pursuer by least-time metric; round-pursuit law $u_R = \lambda/\|\lambda\|$, $\lambda = r_1\cos\theta/\|r_1\| + r_2/\|r_2\|$ (eqs. (7)–(8), Prop. 1); capture condition $|\theta_0| < \frac{v_p}{v_e}(\frac{c_1 + c_2}{c_2})\ln(\frac{c_1 + c_2}{c_1}) - \frac{v_p}{v_e}$ (Prop. 2 eq. (16)) [proven]. Flag: capture Definition 1 (eq. (2)) demands ALL pursuers coincide — inconsistent with the OR-capture used in the analysis (archive §5).
- **Risk-metric feedback** (P077): log-risk $\beta_i = \log(\varphi_T/\varphi_s)$ (eq. (9), p. 700); safe/failing/risky partition of the augmented state (Prop. 1, p. 699); circular boundaries $S_e, F_e$ (Prop. 2) [proven]; relay pursuit model eq. (2).

### 1.7 Two-vs-one and two-vs-two cooperation (analytic barrier blocks)
- **2D1A analytical barriers** (P099, TCYB 2019): speed ratio $\alpha = v_A/v_D \le 1$ only ("the attacker always wins for $\alpha > 1$" — asserted without proof, p. 1037, archive §7); attack-region (AR) method: barrier from Voronoi diagrams ($\alpha = 1$) / Apollonius circles ($\alpha < 1$) + semipermeability; Theorem 1: one-defender-dependence iff condition; Theorem 2: five-part two-defender barrier [proven].
- **Team-of-teams** (P015): 1v1 barrier → 2v1 cooperative region $R_C$ strictly smaller than naive intersection $R_I = R_A(i) \cap R_A(j)$ (eq. (3), p. 2); LGR decomposition into $N_D^2$ local game regions; intruder guarantee $Q_{LG} = \max_G \sum_{k \in G} \max\{q^k, 0\}$ computable as max-weight independent set on a circular-arc graph in $O(N_D^4)$ (eq. (6) + Theorem 1, eq. (7)) [proven]; defender policy Algorithms 1–4 (pp. 5–6).
- **Fishing game (2 pursuers, faster evader)** (P087): barrier $B_1$ via explicit-policy method; $\cos\theta = \frac{(1-a^2)(d_1 + d_2)^2}{2 d_1 d_2} - 1$ (eq. (19)); Theorem 4.1 pursuer win iff $r_{12}(t_0) \le a(d_1(t_0) + d_2(t_0))$ [proven, with invariant-ratio argument $\dot b = 0$]; four-zone game-of-degree synthesis (eqs. (44)–(65)).
- **Three-player lifeline** (P112): barrier $\mathscr{B}_1$ of each sub-game via Isaacs main equation + RPE integration (Theorem 1, eqs. (6), (17)); joint barrier = "bond" of the two sub-game barriers with piecewise switching at the intersection $(\tilde x, \tilde y)$ (Theorem 2, eqs. (18)–(20)) [proven]; note: capture zone of the joint game = union of sub-game zones "because the pursuers play individually" [asserted].
- **Two-lifeline (2 pursuers, 1 evader, nonzero radius)** (P094): terminal payoff $J = \min\{y_e(t_{f1}), d - y_e(t_{f2})\}$ (eq. (4)); reachable region $R = R_1 \cap R_2$ bounded by two Cartesian ovals (Lemma 3.1, eq. (7)) [proven]; $a = 1$ degenerates to hyperbolas.
- **Two cooperative attackers** (P019, TAES 2024): value function $J_1(X)$ for the constrained scenario proved the unique viscosity solution of the HJI equation **including the viscosity condition on singular surfaces** via the $m$-branch Lemma 1 (eq. (2), p. 560) — the only fully proved multi-branch value in this game class [proven]; dynamics eq. (4), state $\mathbb{R}^6$, cost $J = \min_i y_{A_i}(T_i)$ (eq. (5)).
- **2v1 with radii / mixed speeds** (P091): three-curve barrier (Thm 1) and five-curve barrier (Thm 2) for $\alpha_{ji} \le 1$; advantage-angle construction from a Cartesian oval of simultaneous arrival for faster attackers (Thm 3); equal-speed collapse to two hyperbola branches (Thm 6) [proven within the paper's scenario classification].

### 1.8 Capture-the-flag (two-phase, one flag + return)
- **Two-player CTF** (P034, CDC 2018): $\Omega$ compact convex, flag $F$ at origin, return region $S = \{x \ge x_s\}$; flag radius $r_F \ge 0$, defender capture radius $r_D \ge 0$; Phase I/II termination eqs. (2)–(4); Phase I Value $V = \frac{1}{4}(x_A^2 + y_A^2 - x_D^2 - y_D^2)^2 / ((x_A - x_D)^2 + (y_A - y_D)^2)$ (eq. (17)) with HJI proof [proven]; Phase II piecewise Value (eq. (25)), $C^1$ except dispersal surface $\{y_A = y_D\}$.
- **Attack–transition–retreat** (P038, OJ-CSYS 2025): adds the inter-phase Transition phase — D concedes Phase I and optimizes its Phase II entry state; transition optimum continues straight into the Phase II aimpoint (hyperbola condition (57)) [proven with HJI verification, pp. 276–279]; constrained/boundary aimpoint when the unconstrained one leaves $\Omega$ (eqs. (25)–(32)).
- **HJI/level-set CTF** (P039 ICRA 2011; P040 TCST 2015): joint state $\mathbb{R}^4$; equal speed caps "for fairness" (P039 p. 1452); rule violations encoded as the opponent's winning conditions (defender excluded from $\mathcal{F} \cup \mathcal{R} \cup \mathcal{D}^C$); sequential composition $\tilde{R}_A = \mathcal{G}_F \cap R_A$ (P039 eq. (5)) [proven via constrained HJI viscosity theory, P040 eqs. (12)–(15)]; P040 adds Elliott–Kalton formulation, time-dilation augmentation, and Isaacs condition (16).
- **Multiplayer matching-based CTF** (P089): two alliances; speed ratios $\mu_1 = V_{d1}/V_a$, $\mu_2$, $\mu_3$ (eq. (3)); Apollonius circles (4)–(7) and dominance disks $\Gamma_1, \Gamma_2$ (eq. (8)); reduction to 2D-1A/1D-1A subgames; two-phase joint optimal strategy with $C^1$ value satisfying HJI (Theorem 1 + Corollary 1) [proven as claimed]; constrained matching (MBMC) maximizes captures, solved approximately by sequential matching.
- **Convex flag/return regions** (P021, Automatica 2025): two-phase recursion with terminal payoff $J_\mathcal{F}$ (6); phase-2 value = optimum $g^*_\mathcal{R}(X)$ of a **convex** program over the attackers' Apollonius dominance region (Theorem 1); CTF value = optimum $W^*(X)$ of a **quasi-convex** program (19) (Theorems 11–12, 14); defender redundancy $|E| \le 2$ (Theorem 15, II-ES Def. 6) [proven]; iterative two-step convex Algorithm 1.
- **Half-plane flag/return, heterogeneous, radii** (P058, TCYB 2026): strip play region between $y = y_F$ and $y = 0$; via-point (flag-aware) potential $f_{ij}(x, x_F)$ (eq. (2)); evasion space (ES) strict convexity (Lemma 1); straight-line optimality (Lemma 2); necessary and sufficient game-of-kind conditions (Theorems 1–2) [proven].
- **Two-phase ellipse tool** (P018, ECC 2024): thief wins iff target region intersects the open ellipse with foci at thief's initial position and the safe point, major axis $v_T \times$ (fastest guarder's arrival time at safe point) (Thms. 1–2) [proven under complementary assumptions: arbitrary guarder speeds/zero radii vs. nonzero radii/guarders no slower]; cooperative two-guarder case via Danskin (Thms. 3–4); logarithmic-spiral bypassing path for slower guarders.

### 1.9 Lifeline games (reach-a-line with deadline / multiple lifelines)
- **Limited lifetime** (P102, IJC 2021): state $(x_P, x_E, t) \in \mathbb{R}^4 \times \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$; two target sets $\mathcal{T}_1 = \{y_E = 0\}$ (lifeline) and $\mathcal{T}_2 = \{t = T_l\}$ (lifetime); winner decided by which target set is hit first (eqs. (2)–(5), p. 4); **l-Apollonius circle** $\mathcal{A}^l$ (evasion-region boundary, Lemma 3); tangency barrier with/without lifetime (Lemma 7, Theorem 1); region-wise optimal strategies via the OTP construction (Theorems 2–3) [proven]; supporting Lemma 1 (strictly decreasing function $G(\beta)$, eqs. (12)–(18)) [proven, sign-factorization decisive step].
- **Two lifelines** (P094 above; P093): one superior pursuer vs multiple evaders, two lifelines; no-delay barrier (Thm 4.1 eq. (13)) and delay-accumulated barrier $\Delta t = \sum t_j$ (Thm 4.2 eq. (19), Cor. 4.1 eq. (26)); parallel-pursuit sine-rule strategy $\varphi = \arcsin(\frac{1}{a_i}\sin\varphi_i)$ (Lemma 3.1 eq. (6)) [proven]; payoff $J_2$ (28)/$J_1$ (31) = topmost/bottommost reachable y on the updated Apollonius circle; lifeline-selection rule $\min\{J_1, h - J_2\} \le 0$ escape criterion (Cor. 5.1–5.2).
- **Isaacs lineage**: P102 is the limited-lifetime extension of Isaacs' lifeline game; P112/P094/P093 extend to 3 players / 2 pursuers / multiple evaders respectively (P104 Sec. 4.3 recaps the barrier catalog with eqs. (10)–(12), including the lifetime-only horizontal line $y = v_{P_i} t_a/\alpha_{ij}$, eq. (10)).

### 1.10 Reconnaissance / penetration games
- **Two-player reconnaissance, half-planar regions** (P049, L-CSS 2023): disjoint half-planes $\mathcal{T} = \{y \ge l\}$, $\mathcal{R} = \{y \le 0\}$ (eqs. (6)–(7)); two-phase decomposition — Phase I approach (payoff $J_I = l - y_I^s$, eq. (23)) and Phase II retreat ($J_{II}$, eq. (13)); defender faster ($\alpha > 1$); capture on $\partial\mathcal{R}$ counts as intruder win (p. 2); Theorem 1: Phase-II closed-form value via max-over-half-plane of the $\Delta$ function; Theorem 2: Phase-I value verified against HJI (eqs. (57)–(59)) — a step "absent in [20]" [proven]. "Retreat point = projection of the Apollonius center" device.
- **Reconnaissance penetration with territorial defender** (P056, TAC 2022): three players $T$ (target, maneuvering evader), $A$ (attacker), $D$ (defender confined to disk $\Omega_D$ of radius $l$ around base station) (eq. (2) block, p. 2); speeds $V_T < V_A = 1 < V_D$; explicit-policy method replacing HJI (contribution 2); escape-angle theorem for A inside the territory; case split on territory size (Theorems 4.1–4.7) [proven within the case analysis].
- **Multi-stage reconnaissance MARL** (P079): discrete grid world, unequal sensing ranges (5 vs 7), phases approach/reconnaissance (10-step dwell)/retreat; scouts win (+1) only if at least one completes reconnaissance and returns; reward $J = J_{approach} + J_{recon} + J_{retreat}$ (eq. (25), p. 6) [asserted — no continuous dynamics equations exist; black-box simulator].
- **Penetration with line-segment obstacle + partial information** (P095): three-phase decomposition (deployment / asymmetric information / engagement, Defs. 1–3 eqs. (1)–(5), p. 3–4); obstacle tangent to $\|x\| = r_0 + \rho_T/2$; forced zig-zag detour enlarges the defender's guaranteed angular set by $\Delta\theta_l + \Delta\theta_r$ (Theorem 1) [proven]; fairness Assumption 1 restricting $(\rho_T, \rho_I, l_o)$; dynamics never written explicitly — implicitly single integrator, speeds $\nu < 1$ and 1 (archive §5).

### 1.11 Sensing-limited / partial-information variants
- **Sensing-radius defense, 1v1** (P067, TCST 2025): information sets $I_P(t) = \{x_P, v_P, v_E\} \cup \{x_E \mid \|x_E - x_P\| \le r\}$ (p. 2); new concepts: pursuit sensing winning region $\mathcal{W}_P^{\mathcal{S}} = \mathcal{W}_P^{\mathcal{B}} \cap \mathcal{S}$ and **fuzzy region** $\mathcal{F} = \mathcal{W}_P^{\mathcal{B}} - \mathcal{S}$ (Definition 2, p. 5); optimal deployment = target-line midpoint $(l/2, 0)$ [proven via convexity + mean-value theorem, Thm 1]; phase transition at $R^\alpha = (1 + \alpha^{-1})l/2$ (guard midpoint vs retreat to barrier-tangency depth).
- **Np-vs-1 3D convex target, partial information** (P062, TAC 2026): pursuer team limited to sensing region $S$ with pooled communication; non-anticipative strategy sets (2); two-phase decomposition — open-loop **deployment strategy** $\Pi_{dP}$ (before detection) and closed-loop **capture strategy** $\Pi_{cP}$ (after), with feasibility requirement $x_E(t) \in S$ for all $t \in [t_c, t_f]$ (p. 3); Theorem 1: partial-information value bounded below by the complete-information value at detection, $V(x) \ge \|\vartheta^*(t_c) - \chi(t_c)\|_2$, with explicit speed-ratio threshold [proven within the stated class].
- **Heterogeneous multiplayer, detection-limited** (P064, TMech): pursuers know all evader speeds but positions of *detected* evaders only; evaders know pursuer states but not sensing radii (Sec. II-B, p. 2); centralized-coordinator state-feedback pattern; Lemma 1: closed-loop capture strategy with speed-ratio threshold $\alpha_{ij} \ge \sqrt{2}$ keeping the evader inside the sensing disk while heading to the optimal Apollonius point (p. 4) [asserted with "optimality under exact velocity conditions" caveat]; **conservative barrier**: radius-free, ratio-only, evaluated at sensing-range inflation (contribution 2).
- **Asymmetric observations with estimation** (P027, AIS 2024): TP coalition vs one evader; coalition runs a Kalman filter, forms a **virtual evader** (filtered mean + Gaussian perturbation with posterior covariance) and predicts via chance-constrained optimization with inflated feasible set (Sec. 3.2, pp. 5–6); evader has perfect observation; discrete double integrator (eq. (4), p. 4); winning: pursuer within $r_p$ of evader, or evader within attack distance of target (p. 2).
- **RESR robust safe region** (P028, TASE 2025): pursuer-side uncertainties in $\gamma$ and $d$; RESR with closed-form Cartesian-oval boundary (Theorem 1), boundedness/strict convexity (Lemma 1); estimated interception point (EIP) = closest RESR point to $\Omega_{goal}$; feedback OIS with uncertainty compensation guaranteeing $\dot{y}_I \ge 0$ (Theorem 2, eq. (33)) [proven]; proximity strategy (PS, eq. (39)) when RESR meets the goal. Printed typo: evader speed $v_I$ for $v_E$ (archive §5).
- **Two-phase sensing in border defense** (P017, ACC 2026): sensing time $t_s = \inf\{t : \exists i, \|x_A - x_{D_i}\| \le \rho_s^{(i)}\}$ (eq. (4)); Phase I stochastic search (defenders see only defender positions, eq. (5)); Phase II deterministic pursuit with full state; attacker prescribed to descend directly $u = [0, -1]^\top$ before $t_s$ [asserted as rationality assumption, not equilibrium play]; payoff $J = \max\{0, y_A(t_f)\}$ (eq. (9)).
- **Sequential intruders, limited-range intruder sensor** (P071, CDC 2022): full-information phase iff $\|x_A\| < r_T + \rho_T$ **and** $\|x_A - x_D\| \le \rho_A$; otherwise defender sees intruder through the target-as-sensor but not vice versa; intruder prescribed radial max-speed approach until first sensing (footnote 1 admits this is an assumption); terminal state of each 1v1 game = initial state of the next (state carry-over).
- **Limited perception in TAD teams** (P090): binary-visibility adjacency $\mathcal{A}(t)$ (16)–(17) inducing a time-varying directed visibility network; masking matrices $\mathcal{I}_h(t)$ (20) projecting Riccati feedback onto each agent's observable subspace; capture radii $r_{D_i} < R_{D_i}$ (perception radius).
- **Dominance regions under non-anticipativity** (P041): Definition 1 (p. 4, eq. (3)) formalizes non-anticipative strategies; Theorem 4/5: initial dominance region = open-loop reachable region (shortest-path metric $d_L$) — first rigorous proof per authors [proven]; Theorem 7: PELIDR non-anticipative confinement without obstacles with explicit uniform capture-time bound; Remark 9 extends to convex domains; obstacles produce negative results (Theorem 6).

### 1.12 Obstacle environments
- **Isochrone/dominance with polygonal obstacles** (P069, Automatica 2016): Theorem 1 (no obstacles): isochrones concentric circles; Theorem 2 (polygonal obstacles): time-optimal paths are broken lines at obstacle vertices; isochrones are arcs of circles about *generating points* (continuous Dijkstra wavefront) [proven]; Theorem 7 + Remark 1: every dominance-boundary piece is a generalized Apollonius curve (limaçon, Apollonius circle, or vertex-centered circle); singular surfaces type-(p,u,p) lines and hyperbolic dispersal surfaces organize the assembly [proven].
- **Reach-avoid with polygonal obstacles, winning strategies** (P106, TAC 2025): simple motion; **visual-radius capture** — distance $\le r_i$ AND connecting segment obstacle-free (capture condition, p. 817); obstacles shield capture; three winning-region pairs: onsite (Thm 1, expanded Apollonius circles), goal-visible close-to-goal (Thm 2, convex goal-covering polygons GCP), nongoal-visible close-to-goal (Thm 4, ESP + anchored safe distance); constructs: GCP (Def. 5), direction range (Def. 6), first-visible obstacle vertices (Def. 9), GCP-enlargement (Thm 3), ESP reachable region/wavefront/circular-sector decomposition (Defs. 11–12, 14) [proven].
- **Obstacle + partial information TAD** (P095, above): obstacle forces detour that *helps* the defender.
- **Obstacle-avoidance as reward/force terms** (engineering branch): P002 (APF-shaped FACL reward, eqs. (17)–(20)), P072 (potential terms in MA-POCA reward, eqs. (19)–(26); no obstacle map, ray/grid sensors), P084 (repulsion forces (14)–(15); sign inconsistency flagged), P013 (CBF-QCQP safety layer eq. (46)–(49)), P096 (three CBF families + QP (35)).

### 1.13 Dynamics variants beyond simple motion
- **Dubins car defender** (P047): car model (1) vs simple-motion attacker (2); ES-based heading reference (Lemma 1, eq. (6)); worst-case heading-rate bound $\vartheta = \alpha(v_A + v_D)/(r(\alpha - 1))$ (Theorem 1, eq. (14)); finite tracking time; **no value function/barrier for the car game** (p. 1657) — strategy layer only.
- **Multiplayer Homicidal Chauffeur** (P105, Automatica 2024): pursuers are Dubins cars (eq. (1): $\dot\theta_{P_i} = v_{P_i} u_{P_i}/\kappa_i$), evaders simple motion (eq. (2)); pursuit enclosure functions (PEFs) and **enclosure-region pursuit (ERP)** winning: dynamically updated compact strictly convex region containing the evader keeps positive distance from $\Omega_{goal}$; computed by *forward* analysis, not backward HJI (Defs. 1–3); coalition reduction: at most 2 pursuers matter (Theorem 1, support-constraint counting) [proven].
- **Damped double integrators** (P061; P053 pursuer; P054 evaders): see §1.2 and P053's isochron framework (Lemma 2, eqs. (6)–(13)); P054: evaders damped double integrators with max steady-state speed $u_{mej}/\mu$ (p. 3).
- **Underactuated USVs** (P096): Fossen kinematics with sideslip (eq. - **USV penetration, 3-DOF** (P060, CEP 2026): geometric layer on point masses + backstepping controller to 3-DOF USV hulls; lake experiments on three physical USVs; time-difference function $f_{\mathrm{diff},i} = (d_{D_iR} - \rho_{D_i})/\beta_i - d_{AR}$ (p. 4, Lemma 1); closed-loop invariance of the Cartesian oval (Lemma 3, Theorem 1) [proven]; value theorems 2–3 (DDR/ADR) [proven].
- **USV DRL with 6-DOF** (P072): Fossen 6-DOF model (Eqs. (1)–(5)) in Unity3D; printed rotation-matrix typo in Eq. (2) (rows use cosθ/sinθ where cosφ/sinφ required — archive verified the printed matrix is not a valid rotation).
- **Inferior USV penetration, Nonlinear Dyn.** (P107): Apollonius-disk intersection $\tilde{S}$ as the joint safe region; penetration condition (eq. (20)) via KKT argument on the minimum-distance problem (eqs. (16)–(19)) [proven]; two-stage strategy with bang-bang yaw law (eq. (42)) and yaw-rate feasibility admits (31)/(39); indirect point $y_F$ formulas for non-convex (Def. 1, eqs. (9)–(10)) and convex (Def. 2, eq. (11)) targets — **defects flagged**: λ carries no subscript, n̄_i declared unused, derivation not shown (archive §5).
- **Spacecraft CW dynamics** (P108; P111): see §1.3 and the TPBVP archetype in `algorithm_design.md`.
- **Speed-varying second-order plants** (P055): §1.3.
- **Unicycles with centripetal agility bound** (P114, Automatica 2026): angular-rate bound $\min(r v^{-1}, W^{max})$ encodes faster-but-less-agile predator vs slower-but-more-agile prey (Eq. (3)); Alert-Turn two-phase law; not game-theoretic (no Isaacs/value machinery) — the bio-inspired successor to guardable-path arguments.
- **Surveillance of faster fixed-course target** (P092, TAES 2023): observer slower ($\alpha < 1$), observation disk radius $R$ as "capture" range; 3-state relative frame ($x_O, y_O, y_T$); three-region partition with distinct strategies (Theorem 1, eqs. (1), (5)); inter/intra-population Nash definitions (Defs. 1–6); ansatz-reduced coupled backward ODEs. ### 1.14 Task-allocation game formulations (hedonic / matching / auction-style)
- **Anonymous hedonic game** (P045, T-RO 2018): ST-MR assignment as coalition formation; preferences over task-coalition pairs $\mathcal{X} = T^* \times \{1..n_a\}$; **SPAO** (single-peaked-at-one, Def. 4, p. 1537) = social inhibition; Nash stable partition (Def. 3); integral assignment problem eqs. (1)–(3) (no NP-hardness claim made); Theorem 1 (existence) + Theorem 2 (≤ $|A|(|A|+1)/2$ iterations) [proven, with flagged informality in Lemma 1's counting]; Theorem 3 computable suboptimality bound $\alpha \ge J/(J + \lambda)$ (eqs. (8)–(9)); Theorem 4: 50% bound under nondecreasing social utility (eqs. (15)–(22)) [proven]; Problem 2 minimum-requirement tasks via auxiliary utility (eq. (28), Props. 1–2).

(15)) [asserted — Lemmas 1–2 proofs omitted, archive §7(1)].
- **Matching-based CTF / MBMC** (P089; P103): constrained bipartite matching with conflict graph; P103 proves MBMC NP-hard via reduction from 3-dimensional matching (Theorem 4.1) and gives Sequential Matching with 1/3-approximation (Theorem 4.2) [proven]; APX-completeness (Corollary 2).
- **k-WTA encirclement** (P084): ST-MR-TA program (1)–(6) with $\ge k$ pursuers per evader, $k \ge 3$; no differential-game payoff — evader is a scripted repulsion law (17)–(19) plus fatigue rule [asserted]; angle-based capture condition (subtended angle > 100° for k = 3) asserted, not derived from reachability (p. 5993).

- **Coalition ILP** (P100, T-RO 2020): Theorem 5 maximum matching as 0–1 IP (eq. (50)) over execution coalitions (sizes 1–2 by Lemma 5); prior-information vector r of barrier-derived capturabilities (Def. 9); q = exact game value for the pursuit team [proven under the barrier analysis]; NP-hardness inherited from the general constrained matching (special case; the paper says "NP-problem" loosely, archive §7(4)).
