# Physical Experiments in the Library: Hardware Compendium

Every paper in the 117-paper archive that ran (or, for two entries, claims a robotics tier that the archive shows to be simulated) physical/robot-platform experiments for game-strategy validation. Platform details, arena, trial counts, what was validated, deviations from simulation, and the papers' own caveats — all as recorded in the per-paper archives (page numbers propagated where the archive prints them). Papers without hardware do not appear except in the final lessons section.

## 1. Per-paper compendium

### P084 — Multitarget Pursuit-Evasion Based on Distributed and Competitive Mechanisms (IEEE TSMC)
- **Platform**: Crazyflie 2.1 nano-quadrotors (Bitcraze AB); motion capture = OptiTrack, sixteen Prime 17W cameras, accuracy 0.1 mm; Motive broadcasts rigid-body pose over LAN; Ubuntu 18.04, ROS Melodic (Sec. V, pp. 5997–5999). Max communication distance 2 m; detection distance 1 m; floor tiles 0.5 × 0.5 m² used as the spatial reference.
- **Arena**: indoor mocap arena (dimensions n.r.); two 0.6 × 0.6 × 1.2 m³ boxes as obstacles in the avoidance trial.
- **Trial structure**: three experiments — (V-A) static evader, k = 3, 4, 5 encirclement-ring adaptability (Fig. 11); (V-B) 6 pursuers vs. 2 evader Crazyflies, assignment visible at t = 9 s, near-surround at t = 11 s (Figs. 12–13); (V-C) obstacle avoidance with the boxes, p4 detours, both evaders surrounded (Figs. 14–15).
- **Parameters as printed (Tables V–VI, p. 5997)**: PPM gains m = 1, C_P^E = 3, C_P^P = 0.8, C_P^O = 0.5, C_E^P = 1, C_E^O = 0.5, d_c = 0.4 m, d_a = 0.8 m; DMK-WTA γ = 1.5, λ = 5, a = 0.005.
- **What was validated**: encirclement geometry (pursuers self-space on the d_a-ring by k), the full DMK-WTA + PPM pipeline in hardware, and the obstacle terms; a notable observation recorded on p. 5999: "an obstacle can be regarded as a static pursuer while encircling" (p2, p3, p6 encircle e2 with the obstacle's help).
- **Deviations / caveats (archive Sec. 7 obs. 7)**: evader positions come entirely from the external OptiTrack broadcast — onboard perception, localization latency, and communication dropouts are never exercised; evaders are identical Crazyflies running the same reactive law (speed-symmetric players); the fatigue speed drop is not demonstrated in hardware; distance curves fluctuate before t = 7.5 s because pursuers keep exchanging targets.
- Companion simulation tiers in the same paper (useful as the "sim → legged sim → aerial hardware" ladder): Webots R2020b with E-puck (5 × 5 m², 32 ms step, i5-4590, Tables I–II) and Gazebo 9/ROS Melodic with Aliengo legged robots (24 × 24 m², 0.5 s step, max speed 0.5 m/s, sensing 3 m, comms 5 m, Tables III–IV) with online re-allocation at t = 18 s/33 s and encirclement by t = 142 s.

### P059 — TARA-LLM (IEEE TII)
- **Platform**: six quad-rotor AAVs; motion-capture positioning at 100 Hz broadcast over WiFi; communication latency < 30 ms most of the time; control inputs computed on a ground control station (GCS) at 50 Hz as ROS nodes; TARA-LLM runs on the GCS via GPT-4 (temperature 0.0, top_p 0.1, max_tokens 2048) (Sec. VI, pp. 10–11).
- **Airframe (Table III)**: 200 × 200 × 85 mm; wheelbase 140 mm; 118 g with battery; WiFi; 3S 1300 mAh; hover accuracy ±0.05 m; max ascent/descent 2 m/s; max horizontal speed 5 m/s; 8 min flight time.
- **Scenario**: 3 pursuers vs. 3 evaders; goal circle radius 1.3 m; speeds deliberately reduced to v_P = 0.5 m/s, v_E = 0.3 m/s, capture radii r_i = 1.5 m "dictated by platform safety" (p. 11). Initial positions printed per agent.
- **What was validated**: the LLM-generated matching (P1→E1, P2→E2, P3 unassigned) executed end-to-end; E1, E2 captured, E3 reached the goal — "consistent with the matching", i.e., feasibility, not optimality.
- **Deviations / caveats**: the matching is generated **once at game start** because LLM latency precludes high-frequency replanning (open-loop assignment under feedback strategies); TC metric excludes API call latency; single trial; small speed ratios chosen for safety rather than to exercise the theory's α > 1 regime meaningfully.

### P060 — Strategy design of reach-avoid game: Geometric modeling and USV implementation (Ocean Engineering)
- **Site/conditions**: Jimei Lake, Xiamen, China; average wind 10 m/s, temperature 12 °C (Secs. 5–6, pp. 9–10).
- **Platforms (Table 7)**: attacker — 3 m aluminum catamaran (length 3.5 m, width 1.92 m, mass 1500 kg, endurance 4 h); defenders — identical 2 m carbon-fiber catamarans (length 2.1 m, mass 80 kg). Per vessel: GPS antenna, GNSS module, thrusters, master control unit, image/data + 4G transmission, battery (Figs. 15–16).
- **Sensing/compute**: off-board computer with MATLAB/Simulink + three radio transceivers; GNSS 5 Hz, centimeter-level; command updates 10 Hz (100 ms interval). **No lidar/camera/radar — decision-layer testing only; all states shared over radio** (the attacker would not broadcast its state in reality — the authors say so).
- **Game parameters (field)**: v_D1 = 0.95, v_D2 = 1.1, v_A = 0.8 m/s; ρ_D = 6 m; target radius ρ_T = 21 m.
- **Trials**: exactly two. DDR trial — both defenders converged onto the capture circle, d_A𝒯(t_f) = 9.42 m > 0 (interception outside the target). ADR trial — attacker reached the region, both defender distances 9.03 m > ρ_D = 6 m (≈3.03 m intrusion margin).
- **Deviations / caveats (archive)**: one trial per scenario; **no experimental baselines** — both trials use the proposed strategies only, so hardware validates implementability and the DDR/ADR classification, while comparative advantage is left to the 3000-run Monte Carlo in simulation; the identical 9.03 m defender distances are flagged as suspiciously symmetric (rounding or shared reporting artifact); robustness to 10 m/s wind gusts and 100 ms radio latency unquantified.

### P027 — Cooperative Path Planning for Multiplayer Reach-Avoid Games under Imperfect Observation (IEEE/ASME TMech)
- **Platform**: four autonomous ground vehicles; NOKOV motion-capture system with 24 cameras (misspelled "NOVOK" once, p. 11); workstation + onboard Nvidia TX1; control via ROS `cmd_vel`; MPC tracks the receding-horizon trajectories (Sec. 5.3, pp. 11–13).
- **Arena/parameters (Table 5)**: positions ±[4, 4] m; speeds pursuers ±0.25, evader ±0.20, target ±0.15 m/s; acceleration ±0.2 m/s²; 3-D game models downscaled to 2-D.
- **Information emulation**: mocap gives all true positions; Gaussian noise is added to the pursuer coalition's observation of the evader to emulate imperfect observation.
- **Trials**: 3 scenarios, snapshots at k = 0, 3, 6, 8 s (Fig. 9); evader captured while chasing the target, no intra-coalition collision. Video: bilibili (BV1bj411a7on).
- **Quantified deviation from models (Table 6, p. 13)**: the linear double-integrator plan vs. Ackermann-steered AGVs → tracking error average ≈ 0.02 m, maximum ≈ 0.05 m per agent across scenarios — the library's best hardware tracking-error quantification.
- **Caveat (archive obs. 2)**: the real-time configuration L = 10, H = 15, N_ibr = 3, N_m = 3 flagrantly violates the paper's own Lemma-1 sample-count prescription (≈646 for the printed ε, β), so the chance-constraint guarantee is void in hardware; the authors do not comment.

### P028 — Multiple Pursuers vs. One Evader Reach-Avoid Games with Asymmetric Observations (IEEE TSMC)
- **Platform**: three pursuer AGVs + one evader AGV; NOKOV mocap, 24 cameras; workstation runs planning; onboard Nvidia TX1; PID tracking of planned trajectories; ROS `cmd_vel` (Sec. "Hardware experiments", pp. 18614–18615).
- **Parameters**: φ_P = φ_E = 0.25 m (set from AGV physical structure and mocap accuracy); v_P = 0.25 m/s, v_E = 0.2 m/s; γ = 0.8. Initial positions of the four scenarios UNREPORTED in text (visible only in Fig. 11).
- **Trials**: four scenarios — 1–3 exercise OIS, EC, and PS respectively at low noise; Scenario 4 repeats Scenario 2's initial positions with σ = 0.05× nominal, demonstrating the predicted strategy escalation with observation uncertainty ("more pursuers are needed … higher level of uncertainty", p. 18615).
- **Caveats**: no quantitative tables (capture time, miss distance, tracking error); the evader's experimental strategy is only "a similar receding strategy", not exactly the printed (40)–(42); single trial per scenario; video on Bilibili.

### P029 — Adaptive Decision-Making in Attack-Defense Games With Bayesian Inference of Rationality Level (IEEE TSMC)
- **Platform**: three AGVs representing attacker, defender, and protected area; NOKOV mocap with 24 cameras ("NOVOK" typo once); workstation + onboard Nvidia TX1; ROS `cmd_vel`; MPC control (Sec. V-B, pp. 16565–16566).
- **Arena/parameters**: UNREPORTED (AGV specifications, speeds, and game-area dimensions — archive flags this explicitly).
- **Trials**: two illustrative games with different initial positions and initial beliefs (Figs. 8–9); belief trajectory shows level-0 falling within 1–10 s and level-2 converging by t = 4 s; planner-vs-actual trajectories stay close despite the Ackermann-vs-linear-model mismatch, which the authors acknowledge explicitly ("there must be tracking errors … which means our proposed algorithm can be used for practical applications", p. 16566).
- **Caveats**: number of trials and win statistics UNREPORTED; attacker fixed at level-2; no quantitative metrics.

### P014 — Collaborative Constrained Target-Reaching Control (reach-avoid pursuit of a constrained evader) (IEEE TAC)
- **Platform**: four custom ground robots; per robot: four JGB37-520 encoder motors, GY-85 attitude sensor, RPLIDAR-A1, Nvidia Jetson TX2, 12 V 9800 mAh LiPo; SLAM + AMCL for mapping/localization; centralized communication — each robot streams localization to a central server at 50 Hz, which returns relative positions at 50 Hz (Sec. VI, p. 8472).
- **Arena**: 6 m × 8 m; velocity limits consistent with simulation; **capture radius set equal to the robot's length to avoid physical collisions**; attacker human-operated.
- **Trials**: Fig. 11(a)–(c) individual PDG trials with target line inclined left/right/vertical; Fig. 11(d) PBG with a straight–arc–straight hybrid target line; Fig. 12 full CTR with obstacles (3 defenders vs. 1 attacker) — the attacker "continually tries to breach Ω_ch yet is guided to the target area". Video: youtu.be/6z8gUZ7F4GQ.
- **Caveats**: no quantitative experimental tables; capture-radius inflation (robot length) tightens the funnel constraint Σγ ≤ ℓ without discussion (archive obs. 5); human attacker makes repeatability impossible.

### P040 — Automation-Assisted Capture-the-Flag: A Differential Game Approach (IEEE TAES; BEARCAT testbed)
- **Platform**: human agents + computers + a quadrotor UAV + GPS-equipped smartphones (HTC Incredible); the quadrotor senses opponent locations; a PC laptop ground station stores the **precomputed** 4-D value function and partial derivatives, interpolates, and downlinks 2-D reachable-set slices plus the optimal heading; phones display positions, winning regions, and "a blue ring around the attacker … a direction to travel at max speed" (Sec. VII, pp. 1021–1026).
- **Arenas**: small-area 50 m × 50 m square with a 5 m deep return strip (walking 3 m/s, WiFi); large-area ≈ 200 m × 180 m with buildings as obstacles, running capped at 5 m/s, 3G with delays and packet loss (pp. 1024–1025).
- **Scope reduction**: only the flag-return stage was played, to shorten testing (p. 1023).
- **Trials**: "several" small-area trials + 2 deception trials; ≥ 1 large-area game (Fig. 9); 1 UAV-assisted search (Fig. 10). Quantitative outcome metrics: none — win/loss narrative only.
- **What was uniquely validated (and only here)**: (i) HJI winning-region displays as human decision support; (ii) **deception** — an aided attacker deliberately moving opposite to its guidance to pull the defender out of R_D, then reverting, winning repeatedly ("only successful with asymmetric guidance information", p. 1024); (iii) under both-aided play with buildings and 3G, "gameplay much more dependent on deception and managing opponent information, with the HJI guidance used as a decision support tool rather than the primary path-planning tool" (p. 1025); (iv) a new modality: using the HJI winning region as the worst-case adversary capability set to restrict a UAV's search (p. 1025–1026).
- **Caveats (authors + archive)**: single-digit trial counts; no success-rate statistics; simulated sensing in the UAV campaign (state transmitted only in line of sight); GPS-grade state assumed; the unaided-vs-aided win rates are narrative, not tabulated.

### P058 — Pursuit Strategies for Capture-the-Flag Games With Half-Plane Flag and Return Region (IEEE TCYB)
- **Platform**: five autonomous ground vehicles with Mecanum wheels and onboard Nvidia Jetson Nano; four reflective markers per AGV; motion capture at 100 Hz; positions broadcast over Wi-Fi; ground-station PC runs the pursuit strategy through ROS nodes; commands relayed to wheel MCUs; control inputs limited to x/y speeds to emulate simple motions (platform inherited from prior work [31], [37]) (Sec. VI-B, pp. 10–11).
- **Trials**: Experiment 1, 2 pursuers vs. 1 evader (v_E = 0.3 m/s, α = 1.2, r = 0.7 m; only P1 required); Experiment 2, 3 pursuers vs. 2 evaders (sequential matching {P1,P2}→E1, P3→E2; r = 0.7/0.6 m). Both captured; simulation vs. experiment trajectories shown together with "a similar pattern" as the fidelity criterion (Figs. 8–9).
- **Caveats**: no quantitative tracking-error or capture-time tables; v_P never printed (only ratios); flag height in the experimental arena UNREPORTED; low speeds, small arena, no disturbance/communication-failure tests.

### P064 — Heterogeneous Multiplayer Reach-Avoid Differential Game under Partial Information (IEEE/ASME TMech)
- **Platform**: NOKOV optical mocap, 24 cameras, sub-millimeter accuracy; each quadrotor carries a microcontroller (attitude stabilization) and a communication module; a central computer computes strategies and issues commands; fixed-altitude flight; captured quadrotors descend (p. 7).
- **Arena**: 12 m × 5 m (both simulation and experiment), target line 2 m; 2 pursuers vs. 2 evaders; v_P = 0.6 m/s, v_E = 0.2 m/s; heterogeneous sensing radii r1 = 2 m, r2 = 1 m; deployment positions x_opt printed (P2's matches Theorem 2 exactly; P1's printed 0.5 m offset above the predicted position is unexplained — flagged in archive obs. 4).
- **Trials**: one 2-vs-2 run, eight photo stages (Fig. 7) plus timestamped trajectory snapshots (Fig. 8: e.g., t = 25.67 s, 28.30 s) — deployment → detection → matching → capture, all four evader captures achieved.
- **Caveats**: boundary-critical initial conditions (evader starting on the barrier) deliberately excluded due to quadrotor control accuracy (p. 8); single trial; no baselines; no quantitative table; the archive also flags that the tangency geometry means most barrier points lie outside the D²-pursuer's sensing disk, so "inevitable detection" leans on implicit team-level closure.

### P067 — Optimal Defense Strategy under Partial Information in One-Versus-One Reach-Avoid Game (IEEE TCST)
- **Platform**: NOKOV mocap, 24 cameras, sub-millimeter position/velocity accuracy; quadrotors with microcontroller attitude stabilization + communication link; central computer runs the strategy; constant altitude, descend on capture (p. 7).
- **Arena**: 12 m × 5 m room (vs. infinite-plane theory); target line l = 2 m at y = 2; Case 1 r = 1 m (deploy at (0, 0.5), matching Theorem 2), Case 2 r = 2 m (deploy on the line midpoint (0, 2)).
- **Trials**: two. Case 1 experiment stamps at ≈1.54/4.30/7.67/9.34 s (Fig. 7) and sim-vs-exp trajectory panels at detection (exp 7.65 s vs. sim 8.30 s) and capture (exp 9.12 s vs. sim 9.30 s) (Fig. 8). Case 2 detection at exp 21.60 s vs. sim 19.10 s, capture 24.60 vs. 21.40 s (Fig. 9) — the long standoff detection of the barrier-coverage regime.
- **Caveats (authors')**: critical initial conditions on/near the barrier excluded because of quadrotor control accuracy; speed regulation transients ("the speed of the quadrotor is not unstable [sic] and has a non-negligible regulation time", p. 8) explain sim-vs-exp timing offsets; no baseline, no statistics; two exemplar trajectories per case.

### P079 — Hybrid Predictive Network-Based Multiagent Reconnaissance Game (IEEE TCDS)
- **Platform as recorded**: the paper's "physical experiments" tier (pp. 10–12, Figs. 11–13) runs **Gazebo + ROS (Kinetic)** with TurtleBot3-Basic agents, RPLIDAR A1 (0.1–12 m, 10 Hz, 0.5°); arena 4.0 × 2.5 × 0.5 m; speeds < 0.15 m/s; sensing 0.25/0.35 m; capture radius 0.15 m; central workstation receives all robot positions and computes commands, decision latency < 100 ms per step. Note for users of this file: the recorded platform is Gazebo — treat this as a high-fidelity robotics simulation tier, not outdoor hardware.
- **Trials and metrics**: 27 rounds (3 s task), 24 (10 s), 19 (20 s); success rates 0.8889 / 0.7626 / 0.7368 with Bernoulli variances 0.0988 / 0.1704 / 0.1944 — success decreases and variance grows with reconnaissance duration (accumulated prediction error), the paper's headline trade-off. (Archive notes 0.7626 is not k/24 for integer k — internal inconsistency.)
- **Caveat (archive obs. 7)**: the physical information structure is partly centralized (workstation receives all positions), softening the limited-perception narrative.

## 2. Cross-paper lessons

### What the community actually flies/drives
1. **Motion capture is the default localization layer.** Five of the eleven hardware papers use 24-camera NOKOV systems (P027, P028, P029, P064, P067) and one uses 16-camera OptiTrack at 0.1 mm (P084). Sub-millimeter/centimeter claims are routine (P064, P067 "sub-millimeter"; P060 GNSS "centimeter-level" outdoors at 5 Hz). Nobody in the library demonstrates the game strategies under onboard-only perception.
2. **Centralized ground stations are universal.** Every hardware paper computes strategies off-board: GCS at 50 Hz (P059), MATLAB/Simulink shore computer (P060), central workstation + ROS (P014, P027, P028, P029, P058, P064, P067, P079). Decision latency is reported by only two papers (< 30 ms WiFi, P059; < 100 ms/step, P079) and radio command rates by one (10 Hz, P060).
3. **Platforms cluster in three classes**: differential/Mecanum AGVs at 0.15–0.3 m/s (P014, P027, P028, P029, P058, P079-TurtleBot), micro-quadrotors at 0.2–0.6 m/s in 12 × 5 m rooms (P064, P067) or 118 g AAVs at ≤ 0.5 m/s (P059), and full-scale marine vessels outdoors (P060: 80–1500 kg catamarans at 0.8–1.1 m/s). One human-in-the-loop testbed (BEARCAT, P040) is the only outdoor, building-rich, GPS/3G study.
4. **Speeds and radii are scaled for safety, not for the theory.** Field papers cut speeds to 0.2–1.1 m/s and inflate capture radii (0.25 m AGV, P028; 0.6–0.7 m, P058; 6 m USV, P060; capture radius = robot length, P014; 1.3–1.5 m goal/capture circles, P059). The α > 1 speed-superiority regimes that much theory assumes are barely exercised; P059 explicitly says safety "dictated" the small ratios.

### What gets validated — and what does not
5. **Feasibility, not superiority.** Hardware trials overwhelmingly demonstrate "our strategy executes and wins this scenario" (P058, P064, P067, P028, P029) or "the pipeline closes" (P084, P059, P027). Only P014 and P040 hint at comparative claims, and neither tabulates hardware win rates. P060 is explicit: comparative advantage was left to simulation.
6. **Trial counts are 1–4 per scenario.** P060 (2 trials), P058 (2), P064 (1), P067 (2), P027 (3), P028 (4), P029 (2), P084 (3 experiments), P059 (1 game). The only statistical hardware evidence in the library is P079's 19–27 rounds per condition (and that tier is Gazebo). Single-trial evidence is the norm and the archive repeatedly flags it (P060 obs. 10, P064 obs. 7, P067 obs. 8).
7. **Boundary cases are excluded on purpose.** Both quadrotor papers (P064, P067) exclude evader-on-barrier initial conditions "due to quadrotor control accuracy" — exactly the cases where the theory is most delicate. State this exclusion explicitly in your own hardware section; reviewers from this community accept it.
8. **The sim-vs-experiment overlay is the standard fidelity claim.** Trajectory overlays with "a similar pattern" (P058), timestamped side-by-side panels (P064 Fig. 8, P067 Figs. 8–9), and photo sequences with clocks (P067 Fig. 7) are the accepted evidence style. Where quantified at all, it is tracking-error tables (P027 Table 6: ≈0.02 m average, ≈0.05 m max) or qualitative model-mismatch remarks (Ackermann vs. integrator: P027, P029; speed-regulation transients: P067).
9. **Model–hardware mismatch is acknowledged, never propagated.** Every AGV/quadrotor paper notes the linear-model vs. Ackermann/transient gap (P027 p. 13, P029 p. 16566, P067 p. 8) and then absorbs it into "trajectories still close". No paper bounds the effect of the mismatch on the guarantee (e.g., funnel tightening, P014 obs. 5).

### What typically breaks or is left broken
10. **Latency and rate limits appear only as printed constants** (< 30 ms, 100 Hz mocap, 10 Hz commands, < 100 ms/step) and are never analyzed against strategy update requirements; P060's 100 ms command interval with a 21 m target radius is the closest to a scale-mismatch discussion.
11. **Information-structure claims soften in hardware.** "Limited perception" becomes centralized state sharing (P079), observation noise is injected synthetically (P027, P028), and adversarial broadcast (an attacker hiding its state) is acknowledged and skipped (P060). The only genuine partial-information hardware exercise with asymmetric guidance is P040's deception finding — and it showed humans exploit value-function guidance itself as a deception channel.
12. **Environmental disturbance is unquantified.** P060 recorded 10 m/s wind but reports no disturbance metrics; P040 noted 3G delays/packet loss and building occlusion and turned them into findings (deception, decision support) rather than robustness statistics. If your hardware paper must survive review, quantify at least one disturbance channel as P027 quantified tracking error.
13. **Reproducibility artifacts recur**: system-name typos ("NOVOK"/NOKOV, P027/P029; P084's printed "3-meter"/"2-meter" vs. Table-7 3.5/2.1 m lengths), unexplained parameter offsets (P064's 0.5 m deployment discrepancy), and identical reported values (P060's twin 9.03 m distances). When writing up hardware, cross-check every printed platform number against your own table — these are the slips the archives caught.
